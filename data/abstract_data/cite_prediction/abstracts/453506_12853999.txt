Two-stage sampled learning theory on distributions
We study the distribution regression problem: regressing to a real-valued response from a probability distribution. Due to the inherent twostage sampleddifficulty of this important machine learning problem—in practise we only have samples from sampled distributions—very little is known about its theoretical properties. In this paper, we propose an algorithmically simple approach to tackle the distribution regression problem: embed the distributions to a reproducing kernel Hilbert space, and learn a ridge regressor from the embeddings to the outputs. Our main contribution is to prove that this technique is consistent in the two-stage sampled setting under fairly mild conditions (for probability distributions on locally compact Polish spaces on which kernels have been defined). The method gives state-of-the-art results on (i) supervised entropy learning and (ii) the prediction problem of aerosol optical depth based on satellite images.
