Characteristics and Expected Returns in Individual Equity Options
I study excess returns from selling individual equity option portfolios that are leverageadjusted monthly and delta-hedged daily. Strikingly, I find that several measures of risk rise by maturity, although expected returns decrease. Based on my analysis, I identify three new factors –level, slope, and value– in option returns, which together explain the cross-sectional variation in expected returns on option portfolios formed on moneyness, maturity and option value (the spread between historical volatility and the Black-Scholes implied volatility). This three-factor model helps explain expected returns on option portfolios formed on a variety of different characteristics that include carry, VRP, volatility momentum, idiosyncratic volatility, illiquidity, etc. While the level premium appears to be a compensation for marketwide volatility and jump shocks, theories of risk-averse financial intermediaries help us to understand the slope and the value premiums.
