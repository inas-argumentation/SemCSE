Fourier collision detection
In this paper, we investigate a new approach to narrowphase collision detection for rigid objects based on the Fourier transform. This new collision test scales with respect to accuracy, and we are able to rigorously establish an upper bound on the error of our test relating the Hausdorff metric to the number of Fourier coefficients used. Because our new form of the collision test is also a smooth inequality, it can be used as a holonomic unilateral constraint in many applications, such as path planning, rigid body dynamics, nesting or tool placement, replacing the need for more ad-hoc normal/contact based constraint solvers. Moreover, we also show how this constraint can be directly differentiated via Fourier multipliers with only a constant factor overhead, which leads to a simple method for constructing a Jacobian for both normal forces and rotational torques.
