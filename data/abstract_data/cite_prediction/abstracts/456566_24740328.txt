A 1.8-V 6.9-mW 120-fps 50-Channel Capacitive Touch Readout With Current Conveyor AFE and Current-Driven $\Delta \Sigma $ ADC
This paper presents an area- and energy-efficient readout for capacitive touch sensors. An analog front end (AFE) with current conveyor mitigates the requirements of front-end opamp and provides a differential current from adjacent channels, removing sensor’s baseline and interferences. The sensing current from the AFE is directly digitized by a current-driven 2nd-order <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$\Delta \Sigma $ </tex-math></inline-formula> analog-to-digital converter and its direct interfacing with the current signal achieves high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) without suffering voltage saturation in the AFE, and provides reconfigurable SNRs and frame rates with respect to oversampling ratio. An area-efficient sinc<sup>2</sup> filter, whose filter coefficients are commonly provided, enables full parallel implementation of <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$\Delta \Sigma $ </tex-math></inline-formula> ADCs. A 50-channel prototype IC is fabricated in a 0.18-<inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$\mu \text{m}$ </tex-math></inline-formula> CMOS process, occupying only 1.96 mm<sup>2</sup>. Using a 10.1-in <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$28 \times 50$ </tex-math></inline-formula> touch screen panel and a 3.3-V transmitter, this work achieves SNRs of 53.3 and 41.7 dB with finger and 1-mm-<inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$\phi $ </tex-math></inline-formula> stylus, respectively, while drawing only 6.9 mW from 1.8-V supply at 120 fps. This results in the state-of-the-art figure-of-merit of 0.11 and 0.41 nJ/step for finger and 1-mm-<inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$\phi $ </tex-math></inline-formula> stylus, respectively. Moreover, 500-dpi on-glass fingerprint sensor is verified with the same prototype IC and 15-V transmitter, and <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$70 \times 50$ </tex-math></inline-formula> fingerprint image is successfully captured with 150-<inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$\mu \text{m}$ </tex-math></inline-formula> cover glass.
