Postzygotic BRAF p.Lys601Asn Mutation in Phacomatosis Pigmentokeratotica with Woolly Hair Nevus and Focal Cortical Dysplasia.
TO THE EDITOR We have examined a patient with a subtype of phacomatosis pigmentokeratotica (PPK) who had agminated melanocytic nevi, epidermal nevus, woolly hair nevus (WHN) in a mosaic pattern, focal cortical dysplasia (FCD), and cerebral cavernous malformation. We report a postzygotic BRAF mutation in her epidermal and woolly hair nevi. Whereas codon 601 BRAF mutations are found in the cardiofaciocutaneous syndrome in a germline state, they had not been reported in mosaic neurocutaneous syndromes. PPK is defined as the co-occurrence of a verrucous or sebaceous epidermal nevus with a papular nevus spilus, also known as speckled lentiginous nevus (Happle et al., 1996). Extracutaneous neurologic features such as intellectual disability or seizures can be present. Heterozygous postzygotic mutations in the HRAS gene—c.37G>C (p.Gly13Arg) and c.182A>G (p.Gln61Arg)—have been reported both in sebaceous nevus and nevus spilus cells in PPK (Groesser et al., 2013) suggesting an early mutational event in a common progenitor cell. WHN consists of a patch of curly hairs in an otherwise straight scalp hair. In two patients with WHN and hyperkeratotic epidermal nevi, a mosaic HRAS c.34G>A (p.Gly12Ser) mutation—also found in a germline nonmosaic state in Costello syndrome—has been reported in affected skin (Levinsohn et al., 2014).
