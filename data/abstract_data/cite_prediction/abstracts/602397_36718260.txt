The Behavioral Immune System
Many animals engage in many behaviors that reduce their exposure to pathogens. Ants line their nests with resins that inhibit the growth of fungi and bacteria (Chapuisat, Oppliger, Magliano, & Christe, 2008). Mice avoid mating with other mice that are infected with parasitic protozoa (Kavaliers & Colwell, 1995). Animals of many kinds—from physiologically primitive nematode worms to neurologically sophisticated chimpanzees—strategically avoid physical contact with specific things (including their own conspecifics) that, on the basis of superficial sensory cues, appear to pose some sort of infection risk (Goodall, 1986; Kiesecker, Skelly, Beard, & Preisser, 1999; Schulenburg & Müller, 2004).
