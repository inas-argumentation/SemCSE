This technique works for rolls, and could be used as a baseline and a starting point for a stochastic reranking approach, taking, say, the top 30%, and reranking them based on additional local score context. However, the results are not as promising for struggles; this is probably because struggles tend to be in games with a generally flat curve. One possible alternative is to use a different score-related measure, e.g., a matrix of lead changes per time period. A second is to compare intervals with other intervals of the same duration in all games, rather than in the same game.
