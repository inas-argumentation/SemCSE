Bridging HMMs and RNNs through Architectural Transformations
A distinct commonality between HMMs and RNNs is that they both learn hidden 1 representations for sequential data. In addition, it has been noted that the backward 2 computation of the Baum-Welch algorithm for HMMs is a special case of the 3 back-propagation algorithm used for neural networks [6]. Do these observations 4 suggest that, despite their many apparent differences, HMMs are a special case 5 of RNNs? In this paper, we investigate a series of architectural transformations 6 between HMMs and RNNs, both through theoretical derivations and empirical 7 hybridization, to answer this question. In particular, we investigate three key design 8 factors—independence assumptions between the hidden states and the observation, 9 the placement of softmax, and the use of non-linearity—in order to pin down their 10 empirical effects. We present a comprehensive empirical study to provide insights 11 on the interplay between expressivity and interpretability with respect to language 12 modeling and parts-of-speech induction. 13
