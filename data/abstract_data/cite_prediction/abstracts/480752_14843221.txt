Textile-based, contactless ECG monitoring for non-ICU clinical settings
8 Abstract Even though it might be beneficial to have 9 continuous baseline data upfront, today’s hospitalized 10 patients are put on a vital signs monitor not earlier than 11 they become bed-ridden. The aim of this study is to show 12 the feasibility of a broader monitoring concept, embracing 13 many more subjects, by monitoring contactless vital signs 14 with sensors embedded in non-ICU settings. In order to 15 conduct the experiments, a system was designed to mea16 sure electrocardiograms (ECG) unobtrusively by capacitive 17 sensors in various patient fitments e.g., stretchers, hospital18 beds and wheelchairs. Contact ECG and contactless ECG 19 measurements by shielded textile electrodes are contrasted 20 to each other to display the potential of contactless moni21 toring. Our experiments demonstrate that the contactless 22 ECG recordings are of sufficient quality and later used for 23 further standard ECG analysis like QRS-complex detec24 tion. We believe that our results are motivating for the 25 potential future care facility featuring a personal, non26 obtrusive and yet permanent health monitoring system. 27
