Learning from Limited Demonstrations in High Dimensional Feature Spaces
Reinforcement learning (RL) has recently gained a lot of popularity partially due to the success of deep Q-learning (DQN) on the Atari suite and AlphaGo. In these online domains DQN-RL performs favorably thanks to its ability to continuously learn at super human speeds. Unfortunately, in many real world applications, such as in robotics, the learning rate is limited due to the speed at which the agent can interact with its environment. It is therefore of paramount importance to limit the amount of real world training required to achieve exemplary performance on any given task. This paper proposes running RL in a virtual simulator which is aesthetically and kinematically similar to the real world agent and environment before continuing to train on the robot itself. Furthermore, often the reward function required for RL is not intuitive to design. In the application of robotics it is desirable for the reward function to be chosen such that the agent behaves similarly to how a human would for the same task. Learning the reward function from human demonstrations, known as Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL), is one way to achieve this. IRL usually requires a lot of training examples from an agent and environment that are aesthetically similar to those on which RL is to be performed. Often obtaining many training examples is costly in time and money and in some instances gaining demonstrations from aesthetically similar robots is unachievable. This paper investigates a Max-Entropy Inverse Reinforcement Learning approach that leverages the representational power of Google’s pretrained deep convolutional neural network (CNN), Inception, to learn a reward function from a limited number of demonstrations (in many cases one) and an agent with a different embodiment to the one being trained on (in this paper a human hand as opposed to robot arm).
