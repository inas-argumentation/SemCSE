Artful thinking and Augmented Reality in kindergarten: technology contributions to the inclusion of socially underprivileged children in creative activities
This paper presents a set of creative thinking activities which took place in a low income area kindergarten of Piraeus, complying with the principles of Harvard's Artful Thinking Program. The aim of this research study is to examine the contribution of the Augmented Reality application Aurasma to the children's participation levels. All data are based on class observation of learner behavior and attitudes before/after the introduction of AR and preliminary results reveal that the use of the application encouraged learner involvement in creating stories, attracted more student interest in consequent similar activities and increased participation levels of 'quiet' children, especially those who face learning challenges due to their poor socio-economic background, allowing to hope that AR implementation promotes the inclusion of socially underprivileged children in activities leading to the development of high level thinking.
