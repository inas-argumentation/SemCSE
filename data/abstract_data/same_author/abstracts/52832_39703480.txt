Skeletal Structural Descriptions
In this paper we explore the idea of characterizing sentences by the shapes of their structural descriptions only; for example, in the case of context free grammars, by the shapes of the derivation trees only. Such structural descriptions will be called skeletons . A skeleton exhibits all of the grouping structure (phrase structure) of the sentence without naming the syntactic categories used in the description. The inclusion of syntactic categories as variables is primarily a question of economy of description. Every context free grammar is strongly equivalent to a skeletal grammar, in a sense made precise in the paper. Besides clarifying the role of skeletons in mathematical linguistics, we show that skeletal automata provide a characterization of local sets, remedying a “defect” in the usual tree automata theory. We extend the method of skeletal structural descriptions to other forms of tree describing systems. We also suggest a theoretical basis for grammatical inference based on grouping structure only.
