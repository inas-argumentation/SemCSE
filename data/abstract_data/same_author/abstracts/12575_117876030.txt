Azimuthal Distributions of Particles and Projectile Fragments Produced in High-Energy Nucleus-Nucleus Collisions
Azimuthal distributions of the particles and projectile fragments produced in high-energy nucleus-nucleus collisions are important measurements of collective motion and, in particular, the directed and elliptic flows. The flows reflect the anisotropy of transverse particle emission. When two nuclei collide at non-zero impact parameter, high pressure is developed at the impact. The matter will have a tendency to flow in the direction where the pressure gradient is larger. Since the discovery of flow [1, 2], a lot of experimental results have been obtained. The azimuthally anisotropic flow in nucleus-nucleus collisions at Bevalac/SIS energies has been studied in detail by the Plastic Ball experiment [3], EOS [4], FOPI [5], and KAOS [6] Collaborations. The E877 experiment was the first to extend flow measurements to energies above the Bevalac [7]. At AGS and SPS energies, the flow effects have now been studied by the E877 [8], E895 [9], E866/E917 [10], NA49 [11], and WA98 [12] Collaborations. Simultaneously, much theoretical work, e.g., [13, 14], has been devoted to understand the mechanism responsible for the flows.
