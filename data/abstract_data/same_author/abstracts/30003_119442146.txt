Determination of theta(23) in long-baseline neutrino oscillation experiments with three-flavor mixing effects
We examine the accuracy of ${\ensuremath{\theta}}_{23}$ determination in future long-baseline (LBL) ${\ensuremath{\nu}}_{\ensuremath{\mu}}$ disappearance experiments in the three-flavor mixing scheme of neutrinos. Despite that the error of ${sin﻿}^{2}2{\ensuremath{\theta}}_{23}$ is indeed a few percent level at around the maximal mixing, we show that the error of physics variable ${s}_{23}^{2}$ is large, $\ensuremath{\delta}({s}_{23}^{2})/{s}_{23}^{2}\ensuremath{\simeq}10\ensuremath{-}20%$, depending upon regions of ${\ensuremath{\theta}}_{23}$. The errors are severely affected by the octant degeneracy of ${\ensuremath{\theta}}_{23}$, and $\ensuremath{\delta}({s}_{23}^{2})$ is largely amplified by the Jacobian factor relating these two variables in a region near the maximal mixing. The errors are also affected by the uncertainty due to the unknown value of ${\ensuremath{\theta}}_{13}$; $\ensuremath{\delta}({s}_{23}^{2})$ is doubled at off maximal in the second octant of ${\ensuremath{\theta}}_{23}$ where the effect is largest. To overcome this problem, we discuss combined analysis with ${\ensuremath{\nu}}_{e}$ appearance measurement in LBL experiments, or with reactor measurement of ${\ensuremath{\theta}}_{13}$. For possible relevance of subleading effects even in the next generation LBL experiments, we give a self-contained derivation of the survival probability to the next to leading order in ${s}_{13}^{2}$ and $\ensuremath{\Delta}{m}_{21}^{2}/\ensuremath{\Delta}{m}_{31}^{2}$.
