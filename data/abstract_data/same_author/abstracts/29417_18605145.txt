Limits on the Isotropic Diffuse Flux of Ultrahigh Energy {gamma} Radiation
Diffuse ultrahigh energy {gamma} radiation can arise from a variety of astrophysical sources, including the interaction of 10{sup 20} eV cosmic rays with the 2.7K microwave background radiation or the collapse of topological defects created in the early Universe. We describe a sensitive search for diffuse {gamma} rays at ultrahigh energies using the Chicago Air Shower Array{endash}Michigan Muon Array experiment. An isotropic flux of radiation is not detected, and we place stringent upper limits on the fraction of the {gamma}-ray component relative to cosmic rays ({lt}10{sup {minus}4}) at energies from 5.7{times}10{sup 14} to 5.5{times}10{sup 16} eV. This result represents the first comprehensive constraint on the {gamma}-ray flux at these energies. {copyright} {ital 1997} {ital The American Physical Society}
