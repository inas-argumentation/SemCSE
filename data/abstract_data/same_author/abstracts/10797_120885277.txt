Demodulation and detection of correlations in a beam by a scattering detector
The demodulation and the detection of correlations in a beam using a detector that scatters the beam is discussed employing a localized wave packet description of the beam. It is shown that a simple very general formula for the spectral excitation function of the detector can be given; by spectral excitation function we mean the rate of excitation of the detector to a given level. The formula is derived without making specific assumptions on the nature of the detector. The spectral excitation function is naturally decomposed as a sum of four terms: Two of these (the B terms in what follows) depend only on the wavefunction and stochastic distribution of the incident beam; they are proportional to the square (and higher powers) of the flux and contain all the information on the self-beat, Forrester-type effects and demodulation. More precisely, one of these B terms contains the information on the demodulation and the other on the beat effects. The remaining two terms (to be called the A terms) describe the conventional inelastic scattering (proportional to the flux) and some coherent corrections to it (proportional to higher powers of the flux): These A terms behave as noise if the signal of interest is that described by the B terms. The theory is illustrated with many examples to known situations; particular attention is given to some conceptual questions involved.
