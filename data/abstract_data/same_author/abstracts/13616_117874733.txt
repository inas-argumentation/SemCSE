Missing Transverse Energy Reconstruction in the ATLAS Calorimeter
A very good missing transverse energy (ETMiss) measurement is a crucial requirement for the study of many physics channels at the LHC, including but not limited to Standard Model W or top production, production of Higgs bosons decaying to taus, and SUSY events, for example. The most important contribution to this measurement in the ATLAS detector comes from the calorimeters, which provide near hermetic energy reconstruction up to pseudo‐rapidities of |eta|<5. The transverse energy components reconstructed in individual calorimeter cells and clusters of cells, in addition to corrections for losses in inactive materials and cracks, are the basis of the EtMiss calculation. Additional refinement can be achieved by including specific calibrations depending on calorimeter signal shapes used in hadronic calibration approaches, and/or identified particle and jet signals, for example, all applied at the level of the cell and cluster signals.
