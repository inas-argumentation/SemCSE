Cosmological Parameters from Microwave Background Anisotropies and Galaxy Clustering
We compare the latest observations of Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) Anisotropics with the theoretical predictions of the standard scenario of structure formation. Assuming a primordial power spec- trum of adiabatic perturbations, we find that the total energy density is constrained to be Ώtot = 1.02 ± 0.06 while the energy density in baryon and Cold Dark Matter (CDM) are Ώ b h 2 = 0.021 ± 0.04 and Ώ cdm h 2 = 0.12 ±0.02, (all at 68% C.L.) respectively. The primordial spectrum is consistent with scale invariance, (ns = 0.96 ± 0.06) and the age of the universe is to = 14.4 ± 0.7 Gyrs (1 • a). Adding in- formation from the 2dF galaxy redshift survey (2dFGRS) and Type I-a Supernovae, we find strong evidence for a cosmological constant ΩΛ=0.72 −0.06 +0.08 and a value of the Hubble parameter h = 0.69 ± 0.10. Restricting this combined analysis to flat universes, we put constraints on possible ‘extensions’ of the standard scenario. A gravity wave con- tribution to the quadrupole anisotropy is limited to be r < 0.43 (95% cl.). A constant equation of state for the dark energy component is bound by −1.62 < wQ < −0.72 (95% c.L). We constrain the effective relativistic degrees of freedom N v < 6.5 and the neutrino chemical potential −0.01 ≤ ξ e ≤ 0.20 and |ξ μ,τ| ≤ 2.5 (massless neutrinos).
