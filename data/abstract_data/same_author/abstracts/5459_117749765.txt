Experimental Study on the Scaling Law of a Self-Modulated Laser Wakefield Accelerator
We performed experiments to obtain an empirical scaling of a self‐modulated laser wakefield accelerator (SMLWFA) by using laser pulses at a laser power of 2–6 TW and pulse duration of 50 fs. By decreasing the electron density from 1.5 × 1020 cm−3 to 4 × 1019 cm−3 and increasing the laser power, the energy of quasimonoenergetic components increased from 7 MeV to 25 MeV. We obtained the scaling law, according to which the energy gain is inversely proportional to the electron density and proportional to the laser power when the acceleration length is greater than the dephasing length of acceleration. Satellite peaks in the forward‐scattering spectrum suggested that the excitation of a nonlinear wakefield was closely related to the quasimonoenergetic acceleration of electrons.
