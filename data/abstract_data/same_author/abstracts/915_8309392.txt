The ethics and aesthetics of the image interface
Now that we have developed and are introducing media-rich computer human interfaces, it is important to develop a taxonomy that can help us appreciate the power and value of computer systems in order to better quantify them and assess their creative and ethical impact. To date the application of semiosis has been to natural languages and human-human communications. The author suggests that this can now be applied to artificial languages and in particular, their use in human computer interactions, imaging and visualization. The semiology of Charles Sanders Peirce can be applied to this field and may be used to assess the aesthetic (creative) and ethical implications of computer human interface (CHI), imaging and visualization technology and the way it is being developed and used.
