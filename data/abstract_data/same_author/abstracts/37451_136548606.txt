Analysis of the Performance of the Eight Superconducting Quadrupoles for the Lep Low-Beta Insertions
The LEP low-beta quadrupoles are iron-free superconducting magnets having a warm bore of 120 mm in diameter, an effective length of 2 m and a nominal gradient of 36 T m−l. The coils form a two-block approximation of the cosine 2θ distribution, and are wound from monolithic multifilamentary NbTi conductor; mechanical prestress is obtained by means of aluminium shrinking rings. Following manufacture and appraisal of a prototype magnet, eight series units have been produced by industry and thoroughly tested and measured prior to installation into LEP. In this paper, a brief description of the manufacturing technique is given, and the results of tests and measurements are presented and compared with the performance expected from calculations and previous experience. The analysis reveals the tolerances on field quality which can reasonably be obtained with this type of construction.
