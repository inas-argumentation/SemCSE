The Physics of Supernovae and the Equation of State of Dense Nuclear Matter
The collapse of large stars leading to supernova explosions continues until the core reaches supranuclear densities. The large energy of SN(1987)A (Shelton) implies a soft equation of state, which makes it possible to transfer a large amount of energy from the core to the shock wave which carries through the explosion. At nuclear matter density the Landau forward scattering sum rule puts constraints on the Fermi liquid parameters strongly favouring a low-compression modulus. Whereas conventional nuclear physics calculations give a stiffer equation of state than required for supernovae at higher densities, it is pointed out that these do not include meson masses which decrease with increasing nuclear-matter density. This decrease in meson masses is implied by chiral symmetry. With decreasing meson masses the standard mechanism for saturation disappears, and saturation must be obtained from higher-order loops. These give the possibility, at least, of obtaining a low-compression modulus and soft equation of state at higher densities, because of their smooth dependence on density. In any case, this soft EOS seems to be required by the supernova explosion.
