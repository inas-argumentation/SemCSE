Testing CPT Symmetry with Current and Future CMB Measurements
In this paper, we use the current and future cosmic microwave background (CMB) experiments to test the Charge-Parity-Time Reversal (CPT) symmetry. We consider a CPT-violating interaction in the photon sector L-cs similar to p(mu)A(upsilon)(F) over tilde (mu upsilon), which gives rise to a rotation of the polarization vectors of the propagating CMB photons. By combining the 9 yr WMAP, BOOMERanG 2003, and BICEP1 observations, we obtain the current constraint on the isotropic rotation angle (alpha) over bar = -2.12 +/- 1.14 (1 sigma), indicating that the significance of the CPT violation is about 2 sigma. Here, we particularly take the systematic errors of CMB measurements into account. Then, we study the effects of the anisotropies of the rotation angle [Delta alpha((n) over cap)] on the CMB polarization power spectra in detail. Due to the small effects, the current CMB polarization data cannot constrain the related parameters very well. We obtain the 95% C.L. upper limit of the variance of the anisotropies of the rotation angle C-alpha(0) a and Delta alpha((n) over cap). Benefited from the high precision of Planck data, the constraints of the rotation angle can be significantly improved.
