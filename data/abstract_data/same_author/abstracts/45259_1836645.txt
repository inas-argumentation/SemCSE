Computing the visibility graph of points within a polygon
We study the problem of computing the visibility graph defined by a set <i>P</i> of <i>n</i> points inside a polygon <i>Q</i>: two points <i>p</i>,<i>q</i> ε <i>P</i> are joined by an edge if the segment ‾<i><sub>pq</sub></i> ⊂ <i>Q</i>. Efficient output-sensitive algorithms are known for the case in which <i>P</i> is the set of all vertices of <i>Q</i>. We examine the general case in which <i>P</i> is an arbitrary set of points, interior or on the boundary of <i>Q</i> and study a variety of algorithmic questions. We give an output-sensitive algorithm, which is nearly optimal, when <i>Q</i> is a simple polygon. We introduce a notion of "fat" or "robust" visibility, and give a nearly optimal algorithm for computing visibility graphs according to it, in polygons <i>Q</i> that may have holes. Other results include an algorithm to detect if there are any visible pairs among <i>P</i>, and algorithms for output-sensitive computation of visibility graphs with distance restrictions, <i>invisibility</i> graphs, and rectangle visibility graphs.
