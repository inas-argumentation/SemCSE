Benefits of religious beliefs for cancer patients: A response to Dawkins and Hitchens
Two brilliantly written and perhaps surprisingly popular books, The God Delusion by Richard Dawkins and God Is Not Great: How Religion Poisons Everything by Christopher Hitchens, discuss in great detail the potential dangers associated with organized religions and religious beliefs [1,2]. Both writers have unquestionably—and quite eloquently— expressed a coherent perspective on the role of religion in the daily life of individuals and its disconcerting impact on both history and our current geopolitical environment. In their respective books, these authors forcefully discuss how many individuals—including, apparently, themselves— lack a fundamental requirement for a belief in God in the face of serious health concerns, such as advanced and progressive malignant disease. Unfortunately, however, both authors either fail to appreciate, or have chosen not to acknowledge, the extraordinary importance that a very “personal God” may play in the lives of many individuals forced to deal with these profoundly life-changing situations.
