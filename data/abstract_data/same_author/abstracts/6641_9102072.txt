Bilarge neutrino mixing from supersymmetry breaking: The Role of high-scale parameters
We show how a bilarge neutrino mixing pattern can arise in a broken supersymmetric theory, in such a way that the difference with quark mixing is explained. Our basic assumption is that this difference is due to nonrenormalizable interactions involving hidden sector fields. Such interactions lend lepton number violating mass terms to both the right-handed neutrino and sneutrino fields. A superpotential containing an array of gauge singlet chiral superfields, together with a combination of seesaw and radiative contributions lead us to a neutrino mass matrix which, for some natural parametric choices, can have the structure needed for bilarge mixing. Allowed ranges of parameters in the gauge singlet sector are delineated, corresponding to each of the cases of normal hierarchy, inverted hierarchy and degenerate neutrinos.
