Remarks on Machines, Sets, and the Decision Problem
Publisher Summary This chapter presents the remarks on machines, sets, and the decision problem. The basic distinction between monogenic and polygenic systems corresponds to the contrast of calculations with proofs, functions with relations, and machines with production systems. In calculations, there is generally a fixed procedure, such that the answer is completely determined by the question. In addition, in looking for a proof of a given statement in a given formal system, there are an unbounded number of choices at each stage. A monogenic proof procedure—such as the Herbrand expansion procedure for the predicate calculus—need not give a decision procedure. On the other hand, a monotone system, such that by some criterion the conclusion is always longer or more complex than the premises, is always decidable when there are finitely many rules only.
