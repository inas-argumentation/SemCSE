Assessment of vagal activity during transcutaneous vagus nerve stimulation in mice.
To the Editor: Huston et al. (1) recently described that transcutaneous vagus nerve stimulation in mice can efficiently reduce systemic tumor necrosis factor and high mobility group box 1 levels and improve survival during lethal endotoxemia and polymicrobial sepsis, even 24 hrs after the onset of disease. The research group developed a very sophisticated method of activating the efferent vagus nerve via transcutaneous vagus nerve stimulation by alternating direct pressure applied perpendicularly and directly adjacent to the border of the trachea. The stimulation frequencies were 0.5 Hz, 1 Hz, and 2 Hz. As an indicator of specific vagal signaling to A-fibers, the authors measured heart rate in animals receiving vagus nerve dissection, transcutaneous vagus nerve stimulation, or electrical vagus nerve stimulation and observed no significant effects on heart rate with any of these treatments. The question arises whether this concept of anti-inflammatory action of vagal stimulation in septic mice could also completely work in the clinical arena. Patients with septic multiorgan dysfunction syndrome have an attenuated vagal modulation of heart rate which is a strong predictor of poor outcome (2, 3). To date it is not fully understood at what level (afferent, central or efferent fibers, cardiac receptors) this suppression is located. We are wondering if the vagal suppression at the efferent site or at cardiac level—like inhibition of the vagally modified pacemaker current lf. by endotoxin (4)—the might make vagal stimulation less efficient in its anti-inflammatory effect than under normal nonseptic conditions. By reviewing the results of the authors’ very stimulating trial, one may ask whether it would be possible to use the electrocardiogram strips also for analysis of the heart rate variations over time (“heart rate variability”), which provide the utmost useful information concerning the ability of the vagus to modulate heart rate even in mice model (5). Additionally, this approach might provide information about whether the grade of inflammation correlates with the parasympathetic modulation of heart rate in mice. ACKNOWLEDGMENT
