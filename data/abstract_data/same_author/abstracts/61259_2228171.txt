Tumour lysis syndrome after splenic irradiation in a patient with JAK2 V617F post-polycythaemia vera myelofibrosis.
SirdWereport a caseof a66-year-oldmanwhodeveloped tumour lysis syndrome (TLS) after a single 1 Gy fraction of splenic irradiation. He was diagnosed with polycythaemia vera aged 48 years and treated with regular venesection, hydroxycarbamide (formerly hydroxyurea) and prednisolone for many years. He progressed to post-polycythaemia myelofibrosis and was found to have the JAK2 V617F mutation. He developed splenomegaly with marked abdominal discomfort and attended for palliative splenic irradiation in February 2007. On examination he had a palpable spleen extending to the right iliac fossa. He was conventionally simulated and planned for weekly 1 Gy fractions using anterioreposterior parallel-opposed 6 MV photon beams. The field size was 34.5 22 cm. One week after his first treatment he complained of nausea and abdominal pain. An examination revealed a reduction in spleen size and his treatment fieldwas reduced to 27.5 20 cm. Blood results showed the following: haemoglobin 8.5 g/dl, platelets 587 109/l, white cell count 93.2 109/l, neutrophils 79.2 109/l, blasts 2.8 109/l, potassium 6.0 (upper limit of normal 5.0) mmol/l, phosphate 2.96 mmol/l (upper limit of normal 1.55), corrected calcium 1.86 mmol/l (lower limit of normal 2.10), urea 33.6 mmol/l (upper limit of normal 8.8), creatinine 232 mmol/l (upper limit of normal 110). His electrolyte abnormalities were in keeping with laboratory TLS as per the CairoeBishop definition [1]. He was admitted and treated with intravenous fluids and allopurinol. His blood results improved as follows: potassium 4.6 mmol/l, phosphate 1.36 mmol/l, urea 8.5 mmol/l, creatinine 113 mmol/l and he was discharged. A national audit showed that painful splenomegaly is the most common indication for splenic irradiation in theUK [2]. Anterioreposterior parallel-opposed fields are used by 58% of clinicians, 4e6 MV photon energies by 68% and weekly fractionation regimens by 35%, with doses per fraction ranging from 0.2 to 2.0 Gy. The most common side-effects observed are nausea, vomiting and pancytopenia. TLS is an oncological emergency usually caused by lysis of tumour cells resulting in rapid release of potassium, purine nucleic acids and phosphorus, leading to hyperkalaemia, hyperuricaemia and hyperphosphataemia with secondary hypocalcaemia [1]. These metabolic abnormalities can subsequently lead to acute renal failure (ARF) often requiring haemodialysis. These complications may result in multiple organ failure and death. TLS typically occurs after chemotherapy for highly proliferative haematological malignancies, but can occur with steroids, radiation or spontaneously [1]. The current treatment of TLS includes the use of recombinant urate oxidase (Rasburicase), which rapidly reduces uric acid levels and the risk of ARF [3]. There have been two other published reports of TLS after splenic irradiation. A 72-year-old man with non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma developed TLS and pancytopenia 3 days after receiving 5 Gy splenic radiation in 10 consecutive daily fractions and died from massive gastrointestinal blood loss [4]. A 74-year-old manwith chronic myeloid leukaemia was treated with hydroxycarbamide, hydration and allopurinol for 3 days before commencing splenic irradiationwith daily 0.5 Gy treatments [5]. After three fractions of radiotherapy he developed TLS and ARF requiring haemodialysis. Polycythaemia vera is a chronic myeloproliferative neoplasm that causes erythrocytosis leading to splenomegaly and myelofibrosis in 5e15% of cases [6]. The point mutation JAK2 V617F constitutively activates JAK2, a cytoplasmic tyrosine kinase essential for erythropoietin cell signalling, leading to unchecked proliferation of erythrocyte precursors [7]. Inhibitors of JAK2 are becomingavailable andwill beusedfirst for the treatment ofmyelofibrosis. For instance, in a phase I/II trial of over 100 patients with myelofibrosis, INCB018424, a JAK2 inhibitor, led to a rapid reduction of splenomegaly in 93% of patients with associated improvements in constitutional symptoms, includingpruritus, night sweats and fatigue [8]. Phase III trials of JAK2 inhibitors are underway.
