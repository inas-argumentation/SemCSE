Phenomenological Lagrangians in quantum field theory
ConclusionsIn the foregoing sections we have shown how one can construct an effective interaction in a theory of quantized fields and we have considered some of its properties. Above all, it should be noted that the concept of an effective interaction arises naturally in field theory and, as follows from Section 2, an effective action can always be constructed from given Green's functions. This quantity gives an exhaustive characterization of the theory, i.e., it can be used to recover all the Green's functions [information about the two-point Green[s function is excluded from the functional S in Eq. (13)]. The effective action is not only a natural but, in a number of problems, a helpful concept. We have already mentioned how the functional S' defined by Eq. (14) is the most adequate quantity for the description of spontaneous symmetry breaking [2]. in accordance with Section 3 it is just this quantity (or rather, its limiting value) that characterizes the classical limit and the corresponding classical action (in the case of massless fields). Finally, it is the symmetry of the phenomenological action that is experimentally observable. This is especially important for so-called nonlinear realizations of symmetries that relate vertices with a different number of ends since, in this case, one does not, strictly speaking, know the relationships between the unrenormalized coupling constants (the symmetry of a quantizable Larrangian) and the renormalized coupling constants (the symmetry of the effective Lagrangian) because of the unrenormalizable nature of the interaction.
