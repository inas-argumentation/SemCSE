Instabilities of magnetically charged black holes.
The stability of the magnetically charged Reissner-Nordstroem black hole solution is investigated in the context of a theory with massive charged vector mesons. By exploiting the spherical symmetry of the problem, the linear perturbations about the Reissner-Nordstroem solution can be decomposed into modes of definite angular momentum [ital J]. For each value of [ital J], unstable modes appear if the horizon radius is less than a critical value that depends on an anomalous magnetic moment coupling [ital g] and the monopole magnetic charge [ital q]/[ital e]. It is shown that such a critical radius exists (except in the case [ital q]=1/2 with 0[le][ital g][le]2), provided only that the vector meson mass is not too close to the Planck mass. The value of the critical radius is determined numerically for a number of values of [ital J]. The instabilities found here imply the existence of stable solutions with nonzero vector fields ( hair'') outside the horizon; unless [ital q]=1 and [ital g][gt]0, these will not be spherically symmetric.
