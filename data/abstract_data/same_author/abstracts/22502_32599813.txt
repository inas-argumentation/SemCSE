Shell-model plus Hartree-Fock calculations for the neutron-rich Ca isotopes
Nuclear structure properties are mainly determined by the spacing of the single-particle states in the mean field. A sufficiently large gap in the spacing between spherical energy levels gives rise to a closed-shell structure when the levels are filled up to the gap. The properties of these closed-shell nuclei and those around it are dominated by the singleparticle degrees of freedom. When the levels are closely spaced, various collective properties associated with the scattering and correlation of the nucleons among the closely spaced levels are important. This leads to BCS correlations for identical nucleons and to vibrational and deformed correlations between valence protons and neutrons. In a spherical shell-model calculation one starts out near one of the closed-shell nuclei, and the collective properties evolve as valence nucleons are added.
