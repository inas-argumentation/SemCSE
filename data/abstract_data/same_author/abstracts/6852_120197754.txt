Primary Ionization Measurement in a Large Avalanche Chamber for Free Quark Detection
The WA44 experiment is a search for free quarks (1/3, 1/2) produced in high energy v interactions. High detection efficiency for quarks is achieved by measurement of primary ionization in a triggered large avalanche chamber which, moreover, provides the high spatial resolution required for high multiplicity jet-type events. The chamber is 2.35 × 1.25 × 0.6 m3 and is flushed with a mixture of pure He-Ne (80-20%). The chamber is photographed with 3 cameras using image intensifiers giving 1.4 mm resolution in space. The optical gain is such that the chamber can be operated well below the streamer threshold. In order to obtain precise primary ionization measurements and high rejection coefficient for quark detection against charge 1 particles, a good high voltage pulse stability (< 1% FWHM) and a long memory time (at least 100 μs) are essential and have been achieved. Data taking is in progress. Preliminary results show that the avalanche chamber is an excellent triggerable detector for combining good spatial multitrack resolution and accurate primary ionization measurement.
