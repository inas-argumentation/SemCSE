Experiments in the longitudinal phase space with the CERN electron storage and accumulation ring (Cesar)
Abstract The most important of the experiments concerning the longitudinal phase space performed with the CERN electron storage ring model are described and the results are confronted with theory. The “stacking efficiency” for the accumulation of a large number of sequentially injected pulses into a stack was measured as a function of the number of pulses for two filled buckets per pulse as well as for one filled and one suppressed bucket (“missing bucket” scheme). The observation of anomalies in stacking experiments led to a diffusion model for the bucket separatrix in the presence of frequency noise and a series of experiments confirmed this theoretical model. Radio-frequency trapping experiments reveiled the fact that during the first 2 ms after injection a strong blow up of the energy spread, from initially 0.3 keV to about 2 to 7 keV, occured.
