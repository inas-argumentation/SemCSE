The Primeval Populations of the Ultra-faint Dwarf Galaxies
We present new constraints on the star formation histories of the ultra-faint dwarf (UFD) galaxies, using deep 
photometry obtained with the Hubble Space Telescope (HST). A galaxy class recently discovered in the Sloan 
Digital Sky Survey, the UFDs appear to be an extension of the classical dwarf spheroidals to low luminosities, 
offering a new front in efforts to understand the missing satellite problem. They are the least luminous, most dark-matter-dominated, and least chemically evolved galaxies known. Our HST survey of six UFDs seeks to determine if these galaxies are true fossils from the early universe. We present here the preliminary analysis of three UFD galaxies: Hercules, Leo IV, and Ursa Major I. Classical dwarf spheroidals of the Local Group exhibit extended star 
formation histories, but these three Milky Way satellites are at least as old as the ancient globular cluster M92, with no evidence for intermediate-age populations. Their ages also appear to be synchronized to within ∼1 Gyr of each 
other, as might be expected if their star formation was truncated by a global event, such as reionization.
