Better Bounds on the Accommodating Ratio for the Seat Reservation Problem
We investigate the problem of giving seat reservations on-line. We assume that a train travels from a start station to an end station, stopping at k stations, including the first and last. Passengers may attempt to make reservations at any point before they get on the train, and reservations can be made going from any station to any later station. All algorithms under consideration must be "fair", i.e., they are not allowed to reject a request for a reservation which could be made at the time. The "accommodating ratio" compares the performance of an on-line algorithm to that of the optimal off-line algorithm, under the assumption that the only sequences of requests allowed are sequences for which the optimal off-line algorithm could accommodate all requests. The train has a fixed number of seats, and we first consider the case in which all tickets have the same cost, and the seat reservation system attempts to maximize income. We prove improved upper bounds on the performance of First-Fit and Best-Fit, showing that in the worst case, neither algorithm does significantly better than any fair deterministic algorithm would. We also improve the upper bounds on the possible performance of any "fair" deterministic or randomized algorithm. A specific randomized algorithm is also considered. Finally, we consider the case in which the cost of a ticket is proportional to the distance traveled.
