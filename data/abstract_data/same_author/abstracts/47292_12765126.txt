Size-time complexity of Boolean networks for prefix computations
The <italic>prefix problem</italic> consists of computing all the products <italic>x</italic><subscrpt>0</subscrpt><italic>x</italic><subscrpt>1</subscrpt>…<italic>x</italic><subscrpt><italic>j</italic></subscrpt> (<italic>j</italic>=0, …, <italic>N</italic> - 1), given a sequence x = (<italic>x</italic><subscrpt>0</subscrpt>, <italic>x</italic><subscrpt>1</subscrpt>, …, <italic>x</italic><subscrpt><italic>N</italic> - 1</subscrpt>) of elements in a semigroup. In this paper we completely characterize the size-time complexity of computing prefixes with <italic>boolean networks</italic>, which are synchronized interconnections of Boolean gates and one-bit storage devices. This complexity crucially depends upon a property of the underlying semigroup, which we call <italic>cycle-freedom</italic> (no cycle of length greater than one in the Cayley graph of the semigroup). Denoting by <italic>S</italic> and <italic>T</italic> size and computation time, respectively, we have <italic>S</italic> = &THgr;((<italic>N/T</italic>) log(<italic>N/T</italic>)), for non-cycle-free semigroups, and <italic>S</italic> = &THgr;(<italic>N/T</italic>), for cycle-free semigroups. In both cases, <italic>T</italic> ∈ [&OHgr;(log<italic>N</italic>), <italic>O</italic>(<italic>N</italic>)].
