Determination of the K±p forward amplitudes and coupling constants using a finite-energy relation
The real parts of the amplitudes for K ± p forward elastic scattering are estimated from the available differential cross section data. A least-squares fit, based on a finite-energy dispersion relation, is performed to these values and the optimum real parts are calculated as a function of energy. From the fit we find that the KN coupling constants satisfy g Λ 2 + 0.84 g Σ 2 = 8.8 ± 3.0, a value smaller than that required by SU(3) invariance. Unlike the value of the couplings obtained using the conventional forward dispersion relations, the prediction is not particularly dependent on the model used to extrapolate the K − p amplitude into the unphysical region. The least-squares fit also provides a sensitive consistency test of the various parameterizations which have been used to analyse the low-energy K − p scattering data. The test favours the constant scattering length parameterization over the more recent K -matrix parameterizations. In particular, the consistency condition rules out the effective-range K -matrix parameterization due to Kim.
