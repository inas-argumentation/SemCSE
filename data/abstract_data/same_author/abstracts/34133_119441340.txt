Relationship between the Quark-Meson Coupling Model and Quantum Hadrodynamics
Using the quark-meson coupling (QMC) model, we study nuclear matter from the point of view of quark degrees of freedom. Carrying out a re-definition of the scalar field in matter, we transform the QMC into a QHD-type model with a non-linear scalar potential. The potentials obtained from the QMC are compared with those of relativistic mean-field models. Since at present, rigorous studies of quantum chromodynamics (QCD) are limited to matter systems with high temperature and zero baryon density, it is important to build models that help to bridge the discrepancy between nuclear phenomenology and QCD. We have proposed a relativistic quark model for nuclear matter and finite nuclei that consists of non-overlapping nucleon bags bound by the self-consistent exchange of isoscalar, scalar (σ) and vector (ω) mesons in the mean-field approximation (MFA). This model is called the quark-meson coupling (QMC) model. 1) It has also been found in recent theoretical studies that various properties of finite nuclei can be described very well by relativistic mean-field (RMF) models, i.e., quantum hadrodynamics (QHD). 2) In this Letter, we consider the relationship between the QMC and the QHD and study howthe internal structure of the nucleon plays a role in effective nuclear models. In our previous works, 1) the MIT bag model has been used to describe the quark structure of the nucleon. Because the confined quarks interact with the scalar field σ in matter, the effective nucleon mass M � N in the QMC is given by a function of σ through the quark model of the nucleon. (Although the quarks also interact with the ω meson, it has no effect on the nucleon structure, except for a shift in the nucleon energy. 1) ) The (relativistic) constituent quark model (CQM) is an alternative model for the nucleon. Recently, Shen and Toki 3) have proposed a newversion of the QMC — the quark mean-field (QMF) model, in which the CQM is used to describe the nucleon. In the present study, in addition to the bag model (BM), we use the relativistic CQM with confining potentials V (r) of a square well (SW) and harmonic oscillator (HO) type to see the dependence of the matter properties on the quark model. It is assumed that the light (u or d) quark mass mq is 300 MeV in the CQM and 0 MeV in the BM.
