Anomalous processes at high temperature and density in a two-dimensional linear sigma model
We use the two-dimensional $\ensuremath{\sigma}$ model as a toy model to study the behavior of anomalous amplitudes in the limit where the constituent quark mass is small. Symmetry arguments show that the ${\ensuremath{\pi}}^{0}\ensuremath{\rightarrow}\ensuremath{\gamma}$ amplitude should vanish if $\stackrel{\ensuremath{\rightarrow}}{m}0,$ but we show that the one-loop ${\ensuremath{\pi}}^{0}\ensuremath{\gamma}$ amplitude does not satisfy this property because of infrared singularities. However, when a proper regularization (resummation of a thermal mass, for instance) is taken into account, this amplitude vanishes as expected. We also study the amplitude ${\ensuremath{\pi}}^{0}\stackrel{\ensuremath{\rightarrow}}{\ensuremath{\sigma}}\ensuremath{\gamma}$ and show that it does not vanish in the same limit.
