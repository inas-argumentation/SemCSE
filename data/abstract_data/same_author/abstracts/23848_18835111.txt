Chern-Simons-type photon mass from fermion electric dipole moments at finite temperature in 3+1 dimensions.
We study the low-energy effective field theory of fermions with electric and magnetic dipole moments at finite temperature. We find that at one loop there is an interaction term of the Chern-Simons form ${\mathcal{L}}_{\mathcal{I}}={M}_{\ensuremath{\mu}}{A}_{\ensuremath{\nu}}{\stackrel{\ifmmode \tilde{}\else \~{}\fi{}}{F}}^{\ensuremath{\mu}\ensuremath{\nu}}$. The four-vector ${M}_{\ensuremath{\mu}}\ensuremath{\simeq}{d}_{i}{\ensuremath{\mu}}_{i}{m}_{i}^{2}{\ensuremath{\partial}}_{\ensuremath{\mu}}(\mathrm{ln}T)$ is interpreted as a Chern-Simons-type mass of photons, which is determined by the electric (magnetic) dipole moments ${d}_{i}({\ensuremath{\mu}}_{i})$ of the fermions in the vacuum polarization loop diagram. The physical consequence of such a photon mass is that photons of opposite circular polarizations, propagating through a hot medium, have different group velocities. We estimate the time lag between the arrival times of the left and right circularly polarized light signals from pulsars. If the light propagates through a hot plasma (where the temperature in some regions is $T\ensuremath{\sim}100$ MeV) then the time lag between the two circularly polarized signals of frequency $\ensuremath{\omega}$ will be $\ensuremath{\Delta}t(\ensuremath{\omega})\ensuremath{\simeq}\frac{{10}^{\ensuremath{-}6}}{\ensuremath{\omega}}$. It may be possible to observe this effect in pulsar signals which propagate through nebulas at high temperatures.
