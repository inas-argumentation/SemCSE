Learning Quantitative Knowledge for Multiagent Coordination
A central challenge of multiagent coordination is reasoning about how the actions of one agent affect the actions of another. Knowledge of these interrelationships can help coordinate agents -- preventing conflicts and exploiting beneficial relationships among actions. We explore three interlocking methods that learn quantitative knowledge of such non-local effects in TAEEMS, a well-developed framework for multiagent coordination. The surprising simplicity and effectiveness of these methods demonstrates how agents can learn domain-specific knowledge quickly, extending the utility of coordination frameworks that explicitly represent coordination knowledge.
