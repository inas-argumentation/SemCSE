GALACTIC MODEL UNCERTAINTIES IN INDIRECT DARK MATTER SEARCH
If Dark Matter (DM) particles are a thermal relic from the early universe, they are expected to annihilate into quarks and leptons in order to reduce the number density with respect to the number of photons. The annihilation into quarks is expected to be dominant which may lead to a significant excess in gamma rays, antiprotons and positrons, as discussed in recent reviews. An excess in gamma rays above the background from interactions between cosmic rays (CR) and the interstellar medium has indeed been observed by the EGRET gamma ray spectrometer on board of the NASA Compton Gamma Ray Observatory, which has been taken data in space between the years 1991 and 2000. This excess shows all the properties of the annihilation of a 60 GeV Weakly Interacting Massive Particle (WIMP), as discussed by de Boer et al. . Especially the distribution of the excess could be used to predict the shape of the rotation curve of the Galaxy, thus proving that the excess is a tracer of Dark Matter. The main assumption in this analysis was that the shape of the CR background is the same everywhere in the Galaxy, as is expected from the fact that the energy loss time for protons is large compared with the diffusion time. The interaction of CR protons with the interstellar gas is the major source of background for gamma ray energies in the few GeV range. The CR electrons have large energy losses by inverse Compton scattering and Bremsstrahlung, which are additional sources of gamma rays. It is the main purpose of the present note to study the uncertainties in the shape of the CR background and the corresponding uncertainties in the gamma ray spectra. This was done with the publicly available GALPROP propagation model after modifying it to include DMA. In addition the effects of the inhomogeneous local solar neighborhood were studied. Furthermore anisotropic diffusion, expected in spiral galaxies with strong anisotropic magnetic fields, was studied which increases the uncertainties of charged secondary particles by a wide margin. The latter is relevant, since in a recent paper Bergstrom et al. claimed that the flux of antiprotons excludes the DMA interpretation of the EGRET excess. This conclusion was based on their isotropic propagation model in the DarkSusy program.
