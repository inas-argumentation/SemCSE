Human papillomavirus vaccines to prevent cervical cancer
Worldwide about 500 000 new cases of cervical cancer are diagnosed each year - 80% in developing countries - resulting in more than 250 000 deaths. Inadequately treated symptoms (especially pain) also result in profound suffering. When discovered early surgery can be curative and recent data have shown the survival benefit of combined radiation and chemotherapy. In metastatic or recurrent disease however current management options produce only a limited benefit. Unfortunately patients in many countries are unable to take advantage of therapeutic advances. In the developed world screening for pre-cancerous abnormalities or early stage cervical cancers has been a major public-health success and the overall incidence of the disease has been substantially reduced over the past several decades. Meanwhile screening for cervical cancer is far more problematic in the developing world. (excerpt)
