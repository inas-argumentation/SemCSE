Elliptic flow in heavy ion collisions at varying energies: Partonic versus hadronic dynamics
We examine whether the “ideal fluid” observed in high energy heavy ion collisions is related to the onset of deconfinement by testing the hypothesis that hydrodynamics and parton coalescence always apply, but are obscured, at lower energies, by variations in the widths of quark and anti-quark rapidity distribution. We find that this effect is enough to spoil quark number scaling in elliptic flow. A lack of scaling in data therefore does not signal the absence of partonic degrees of freedom and hadronization by coalescence. In a coalescing partonic fluid, however, elliptic flow of anti-baryons should be the greater than that of baryons, since antibaryons contain a greater admixture of quarks from the highly flowing midrapidity region. Hence, the opposite result observed in experiment is a good indication that something more complex is happening at lower energies, although we demonstrate that purely hadronic dynamics has a similar dependence of baryon-anti-baryons v2 as purely partonic dynamics. We finish by discussing possible explanations for the scaling of baryonic v2.
