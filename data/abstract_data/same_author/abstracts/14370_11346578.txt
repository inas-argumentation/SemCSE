QCD EFFECTS IN PARTICLE PRODUCTION ON NUCLEI
I discuss some questions related to hard scattering processes in nuclei and corrections to the leading twist approximation. The QCD factorization theorem requires that high energy partons do not lose energy while traversing the nucleus. I explain the physical reason for this. The theorem also states that spectator partons, not involved in the hard collision, have no influence on the inclusive cross section. Important spectator effects are, however, seen in the data for certain reactions and in some kinematical regions. I discuss the reasons from a phenomenological and theoretical point of view. Finally, I mention some methods for analyzing hard processes in regions of very large x, where the leading twist terms are not dominant. Studies of the dependence of hard collisions on the nuclear number of the target and/or projectile have revealed a large number of interesting, diverse and often surprising QCD effects. The nucleus has been, and will continue to be, a versatile tool for uncovering reaction mechanisms and indicating where our standard methods are inadequate. In this talk I shall confine myself to two particular questions, related to the energy loss of partons in nuclei and the importance of higher twist corrections due to spectator parton interactions. For a wider view of nuclear effects, I refer to recent reviews 1 .
