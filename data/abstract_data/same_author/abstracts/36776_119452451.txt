On the quest for unification—simplicity and antisimplicity
The road towards unification of elementary interactions is thought to start on the solid ground of a universal local gauge principle. I discuss the different types of bosonic gauge symmetries in gravitational and nongravitational (standard model) interactions and their extensions, both fermionic and bosonic, and with respect to spacetime dimensions. The apparently paradoxical size and nature of the cosmological constant is sketched, which at first sight does not readily yield a clue as to the enveloping symmetry structure of a unified theory. Nevertheless a tentative outlook is given, encouraging one to proceed on this road.
