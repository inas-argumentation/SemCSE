Electroweak physics at a future EIC
We discuss potential electroweak measurements that represent a new opportunity from the potentially large luminosities that are envisioned in design studies of a polarized lepton light ion collider (EIC). At integrated luminosities of greater than 10 fb−1 and center of mass energies between 10 and 100 GeV, it becomes possible to measure semi-leptonic weak neutral current amplitudes mediated by the Z0 boson via electroweak interference measurements in a collider environment for the first time. We explore the new physics topics that are opened up due to precision measurements of these weak neutral current amplitudes, with the inclusion of longitudinal polarization in both the lepton and hadron beams. One can also achieve new sensitivity in the search for lepton flavor violation in e− τ conversion, and explore novel aspects of quark densities in nuclei in e-A collisions.
