The Helium Cryogenic System for the Superconducting High-Luminosity Insertion at the CERN-ISR
In 1977, CERN decided to add a superconducting high-luminosity insertion, i.e. eight high-gradient quadrupole magnets, to the ISR. The idea was to assess interference between a superconducting installation and the high-energy particle storage rings and, at the same time, to give an immediate benefit of increased particle collision rates for the high-energy physics experiments1.
