Rare Particle Searches with the high altitude SLIM experiment
The search for rare particles in the cosmic radiation remains one of the main aims of nonaccelerator particle astrophysics. Experiments at high altitude allow lower mass thresholds with respect to detectors at sea level or underground. The SLIM experiment is a large array of nuclear track detectors located at the Chacaltaya High Altitude Laboratory (5290 m a.s.l.). The preliminary results from the analysis of the first 227 m 2 exposed for more than 3.6 y are here reported. The detector is sensitive to Intermediate Mass Magnetic Monopoles, 10 5 < mM < 10 12 GeV, and to SQM nuggets and Q-balls, which are possible Dark Matter candidates.
