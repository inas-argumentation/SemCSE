Analytical studies of particle dynamics in bending waves in planetary rings
Particles inside a planetary ring are subject to forcing due to the central planet, moons in inclined orbits, self-gravity of the ring and other forces resulting from radiation drag, collisional effects and the Lorentz force arising from the magnetic field of the planet. We write down the equations of motion of a single particle inside the ring and solve them analytically. We find that the importance of the shear caused by variation of the radial velocity component with the local vertical direction cannot be ignored and it may be responsible for damping of the bending waves in planetary rings as observed by the Voyager data. We present the wave profile resulting from the dissipation. We estimate the surface mass density of the C-ring to be of the order of σ ∼ 1.2-1.6 g cm -2 , and the height h ∼ 2.2-2.4 m. These theoretical results are in agreement with observations.
