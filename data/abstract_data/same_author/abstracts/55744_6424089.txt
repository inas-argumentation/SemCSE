Feasibility and complexity of broadcasting with random transmission failures
We consider fault-tolerant broadcasting in the message passing and radio models under a probabilistic failure model. At each step, the transmitter of each node may fail independently with fixed probability <i>p<1</i>. We study both omission and Byzantine transmission failures. Our goal is to establish conditions on feasibility and to estimate the complexity of <i>almost-safe</i> broadcasting (i.e., broadcasting which is correct with probability at least <i>1-1/n</i> on <i>n</i>-node graphs for sufficiently large <i>n</i>) under these scenarios. If only omission failures are assumed, almost-safe broadcasting is feasible for any <i>p<1</i>, in both communication models. For Byzantine faults, almost-safe broadcasting is feasible in the message passing model iff <i>p<1/2</i> and in the radio model iff <i>p<(1-p)<sup>Δ+1</sup></i>, where <i>Δ</i> is the maximum degree of the network. For the time complexity of almost-safe broadcasting, we give the following upper and lower bounds. Consider an <i>n</i>-node graph <i>G</i> with a given source <i>s</i>, and denote by D the radius of <i>G</i> w.r.t. <i>s</i> (namely, the largest distance from <i>s</i> to any node in <i>G</i>). Then for the message passing model we show that assuming omission faults, the optimal almost-safe broadcasting time is <i>Θ (D + log n)</i>. Assuming Byzantine faults, almost-safe broadcasting is possible in time <i>O(D+log <sup>α</sup> n)</i>, for any constant <i>α > 1</i>. For the radio model we show that almost-safe broadcasting in time <i>O (opt + log n)</i> (where <i>opt</i> is the optimal fault-free broadcasting time) is impossible for some graphs, even with omission failures, and we give an almost-safe broadcasting algorithm of time <i>O(opt • log n)</i> for any graph, for both types of failures.
