Applications of Temporal Logic to the Specification of Real-time Systems
Traditionally, temporal logic, and in particular, its application to the specification and analysis of reactive and concurrent systems, have been considered appropriate for the qualitative (nonquantitative) treatment of time. This can be seen by some of the abstractions that are inherent in the temporal methodology. To mention some of them, the modeling of concurrency by interleaving, the concept of eventuality, which guarantees an eventual occurrence of an event, but provides no bound on how soon; and the notion of fairness which requires that frequent attention be directed to certain components of the system, but places no bound on the frequency. All these, represent conscious efforts to deal with essentially quantitative phenomena in a qualitative way.
