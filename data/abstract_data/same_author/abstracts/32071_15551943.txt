Probing Lepton Flavor Violation at Future Colliders.
Supersymmetric theories with significant lepton flavor violation have $\stackrel{\ifmmode \tilde{}\else \~{}\fi{}}{e}$ and $\stackrel{\ifmmode \tilde{}\else \~{}\fi{}}{\ensuremath{\mu}}$ nearly degenerate. In this case, pair production of ${\stackrel{\ifmmode \tilde{}\else \~{}\fi{}}{e}}^{+}{\stackrel{\ifmmode \tilde{}\else \~{}\fi{}}{e}}^{\ensuremath{-}}$ and ${\stackrel{\ifmmode \tilde{}\else \~{}\fi{}}{\ensuremath{\mu}}}^{+}{\stackrel{\ifmmode \tilde{}\else \~{}\fi{}}{\ensuremath{\mu}}}^{\ensuremath{-}}$ at LEP II and at the Next Linear Collider (NLC) leads to the phenomenon of slepton oscillations, which is analogous to neutrino oscillations. The reach in $\ensuremath{\Delta}{m}^{2}$ and ${sin}^{2}2\ensuremath{\theta}$ gives a probe of lepton flavor violation which is significantly more powerful than the current bounds from rare processes, such as $\ensuremath{\mu}\ensuremath{\rightarrow}e\ensuremath{\gamma}$. Polarizable ${e}^{\ensuremath{-}}$ beams and the ${e}^{\ensuremath{-}}{e}^{\ensuremath{-}}$ mode at the NLC are found to be promising options.
