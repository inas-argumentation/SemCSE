Triple constructs consisting of nanoparticles and microspheres for bone-marrow-derived stromal-cell-delivery microscaffolds.
The formation of polyelectrolyte complexes via layer-by-layer electrostatic self-assembly (ESA) is considered tobeanoveland promising technique for surface modification or enhancing the design of devices.Recently, several attempts havebeenmade to create biomimetic constructs using this technique. Studies have been conducted to evaluate designs in which alginate/polylysine, chitosan/dextran sulfate, hyaluronic acid/poly(lysine), poly(lysine)/poly(glutamic acid) (PGA), and collagen were used to fabricate polyelectrolyte complexes, and theresultsof thesestudieshavedemonstratedthat it is important that the materials used have an excess of alternatively positive and negative charges. This quality constitutes the motor by which the complexes build up, and also allows the constructed films to absorb a large variety of compounds, including dyes, particles, and proteins, via simple contact. In a previous study, we reported a simple and highly efficient method for the complete stabilization of threedimensional (3D) nanostructures via their embedment into a microspherical structure. This system functions using electrostatic interactions between heparin/poly(L-lysine) nanoparticles and polyethyleneimmine (PEI)-coated poly(llactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA) microspheres as 3D scaffolds for stem cell delivery. In the present study, 2D assemblies of various nanoparticles (NPs) are simply and efficiently stabilized via embedment into PLGA microspheres for use as cell delivery vehicles.
