Exploring the Mysteries of Strong Interactions—The PANDA Experiment
The physics of strong interactions is undoubtedly one of the most challenging areas of modern science. Quantum Chromo Dynamics (QCD) is reproducing the physics phenomena only at distances much shorter than the size of the nucleon, where perturbation theory can be used yielding results of high precision and predictive power. As the coupling constant rises steeply at nuclear scales (see Figure 1), perturbative expansions diverge and a different theoretical approach is required. However, the strong interaction keeps providing new experimental observations, which were not predicted by “effective” theories. The latter retain the fundamental symmetries of QCD, but have problems in describing all the observed phenomena simultaneously.
