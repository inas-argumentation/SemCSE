Higher Education, College Characteristics, and Student Experiences: Long-Term Effects on Educational Satisfactions and Perceptions.
This article examines student satisfactions and reports of their experiences as outcomes of higher education. Alexander Astin puts the issue most trenchantly: "Given the considerable investment of time and energy that most students make in attending college, the student's perception of value should be given substantial weight. Indeed, it is difficult to argue that student satisfaction can be legitimately subordinated to any other educational outcome" [4, p. 164; emphasis ours]. We contrast the socialization and allocation theories of the effects of higher education on students. If Meyer's allocation-placement thesis [34] is correct, former students should reflect positively on their educational experiences to the extent that the educational process or degree is perceived to open the door to higher statuses.
