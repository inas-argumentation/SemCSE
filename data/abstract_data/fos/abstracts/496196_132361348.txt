Petrochemical Study on the Rocks in the Hokuroku District, Akita Prefecture, Northeastern Japan
Over 1900 rock samples from drill core of Hokuroku district were chemically analysed and subjected to Principal Component Analysis and Cluster Analysis hoping to delineate the interrelationship among the major and minor elements in relation to the alteration and mineralization associated with the kuroko deposits. The principal component analysis on all of the analytical data (N=1917) showed that factor 1 governed the major oxide chemistry of the rock, i.e., the contribution of Fe, Ti, Mg and Ca were significant while Si showed very significant negative contribution. Basic rocks thus showed higher factor scores while acidic varieties showed lower scores. In factor 2 on the other hand, Cu, Pb, Zn and Ba had large contribution while Na and Al showed large negative contribution. This combination and behaviour of the elements were thought to be of prime interest since this factor might have a possibility to suggest the existence of mineralization with associated alteration. The fact that Na does not show high contribution in factor lwhile it does in factor 2 is another very interesting finding. This is more apparent in the case of the subset of igneous rocks (N=1047). Younger dacitic intrusives are thought to be the least affected by alteration associating mineralization. They were subjected to a principal component analysis and were found that the contribution of Na in factor 1 also negligible. Here factor 2 was thought to be controlling the diagenetic zeolitic alterations. The cluster analysis showed several interesting clusters of which further investigation should be carried on in association with the data on geology, X-ray diffraction and others. Introduction There are many producing kuroko deposits and occurrences as well as other types of mineralization in the Hokuroku district. Since the district is generally considered to have the greatest mineral potential in Japan, Metal Mining Agency of Japan (MMAJ) has been deeply involved in the exploration since 1964. MMAJ has drilled 292 diamond drill holes totalling 163 km up to 1982 together with various types of explorational works in the district. The mining companies operating in the district also have done extensive exploration works. As a consequence of these, there existed a general agreement among the concerned explorationists that a comprehensive analytical study on the data accumulated so far in enormous amount should be done. Thus MMAJ undertook a comprehensive study on geology of Hokuroku district during 1980 to 1982 (MITI, 1983). In the course of the study, a serious lack of petrochemical and geochemical data became apparent together with general paucity of quantitative data on the geologic description in the drill logs. Such was the situation, MMAJ carried out sampling of the drill core and petrochemical/ geochemical analyses on them. The bulk of the drill core chemically analysed were derived from MMAJ's diamond drill core and some from that of individual mining companies where there were no drill holes of MMAJ at all and where there were known kuroko deposits with established stratigraphy.
