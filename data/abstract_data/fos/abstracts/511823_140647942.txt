Neritic, southern extratropical foraminifera and the terminal eocene event
If the “Terminal Eocene Event” (TEE) records a major bolide impact or other extraterrestrial happening, then there ought to be disruption of benthonic foraminiferal communities especially in rather shallow neritic environments. We record the arrivals and departures of 180 species in a composite succession through the later Middle Eocene, Late Eocene and earliest Oligocene in southern Australia. The rate of new arrivals increases at each of several transgressions, each of which also marks a climatic amelioration. The sharpest increase in rate of departure is found at a regional regression within the latest Eocene. There is no evidence of major disruption of the fauna at the end of the Eocene. The TEE, on this data, was not extraterrestrial in origin and was hardly cataclysmic. The study suggests, instead, that the origins of late Eocene biotic change should be sought through earthly configurations of climate and sea level.
