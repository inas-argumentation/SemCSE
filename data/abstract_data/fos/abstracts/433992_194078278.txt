Readers should be especially grateful for the care given to the frate’s highly visual style and the peculiarities of his language. If Italianisms persist in the English, such as the colloquialism describing Florence as the “navel of Italy” (62), they are few in number, and their meaning is clear to readers familiar with Italian. The Savonarolan sections also contain the most useful footnotes, directing readers to Thomistic, Aristotelian, biblical, and medieval sources that formed the foundation of the preacher’s learning. The notes also make reference to recent scholarship, but less successfully. Full citations of works by Weinstein, Polizzotto, McGinn, and others recur frequently, and the repetitions could have been eliminated with a comprehensive bibliography at the end of the volume. Similarly, the black-and-white images were poorly reproduced. With the exception of the woodcuts, the only images over which Savonarola “could conceivably have had some control” (nn.), most of the pictures do little to enhance the reader’s appreciation of the preacher and his movement. The index also needed extra attention. At least one mention of the tiepidi goes overlooked (215); references to the cappucci, or robed ones, do not appear; and the footnotes, rich in references to primary and secondary sources, are excluded completely. One can hope that subsequent editions of this useful volume will include improvements to the back matter while preserving the quality of the translations and scholarship that precede it.
