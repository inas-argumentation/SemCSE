Explanation for the resistivity law in quantum Hall systems.
We consider a 2D electron system in a strong magnetic field, where the local Hall resistivity ${\ensuremath{\rho}}_{\mathrm{xy}}(\mathbf{r})$ is a function of position and ${\ensuremath{\rho}}_{\mathrm{xx}}(\mathbf{r})$ is small compared to ${\ensuremath{\rho}}_{\mathrm{xy}}$. Particularly if the correlations fall off slowly with distance, or if fluctuations exist on several length scales, one finds that the macroscopic longitudinal resistivity ${R}_{\mathrm{xx}}$ is only weakly dependent on ${\ensuremath{\rho}}_{\mathrm{xx}}$ and is approximately proportional to the magnitude of fluctuations in ${\ensuremath{\rho}}_{\mathrm{xy}}$. This may provide an explanation of the empirical law ${R}_{\mathrm{xx}}\ensuremath{\propto}\frac{\mathrm{Bd}{R}_{\mathrm{xy}}}{\mathrm{dB}}$ where ${R}_{\mathrm{xy}}$ is the Hall resistance, and $B$ is the magnetic field.
