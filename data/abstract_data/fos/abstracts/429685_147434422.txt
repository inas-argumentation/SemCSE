Institutional Response to Student Difficulties with the "Language of Instruction" in an Arab Medical College.
The use of a nonindigenous "language of instruction" (LI) creates a major difficulty throughout institutions of higher education in developing societies. Students are expected to be proficient in a metropolitan language (usually English, French, or Spanish) because insufficient teaching materials textbooks, research journals, and teaching outlines exist in the native language. Little has been written in it or translated into it from metropolitan languages; this in turn is largely due to a lack, within the indigenous language, of a suitable technical vocabulary in the field of study. The deficit occurs not only in scientific fields but also in burgeoning nonscientific fields such as accounting and librarianship. So long as a nonindigenous language is used, difficulty will ensue.'
