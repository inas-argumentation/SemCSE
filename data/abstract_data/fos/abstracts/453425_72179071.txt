Hemodynamics of the transitional aortic arch patterning at a key embryonic stage
Purpose: This study examines the morphologic and hemodynamic changes occurring in the early embryonic aortic arch (AA) vessels. Methods: We applied fluorescent dye microinjection and optical coherence tomography to measure the diameters of the bilateral embryonic chick AAs at stages 18, 21, and 24 (3, 3.5, and 4 days, respectively). Polymeric casts were used to generate 3D models of the AAs at each stage, reconstructed from micro-computed tomography scans. Pulsatile blood flow was simulated using computational fluid dynamics (CFD), with stage-specific inlet flow based on Doppler ultrasound. Results:We found that the AAs present at stages 18 and 24did not vary from embryo to embryo while the intermediate stage 21 presented with four possible configurations. This variability was associated with increasedwall shear stress (WSS) levels at stage 21 compared to stages 18 and 24, as computed from CFD results. Diameter measurements showed asymmetric growth of AA pair IV, where both laterals increased from stage 18 to 21 while only the right lateral increased from stage 21 to 24 (Pb.05). CFD revealed that the right lateral flow of AA pair IV was more than 1.8 times that of the left lateral at all stages. Regression analysis revealed a quadratic relationship between a change in WSS and a change in AA diameter (P=.002). Conclusions: Our study provides the first comparison between quantitative in vivo data and CFD-predicted hemodynamics of the early embryonic AA. Changes in flow distribution and WSS were associated with increased morphogenetic activity of the stage 21 AAs.
