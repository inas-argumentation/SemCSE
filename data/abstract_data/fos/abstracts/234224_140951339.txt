Attitudes of School Administrators toward Selecting Teachers for Permanent Positions
In this article, the authors draw upon survey data collected from 68 administrators representing 12 states and Washington, D.C. Findings indicate that administrators determine the pre-interview and interview process for hiring high quality teachers. The research reported offers shared attitudes of how public school administrators, those participating from the certain states, have a profound concern for teacher candidates of high intellect, character, and concern for student success. Introduction Various strategies have been implemented by administrators for hiring teachers for permanent positions. According to Koenigsknecht (2006, p. 2), "hiring a teacher is arguably the most important decision school administrators make. Great consideration must be given to the interview when interviewing those who will teach the future of our communities." While the hiring practices and processes may be varied, teacher candidates must be cognizant of how they relate their preparation for the position in regards to their knowledge, skills, philosophy, and abilities. Purpose of the Study The purpose of this study was to evaluate attitudes of public school administrators relative to hiring teachers. Information needed was "to give teacher candidates the skills, techniques, and strategies, and understanding of the employment process when seeking a teaching position" (Quezada, 2009, p. 3). Methodology This study investigated the attitudes of public school administrators that participated in the Education Career Fair held annually at a state university on the east coast. Attitude refers to "a mental position with regard to a fact or state" (Merriam-Webster Online Dictionary, 2009). The survey questions focused on the pre-interview process and interview process for selecting teachers in permanent positions. The population for this study comprised 68 administrators who were surveyed electronically with the questionnaire being provided voluntarily. The 68 administrators represented individuals from 12 states and Washington, D.C. The questionnaire was developed by three administrators and three faculty members. To validate the survey instrument, five administrators from different school districts were sent an electronic message (with the attachment) explaining the purpose of the study and the initial draft of the questionnaire. Comments and suggestions from the administrators were incorporated. To determine the appropriateness of the content, the procedure used by McCaslin and Walker (1979, p. 34) was implemented. They recommend content validity be assessed by having experts in the field "review and rate" the questions. The aforementioned researchers suggest a panel ranging from three to nine authorities as the desired approach. Three university faculty members within the College of Education were used as the panel. The questionnaire was then administered to a sample of three public school administrators. A proposal and the survey instrument were forwarded to the Institutional Review Board at the university. After approval by the Institutional Review Board, an electronic version of the survey was developed and administered to the 68 administrators. The 40 questions were designed to obtain the data needed to accomplish the purpose of the study. The questions sought to determine the attitudes of public school administrators toward hiring teachers. The information obtained would assist the university's College of Education with its curriculum and future seminars for teacher candidates enrolled in both the undergraduate and graduate programs. Data Analysis The study used descriptive statistics to analyze the data from the electronic survey. Frequencies were used to interpret the responses.
