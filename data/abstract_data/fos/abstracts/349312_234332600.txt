Chemical analysis of ceramics fragments reveals that despite Cyprus’s multiculturalism and despite a major shift in style and makeup of ceramics, the people of Cyprus consumed more or less the same mixture of meat and vegetables throughout the tumultuous upheavals of Lusignan and Venetian rule. For an art historical study with sixteen entries, the small number of color illustrations (sixteen) may seem insufficient. The placement of some figures pages away from the text where they are discussed can be a source of frustration to the reader. Finally, the choice by the editors not to have a common transliteration of Greek to Latin, as well as the lack of maps (given the focus on more obscure rural locales as well as well-known urban settings), may provide some challenges to scholars who are not Cyprus experts. These are small issues that should not detract from the overall value of these essays.
