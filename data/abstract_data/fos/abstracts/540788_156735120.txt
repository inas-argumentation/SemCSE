Economic Transition at the Edge of Order and Chaos: China's Dualist and Leading Sectoral Approach
No other place in the present world and at no other time in history have we experienced such drastic changes in the artificial experiments of communism and the subsequent experiments in economic transition from a planned economy to a market economy than in both China and Russia.' Few macro policies other than those of economic transition in China and Russia have caused such dramatic consequences for the well-being of so many people in such a short period of time. While the experiments with communism ended up invalidating the implemented ideology, the experiments with economic transition toward a market economy do not constitute a validation of neoclassical economics. Rather, they seem to provide empirical support for the theoretical attack on the neoclassical perspective by both institutional economists and evolutionary systems theorists [Koslowski 1992]. Most policy debates on economic transition from a planned economy to a market economy have centered on the dichotomy between the gradualist approach and "the big bang" approach (shock therapy). While the failure of Russia's economic transition has led to the discrediting of "the big bang" approach [Griffin and Khan 1994], the Chinese success has been explained within the framework of the gradualist approach. This paper argues that the dichotomy between "the big bang" and the gradualist approaches is too simplistic and fails to grasp the fundamental feedback structures that are embodied in the economic transition processes in Russia and China.
