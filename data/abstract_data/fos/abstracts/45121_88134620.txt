Potassium Availability in Selected Bran Products: Implications for the Renal Patient
■ Objective: To conduct an in vitro analysis to determine the extent to which potassium as a monovalent cation is disassociated from selected bran fibers and thus available for absorption in the gastrointestinal (GI) tract. ■ Design: One-gram samples from eight selected bran cereal products were analyzed by dry ashing, water leaching, and acid leaching using the Official Methods of Analysis of the Association of Official Analytical Chemists. ■ Measurements: Three procedures were used for analysis: (1) One-gram cereal samples were dry ashed and analyzed for total potassium content; (2) one-gram cereal samples were leached with water to determine solubility and the water analyzed for total potassium content; and (3) one-gram cereal samples were leached with a 0.1 normal hydrochloric acid (0.1 N HCI) solution to approximate solubility within the GI tract, and the solution was analyzed for total potassium content. ■ Main results: The dry ash potassium results remained within range of the manufacturer's analysis on the majority of the cereal samples. The mean potassium solubility of the cereal samples using the water-leaching method was 41%. The mean potassium solubility of the cereal samples using the acid-leaching method was 87%. ■ Conclusion: This preliminary research indicates that bran fibers do not bind potassium sufficiently, and the bulk is biologically available for absorption. The acid-leaching method seems to provide the closest estimate to the percent of potassium potentially available for absorption in the GI tract. However, because of the limited sample size of this study, caution should be used when extrapolating these results for dietary counseling of renal patients. It is the intent of the investigators to add to the data base to validate the findings and provide a more meaningful and clinically significant recommendation for patient counseling.
