Unambiguous evidence for nodeless s-wave gap symmetry in the bulk of electron-doped cuprates
The phenomenon of superconductivity involves the pairing of electrons into Cooper pairs. The internal wavefunction (gap function) of a Cooper pair obeys a certain symmetry which reflects the underlying pairing mechanism. It is known that conventional superconductors (e.g., Pb and Nb) possess an s-wave gap symmetry that reflects the phonon mediated electron-electron pairing. On the other hand, the gap symmetry of high-temperature cuprate superconductors has been a topic of intense debate for about twenty years. Some surface and phase-sensitive experiments \cite{Review} support d-wave order-parameter symmetry for both electron-doped (n-type) and hole-doped (p-type) cuprates. Probing only the top CuO_2 plane, low-energy angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy shows a non-monotonic d-wave gap symmetry in an electron-doped cuprate. Recent x-ray photoemission spectroscopy has clearly shown that the electronic structure of the top CuO_2 plane is very different from that of the bulk. This also suggests that the gap symmetry of the bulk might be different from that of the surface. Here we compare the data of Raman scattering, pair-breaking effect of nonmagnetic impurities, and point-contact tunneling of optimally-doped n-type cuprates with theoretical predictions based on d-wave and nodeless s-wave gap symmetries. We find that all these data are in quantitative agreement with nodeless s-wave gap symmetry and that the pair-breaking effect of nonmagnetic impurities completely rules out d-wave gap symmetry. Our analyses provide unambiguous evidence for nodeless s-wave gap symmetry in the bulk of optimally-doped n-type cuprates.
