Electromagnetic and Ozone Emissions from Dielectric Barrier Discharge Plasma Actuators
A significant challenge associated with the implementation of plasma actuators for flow control is the high voltage signal required for their operation. In addition to logistical challenges, high voltage exposure from plasma actuators is a concern with regard to human health effects. Further health risks are posed by the ozone produced during plasma discharge. The objective of the present work was to evaluate both electromagnetic and ozone emission generated by typical plasma actuator operation. The electric and magnetic fields were characterized to determine the locations surrounding an active plasma actuator at which the maximum exposure limits to prevent adverse health effects were met or exceeded. The rate of ozone production was determined within an enclosed area and compared to safety standards for human exposure to ozone. Various operating voltages and frequencies within the range of values commonly reported in plasma actuator research were used with a dielectric barrier discharge (DBD) plasma actuator made of conventional materials (copper electrodes on acrylic dielectric). It was shown that electromagnetic field strength increases with operating voltage and proximity to the active device. Field strengths were independent of operating frequency. Ozone production, however, was shown to increase with both applied voltage and frequency. The results of the current work demonstrate the importance of developing safe operating procedures for plasma actuator research.
