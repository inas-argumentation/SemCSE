Relationship between normalized corn yields and monthly rainfall for the midwestern United States
The summertime climate of the central United States is characterized by high spatial variability in precipitation, resulting in high spatial variability in soil moisture, crop stress, and crop yield. Monitoring rainfall conditions and identifying areas of potential crop damage from deficient or excessive rainfall can be problematic because the existing network of precipitation observations is not of sufficient spatial resolution to identify small-scale variations in precipitation. Further, delay in the availability of quality controlled cooperative rainfall data prevents timely monitoring of precipitation. This study examines the relationship between high resolution (15-km) multi-sensor rainfall estimates obtained in near-real time from the National Centers for Environmental Prediction (NCEP), and county-level corn yields for a 9-state region (Fig 1).
