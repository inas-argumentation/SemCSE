The Pleasure of Discernment: Marguerite de Navarre as Theologian. Carol Thysell
The book is persuasive in showing the attempts of these women to construct independent political roles, but cannot make the case that their successes signified a rising status for women. Rather, as Matarasso herself comments, their stories suggest the old irony that "only by vowing obedience,. . . could a woman attain to some degree of autonomy (1)." These three women of power presumably did understand the folly of the many Italian adventures that squandered so much of the potential of the monarchy and realm, and may indeed have been far wiser and more capable than the men through whose authority they had to act. But overwhelming circumstances rendered all the accomplishments of the first two ephemeral and the third triumphed only as the devoted mother of a King. PAUL SOLON Macalester College
