Cenozoic evolution of continental humidity and paleoenvironment, deduced from the kaolinite content of oceanic sediments
Abstract The distribution of detrital kaolinite in Cenozoic sediments of the Atlantic and West Pacific oceans has been used to reconstruct the variations of the continental humidity and associated paleoenvironmental factors. Five stages have been recognized. 1. (1) During the Late Oligocene (Chattian, c. 30 m.y., P21 and NP24), increased kaolinite content at every site marked an increase of global humidity. 2. (2) During the Early Miocene (Aquitanian, c. 20 m.y., N5–N6 and NN2–NN3), increasing continental aridity is deduced from low kaolinite contents in the Atlantic Ocean and Philippine Sea. 3. (3) During the Middle Miocene (Langhian, c. 14 m.y., N10–N12 and NN5–NN6), increased kaolinite contents at most of the sites marked a further increase of global humidity, which was especially important at low latitudes in Africa. 4. (4) During the Late Miocene (Tortonian, c. 10 m.y., N16 and NN10–NN11), kaolinite content and inferred continental humidity increased toward high latitudes but decreased toward low latitudes, especially off tropical Africa where it marked the development of arid conditions. 5. (5) During the Late Pliocene (Piacenzian, c. 3 m.y., NN15–NN16), kaolinite content and humidity increased again toward high latitudes while these conditions decreased again toward low latitudes, emphasizing the latitudinal climatic zonation. Periods of increasing humidity show good correlation with the development of polar ice. Locally, volcanism, tectonic activity, oceanic circulation and migration of lithospheric obliterated the climatic significance of clay mineral associations, which therefore express complementary aspects of the continental and marine paleoenvironments.
