A Sequential Three Hypothesis Test for Determining the Mean of a Normal Population with Known Variance
1. Summary. This paper examines a sequential testing procedure for choosing one of three simple hypotheses concerning the unknown mean A of the normal distribution when the variance is known. The test is conducted by plotting Sn , the sum of the observations, versus n, the current sample size, until the point (n, S") is contained within one of three triangular regions. When this occurs, sampling is terminated and the region containing (n,.Sn) determines which state of nature is accepted. Although we shall formally view the problem as one with only three states of nature (,u = An , A2 or Ut3), we shall proceed with the usual understanding that the performance of the test procedure should be evaluated for a wider class of states (-co < ,u < co ). The test is approximated by a corresponding exact test for the Wiener process. Formulas are derived which approximate the operating characteristics (OC) and the average sample size (ASN) for all values of ,u. The ASN function is compared with theoretical lower bounds.
