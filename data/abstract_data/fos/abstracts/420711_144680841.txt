Motives for Parenthood: A Comparison of Students' and Parents' Values
Despite various reports that parenthood is favorably regarded by the vast majority of American couples (Pohlman, 1969; Rabin, 1965) there are few data on which values motivate people to have children or why children seem to satisfy them (Hoffman & Hoffman. 1973) . The present study compared the perceived needs which children are expected to fulfill in two research groups, college students and their parents. A 22-item questionnaire was administered to a sample of 4 3 normal college age students (24 male, 19 female). A matching questionnaire was mailed to the parents or guardian of each respondent. The questionnaire covered reasons for having and not having children. Respondents rated the importance of each item on a five-point scale ranging from very important to unimportant.
