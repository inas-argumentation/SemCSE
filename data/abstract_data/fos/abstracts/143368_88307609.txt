Inhibitory effects and action mechanisms of DTBrC on human cervical cancer cells
The purpose of this study was to investigate the inhibitory effects and molecular mechanisms of DTBrC, a synthesized theanine derivative, on the growth of highly-metastatic human cervical cancer cells using the MTT assay. Western blotting was employed to analyze the protein expression and possible targets of the action related to the growth inhibition and apoptosis in the human cervical cancer cells treated with DTBrC. Results showed that DTBrC enhanced the growth inhibition of the human cervical cancer He La cells by 7 folds compared with theanine. DTBrC markedly reduced the protein level of VEGFR2 receptor and NF-κB. Moreover, DTBrC significantly inhibited the protein expression and phosphorylation of Akt kinase. In addition, DTBrC greatly decreased the expression of antiapoptotic Bcl-2 protein and remarkably increased the level of proapoptotic Bax protein, leading to the reduction of Bcl-2/Bax ratio. The molecular mechanism of DTBrC action may be related to its suppression of the signaling pathway of VEGFR2 – Akt/NF-κB. These results suggested that DTBrC may have therapeutic and/or adjuvant therapeutic applications to the treatment of highly metastatic human cervical cancer.
