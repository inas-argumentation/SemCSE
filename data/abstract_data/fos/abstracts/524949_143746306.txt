Comparison of Yes-No and True-False Forms of the Children's Social Desirability Scale
Crandall, Crandall, and Katkovsky ( 1965 ) have expressed concern over the possibility of a negative response set contaminating scores on the yes-no form of the Children's Social Desirability Scale. In converting true-false items into a direct question (yes-no) form for use with young children, they were unable to retain an equal division of items keyed yes and no. As a result, 34 of the 47 items were keyed in a negative direction. In the present study 43 fifth grade children were administered both the truefalse and yes-no forms of the Children's Social Desirability Scale with a 1-wk. interval between testing. The true-false form was administered first for all Ss. The means for the true-false and the yes-no scales were 18.07 (SD = 9.37) and 16.60 (SD = 10.27) respectively. This difference was not statistically significant (t = 1.87, df = 42, p > .05). The correlation between the true-false and yes-no scales was significant ( r = 0.85, df = 42, f l < .001). A further analysis was performed to determine the correlation between the positively and negatively keyed items on the two scales. The resulting rs were significant ( f i < .001, df = 4 2 ) : true-yes ( r = 0.78), true-no ( r = 0.72), false-yes ( r = 0.65), and false-no ( r = 0.83). The magnitude of these values tended to negate the possibility of a negative response set contaminating scores on the yes-no form of the Children's Social Desirability Scale. In fact, the strongest relationship was found in the correlation of the negatively keyed items.
