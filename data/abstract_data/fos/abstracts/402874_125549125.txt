Anisotropic Fermi surface and in-plane magnetization effects on Quantum capacitance for FM/TI interface
Topological insulators(TI) are a unique class of materials with exotic surface electronic properties. When a ferromagnetic (FM) layer is added on the top of a thin film 3D TI, the surface states gain magnetization. We point out here that the in-plane magnetization and the Fermi surface anisotropy due to hexagonal warping mediate changes in the quantum capacitance of the thin film warped FM/3D TI interface in the presence of an out of plane magnetic field. To first order, the quantum capacitance change due to hexagonal warping is always negative, and increases in magnitude monotonically with the energy difference from the charge neutrality point. In contrast, the change in the quantum capacitance due to in-plane magnetization oscillates with the energy, and can be zero for all energies for some combinations of the inter-surface coupling, and out of plane Zeeman energy splitting and magnetic field strength.
