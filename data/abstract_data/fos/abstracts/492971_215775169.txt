The versatility of DABCO: synthetic applications of its basic, nucleophilic, and catalytic properties Part 4*. Ionic liquids and reagents (Selectfluor, DABSO) derived from DABCO, DABCO as piperazine source
The previous parts of our review were devoted to the applications of 1,4-diazabicyclo[2.2.2]octane (DABCO) as a unique base, combining the properties of a strong nucleophile and a good nucleofuge. Reactions catalyzed by DABCO were considered in detail, including the Morita– Baylis–Hillman and Knoevenagel reactions, as well as the Michael and Biginelli reactions. The use of DABCO in nucleophilic substitution, cycloaddition, and isomerization reactions has been described before. In the fourth part of this review we will focus on the applications of DABCO as a component of ionic liquids, as a starting material for the design of new reagents and their uses in synthetic practice, as well as the role of DABCO as a readily available building block for the preparation of substituted piperazines.
