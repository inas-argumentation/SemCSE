The taxonomy and stratigraphic record of Globorotalia mayeri Cushman and Ellisor in New Zealand
Abstract Scanning electron microscope photographs of Globorotalia mayeri Cushman and Ellisor and G. peripheroronda Blow and Banner from both Trinidad and New Zealand illustrate differences in wall structure that suggest the two species are unlikely to be related. The wall of G. peripheroronda shows an uneven pore size throughout the test, with a major size difference between the ultimate and previous chambers, a smooth, flat area between the pores, and a pustulose umbilical area. In contrast, the pores of G. mayeri are more even in size and density throughout the test, with raised or ridged areas between the pores, and an umbilical area that lacks pustules. Stratigraphic variation in Globorotalia mayeri of changes in chamber separation, number of chambers in the final whorl, size of chambers in the final whorl, height of aperture, size of apertural lip and peripheral inflation are considered to be insufficient to warrant seperate specific designation. However, these characters may reflect environmental parameters as yet poorly understood and the retention of names, either as subspecies or formae, for specimens with such characters may have merit. Thus the synonymy of G. pseudocontinuosa Jenkins, G. semivera (Hornibrook), G. continuosa Blow and G. mayeri nympha Jenkins with G. mayeri extends the range of New Zealand G. mayeri back to the Oligocene, as occurs in tropical-equatorial areas. The ancestor of Globorotali (Globorotalia) mayeri is probably G. nana Bolli, and the ancestor of Globorotalia (Fohsella) peripheroronda is possibly G. kugleri Bolli.
