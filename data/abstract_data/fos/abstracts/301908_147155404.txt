Instructional Evaluation: Current Issues and Possible Improvements
A recent article by Herbert Marsh in the Journal of Educational Psychology [6] is an excellent summary of what we currently know about instructional evaluation, particularly the use of student ratings in instructional evaluation. It is clear that a great deal of progress has been made in the last two decades and that we know a good deal more than we use. Have we now run ourselves out of business? Are the basic questions answered so that this line of research can be put aside while others are explored? My answer to these questions is, "no." To justify my answer I want to address first the question. "What do we evaluate?" and second, the question, "How can we improve the effectiveness of instruction evaluation?" In both cases I shall argue that contemporary cognitive and motivational psychology offers insights that point to possible improvements in current practices.
