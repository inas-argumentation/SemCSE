Posada's Broadsheets: Mexican Popular Imagery 1890-1910 (review)
At each step of the way, Rugeley demonstrates how religious institutions rooted in the colonial period and before, adapted to modernization, the Caste War, and the rising tide of anticlericalism. A perfect case in the point was the ability of local cofradfas to respond to these stimuli. In the aftermath of the War, and, as the henequen boom took root, the religious brotherhoods reconfigured themselves on the fiber estates, resisting the claims of neighboring communities, the hierarchy and local curas, while defending the interests of its members.
