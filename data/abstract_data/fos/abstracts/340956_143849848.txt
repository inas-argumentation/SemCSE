Criterion-Referenced Measurement (CRM) in the Initial Acquisition of a Psychomotor Skill with Exceptional Children
'This article is the result of coordinating and updating two previous unpublished manuscripts completed at Southern Illinois University at Carbondale: "Noncontingent vs. Contingent Reinforcement in the Acquisition of Typewriting Skill" and "Response Competition in Transition from Sight to Nonsight Typing." Taxonomies for specifying and class*fying educational objectives have been extensively developed in the cognitive (Bloom. 1956). affective (Krathwohl. Bloom. & Masia, 1964). and psychomotor (Guilford, 1959. 1966) domains. The act of specifying criteria for student behavior and defending their specification implies some value system which, though rational, is essentially arbitrary. Still, the specification of objectives makes teaching-learning a purposeful, rather than random, activity. Many of the procedures in the development of criterion-referenced measurement (CRM) have their counterparts in classical norm-referenced measurement (NRM) (Gorth & Hambleton, 1972; Shoemaker, 1972). However, the focus in the present discussion is upon those features unique to CRM in the specification and refinement of educational objectives.
