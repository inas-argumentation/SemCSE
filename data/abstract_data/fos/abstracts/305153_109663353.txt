Diurnal Temperature Fluctuations in Multi-Airspaced Buildings
DESIGNING buildings to create a reasonable indoor thermal environment generates an extensive array of interacting variables defining building components, materials, orientation, geometry, occupancy, ventilation, user requirements and other factors. The large number of variables and the complexity of their interactions have forced many simpli-fying assumptions in the thermal design process, One common assumption in the design of agricultural struc-tures is that of a steady-state tem-perature condition. Assuming steady-state conditions during cold weather may be reasonable when temperature differences between inside and out are large relative to daily variations. However, for a significant part of the year, diurnal fluctuations will dom-inate the difference between the inside and outside temperatures.
