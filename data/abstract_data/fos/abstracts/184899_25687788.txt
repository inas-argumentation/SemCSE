INFLUENCE OF PARAQUAT, GLYPHOSATE, AND CADMIUM ON THE ACTIVITY OF SOME SERUM ENZYMES AND PROTEIN ELECTROPHORETIC BEHAVIOR (IN VITRO)
In vitro study for the determination of the toxicity of some pesticides (gly-phospate and paraquat) and cadmium chloride (CdCl2) on the activities of serum acetylcholinesterase (AChE), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), alkaline phosphatase (AlP), and acid phosphatase (AcP) is described. Changes in electrophoretic patterns of serum proteins were also tested. Results revealed that glyphosate was effective on all enzymes except AcP. Its IC50 values (the concentration of compound that inhibits 50% of the enzyme activity in 1 h at 37°C) were 714.3, 750, 54.2, 270.8, and 71.4 mM for AChE, LDH, AST, ALT, and AlP, respectively. The inhibitory effect of paraquat varied markedly among all enzymes. The IC50 values of paraquat were 321.4 and 750 mM for AST and ALT, respectively. It had mild effect on AChE and LDH; and no effect on the activities of AlP and AcP. The effect of CdCl2 was pronounced with AChE, ALT, AlP, and AcP, and no effect on LDH and AST was found. The corresponding IC50 values were 77.7, 22.2, 33.3, and 83.3 mM for AChE, ALT, AlP, and AcP, respectively. Polyacrylamide gel electrophoretic patterns of serum proteins showed marked differences with glyphosate and CdCl2 but not with paraquat. The results suggest that the in vitro enzyme-activity test seems to have a potential for the assessment of pesticide and heavy metal toxicity.
