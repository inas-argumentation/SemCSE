Donor identification in neutron-transmutation-doped GaAs and InP
Neutron transmutation doping (NTD) is used to introduce a controlled amount of shallow donor impurities into GaAs and InP. Changes in the far-infrared (FIR) donor spectrum produced by transmutation doping are studied by photoconductivity techniques. Germanium in GaAs and tin in InP are identified as the dominant shallow impurities introduced by NTD. Selenium donors are also observed in NTD GaAs, but in smaller quantities than expected. High pressures and etching have been used to improve the resolution of the shallow donors and to ensure that distorting effects arising from the strong absorption are not present. These experiments have demonstrated that the chemical shift of the selenium donor in GaAs is very similar to that of the silicon donor and that consequently there is a serious risk of misidentification of the 'X1' component of the donor spectrum in either far infrared or photoluminescence experiments.
