Indeed, one might say (and I embellish the words of one of Michelangelo’s finest scholars) that the statue is in its aura of power and ferocity an amalgam of Moses, Pope Julius II, and Michelangelo—all wrathful. To adapt Vasari’s words, terribilissimi principi. As Vasari wrote, Michelangelo presided over the principato of the arts. Even in its reduced form, the tomb of Pope Julius II is not only a compelling work of art. It is a crucial image in the forging of Michelangelo’s grandiose, mythic persona.
