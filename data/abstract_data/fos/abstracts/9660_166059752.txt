His fundamental tactics never varied: invent nothing, but omit whatever seemed irrelevant to his purposes— above all, anything contradicting Calvin’s doctrinal positions—and abridge his citations. Crespin was generally skeptical of any miraculous circumstances narrated about martyrs and invariably hostile to references about possible relics of martyrs, but unable to resist juicy tales of swift and ugly punishments afflicting those who executed them. Crespin had the good fortune to rely upon two careful contemporary martyrologists, the Anglican John Foxe and the Luthean Ludovicus Rabus. Professor Tucker has the good fortune to say something pertinent while standing upon some sturdy academic shoulders. The two largest belong to Catholics: Crespin’s bibliographer, Jean-Etienne Gilmont, in Europe, and the dean of comparative martyrologists, Brad Gregory, in America.
