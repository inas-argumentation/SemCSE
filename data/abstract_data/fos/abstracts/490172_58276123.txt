Quality Assessment of Laparoscopic Paediatric Urology: Results of the Danish Nationwide Complication Database
Purpose Laparoscopy has recently started to have an impact on paediatric urology, attributed mainly to technologic advances and evidence of the benefits of minimally invasive procedures in children. Since 2003 all urologic laparoscopic procedures in Denmark and their complications were registered prospectively in a nationwide database ‘UROLAP’. We assessed the complication rate in the paediatric population. Material and Methods Data from all departments performing urological laparoscopy are registered in a central database. We analysed the files of all children who underwent a urological laparoscopic procedure in Denmark during the period January 2003 to July 2007. Additionally, some of the data were compared to data from the adult population. Results A total number of 2268 laparoscopic urologic procedures were registered. Of these 370 were performed in children: 80 were diagnostic procedures and 290 were laparoscopic operations. In 26.5% the laparoscopy was robot assisted. Laparoscopic nephrectomies and heminephrectomies accounted for 15%, laparoscopic pyeloplasties 22.8%, laparoscopically assisted orchidopexy for intra-abdomninal testes 50% and other minor laparoscopic procedures approximately 12%. The median length of hospital stay was 1.2 days. There were no major intra-operative complications. The total conversion rate was 1.6%. Postoperative complications were minor (4.1%). In adults the intra-operative complications were more frequent (9.3%) and tend to be more severe which leads to a higher conversion rate (8.2%) and more postoperative complications (10.6%). Conclusions Laparoscopic urologic procedures appear to be safe for a wide range of indications in children. The hospital stay is short, complications are minor and the conversion rate is small.
