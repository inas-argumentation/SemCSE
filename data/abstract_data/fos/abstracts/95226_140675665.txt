Origin and evolution of the Jurassic multicostate zeilleriid brachiopods Eudesia king and Flabellothyris deslongchamps
New biostratigraphical and palaeobiogeographical data make possible a reinterpretation of the origin and evolution of the multicostate zeilleriid brachiopods Eudesia and Flabellothyris . These two genera evolved, during the Bajocian-Bathonian, on both the north and south coasts of the Tethys in western Europe. In spite of some morphological and anatomical differences and a long chronological gap between the Toarcian and the Late Bajocian, they could have derived from Fimbriothyris Deslongchamps which is known from several different species in the Lower and Middle Lias of France, Morocco, Sicily and perhaps Venezia in Italy. The evolution of smooth zeilleriids into multicostate forms ( Eudesia and Flabellothyris ) was closely linked with their environments and occurred in widely separated areas in similar ecological settings (Vicariance biogeographys).
