Poetry and Politics in the Comento of Lorenzo de' Medici
T HE EN C O MI UM of the Tuscan literary language which opens the Comento sopra alcuni de' suoi sonetti of Lorenzo the Magnificent has often been excerpted from its context and viewed in terms of its modernistic impetus to the furtherance of the volgare.1 Governing its survey of Tuscan tradition, however, is the spirit of cultural reminiscence which generally pervades the Comento. Lorenzo's stress on Fortune and his repudiation of the Latin patrimony as a stylistic guide point to an ambivalent conception of the relationship between politics and culture.
