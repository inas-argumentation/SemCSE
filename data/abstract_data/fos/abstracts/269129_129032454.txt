Geology of Plancha L-12, Colombia, South America: A Reconnaissance
Plancha L-12 (quadrangle) of the Map of Colombia, covering 4800 sq km, includes part of the easternmost Andean chain of Colombia, Cordillera Oriental, its eastern foothills, and the adjacent flatlands. The exposed rocks have a cumulative thickness of about 7000 m and are divided by the author into ten major units. Pre-Devonian(?) granodiorite, phyllites, and metasandstones are unconformably overlain by 800 m of Middle to Upper Paleozoic argillites, sandstones, and limestones at the center of an anticlinorium which constitutes the core of Cordillera Oriental. A major reverse fault marks the eastern limit of Cordillera Oriental. The foothills display asymmetrical folds, thrust faults, and what appears to be large-scale slumping. The rocks exposed include 600 m of conglomerates, hales, and sandstones of Mesozoic age; 1700 m of sandstones and shales which range from Eocene through Oligocene or early Miocene; and about 3000 m of conglomerates, conglomeratic sandstones, and mudstones of Miocene age. In the eastern and southern half of Plancha L-12, the structures of the foothills disappear under Pliocene(?) to Recent alluvial cover in lowlands which make up 75 per cent of Plancha L-12. Some of the unconsolidated fluvial deposits of Pliocene(?) to Recent age have been tilted and folded. Analysis of the stream pattern of the lowlands suggests tectonic control of modern physiographic features in the eastern part of Plancha L-12. There is evidence of regional uplift and folding in Early Paleozoic, Jurassic(?), late Eocene or early Oligocene, Miocene, and Plio-Pleistocene time. The main uplift of Cordillera Oriental appears to have occurred in late Miocene time.
