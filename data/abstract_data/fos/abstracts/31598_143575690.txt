Youth cultures in Britain: the middle class take-over
This article discusses the character of Britain's youth cultures at the end of the 1980s using evidence from the ESRC 16–19 Initiative's longitudinal studies among representative samples from four parts of Britain. The females had the highest social contact scores and equalled the males' levels of overall leisure activity. The females' leisure was not particularly restricted or centred around a bedroom culture although males were the more active in sport, and over-represented among the most committed minorities in all kinds of public leisure. The main social class differences were that middle class youth were slightly the more leisure active overall, had closer relationships with their friends, and, like the males compared with the females, were over-represented among the most committed participants in out-of-home leisure in general.
