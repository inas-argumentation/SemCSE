Accelerated Pacific plate movements and climatic changes on time-scales of 102–103 years in the late quaternary: A synthesis
Abstract Sea-level curves, constructed for the Japan region for the period 14,000 to 10,000 years B.P., reveal inconsistencies in sea level during the intervals 10,000–10,500, 11,500–12,000 and 13,000–13,500 years B.P. These intervals correspond to episodes of global climatic deterioration and catastrophic volcanic activity in Japan. These inconsistencies are explained, on the basis of field observation, by geoidal change and reactivation of the Pacific plate movements. From a glacio-eustatic sea-level curve, it is supposed that the reactivation of the Pacific plate movements was induced by mass redistribution within the Earth due to rapid melting of polar ice. Furthermore, the rapid spreading within the ocean basins greatly increased upwelling and this change in the ocean circulation resulted in abrupt global climatic cooling. Similar phenomena are recognized after 8000 and 130,000 years B.P.
