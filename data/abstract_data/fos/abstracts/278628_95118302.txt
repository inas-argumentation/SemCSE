Accelerated degradation of carbofuran in standing water from carbofuran‐retreated Azolla plots
Abstract Carbofuran (2, 3‐dihydro‐2, 2‐dimethyl‐7‐benzofuranyl N‐methylcarbamate) was mixed with standing water from six flooded Azolla (a fern harboring a nitrogen fixing alga, Anabaena azollae) plots that had been regularly treated with carbofuran before. The insecticide completely disappeared in 5 to 10 days when mixed with water from three of the six plots. The enrichment culture, prepared by further additions of carbofuran to the standing water from an Azolla plot, degraded bendiocarb (2, 2‐dimethyl‐l, 3‐benzidioxol‐4‐yl‐N‐methylcarbamate), carbofuran and carbosulfan [2,3‐dihydro‐2,2‐dimethyl‐7‐benzofuranyl (di‐n‐butyl‐aoinosulfenyl) methyl‐carbamate ] in that order. Enrichment culture, upon sterilization by autoclavlng, lost its ability to degrade carbofuran. Evidently, accelerated degradation of carbofuran in standing water from retreated Azolla plots was mediated by microorganisms.
