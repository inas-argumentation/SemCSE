Are birth defects caused by spermicidal agents? [letter]
Spermicidal agents used for birth control by millions of couples are known to be less than 100% effective; 20%-40% of women may conceive after use. From a sperm count of 70 million per ml several million will survive somehow and carry on to the fallopian tubes. Some may be in perfect biological condition some others may not fare so well. What damage to the sperm may result? Structural or chemical damage may occur to cell membrane mitochondrion cytoplasm and especially to the 23 chromosomes (and their many loci carrying 50% of the characteristics of the future embryo). A sperm which temporarily contacts a spermicidal agent and survives may be only slightly damaged and be able to fertilize an ovum. The selectivity of the ovums membrane is not all that selective (many male sex linked characteristics are transmitted). If the couple had used chemicals these possibilities would not exist. Since jellies and foams are available anywhere in drugstores it may be important to investigate their potential injury to future generations. They perhaps should be stopped until studies are available to prove that no such damage to offspring exists. I would invite further comments on this thought.(FULL-TEXT)
