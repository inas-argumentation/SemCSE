Renal function tests also exist.
We have read carefully the interesting paper published in your journal entitled: Renal tract abnormalities missed in a historical cohort of young children with UTI if the NICE and AAP imaging guidelines were applied [1], in which Hassib Narchi et al. exposed that if the NICE and AAP guidelines had been implemented, the diagnosis of renal scarring would have been missed in 62% of their children, and the diagnosis of VUR >grade II would have been missed in 31%. In their paper, they corroborated the conclusion previously reported by Logvinenko et al. in your journal [2]. Noticing that the usefulness of renal function tests is not mentioned in any of the algorithms used in studies of children with UTI or urinary tract dilatation, we conducted a study to analyze if the association of morphological (sonography) and three renal function tests (albumin/creatinine urine ratio, N-Acetyl-glucosaminidase (NAG)/creatinine ratio, and maximum urine osmolality after desmopressin) are useful in determining the patients for whom initial VCUG should not be practiced. The advantage of renal function tests is that they are non-invasive, inexpensive, and produce no radiation damage. With this aim, we analyzed all of the records from 79 infants suffering from mild or moderate pyelectasis (57 males, 22 females). The results concluded that sensitivity of: the maximum urine osmolality to detect VUR was 72.7% (specificity 63.2%, predictive negative value 93.4%); the albumin/creatinine ratio to detect VUR was 62.5% (specificity 75%, predictive negative value 93.4%); the NAG/ creatinine ratio was 22.2% (specificity 81.8%, predictive negative value 83.7%) [3].
