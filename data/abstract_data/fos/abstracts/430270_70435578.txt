Urethral Obstructive Pathology in Boys Can Not be Predicted by a Statistical Model
Purpose In boys presenting with voiding disorders in tertiary care centres, urethral obstructive pathology ideally has to be excluded before conservative treatment is continued. As MCUG is bothersome and cystoscopy needs general anesthesia, we tried to construct a statistical model predictive for boys needing this examinations. Material and Methods All 27 boys aged > 5 years who underwent a cystoscopy during the last 2 years were included. Anamnesic parameters included history of UTI, hematuria, painful micturition, constipation and urinary incontinence. The 48-h voiding chart was screened for frequency, total output, morning voided volumes, maximum voided volume and average voided volume during daytime, related to bladder volume expected for age. Voiding time, maximum flow rate, and time to maximum flow were extracted manually from minimally 3 uroflows and related to total voided volume. Bladder wall thickness and post void residual urine was measured by ultrasound. Results Boys with or without urethral pathology did not differ significantly concerning presence of UTI, hematuria, constipation, urinary incontinence, or urinary frequency. Nor the average flow rate, nor time to maximum flow or total voiding time related to voided volume made uroflowmetry predictive. Bladder wall thickness of > 4 mm or a post-void residual of > 10% of the average bladder capacity couldn't predict obstruction. The odds for having obstruction is 3 in case of post-void residual, 4.5 for a thick bladder wall, and 2.8 for a non-bell-shaped uroflow. The log-linear model was unable to construct a predictive model for the parameters registered. Conclusions We were unable to construct a predictive model for urethral pathology for boys presenting with obstructive voiding. Performing a diagnostic cystoscopy in all boys with voiding disorders would maybe help, but we feel this unethical.
