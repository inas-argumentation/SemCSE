This designation is both descriptive and appropriate, for some of the text's manifestations are titled doctrina pequena or, in Nahuatl, tepiton teotlahtolli, ''little sacred speech.'' The Little Doctrine is a list of questions and answers, 17 to 29 in number, intended to encapsulate key points of Christianity in a manner suited to people of limited education. The text could also be called the ''Castano catechism,'' as most versions derive from the 24-question Cate- cismo breve de lo que precisamente debe saber el cristiano that Jesuit father Bartolome ´ Castano published in 1644. However, the Little Doctrine predates Castano's publication and undergoes many permutations independent of that work. Because the Little Doctrine circulated in at least 11 indigenous languages, it opens a window onto a widespread catechetical practice, revealing clergy- men's diminishing expectations regarding indigenous Christianity and native people's experience with Christian teachings.
