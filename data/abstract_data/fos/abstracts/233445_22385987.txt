Differential Alterations of Receptor Densities of Three Muscarinic Acetylcholine Receptor Subtypes and Current Densities of the Corre-sponding K+ Channels in Canine Atria with Atrial Fibrillation Induced by Experimental Congestive Heart Failure
Parasympathetic tone and congestive heart failure (CHF) are two of promoting factors in initiation and perpetuation of atrial fibrillation (AF). Recent studies indicate co-existence of multiple muscarinic acetylcholine receptor subtypes (mAChRs) that mediate several distinct K<sup>+</sup> currents in the heart; inward rectifier K<sup>+</sup> current I<sub>KACh</sub> by the M<sub>2</sub>, and two delayed rectifier K<sup>+</sup> currents I<sub>KM3</sub> and I<sub>K4AP</sub> by the M<sub>3</sub> and M<sub>4</sub> receptors, respectively. We studied the alterations of atrial mAChRs and their coupled K<sup>+</sup> channels in the setting of AF in dogs with ventricular tachypacing-induced CHF. Whole-patch-clamp recordings showed that the current densities of I<sub>KACh</sub> (induced by 1 mM acetylcholine) and I<sub>K4AP</sub> (induced by 1 mM 4-aminopyridine) were ∼45% and ∼55% lower, respectively, while that of I<sub>KM3</sub> (induced by 10 mM choline) was ∼75% higher, at a plateau voltage of 0 mV in atrial myocytes from CHF than those from healthy hearts. In healthy hearts, I<sub>KACh</sub> comprised >60%, and I<sub>KM3</sub> and I<sub>K4AP</sub> <30%, of the total outward K<sup>+</sup> currents mediated by mAChRs at depolarized potentials (between –20 mV and +50 mV). In AF atria of CHF dogs, however, the contribution of I<sub>KM3</sub> increased to ∼50%, exceeding those of I<sub>KACh</sub> or I<sub>K4AP</sub>. Western blot analyses with atrial membrane protein samples indicated that receptor densities of the M<sub>2</sub> and M<sub>4</sub> subtypes decreased by ∼33% and ∼22%, respectively, whereas that of the M<sub>3</sub> subtype increased by ∼2.3 folds, in parallel to the alterations of the corresponding K<sup>+</sup> currents. We conclude that differential alterations of mAChR subtypes underlie differential alterations of their coupled K<sup>+</sup> channels in AF atria and these differential alterations may contribute to atrial remodeling in AF induced in the setting of CHF.
