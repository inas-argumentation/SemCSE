During this time he also met his lifelong wife Ilse a micropaleontolologist with whom he co-authored a number of substantial papers on Jurassic foraminifers. they married in 1952. In 1954 he returned to the university of tübingen, where he worked as an associate Professor until he received an offer for a full professorship at the Christian albrechts university (Cau) in Kiel in northern Germany. Eugen Seibold started off as a true field geologist, concentrating at first on the area of his family roots and publishing on its tectonics (the Swabian “Lineament”) as well as the peculiar alternations of strata richer and poorer in carbonate of the upper Jurassic. Even after becoming a professor at Kiel university and occasionally sitting as president of the German Research Foundation, he continued to guide excursions and field courses in this part of Germany. His results became part of the formal geological map 1:25,000 of baden-Württemberg, Sheet 7126 aalen. the move to Kiel presented a big challenge to him and his family. His predecessor had been leading the institute (“Geologisch-Paläontologisches Institut und Museum = GPI”) since the end of the war. He was a wellknown Quaternary geologist with substantial merits in research on the geological history of Schleswig–Holstein, but the working atmosphere within the institute was still suffering under the consequences of the second world war. He was our highly esteemed academic mentor and colleague. Eugen Seibold was born in Stuttgart into a Swabian farmer’s family and learned early to love fossils. He decided to study geology, started at the university of tübingen, but was then drafted into the German army during the 2nd World War.
