Echinoid distribution in the Cenomanian: an analytical study in biogeography
Faunal lists of Cenomanian echinoids from sixteen geographical regions within Europe, North and South America, Africa and the Middle East are compiled and subjected to quantitative analysis. After revision 177 species and 78 genera are accepted. Species richness, corrected for sampling bias, shows no latitudinal variation in alpha diversity. However, beta diversity shows an apparent decline in temperate palaeolatitudes, possibly due to a decrease in habitat diversity. Distributional patterns are best interpreted as a mosaic of overlapping geographical ranges and not as a pattern of discrete provinces. Distance matrix clustering, parsimony analysis of endemism and cladistic vicariance analysis are all applied to the data. Specific and generic data produce slightly different topologies with Charente-Maritime and Portugal switching affinities. This correlates with the development of oceanic rifting in the Bay of Biscay, an event so closely predating the Cenomanian that it is reflected in species but not generic data. Patterns of area relationships generally reflect proximity of regions palaeogeographically, as expected. Both latitudinal and longitudinal gradational faunal changes are identified. Evidence, though not conclusive, suggests that “Atlantic” and “Tethyan” elements were by this time already differentiated. South Atlantic echinoid faunas show closest relationship to the Texas region, not with North Africa.
