El apóstol de los Andes: el culto a Túpac Amaru en Cusco durante la revolución velasquista (1968–1975) by Raúl H. Asensio (review)
Dorr aims to make critical interventions into a number of cutting-edge academic discussions regarding race, gender, sexuality, nation, and diaspora. She does so with a remarkable cogency that demands and rewards multiple re-readings. But this very intellectual ambitiousness works against the book’s stated intention to provide “an experience with rather than an account of South American musical transits” (15). An experiential immersion requires more attention to the sensory qualities of sound itself than Dorr gives us. When she does describe the vocal sounds of Sumac, Wendy Sulca, Susana Baca, and others, she does so beautifully, and turns her readers into more deeply attuned listeners.
