Nondestructive Evaluation of Carbon Fiber-Reinforced Polymer-Concrete Bond Using Infrared Thermography
The FHWA estimates that 230,000 of the 575,000 U.S. bridges are structurally deficient of functionally obsolete. Many of these bridges can be repaired or strengthened with CFRP composites. Much work has been conducted on the structural aspects of bonded repair systems; however, long-term performance characteristics of the system are still largely unknown. With the likelihood of increased use of CFRP systems in structural applications, it is imperative that a simple and effective method for nondestructive evaluation (NDE) be developed. Infrared (IR) thermography can be potentially used to detect flaws in bonded repair systems, including installation defects and long-term degradation. The work given in this paper provides fundamental qualitative and quantitative data indicating IR inspection to be a viable method for the NDE of bonded CFRP systems.
