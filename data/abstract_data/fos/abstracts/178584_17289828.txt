Justice Improved: The Unrecognized Benefits of Aggregation and Sampling in the Trial of Mass Torts
Commentators of every persuasion agree that mass torts, particularly trials of asbestos-induced diseases, confront the justice system with serious problems for which it has yet to find solutions. While merely disposing of these cases has proven a formidable burden to courts, actually doing justice in them seems daunting. A procedure is evolving in the trial of these cases whereby samples of cases are tried and the resulting damages are then applied to the remaining population of cases.
