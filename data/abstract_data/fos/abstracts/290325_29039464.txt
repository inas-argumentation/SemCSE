Isolation of soluble antigens from connective tissue of several human organs.
Extracts containing complex mixtures of antigens were prepared by sonicating extensively washed low speed sediments of human liver, kidney and placenta homogenates. Rabbit antisera to these antigens reacted with the sonicates of all three organs in agar diffusion tests, revealing the presence of ten to fifteen antigens in each organ. The antisera reacted also with organ sections in fluorescent antibody tests, staining exclusively connective tissue structures: vessel and sinusoidal walls, basement membranes, sarcolemma. The staining patterns of the three antisera were identical, showing the extensive cross-reactivity of connective tissue antigens in the organs tested. The anti-liver serum stained also connective tissue of other species: rat and dog. These experiments demonstrate the feasibility of preparing connective tissue antigens in true solution and with little denaturation.
