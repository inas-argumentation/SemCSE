The Correlation of the Disease Severity of Banana Fusarium Wilt and the Population Density of the Pathogen in Soil
The objective of this study was to explore the correlation of the disease severity and the population density of the pathogen in soil. A selective medium and molecular method were used to monitor the dynamic change of Fusarium oxysporum f.sp.cubense race 4(FOC4)in banana rhizosphere soil. The results showed that the KP medium was able to detect FOC4 population effectively in soil,and the detection efficiency was more than95%. In the analog inoculation of 10,103,106CFU/g FOC4 spores in the soil,the population of FOC4 was detected in the 21stdays of 10 CFU/g and 103CFU/g inoculation and the 1st day of 106CFU/g inoculation.Conventional PCR could detect FOC4 when its colony count was greater than 1.7 ×105CFU/g. The greater initial soil inoculum density would lead to more severe the disease severity. The disease severity index of all treatments was the highest in the 54-61 days after inoculation. When the initial inoculum density was middle and low,such as10 CFU and 103CFU,in 1～61 days after inoculation,FOC soil population density and disease severity was positively correlated with a correlation coefficient of 0.967 6 and 0.829 2. When the initial inoculum density was high,such as 106CFU,banana Fusarium wilt developed rapid,and its severity was higher. Therefore,KP medium could detect and monitor FOC population in soil effectively. The severity of banana Fusarium wilt increases with the initial amount of FOC inoculum in soil. Through the establishment of the regression equation between the disease severity and the pathogen population density in soil,it will provide a good basis for planting banana,developing disease prevention and control strategies and disease risk assessment.
