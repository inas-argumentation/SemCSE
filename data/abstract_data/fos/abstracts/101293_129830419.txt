Terrestrial palaeclimatology in the Tertiary: a methodological critique
The state of discussion on Tertiary climate is reviewed with emphasis on the Lower Rhine Embayment (LRE). Serious methodological deficits and the absence of climate reconstructions by utilizing limited area models seem to be major drawbacks to present Tertiary paleoclimate reconstruction efforts. Since close cooperation of modern quantitative evaluation and estimation of palaeontological-geological remains and numerical experiments with (mostly) global and (rarely) regional climate models proves to be very fruitful in the Quaternary climate reconstruction context, we propose a combined approach using mesoscale climate modeling and palaeontological-geological analyses for regional Tertiary climate studies; this being applicable when a locally dense net of relevant probes related to one comparatively homogeneous “time slice” of about 1 Ma (e.g. in the Miocene) is available. This data base enables optimal estimates of surface climate parameters by combining tolerance bounds of different taxa and stable regression techniques when possible. Using a trial-and-error method, the model's output is fitted to the data base by minimizing an error function. The computed model climatic statistics, in turn, serve as a guideline for critical examination of the palaeontological-geological data base by determining inconsistencies. An a posteriori by-product is a ranking of different estimation methods using climate parameters from palaeontological indicators. We discuss the advantages, drawbacks and a possible setup of our proposed combined approach.
