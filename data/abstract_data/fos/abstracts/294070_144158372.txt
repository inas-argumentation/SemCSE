Application of Keyword Mnemonics to Learning Terminology in the College Classroom
AbstractA memory technique called “keyword method” was applied to the college classroom to teach medical terminology. In this method, sound-alikes (keywords) and visual images were presented to students to enhance learning and retention. The method was tested on intact classrooms, with measures over two lessons, and at three different time periods and with replication over a second semester. Students were unaware of their participation in this experiment and studied usual course content. Results indicate that medical terminology students scored significantly higher with the keyword method than with a traditional method in both initial learning and retention of medical definitions for as long as 8 weeks. The keyword method was applied in both a conventional classroom setting and in individualized learning, and it proved equally effective in both.
