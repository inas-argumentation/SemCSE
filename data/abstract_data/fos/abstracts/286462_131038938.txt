Turtle, crocodilian, and champsosaur diversity changes in the Cenozoic of the north-central region of western United States
The generic diversity of large-bodied reptiles (turtles, crocodilians, and champsosaurs) in the northern Great Plains and eastern slope of the northern Rocky Mountains of the United States is greatest in the early Cenozoic, lowest in the middle Cenozoic and moderately high in the late Cenozoic with the exception of a major decline in the latest Tertiary. The record of large tortoises indicates that these declines were not the result of critically cool winter temperatures except in glacial periods, recent times and the Paleocene. The major declines in the middle Cenozoic and latest Tertiary appear to reflect mild winters (about 13°C or above for cold month mean temperature) but with annually or seasonally dry climates resulting in few bodies of permanent surface waters.
