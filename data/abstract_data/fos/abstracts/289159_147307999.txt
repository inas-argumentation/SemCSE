Higher Education in Pakistan: Problems That Beset the Student Community
H E WHOSE campus calendar has darkly chronicled its share of skip days and pantie raids soon finds a moonshine of maxims to brighten his almanac. He tells himself (and his president) that his problems are not unique. He cherishes newspaper clippings of the much greater property damages at That Other University last year. And he prophesies that Dean Smugface up the pike had better enjoy his quiet spring quarter while he's got it, because he's sure to be up to his goiter in beer cans by Hallowe'en. Even our most dreadful dreams in our most fitful slumber, however, do not forebode any really new specters to haunt our student personnel work. Those that we haven't grappled with personally are known to us through fable. This is because our American college students and their schools-from Alaska to Florida-are more homogeneous than we realize. The imaginings that bedevil our deans differ more in degree than in kind. On the opposite side of the world there are, of course, the same student problems that are old-home week to a visitor from the United States. There are others, however, that are rarely experienced by the student personnel worker on the American campus. Like our own problems, these, too, are legacies of a centuries-old Tree of Life onto which, only yesterday, was grafted a formal education.
