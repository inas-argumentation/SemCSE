But where the study of mortality in British towns and cities is concerned, Armstrong's valuable work on Carlisle has remained until very recently a lone contribution; and even now, with the addition of Huck's work, information is available on only a handful of small towns.4 This is almost certainly because of the relative paucity and inaccessibility of reliable, relevant demographic evidence for this particular period, the 'dark age' of Britain's modem historical demography, 1780-1850.
