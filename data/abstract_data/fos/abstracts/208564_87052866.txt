Homocysteine and folic acid′s effect on tPA secretion and mRNA gene expression in HUVEC
Objective In order to elucidate the relationship between homocysteine (Hcy) and the fibrinolytic system, we evaluated the effect of Hcy, with or without folic acid, in the secretion and mRNA gene expression of tissue-type plasminogen activator (tPA) from human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVEC). Methods Ten groups were divided at the following concentrations of Hcy (0, 10, 50, 200, 500μmol/L ) and folic acid at a concentration of 15μmol/L with Hcy of the above levels. The tPA levels of HUVEC′s supernatant were measured by ELISA. Total RNA was extracted using the guanidinium isothiocyanate method. The semi-quantification of tPA mRNA in HUVEC was carried out by reverse transcriptase-polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR). Results Compared with the control group(0μmol/L Hcy), tPA mRNA and antigen levels of 10μmol/L of Hcy were much higher (P0.05 ), while it decreased in a dose dependent manner at concentrations greater than 10μmol/L of Hcy. Compared with the concentration of 10μmol/L of Hcy, however, significant depression of tPA mRNA gene expression and secretion levels were observed at the concentration of 500μmol/L of Hcy(P0.05). The addition of folic acid obviously stimulated both tPA mRNA expression and antigen levels at concentrations of 500μmol/L of Hcy, compared with only Hcy group(P0.05). Conclusion These results suggested that hyperhomocysteinemia increased the incidence of cardio cerebral vascular disease, which may be caused by the decreased activity of fibrinolytic system, whereas, folic acid may play a protective effect against the toxic action of Hcy. The physiological concentration of Hcy may decreased the incidence by enhancing the activity of fibrinolytic system. [
