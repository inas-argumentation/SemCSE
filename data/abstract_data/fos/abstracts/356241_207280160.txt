Technique for fabricating metal-mesh reflectors on curved substrates.
Metal meshes, described in some detail by Ulrich, have proven to be versatile reflectors at millimeter and submillimeter (MSM) wavelengths. As freestanding structures or stretched across suitable substrates, they are widely used as beam splitters, in filters, in Fabry-Perot resonators, and in other resonators which incorporate flat reflectors. The complementary capacitive structure, deposited on a MSM transmitting substrate, has also been used in filters but is usually employed in the construction of laser output couplers. We describe here a photolithographic technique whereby metal meshes or their complements can be produced on curved substrates. This permits these useful partially reflective structures to be applied to a wider range of MSM optics.
