Studies on the mechanism and clinical significance of low immunogenicity of hepatitis B virus "a" determinant 129Leu variant
Objective To study the mechanism and clinical significance of low immunogenicity of HBV "a" determinant 129Leu variant. Methods Four recombinant plasmids containing S gene of wild type(p3.8Ⅱ) and 129Leu (p3.8Ⅱ129L), 129His(p3.8Ⅱ129H), 145Arg(p3.8Ⅱ145R) variants were constructed and separately used to transfect HepG2 cells. HBsAg, HBeAg in supernatant and HBV DNA replication in intracellular were assayed. These plasmids were also used to immunize BALB/c and C57BL/6 mice. Antibodies and cytokines induced in splenocytes of immunized mice were examined. The hydrophobicity of residue 129 was compared between the wild type and mutated "a" determinant by computer based analysis. Mutations of "a" determinant were detected in 134 HBsAg positive hepatitis B patients by PCR SSCP and sequencing technology. Results The titer of anti HBs and the level of IFN γ induced by splenocytes in two strains of mice immunized with p3.8Ⅱ129L plasmid DNA were both lower than those immunized with wild type and p3.8Ⅱ129H DNA ( P 0.05). Totally, nine serum samples from 134 HBsAg positive hepatitis B patients showed "a" determinant amino acid substitutions (6.9%) , and 1/9 was 129Leu variant. Conclusions The cellular and humoral low immunogenicity of variant 129Leu was most likely caused by the substitution of 129 amino acid from glutamine to leucine, because when 129 amino acid was substituted to leucine, the hydrophobicity of this residue was markedly increased.
