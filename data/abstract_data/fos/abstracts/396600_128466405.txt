Petrography and depositional environment of coal seams in the Carboniferous Morien Group, Sydney Coalfield, Nova Scotia
The Morien Group (Westphalian A – Stephanian) of the Sydney Basin, Nova Scotia, contains numerous coal seams, many of which can be traced across the onshore part of the basin. Depositional environments during coal formation range from proximal, fluvially dominated to intercalated distal fluvial, to restricted marine. Most of the coal samples analyzed in this study come from the Bras d'Or section, a near-continuous coastal exposure of the Sydney Mines Formation. Petrographic compositions of the samples are discussed in the context of the depositional setting of the enclosing strata. The coal seams have a high vitrinite content (70–90 vol. %), with moderate inertinite and low liptinite. Petrographic indices suggest that most coals of the Sydney Mines Formation are similar to seams deposited in interdistributary or lower delta plain settings in other basins and provide support for the interpreted sedimentological setting of this sequence.
