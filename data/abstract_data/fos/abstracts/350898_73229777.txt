Tum or Pum in Patients with Persistent Ugs and Cloacal Malformation? An Attempt to Explore the Morphological Routes of the Operation and Delineate the Structures which are Involved
Purpose Total urogenital or partial urogenital mobilizations are performed in correcting persistent urogenital sinus or persistent cloaca (J Pediatr Surg 32:263-268, 1997; BJU 83:820-822, 1999; J Pediatr Surg 39:470-479, 2004; Curr Opin Pediatr 17: 394-401, 2005). The safety regarding urinary continence and genital sensation is still under discussion. The aim of this investigation was to explore, from the morphological potential point of view, the risk for neural or vascular damage during these operations. Material and methods The anatomy of the plane of dissection of the UGS with special attention to the nerve supply of the urinary sphincter complex and the clitoris was investigated in 31 plastinated female pelves from 9th gestational week to term. The morphological routes of the operation are shown up and delniate the structures which are involved. Results In previously unoperated cases the dissection can be performed without disturbing the innervation or vascularization (branches from pudendel nerve and arteries). Conclusions The technique of TUM simplifies the surgical correction of UGS malformations. Urinary continence and genital sensation should not compromised by this technique in primary cases. In secondary cases or with additional spinal malformation incontinence can occur, because the plane of dissection may be scarred or the nerve supply not be well developed. Cautery should be used cautiously to avoid damage of the nerve/blood supply.
