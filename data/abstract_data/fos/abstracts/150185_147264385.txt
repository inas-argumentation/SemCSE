Gaining social capital through classroom functioning
A growing body of scholarship links classroom functioning to social capital gain. This article looks at the ‘what’ of classroom functioning to contribute to social capital gain. A qualitative investigation was undertaken based on individual interviews conducted at three primary schools in Gauteng, KwaZulu-Natal and Northwest Province. Although the selected schools are classified as resource poor due to constrained finances, pupils perform well in annual standardised assessment, which anticipated the probability of notable social capital facilitation in classrooms. The findings show that social capital gain, which manifests itself in network-related engagement based on norms of reciprocity and social trust, equips pupils with knowledge and skills for appropriate conduct and possible social mobility. Functional procedures ensure order and focus that engender constructive behaviour. Positive relationships and living with respect for one another foster considerate behaviour and a sense of belonging in the classroom. Respect for lesson time engenders a sense of punctuality, resulting in adequate time for the facilitation of required knowledge and skills to ensure self-actualisation. The findings contribute to the discourse on classroom functioning for social capital gain.
