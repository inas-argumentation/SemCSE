In fact, Speroni also experimented with a paradoxical fashion of dialogue, seemingly defending the use of discord and usury (chapter 3), and this development is also what makes these texts difficult to interpret and historicize. Moving away from Speroni’s anti-Aristotelian ethical posture, Katinis’s chapter 4 deals with sixteenth-century interpretations of Dante’s poetry as a kind of sophistry. Almost a monograph within the monograph, this treatment is nourished by an analysis of horizontal academic networks linking in and out Tasso’sDiscorsi del poema eroico (1594). Without accounting for the intricacy of this debate, which starts from a consideration of the role of appearance and culminates with Jacopo Mazzoni’s defense of Dante’s metaphors of painting, it is sufficient to note here that Katinis is most certainly correct in his stubborn intuition that one could retrace Dante’s disputes to the Sophists and conclude that it was persuasion, instead of truth, needed to stabilize public belief.
