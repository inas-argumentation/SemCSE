Composition of Renal and Bladder Calculi in Pediatric Stone Formers
Purpose To analyze the chemical composition of the core and surface of renal and bladder calculi and its importance in determining etiology of stone formation. Material and Methods The mainstay of stone analysis was infrared spectroscopy. In stones with compound mixtures chemical examination was undertaken. Stones with Amonium Hydrogen Urate (AHU) in the core were evaluated by scanning electron microscopy (SEM)to determine crystal structures and stones Results Of the 2039 stones analyzed, 726 (36%) were composed purely of a single compound salt and 64% were mixed stones. Overall frequency of compounds in stones was AHU in 58%, calcium oxalate monohydrate (COM) in 63%, calcium oxalate dehydrate (COD) 20%, uric acid (UA) 6%, calcium phosphate apatite (CAP) 12% and struvite in 8%. Surface and core analysis was possible in 1274 renal stones. Frequency of compounds in core of renal stones were AHU 64%, CaOx 41%, UA 3%, CAP 9% and struvite 5%. In the surface analysis, AHU reduced to 30% and CaOx increased to 72%. Stratification of pure stones by age groups, A ( 10 years), showed differences in pattern of stones types. Separate core and surface analysis was possible in 198 bladder stones. Frequency of compounds in core of bladder stones was AHU 61%, CaOx 35%, UA 10%, CAP 7%, and struvite 6%. Surface analysis again showed low AHU (37%), high CaOx (52%) and increased UA (16%), CAP (12%) and struvite (15%). Conclusions The presence of AHU in kidney and bladder calculi suggests a common cause in our population i.e. endemic calculi due to poor nutrition, diarrhea and dehydration. Increasing age gives pattern similar to developed world where calcium oxalate predominates.
