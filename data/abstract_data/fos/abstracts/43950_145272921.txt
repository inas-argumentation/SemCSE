Order of Content Vs Grammatical Emphases in Theme Grading
Follman, Holland, and Miller (1970) recently recommended that graders grade English composition themes twice, once for holistic quality (or content quality) and once for grammatical quality. Implicit was the notion that graders might develop a set under one grading focus that might carry over and influence the level of grades awarded under a later different focus. The aim of the present study was to collect pilot evidence on this question to indicate if more research is in order. The specific aim was to determine if gross differences in levels of grades would be found with reversed order of two grading foci, Content vs Grammar. Ss, 15 University of South Florida English methods seniors in October, 1970, were randomly assigned to two experimental groups. Group I ( N = 8 ) graded 10 themes for grammatical, mechanical, punctuation, spelling quality only, then graded the same 10 themes for content, development of ideas, organization, style quality only. Grading order was reversed for Group I1 ( N = 7) . PlusMinus A, B, C, D and F grades were used. These were converted to raw scores with A+ = 15, A = 14, A= 13, B+ = 12, B = 11, B= 10, C+ = 9, C = 8 , C = 7 , D + = 6 , D = 5 , D = 4 , F + = 3 , F = 2 , F = l . For a description of the themes and the other experimental conditions see Follman, Holland, and Miller ( 1970) and Follman, Lowe, and Miller ( 197 1 ) . Analysis of variance adjusted group reliability estimates for total themes were .28 for Grammar first and .65 for Content second for Group I, and .88 for Content first and .91 for Grammar second for Group 11. The low Grammar first estimate is seen as idiosyncratic sample variance. Means and standard deviations respectively for total of the 10 themes were 9.79 and 3.02 for Grammar first and 9.59 and 3.25 for Content second (Group I ) , and 9.60 and 3.25 for Content first and 9.64 and 3.67 for Grammar second (Group 11). Each Grammar group mean was slightly higher than its corresponding Content mean. The t test of difference scores indicated non-significant values between the two Grammar groups, and also between the two Content groups. I t is concluded that it makes little difference on level of grades whether themes are graded for Content or Grammar first.
