The Spectrum of Individual Interaction Characteristics: An Inter-Dimensional Analysis
The research reported in this paper was undertaken with five objectives. The major of these was to replicate in part the work reported by Carter ( 2 ) in order to determine whether or not a factor analysis of rankings of behaviors of Ss in selected small group situations would yield similar findings. A second was to determine the extent to which certain measures of sociometric status in the groups studied were related to variations in rankings on trait names and behavioral descriptive categories. The third objective was to examine the relation of the rankings as defined on the factor structure to the way persons participated in the small group situations as scored by a trained observer. A fourth objective centered on an analysis of the relationship between certain measures of social perception and the scores on selected interaction characteristics. Finally, we were interested in the effect of the assignment of individuals to specific roles in the group on their interaction characteristics and how this would be reflected in rankings made by peers. On the basis of his own analyses and from parallel studies by several other researchers, Carter ( 2 ) has concluded that three factors account for most of the observable variance of individuals in interaction. He describes the factors as follows. Factor 1 is called Individual prominence and achievement. "These are behaviors of the individual related to his efforts to stand out from others and individually achieve various personal goals." The second factor is called Aiding attainment by the group. "These are behaviors of the individual related to his efforts to assist the group in achieving goals toward which the group is oriented." Factor 3 is called Sociability. "These are behaviors of the individual related to efforts to establish and maintain cordial and socially satisfying relations with other group members." Carter drew several implications from his findings. First, he suggested that whenever a system of rating of interaction behavior was utilized, provision should be made to obtain a reliable estimate of the behavior of the actors for the three factors noted above.
