Electrochemical Performance of Nanocrystalline Vanadium Pentoxide Thin Films Grown by RF Magnetron Sputtering
Nanocrystalline V 2 O 5 thin films were prepared by radio frequency (RF) magnetron sputtering and explored as potential electrodes for Li-ion microbatteries and supercapacitors based on microstructure and electrochemical properties. All the films grown in the substrate temperature ( T s ) range 250–350°C exhibited predominant (001) orientation corresponding to the orthorhombic V 2 O 5 layered structure. However, notable changes were observed in the surface morphology and crystallite size of the grown films by varying the substrate temperature. The films deposited at T s of 250°C showed uniformly distributed spherical grain morphology and demonstrated pseudocapacitive behavior with a specific capacitance of 960 mF cm −2 at current density of 1 mA cm −2 with good cycle stability. The films deposited at T s of 350°C showed needle like nanorod structure with an average crystallite size of 36 nm.
