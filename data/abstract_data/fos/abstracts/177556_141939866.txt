Thriving in a Faculty and a College Setting.
This paper takes a look at the two aspects of campus lives--one centred on the faculty and the other on the hostel. They both play equally important and complimentary roles in the intellectual and social development of students. ********** Background Universities occupy a unique place in a human society. They play crucial roles in the development of a nation's human capital. The dons whose knowledge and views are assiduously sought are among the elite and the esteemed group of a nation. Universities are also institutions where selected adolescents spend a part of their youthful years for intellectual growth and social development. Hopefully in later years they can contribute their fair share to nation--building. Hence in every university pertinent infrastructure is put in place to cater to the needs of the student community. In Universiti Kebangsaan Malaysia (UKM) as elsewhere, surveys are conducted yearly to gauge the students' perceptions of the available infrastructure [1]. From the feedback we get to know what needs to be done. We constantly strive to improve. Together with our Ministry of Higher Education we also conduct the annual study on the employability of our graduates [2]. In short we provide an environment where competition is revered and comradeship valued. The first mission is provided by the faculties and the second objective is largely met by the colleges or hostels. Faculty Setting Universities are largely hierarchical in structure and meritocratic in culture. Students know that at the end of the day, not all will attain the coveted First Class Honours. The competition is fierce and only the fittest survive. This is evident particularly at the faculty level. Students compete with each other in almost every facet of campus life; examinations, presentations, assignments, thesis, lab reports and what have you. Ali these impose stress and pressure. It can give rise to jealousy and selfishness among the cohort. Hostel Setting Hostels provide a much--needed sanctuary for shelter and solace. It is not merely a place for bed and breakfast. Those who share the place with you are usually not your rival course mates. You tend to feel more at ease with them. You learn aspects of other disciplines from your fellow hostelites. You also learn their cultures. It broadens your horizon and enlarges your circles of friends. You become empathic. Hence hostels provide a more chummy environment. No wonder alumni associations can be based on allegiance to hostels alone. It builds a sense of togetherness. Knowing the important roles played by hostels, UKM for example has from time to time introduced activities which are hostel-based. The latest is the English -Speaking Zones.
