Frequencies of IL‐15R&agr;+ cells in patients with Behçet’s disease and the effects of overexpressing IL‐15R&agr;+ on disease symptoms in mice
&NA; It has been suggested higher serum levels of IL‐15 and lower expression levels of IL‐15 receptor alpha (IL‐15R&agr;) are correlated with pathogenesis of Behçet's disease (BD). However, whether overexpressing IL‐15R&agr; could be used as a therapeutic candidate for BD is currently unclear. Therefore, the purpose of this study was to determine whether overexpressing IL‐15R&agr; could affect BD symptoms in a mouse model. IL‐15/IL‐15R&agr; complex expressing vector or protein complex of IL‐15/IL‐15R&agr;‐Fc was used to treat BD mice. Frequencies of IL‐15R&agr;+ cells in peripheral blood leukocytes (PBL) and lymph node cells were determined using a flow cytometer. BD symptoms in mice improved after treatment with IL‐15/15R&agr; expression vector or IL‐15/IL‐15R&agr;‐Fc protein complex. In addition, treatment with pIL‐15/15R&agr; significantly (p = .016) decreased disease severity score of BD mice compared to treatment with control vector. Frequencies of IL‐15R&agr;+ cells were also significantly (p = .01) higher in peritoneal macrophages of pIL‐15/15R&agr; treated BD mice than those of mice treated with control vector. Frequencies of IL‐15R&agr;+ PBL were also significantly higher in BD mice treated with IL‐15/IL‐15R&agr;‐Fc protein complex than those in the control group. These results suggest up‐regulating IL‐15R&agr;+ cells could be used as novel therapeutic strategies to control BD in the future.
