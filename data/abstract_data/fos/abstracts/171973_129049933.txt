Late Precambrian glacially-related grooved and striated surfaces in the Tillite Group of Central East Greenland
The Tillite Group of Central East Greenland comprises five formations of which two are dominantly glacigenic; the Ulveso Formation (formerly Lower Tillite) and the Storeelv Formation (formerly Upper Tillite), both of which embrace a wide range of facies including glaciomarine, nearshore and terrestrial glacial sediments. Evidence of periodic terrestrial conditions is indicated especially by a variety of grooved and striated surfaces on the island Ella O. In the Ulveso Formation, grooves of complex geometry 0.4 m wide and 0.3 m deep were cut into a cemented conglomerate horizon. They are interpreted as having formed as a result of subglacial meltwater cavitation. At the base of the Storeelv Formation, boulders thought to have been dropped into partially cemented sandstone by floating glacier ice, were largely planed off by a subsequent advance of grounded ice. In the middle of the Storeelv Formation a striated boulder pavement occurs within diamictite. On the basis of stone fabric, striation orientation and cross-bedding data, this pavement is inferred to have formed by the packing of stones along a shear surface within an accumulating lodgement till. Such features have rarely been observed in pre-Pleistocene glacigenic sequences. They imply that each glacigenic formation accumulated during a complex series of advances and retreats rather than just one.
