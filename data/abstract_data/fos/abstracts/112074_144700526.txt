Competitive Admission Practices When the SAT Is Optional.
Substantial public misunderstanding exists about the uses of standardized testing in college admissions [7, 8, 13]. Beliefs that the Scholastic Aptitude Test (SAT) and similar instruments are universally required, prepotent determinants of applicants' access to higher education are not borne out by the evidence [7]. In fact, procedures and standards for admission to postsecondary education institutions vary widely. Dramatic differences in admissions standards are partly attributable to the variety of postsecondary schools, including public, private, liberal arts, specialized, two-year, and four-year institutions. Even within each category, major differences exist in admissions standards, including the uses made of standardized test scores. An indication of current patterns is found in a 1979 survey conducted jointly by the College Board (CB) and the American Association of Collegiate Registrars and Admissions Officers (AACRAO) [1]. Completed questionnaires were received from 1,463 of 2,623 institutions (56 percent). Standardized tests were reported to be "routinely considered in reaching overall judgment regarding admissibility of practically all freshman applicants" [1, p. 13] by 55 percent of the responding schools. By contrast, 21 percent of the total sample reported not requiring and rarely using standardized test scores. Thirteen percent of responding institutions indicated standardized aptitude tests were "required or recommended, but seldom play any part in admission or placement of individual applicants" [1, p. 13].
