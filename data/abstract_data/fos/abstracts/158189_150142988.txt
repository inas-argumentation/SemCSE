That control would last for decades and eventually provoked the histrionic reaction known as the 1959 Cuban Revolution. Martí’s thinking about race would be relegated to deep background during the go-go days of US control over Cuba: tourism, corruption, investment, rum, and fun would define the two nations’ relationship until the barbudos arrived. Ann Fountain is correct to note that scholars and students are now revisiting Martí’s writings, to see the work of a literary giant, comfortable in both Americas, whose humanity was clarifying, unifying, and mostly race-neutral.
