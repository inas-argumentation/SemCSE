Influencing America's Tastes: Realism in the Works of Wharton, Cather, and Hurst (review)
Notably, although literary historians may not be able to discover the responses of "actual readers," Martín-Rodríguez provides a laudable strategy for approaching readers and reading. Instead of locating the reader in a solitary, fixed position, Life in Search of Readers attempts to engage the multiple sites that readers and reading occupy. He addresses the construction and division of readership based on regional, national, cultural, and linguistic conditions. He incorporates writers and critics as actual readers of texts, culture, and historical conditions. Moreover, he allows a discourse to emerge from the readers that were imagined by authors, presses, and editors across time and culture. This multifaceted approach permits Life in Search of Readers to elucidate the complex relationship involved in the creation and reception of literature. Ultimately, Life in Search of Readers puts forth a new vision for Chicano/a literary history and a multifaceted approach for taking readers and reading into account. In each case, Martín-Rodríguez grounds his analysis historically and culturally, implicitly taking into consideration the role of power and silence in the networks that form Chicano/ a literary history. In addition, the careful crafting of the study allows its insightful theoretical possibilities to remain fairly accessible to scholars and students alike. Finally, although Martín-Rodríguez articulates his project as specific to the Chicano/a literary tradition, Life in Search of Readers should potentially influence reexaminations of disparate literary histories with regard to reception and power. In an era where recovery projects and reevaluations of literary histories are considerable, Martín-Rodríguez has significantly contributed to future scholarship.
