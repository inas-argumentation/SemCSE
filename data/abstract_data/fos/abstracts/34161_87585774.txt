P142 miR-146b: IL-10-dependent negative regulator of inflammation
Introduction Negative regulation of the immune response plays an important role in controlling homeostasis of the immune system and preventing development of autoimmune diseases. miRNAs are small (21–24 bp) non-coding RNAs that have been shown to modulate several processes but their role in inflammation is still poorly understood. Methods Human monocytes were obtained from healthy donor buffy coats and THP-1 monocytic cell line from ATCC.The 3′UTR of the putative targets were amplified from genomic DNA and cloned in the psiCHECK vector for the evaluation of miRNA activity by luciferase reporter assay. The targets were validated either by Western Blotting or by flow cytometry. miRNA/lentiviral-based expression vector pRRL-miR-146b was generated cloning in the pRRLSIN.cPPT.PGK-GFP.WPRE vector a region encompassing the pre-miR-146b.The transcriptional regulation was evaluated by chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP). Results In human monocytes, the stimulation with the anti-inflammatory IL-10 induced the expression of miR-146b and it was able to potentiate the effect of the LPS that induce miR-146b expression only at later time points. Blocking endogenous IL-10 autocrine signaling using an anti-IL-10R blocking monoclonal antibody resulted in a 40% reduction of miR-146b induction by LPS and when the JAK/STAT signaling pathway activated by IL-10 was blocked using the AG490 compound, the LPS-mediated induction of miR-146b as well as the production of IL-10 were significantly reduced.To gain additional insight into the role of IL-10 in the transcriptional regulation of miR-146b in monocytes, by ChIP assay we demonstrated the recruitment of Pol II to the miR-146b promoter region after the stimulation with IL-10 and the enrichment of STAT3 at the level of the two recognition sites, consistent with IL-10-mediated upregulation of miR-146b expression. To demonstrate the role of miR-146b in the context of inflammation, we transduced the THP-1 cell line with the lentiviral-based expression vector pRRL-miR146b and the control lentiviral construct pRRL-ctrl. We demonstrated that miR-146b targets the major components of the TLR pathway like TLR4, MyD88, IRAK1 and TRAF6 and this induces a significant reduction in the production of several pro-inflammatory cytokines, including IL-6, TNF α , CXCL8, CCL2, CCL3, IL12p40, CCL7 and CXCL10, after LPS (TLR4 agonist) or pam3CSK4 (TLR2 agonist) treatment. Conclusion Our results identify miR-146b as an IL-10-responsive miRNA with an anti-inflammatory activity in monocytes based on multiple targeting of components of the TLR4 pathway and candidate miR-146b as a new feedback modulator of LPS response possibly involved in the resolution of inflammation.
