Secure versus Insecure Forms of Knowing in University Settings: Two Archetypes of Inquiry.
To discuss in a meaningful way the idea of different styles of inquiry as they are manifested in university settings is ultimately to end in a paradox. The nature of this paradox can be seen in the following considerations. A style of inquiry is, simultaneously, an individual and a social or institutional phenomenon. The modem psychology of the professions and sciences has been able to show rather convincingly that individuals differ systematically in the styles of inquiry they naturally prefer and manifest in their professional and scientific work [12, 13]. At the same time, the different disciplines and professions also differ systematically in the style of inquiry they approve and/or sanction in their individual members [8, 17]. Psychologists, although it should be noted exceedingly few in proportion to the total number of psychologists, have studied the cognitive and/ or inquiry styles of different professionals and scientists. Sociologists of science have studied the social structure of the professions and of the sciences. That is, sociologists have studied the normative structure of the various professions and sciences, for instance, what constitutes acceptable professional and/or scientific behavior, how scientific sanctions and rewards function to secure good science, and so on [8, 11, 19, 20].
