100 : Mitochondrial STAT3 supports K-Ras driven myeloid leukaemia in vivo
Juvenile Myelomonocytic Leukaemia (JMML) and other myeloproliferative neoplasms (MPN) are genetically heterogeneous but frequently display activating mutations in Ras GTPases and activation or over-expression of Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription 3 (STAT3). Altered STAT3 expression is observed in up to 50% of JMML correlating with poor treatment response. Activated STAT proteins, classically associated with tyrosine phosphorylation, support tumour development as transcription factors, but alternative STAT functions independent of tyrosine phosphorylation have recently been documented, including a role for serine phosphorylated STAT3 in mitochondria that supports transformation by N- and K-Ras. We examined the requirement for STAT3 in K-Ras-dependent hematopoietic malignancies in mice. We found that STAT3 is phosphorylated on S727 but not on Y705 in diseased animals. Moreover, a knockin mouse with a point mutation abrogating STAT3 S727 phosphorylation displayed decreased disease severity with significantly extended survival. Activated K-Ras required STAT3 to support cytokine-independent growth of myeloid progenitors, and both mitochondrially restricted STAT3 and STAT3-Y705F transcriptionally inert mutants supported factor-independent growth. Abrogation of STAT3 S727 phosphorylation impaired factor-independent K-Ras-driven progenitor growth. These data document that mitochondrial-STAT3 supports hematopoietic malignancies induced by oncogenic K-Ras.
