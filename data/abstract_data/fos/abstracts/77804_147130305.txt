Performance Funding and Power Relations in Higher Education
An important correlate of the growing concern about and emphasis upon public accountability is the increasing attention being afforded to performance funding by a variety of interested parties, including state legislatures and executive offices, statewide coordinating agencies and governing boards, and institutions of and professionals within public higher education. A review of major state-level experiences with "performance budgeting," "performance audits," and "program reviews," together with a clarification of select conceptual, operational and political issues, appears in a recent publication edited by John K. Folger [34]; and it warrants the attention of all those with a serious interest in the subject. The present inquiry involves a case study in performance funding; specifically, it is based upon the execution and outcomes of the THEC Performance Funding Project at Tennessee Tech University, a pilot project conducted during the academic years 1976-77 and 1977-78.'
