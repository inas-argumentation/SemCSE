Experimental verification of a mathematical model for simulation of industrial refrigeration plants
Abstract A mathematical model, proposed for simulation of food refrigeration processes, was tested against experimental data collected in a New Zealand meat processing plant which had a total refrigeration capacity of 2.5 MW. Predicted air temperatures in freezing and chilling operations followed the same trends as measured data. Differences were shown to be more attributable to uncertainties in data than to deficiencies in the formulation of the mathematical model. The model is considered suitable for use in simulation of a wide range of food refrigeration processes. Such simulations provide useful information about plant performance that cannot be obtained by steady state analysis. Predictions were worst for short times immediately following step changes in plant operation; should more accurate simulation of these periods be required a different form of mathematical model is needed.
