Yet this is a project of literary recovery, and there is a lack of interest in exploring the implications of this perceived relation. A recent study which does so, and which compares with and supplements this book in interesting ways, is Neil Rhodes’s, Shakespeare and the Origins of English (2004). Exploring the implications of this matters more than Keilen might allow, since the redefinition of England’s relationship with ancient and modern Rome coincides with what is generally recognized as the beginning of the English imperial project. A study a little less interested in establishing the autonomy of the literary text would have a lot more to say about this. JENNIFER RICHARDS University of Newcastle
