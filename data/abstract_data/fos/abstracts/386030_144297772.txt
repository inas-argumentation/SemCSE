Whose History? Social Studies in an Elementary English Class for Speakers of Other Languages
lm Elementary School (not its real E name) is located in the southern section of Count County, Georgia. More .than half of the population served is Latino, and nearly 79 percent of the students qualify for the subsidized lunch program. The Georgia Report Card for the last two available years (1997 and 1998) ranked the school among the lowest achieving in the county. In the past two years, a new administration and an almost entirely new staff arrived at the school. New forces working countywide are attempting to better the services to English Language Learners (ELL), including training classroom teachers to serve English for Speakers of Other Languages (ESOL) students. In spite of such efforts, only 15 percent of teachers at the Elm school have received training at the time of this writing. Teaching ESOL is an opportunity for teachers to expose students to a variety of genres and academic subjects. In addition to learning the language, the children study academic subjects.
