P029 The human cytomegalovirus UL76 gene as a multitasked protein
Introduction Herpesviruses are important and ubiquitous pathogens of man with lifelong persistence. Within this family, human cytomegalovirus (HCMV) is the prototype of the β-herpesvirus. Unpublished results of our group show that intracellular expression of HCMV UL76 gene is sufficient to induce secretion of IL-8. Significantly, the UL76 gene belongs to the UL24 gene family which is conserved in all herpesvirus, yet has no demonstrated cellular homologues. Surprisingly, we have also demonstrated that UL76 homologues of α and γ human herpesvirus also induce cell cycle arrest. Recently, a possible connection for these two activities was revealed through ATM and NF-KB signaling in response to genotoxic stress. Our yet unpublished demonstration that the UL76 gene of HCMV induces the expression of IL-8 through NF-kB is therefore highly relevant. Natural killer cells (NK) can be activated through a number of pathways: by binding antibodies through FcRs; through the action of cytokines, such as IL-2; and by direct interaction with target cells. Natural killer group 2, member D (NKG2D) is an activating receptor present on the surface of NK cells, some NKT cells, CD8+ cytotoxic T cells, γδ T cells, and under some-conditions CD4+ T cells. Engagement of NKG2D by its ligands leads either to the direct activation of killing and cytokine secretion by NK cells or to a costimulation of cytotoxic T-lymphocyte (CTL) cytotoxicity. It has been also shown that the DNA damage response participates in alerting the immune system by regulating the ligands of NKG2D receptor. Since NK cells are crucial in controlling herpesviruses infection and cytomegalovirus in particular, there is a need to fully understand the interactions of virus with these immune cells. The HCMV infection efficiently activates expression of ligands for the NKG2D receptor, and at the same time suppresses the presentation of these ligands to the cell surface. Methods To address the hypothesis that UL76 would induce IL-8 and ligands for NKGD2 through a common pathway by activating the DNA Damage we used lentiviral transduced cells expressing UL76 or an empty plasmid. Transduced cells were processed for flow citometry analysis by using a NKGD2-Fc receptor followed by antibody staining. Results Surface levels of NKGD2 ligands were increase by 20% in cells expressing the UL76 gene. In humans, the ligands of NKG2D are MICA, MICB and UL16-binding proteins 1–5 (ULBP1–5). To determine which of the ligands was, or were, up regulated we used as a first approach the quantitative RT-PCR for all ligands in the same conditions as for FACS analysis. The results obtain revealed a high increase on ULBP2 and ULBP3 and a moderate increase on MICA. There was no relevant change on ULBP1 and MICB. Conclusion The impact of UL76 in all of these mechanisms needs to be studied at the level of viral infection by comparing the wild type and UL76 mutant viruses which are already available in the laboratory. Identifying the cellular targets manipulated by UL76 will help to define the mechanism of their impact on host cell function and also allow us to find new cross-talks between different pathways till now unrelated.
