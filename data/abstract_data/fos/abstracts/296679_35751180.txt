Comparability of WISC and WISC-R Scores for 30 8- and 9-Year-Old Institutionalized Caucasian Children
The revised 1974 edition of the Wechsler Intelligence Scale for Children (WISC-R), like its 1949 predecessor, was designed as a test of general intelligence. The WISC-R contains 12 subtests that have identical names to those of the WISC. A total of 72% of the WlSC items were rerained in the WISC-R, intact, virtually intact, or with substantial modification, and the items in the Coding subtest are the same as in the original (Wechsler, 1974) . This study compared WISC and WISC-R scores of an atypical group of 30 third and fourth grade school children. All 30 were Caucasian children who were in a private child care institution in south central Alabama. The 15 females and 15 males ranged in age from 98 mo. to 110 mo., the mean age being 102 mo. They were from low socioeconomic homes which had been or were being dissolved due to crisis, e.g., desertion by parent(s) , death of parent(s) , child abuse. One Caucasian male examiner administered the WISC and WISC-R in counterbalanced order on consecutive days.
