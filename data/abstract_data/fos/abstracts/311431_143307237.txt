Functions on the Job in Relation to Data, People, and Things among Agricultural Students from Southern Land-Grant Universities.
This paper uses data from a sample of agriculture graduates from selected land-grant universities in the south to examine workers' functions on the job in relation to data, people, and things as described in the Dictionary of Occupational Titles. Tabular analysis was conducted using gamma and pearson's correlation as measures of association. Findings indicate that agribusiness job holders were more involved in coordinating several data management task than non-agribusiness job holders. Regarding functions on the job dealing with people, non-agribusiness job holders performed less complex task of taking instruction without responsibility of supervising people. Agribusiness job holders less often performed more complex task of "precision work" with things in addition to the responsibility of handling things. Agribusiness job holders were more likely involved in coordinating several data management tasks, involved in more complex people related tasks of supervising or instructing but less complex task of handling things. ********** The past twenty years have witnessed increased enrollment in Land-Grant Colleges of Agriculture in the South. However, we do not know the complexity of task former agribusiness students are doing on their jobs after leaving college in relation to Data, People, and Things as explained in the Dictionary of Occupational Titles (1991). No research has examined how former students function on their jobs in relation to Data, People, and Things. Thus, building on previous research (Litzenburg and Sctmeider1988; Zekeri and Wheelock 1995; Zekeri and Warren 2011; Zekeri 1994; 1992; 1989), the purpose of this research is to examine how former students function on their jobs in relation to Data, People, and Things. The more that is known about the nature of work that former students are doing in relation to Data, People, and Things and the more they are taken into account in training and curriculum development at the university level, the more competitive future college graduates will be in a globalized labor market. A generally accepted scheme to compare the "nature of work" across all jobs has been developed in the Dictionary of Occupational Titles (Census of Occupation). The scheme is based on the assumption that every job requires a worker to function in some degree with three phenomena: Data, People and Things. These functions can be expressed in terms of their complexity. As a general rule, worker functions involving more complex responsibility and judgment are assigned lower numbers in the three lists while functions that are less complicated have higher numbers (Table 1). For example, the Worker Functions Code (382) signifies that the worker is "compiling" (3) in relation to data; "taking instructions-helping" (8) in relation to people; and "operating-controlling" (2) in relation to things. Since these functions taken together indicate the total range of complexity at which workers function, we do not expect agribusiness job functions at the B.S. level to be different from other jobs in general.
