Kafka's Travels: Exoticism, Colonialism, and the Traffic of Writing (review)
She seeks to put the politics back into cosmopolitanism and, in so doing, has coined a useful new term, cosmopolitical, that will, no doubt, enjoy a rich if contested future. More than this, however, When Borne Across is also a book about contemporary Indian identity, and the impact upon India and Indians of the key cultural and political events in recent years, including Indira Gandhi's period of emergency rule, the Satanic Verses affair, the economic crisis of 1991, the rise of "Hindutva", and the 1992 Babri Masjid massacres. As such, this is a study relevant not just to students of literature and literary theory but also to those interested in Indian politics more generally and, indeed, to those interested in the impact of globalization upon culture, and in the potential culture has to, in Roy's words, "globalize dissent" (qtd. in Ghosh 127).
