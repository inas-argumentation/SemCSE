Special Education Services for the "Mildly Handicapped"
Dr. Newcomer takes the position that many of the diagnostic and educational procedures used to remediate certain &dquo;mild&dquo; learning disabilities are not the most efficient or expedient means for doing go. Futher, she maintains that since most learning disabled (LD) children differ little in external physical appearance from their &dquo;normal&dquo; peers, there is no reason to assume that the cause for their learning disability is the result of some internal deficit. Consequently, she argues against the current practice of using standardized tests. in diagnosing LD children and placing them in special classes designed to remediate a particular deficit indicated on a test. This focus on learning problems as child-centered disorders, she maintains, deemphasizes the teacher-learner aspect of the learning process, and discounts the possibility that the learning problem may be a result of some experience which occurs only in the regular classroom.
