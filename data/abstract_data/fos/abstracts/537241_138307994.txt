Evolution Mechanism of Oxide Scale of Austenitic Stainless Steel in Multi-pass Hot Rolling Process
Oxide scale on austenitic stainless steel under industrial Steckel Mill operation has remarkable effect on surface quality and pickling process.Specimens were cut from 304 austenitic stainless steel blanks.Using rolling device and gas distributing system developed on thermal-mechanical testing machine,tests were performed to simulate industrial Steckel Mill process.Experimental results show that the forming process of oxide scale during multi-pass hot rolling process starts with nucleation of iron-rich oxides,subsequently Cr2O3 particles grow in metallic matrix.With oxidation progressing,Cr2O3 in nearby metallic matrix become thermodynamically unstable,and they convert to stable phase FeCr2O4 which form the healing layer.Consequently,the diffusion of iron from metallic matrix toward so the surface form iron oxides,and simultaneously oxygen diffuse inward so internal oxidation arises.The process above repeats itself during oxidation process,finally leading to a complex multilayer oxide scale.During hot rolling process,the multilayer oxide scale is destroyed,but it forms again after oxidation,and the process repeats itself in multi-pass hot rolling process.When rolling pass is increased,the time for formation of the continuous healing layer is decreased.
