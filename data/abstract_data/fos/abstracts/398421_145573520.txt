The Effect of Elaboration on the Acquisition of Conceptual Problem-Solving Skills from Prose
This research evaluated the effects of different types of conceptual elaborative activities on subjects’ ability to recall, classify, and apply their conceptual knowledge. Subjects were 94 undergraduates at a large university in the Southwest. All subjects studied a passage on four psychological concepts that contained application adjunct questions preceded by a definition adjunct question inserted after selected paragraphs. Application questions asked subjects to identify novel examples of target concepts, whereas definition questions asked subjects to identify appropriate definitions. Subjects were assigned to one of four different elaboration treatments: learner-generated specific elaborations or varied elaborations and author-generated specific elaborations or varied elaborations.
