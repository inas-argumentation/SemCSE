Geometric and surface chemical origin of the piezoresistance in silicon nanowires
A wide variety of apparently contradictory piezoresistance (PZR) behaviors have been reported in p-type silicon nanowires, from the usual positive bulk effect to anomalous (negative) PZR and giant PZR two orders of magnitude larger than the bulk PZR. The origin of such a range of diverse phenomena is unclear, and consequently so too is the importance of a number of parameters including nanowire type (top down or bottom up), the magnitude of the local stress, possible quantum confinement, electrostatic field effects, or surface chemical functionalization. Here we observe all these PZR behaviors in a single set of ungated, top down, nominally p-type, $\langle 110 \rangle$ oriented silicon nanowires at uniaxial tensile mechanical stresses up to 0.5 MPa. A combination of micro-Raman spectroscopy and PZR measurements on chemically treated nanowires suggests that the measured longitudinal $\pi$-coefficients which vary from $-800\times10^{-11}$ Pa$^{-1}$ to $3000\times10^{-11}$ Pa$^{-1}$, are the result of a combination of stress concentration in the nanowires and a stress related modification of the acceptor-like Si/SiO$_2$ interface charge.
