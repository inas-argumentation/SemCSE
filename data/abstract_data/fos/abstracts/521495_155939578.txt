Politics of American China Policy, 1945: Roots of the Cold War in Asia
"The vital importance of 'saving China' cannot be exaggerated," wrote Senator Arthur Vandenberg, as civil war raged between Nationalist and Communist Chinese in October 1948. "But there are limits to our resources and boundaries to our miracles."1 Vandenberg's despair was part of a signal postwar occurrence: the triumph of the Chinese Communist revolution and the transformation of America and China, traditional allies, into enemies. Analyzing the cold war in Asia, historians have dated widespread American concern with postwar China to the failure of the Marshall mission and the convening of a Republican Congress in 1947. Earlier, the nation was reputedly indifferent to East Asia, while Europe and the Middle East occasioned more visible SovietAmerican disputes.
