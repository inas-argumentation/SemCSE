The classicist Erich Segal managed to wring 131 pages (and who knows how many millions of dollars) out of the short life of his fictional creation Jennifer Cavilleri, the heroine of Love Story. My subject is a real girl, the painter Irene di Spilimbergo, whose death at the age of twenty-one in 1559 evoked a tribute much more substantial, although probably not so lucrative for its editor, Dionigi Atanagi: a memorial volume containing 279 Italian and I02 Latin poems, a few anonymous but most written by 143 named contributors.2 Among them, alongside a host of socially elite amateur versifiers, were such major figures as Scipione Ammirato, Giuseppe Betussi, Lodovico Dolce, Lodovico Domenichi, Girolarno Muzio, Luigi Tansillo, Bernardo and Torquato Tasso, Benedetto Varchi, and Tiziano Vecellio-thefiorfiore of Italian culture at that time.3
