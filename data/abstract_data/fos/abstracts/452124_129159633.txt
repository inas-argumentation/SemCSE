Biodynamic effects of the messinian salinity crisis on the evolution of planktonic foraminifera in the mediterranean
The finding of an evaporitic formation, Messinian in age, buried beneath the floor of the main basins of the Mediterranean Sea (viz. Balearic, Tyrrhenian, Ionian and Levantine) represents one of the highlights of Leg XIII of the Deep Sea Drilling Project. The lateral continuity of a strong seismic reflector (Horizon M), which has been shown by deep-sea drilling to coincide with the top of the evaporitic formation, clearly indicates that the Messinian evaporites are basin-wide in extent. Since the evaporites indicate an essentially abiotic environment, their basin-wide extent suggests the destruction of the pre-evaporitic marine fauna; i.e., a sterilization of the Mediterranean. Some lines of evidence supporting the above assumption are discussed, with special reference to lineages of keeled globortalias having a fairly rapid evolution close to the Miocene/Pliocene boundary. Globorotalia tumida, G. menardi and G. hirsuta are species presently living in the open oceans, but are absent (or only occasionally recorded) in the Mediterranean today. They are not found in Pliocene sediments from that area, whereas their ancestors populated the Mediterranean waters in Late Miocene times. The phyletic lineages to which they belong were interrupted by the “salinity crisis” when they had reached the Globorotalia plesiotumida, G. menardii and G. miozea stages, respectively. According to the deep-basin desiccation model, the entire planktonic fauna should have been reintroduced into the Mediterranean at the beginning of the Pliocene along with Atlantic water masses — initially cold and without a distinct thermal layering (an assumption supported by geochemical analyses). The first record of Globorotalia margaritae in the Lower Pliocene indicates the re-establishment of “normal” paleoceanographic conditions in the Mediterranean, including the re-establishment of a permanent thermocline, after its isolation and consequent desiccation. The taxon under discussion is the only keeled species belonging to the genus Globorotalia , recorded from Lower Pliocene of the Mediterranean area. Its unusual abundande and its great morphological variability are interpreted as a response to the lack of competitors in the water column. Globorotalia margaritae , considered mesopelagic in habitat on an actualistic model, occupied the ecologic niche that it shared in the open oceans with representatives of the phyletic lineages of Globorotalia tumida, G. menardii and G. hirsuta , the lineages which were interrupted in the Mediterranean by the Messinian crisis.
