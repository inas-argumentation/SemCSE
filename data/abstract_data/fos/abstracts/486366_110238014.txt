Soil electrical conductivity as a covariate to improve the efficiency of field experiments
An accurate comparison of treatment means over a range of conditions is a primary objective of most field 
experiments. The spatial variability of soil properties, including soil texture and moisture, can have adverse effects on the 
accuracy and efficiency of field trials. Apparent soil profile electrical conductivity (ECa) has been shown to be a useful 
indicator of soil variability. Compared to conventional soil sampling, measurements of ECa are rapid and inexpensive. In this 
study, soil ECa was used as a covariate in evaluating the effect of manure application on soil phosphorus (P) concentration. 
Compared to a standard analysis of variance (ANOVA), an analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) with soil ECa as a covariate 
reduced standard errors for treatment means and thereby improved the accuracy of estimates of P concentrations for both 
manure application treatment means and aeration tool tine disturbance position means within treatments. For example, the 
standard error for tine disturbance position means from ANOVA was equal to 11.0, while the standard error with ECa as a 
covariate was equal to 6.6. Different conclusions were drawn regarding treatment effects with and without ECa as a covariate. 
For example, an ANOVA-based conclusion was that the control (no manure) treatment was not different from the 
surface-applied manure treatment. The ANCOVA-based conclusion was that only in soils with low ECa values were the two 
treatments not different. In soils with medium and high ECa values, the control treatment had a significantly lower P 
concentration.
