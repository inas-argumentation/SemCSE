Effects of atmospheric pollution on deciduous woodland I: Some effects on leaves of Quercus petraea (mattuschka) leibl
The levels of SO2 and particulate matter in the atmosphere around a Smokeless Fuel Plant in S. Wales have been measured in an adjacent area of deciduous woodland by the corrosion rate of zinc battery cases and from analyses of leaves of Q. petraea. Results show there exists a marked seasonal trend in SO2 levels in the woodland, which was not apparent in an earlier study, although there is no marked spatial variation. In contrast the particulate matter is a serious pollutant in the vicinity of the Plant and there exists a definite gradient of deposited matter on the oak leaves, decreasing with distance from the source. This surface deposit results in a significant increase in the number of stomata with particulate matter in their pore and probably affects epidermal permeability. SO2 uptake into these leaves is probably enhanced resulting in higher levels of total sulphur. Increased sulphur presumably accumulates as free sulphate which results in more acidic conditions within the leaf that affect the degradation of chlorophyll a bringing about the earlier senescence of leaves in the vicinity of the Plant.
