Goffman's model of mental illness.
In this paper, we have examined Goffman's image of the mental hospital as a total institution, using two methods. First, we related his examples from life in mental hospitals to our conceptual models. Second, we used the dimensions of our models to construct Goffman's model of mental illness, and then identify it as a conspiratorial model. We found that while Goffman gives only one example of mental hospital life which fits the model used in concentration camps, he nevertheless gives the impression that a mental hospital is a concentration camp by failing to describe the rights and duties of patients, i.e. the sick role. We have called attention to the peculiar difficulties of maintaining psychiatric patients in the sick role, which results in those demoralized hospitals so vividly described by Goffman.
