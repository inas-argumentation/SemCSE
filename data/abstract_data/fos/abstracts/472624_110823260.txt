Nozzling Considerations for Center Pivots with End Guns
THIS paper provides a technique for analyzing end gun applications on center pivots and looks specifically at several typical situations where they can be used. By use of field verified computer computational techniques, the effect of cycling an end gun on and off was studied in depth for a typical center pivot condition for considertion in selecting sprinkler nozzles for flow uniformity. Mean flow ratios, which are the actual flows at sprinkler locations divided by the theoretically ideal flow at that location, weighted in the radial direction for area covered, deviated from ideal by about IVi percent for both possible nozzling philosophies when operated in the converse fashion. The two philosophies are: (A) In-terior sprinklers are nozzled assuming that the end gun is in operation, and (B) interior sprinklers are nozzled assuming the end gun in not in operation. The converse fashions of operation are respectively: end gun not operating, and end gun operating. The mean flow ratios can be weighted in the cir-cumferential direction for the time of end gun opera-tions. This ratio is called the circular mean flow ratio (CMFR) and was found to deviate from ideal by 5.4 per-cent and 2.0 percent respectively. Because converse operation must occur when an end gun is cycled on and off, converse operation is unavoidable. The difference in deviations in CMFR's for the two philosophies indicates that one philosophy is superior. The superior nozzling philosophy is to nozzle interior sprinklers assuming the end gun is not in opera-tion.
