Evidence for post-ore metadiabase at the Horne Mine, Noranda, Quebec
Contacts between metadiabase dikes and massive sulfide ore in the Home Mine exhibit patterns of dike chill and sulfide alteration that show the dikes to be younger than the sulfldes. The sulfides are thus older than all intrusive igneous rocks in the mine area and an Archean volcanogenic origin for these ores seems likely. The relationship between massive sulfide ores and late diabase dikes at the Home Mine has been reported by Mookherjee and Suffel (1968). They found that apparent indications of sulfide epigenesis were in fact produced by later intrusion of the dikes, and that the ores had been formed before these dikes. Conclusions resulting from a similar detailed examination of contacts between sulfide ore and the much older, pre-Kenoran metadiabase dikes are briefly reported here, pending a documented account of the work. Examination of sulnde-metadi abase contacts suggests that the alteration of sulfides by crosscutting pre-metamorphic diabase dikes was a three-stage process. Gaseous sulfur compounds migrating up a fracture ahead of the dike magma produced a change in wallrock mineralogy according to the sequence: hexagonal pyrrhotite —» monoclinic pyrrhotite -> pyrite. The resulting zonal arrangement of iron sulfides comprises a near-dike pyrite zone, an intermediate monoclinic pyrrhotite zone, and beyond that unaltered (predominantly hexagonal) pyrrhotite. Later, during intrusion and solidification of the dike magma, a very narrow zone of new monoclinic pyrrhotite was formed by thermal decomposition of pyrite along the dike margins. This, and a wider zone of magnetite that resulted from the high oxygen fugacity of the diabase melt was superimposed on the pyrite portion of the earlier zoning pattern.
