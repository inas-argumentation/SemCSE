Component Analysis of Within Correlations of Language-Skill Tests in Normal Children
D. J. CROCKETT, Ph.D. University of British Columbia The development of language between the ages of 18 months and 5 years has been well documented (McNeill, 1970; Slobin, 1971 ). However, the 5-yearold lacks the language facility of an adult speaker. Beginning with Templin (1957), researchers (Anisfeld, Barlow, & Frail, 1968; Anisfeld & Gordon, 1968; Anisfeld & Tucker, 1967; Berko, 1958; Bruce, 1964; Menyuk, 1968) have convincingly demonstrated the changes which occur in the child’s ability to identify and articulate language sounds after the age of 5. Studies (Loban, 1966; Menyuk, 1964; O’Donnell, Griffin, & Norris, 1967) have also demonstrated the steady development in syntactic form from age 5 to adolescence.
