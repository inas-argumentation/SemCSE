Solid-propellant burning along a tube heated by combustion gas influx.
Burning rate along tubes embedded in solid propellant can be controlled by regulating combustion gas flow into the tubes. Analytical and experimental studies were conducted to obtain an understanding of the governing physical mechanisms and the interrelationships between basic propellant burning rate, combustion gas influx, tube geometry, and propellant and tube thermophysical properties. Window bomb photographs of burning propellant strands containing tubes were made to obtain burning rate data and insight into the physical phenomena. Strands were extinguished so that regions near the cone apex region could be studied. Tube burning rates were computed after solving the energy equation for tubes subjected to convective and conductive boundary conditions. Aluminum, stainless steel, titanium, and plastic tubes with internal diameters ranging from 0.017 to 0.037 in. and embedded in two different composite propellants were studied. Measured burning rates (at 300 psig) varied from 0.2 in./sec without flow to 14 in./sec with bleed flows on the order of 0.00015 Ibm/sec. Analytical and experimental results are in reasonable agreement.
