Conodonts from the Davidsville Group, north-eastern Newfoundland: Discussion
Stouge (1980) has reported a new record of a Middle Ordovician conodont fauna from the basal part of the Davidsville Group at Weir's Pond, north of Gander. This occurs on the east side of the Reach Fault, which has been considered (McKerrow and Cocks 1977) to represent the suture along which the Iapetus Ocean finally closed. To the west of the Reach Fault, on New World Island, the Cobbs Arm Limestone has yielded similar conodonts (Bergstrom et al. 1974), though some additional taxa are present in this limestone. Stouge (1980, p. 270) has concluded that the similar conodont faunas on either side of the suture implies that that boundary was insignificant. However, it has long been known that the North Atlantic conodont province was present on both sides of the Iapetus Ocean (Bergstrom 1971, Fig. 12): similar faunas are present in the Appalachians and Scotland (both northwest of the suture) and Scandinavia (east of the suture). Indeed, many of the Davidsville species recorded by Stouge were originally described from Scandinavia. During the Middle and Late Ordovician Epochs, only benthic faunas (like trilobites and brachiopods) were clearly distinct on either side of the Iapetus Ocean (McKerrow and Cocks 1976). As Bergstrom and Cooper (1973) have shown, many pelagic conodonts and graptolites were able to cross the Iapetus Ocean from Llanvirn and Llandeilo times onwards. As Middle Ordovician conodont distributions do not distinguish either side of the Iapetus Ocean, Stouge's criticisms of our conclusions are unfounded. The benthic faunas still indicate that the Reach Fault marks the site of the Iapetus suture.
