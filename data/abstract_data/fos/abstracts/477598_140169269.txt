Sequential palynological changes across the composite Cretaceous-Tertiary (K-T) boundary claystone and contiguous strata, western Canada and Montana, U.S.A.
The boundary claystone in the western Canada Basin is composed of three intimately associated layers with a regional distribution: a basal brownish-grey hackly claystone, a middle brownish-black satiny claystone, and an upper brown laminated shale. These layers appear to represent a graded succession in terms of grain size and depositional rate. An abrupt reduction in the relative abundance of gymnospermous pollen, representing canopy vegetation, immediately precedes the hackly layer. The hackly claystone is accepted as being originally formed of microtektites from a ballistically transported ejecta blanket deposited within minutes or hours of the bolide's impact and associated with a heat pulse. The variable miospore content of the hackly layer is considered to be from local, reproductively active understorey vegetation.
