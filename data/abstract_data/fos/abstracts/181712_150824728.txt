The United States Government: An Overview
The government of the United States consists of three equal branches of government: the legislative, executive, and the judicial. The legislative branch consists of the Congress, the executive branch of the President, and the judicial branch of the Supreme Court of the United States. This paper outlines the functions and qualifications of members of these branches. In the event of a conflict between legislative and the executive branches, the decision of the Supreme Court is final and binding. The purpose of this article on government of the United States is to familiarize the reader with the basic concepts an average person needs to have to understand how the federal government works. It is hoped that this article will accomplish this objective. The United States Constitution provides for three separate but equal branches of government, namely, (a) the Legislative Branch, represented by the Members of Congress, (b) the Executive Branch, represented by the President, and (c) the Judicial Branch, represented by the Supreme Court of the United States, with nine judges whose contracts are for life. The framers of the Constitution of the United States were smart in that they formed the basic structure of the U. S. government with three separate but equal branches and gave each branch its duties and obligations which it must fulfill for the good of the nation and its people. It is the function of the legislative branch, known as the Congress of the United States, to make the laws and it is the function of the executive branch to implement the laws as they have been passed by the legislative branch. It is the function of the judicial branch to interpret the Constitution of the United States and pass judgments, based on their understanding of the U. S. Constitution. In the event of a conflict between the executive and the legislative branches, the decisions of the Supreme Court are binding upon the parties unless one of the parties involved decides to appeal for a reassessment to a lower court. A typical case in which the Supreme Court's intervention was sought, one may remember, is that of President Richard M. Nixon and his tapes. Mr. Nixon had to give up his Watergate tapes even though he did not want to surrender them to the Watergate prosecutor. The Congress - The Legislative Branch In the American form of government, Congress is the most important branch according to Article I, which states that "All legislative powers herein granted shall be vested in a Congress of the United States, which shall consist of a Senate and House of Representatives" (McClenaghan, 1987, p. 293). The Congress of the United States is divided into two groups: (a) the United States Senate and (b) the House of Representatives. There are 100 U. S. senators - two for each state- and 435 House of Representatives from the 50 states. The number of representatives in the House from each state depends on the population in that state, the 48 contiguous states plus Hawaii and Alaska. The United States senators are elected for six years. The senators can run for elections as many times as they want to. The members of the United States House of Representatives are elected for two years. The U. S. senators and the members of the House of Representatives can keep on running for the Senate/House seats until they can run no longer because of old age or any kind of physical or mental disability. Qualifications to become a United States senator. The person running for a seat in the United States Senate must be at least 30 years old, must have been a citizen of the United States for at least nine years, must live in the state from which he/she is elected, and is required to have read the United States Constitution. Qualifications to become a United States congressman. The person running for a seat in the House of Representatives must be at least 25 years old, must have been a citizen of the United States for at least seven years, and must have lived in the state from where he/she is seeking the election. …
