Law and Social Change in Latin America: The Alliance for Progress
T IE ALLIANCE FOR PROGRESS involves the task of providing an answer to Latin American aspirations for progress and social justice. These goals are to be achieved, as the late President Kennedy stated in his historic speech of March 6, 1961, "nithin a framework of democratic institutions." Thus the law, conceived both as a chisel and a mold, as a means and as the final resilient shape, is essentially involved in bringing about what an Alliance official recently described as "a cultural renaissance for the whole of Latin America." Resort to violence as a means of attaining material progress and consequent abjuration of the democratic processes of government have been impelling factors in the present hemispheric crisis. Moreover, by merely attempting to raise the standard of living in Latin America without concern for the role of law in the respective societies, our economic palliation is bound to have little if any permanent effect upon present standards. With this as our major premise, we shall endeavor, first, to analyze briefly and non-technically the concepts of "law" and "justice," as present at different stages of the history of Latin America. Secondly, an attempt will be made to find and evaluate the roots and effects of these concepts upon the role played by law, as a peaceful means of attaining social progress. Finally, the function of legal devices in attaining Alliance for Progress goals will be analyzed.
