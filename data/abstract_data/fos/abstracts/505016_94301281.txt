Recent Developments in Experimental Methods for Heat-Capacity Measurements
Recent significant advances in the experimental methods of heat-capacity measurement are surveyed in this review. Among them, Iaser-Dash calorimetry and the high-resolution heat-capacity calorimeter, both of which have been developed in Japan, are discussed in some detail. The former is applied for measuring the heat capacity of small samples in the range 80-800°K with an imprecision of ±0.5% or less, and up to 1100•K with but slightly more imprecision. The latter enables to determine the heat capacity in the vicinity of phase transition with a temperature resolution within 3 p.K, and an imprecision of ±0.05% or less in the heat capacity. Some of the recent results obtained by these techniques are also presented.
