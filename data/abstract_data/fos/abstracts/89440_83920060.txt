Antiviral agents and immunity
Recovery from viral infection is determined by the host's immune response towards the antigenic stimulus. In principle, both the original pathogen, as it is inoculated in the organism, and the progeny virus particles originating from the virus inoculum, can serve as the antigenic stimulus. These antigenic stimuli can generate both humoral and cellular immune responses, and one of the manifestations of cellular immunity is that interferon and other lymphokines are produced. The humoral and cellular immune responses will be accelerated and intensified if the lymphocytes have been sensitized by previous contact with the immunogen. The outcome of a virus infection is not uniformly favorable, and various viral infections are notorious for their lethal course or severe sequelae. In particular, immunoeompromised patients are the most prone towards protracted and often life-threatening virus infections; and this is illustrated not only by such fatal diseases as acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS), cytomegalovirus (CMV) pneumonia, and Epstein-Barr virus (EBV)-associated lymphoproliferative disorders, but also by those virus infections, i.e., herpes simplex virus (HSV) and varicella-zoster virus (VZV), that are usually mild and benign in healthy individuals but often serious and occasionally fatal in the immunosuppressed host. An efficacious, safe, and hence successful, antiviral drug should shut off the virus replicative cycle and thereby decrease the pathologic manifestations of the disease and promote a rapid and complete recovery of the host (Fig. 1). Any interference of the antivirai agent with the host's immune response toward the viral antigen may counteract the host's ability to cope with the infection and delay or impede the healing process. This is more true for immunocompromised patients in whom further suppression of the immune response may irrevocably thwart the recovery from the virus infection. Thus immunocompromised patients are the most likely to benefit from an effective antiviral chemotherapy, but not at the cost of an extra burden to their immune system.
