Yet, while the presence of commercial interests is becoming increasingly noticeable, the Internet-and the World Wide Web (WWW) in particular-remains the main area for developments in information technology (IT) of direct benefit to historians. The subject of this year's report is those resources suitable for academic research in social and economic history that are currently available or are under development. This report concentrates on resources and initiatives based in the UK, but with networked information it is often hard to draw clear lines of demarcation as several of the projects described below also have an international dimension while others provide access to resources held at foreign institutions. The leading force in the development of networked resources for UK higher education is the Joint Information Systems Committee (JISC). Directly supported by the four higher education funding bodies,1 JISC supports initiatives designed to generate, catalogue, disseminate, and encourage the use of electronic resources in all areas of UK higher education. Its overall vision has recently been published, and among the 'strategic objectives' listed is an explicit commitment to the improvement of the quality of research.2 The role JISC sets itself is primarily to support research through the provision of an appropriate network infrastructure, i.e. the JANET and SuperJANET networks, but the document also points to services funded by JISC that offer a more direct benefit to the academic researcher. Among the areas targeted for further development, explicit reference is made to the use of electronic networks for 'improved access to bibliographical records', and 'digital images of historic . . . material'.
