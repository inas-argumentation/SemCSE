An Academic Survey of Engineering Student Athletes at a Division I University
This paper explores the academic success of NCAA Division I collegiate student athletes that enroll in engineering majors. At the University of South Carolina, which is a member of the NCAA Division I Southeastern Conference, nineteen engineering students were on an active athletic roster during the spring semester of 2005. The mean cumulative grade point average of these engineering student athletes is 3.24 on a 4.00 scale, which is well above the college mean. Twelve of these student athletes responded to an academic survey that was developed to identify what attributes and behaviors contribute to their success in engineering. It was found that time management, organization, and problem solving are skills that engineering student athletes excel at. In addition, these students take from athletics the ability to concentrate and the desire to succeed and apply them to academics. Even though the demands on time are high, engineering student athletes spend out-of-classroom time with their engineering peers, their professors, and their faculty advisor. Time spent with the faculty advisor is perceived as being especially critical, since all twelve students agreed that course planning and management is very important to their academic performance. INTRODUCTION There is the perception in the general public that collegiate student athletes, especially those attending high profile Division I institutions, are not academic achievers. Furthermore, there is the belief that these same student athletes do not and cannot endure the rigors of some of the most demanding academic programs like engineering. Of course many student athletes, and non-athletes for that matter, are not inclined to pursue engineering majors. Those that do choose engineering are often remarkably successful in the classroom. Using an academic survey of engineering student athletes at a Division I university, this paper highlights some of the common academic behaviors of such students. The survey results presented in this paper can be used as a source of guidance for academic advisors of engineering student athletes. The results can also be extended and applied to the advisement of student athletes in other academic programs. CASE STUDY: SURVEY AT THE UNIVERSITY OF SOUTH CAROLINA In the spring semester of 2005, nineteen student athletes were enrolled in the College of Engineering and Information Technology at the University of South Carolina. The university is a member of the Southeastern Conference (SEC), which is widely regarded as one of the most competitive Division I athletic conferences. At the completion of the semester, the mean cumulative grade point average (GPA) of these nineteen students was 3.24 on a 4.00 scale. Considering the high expectations and rigors of SEC athletics and engineering curricula, achieving and maintaining such a high mean GPA is remarkable. To determine what drives these student athletes to succeed in engineering, a three-page survey was created and distributed to each student during the summer months of 2005. The survey was composed of 14 questions, and most questions were divided into two parts. Almost all of the questions provided a list of options from which each student circled his/her most appropriate answer. The actual questions are shown in a subsequent section on survey results. The survey response was sufficient to generate data for an assessment of this small population of student athletes. Nine students responded to the first mailing, and three more students responded to a second mailing. In total, the response rate was 63%, with 12 of 19 surveys returned. Since the survey was conducted by mail during the summer, it can be reasonably assumed that each respondent completed the survey independently and without the influence of other engineering student athletes.
