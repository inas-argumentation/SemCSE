Constructions of Social Hierarchy: The Family, Gender, and Power
The economic analysis of households is a relatively recent development in economic theory, and a rather peculiar one in some respects. At least in orthodox neoclassical accounts the new home economics(NHE), as it is sometimes called-turns familial relationships into a form of self-interested calculation of prospective benefits (utility) to be obtained from consumer durables, like children, and internationial trade with one's spouse.' We have found colleagues outside of economics to be freshly astonished each time we recount the marital metaphors of the orthodox approach, although fellow economists seem quite inured. After all, all human behavior consists of choices, and choice is viewed as the basic concern of orthodox theory. Rational individuals make good choices, in their marriages as in all other things.
