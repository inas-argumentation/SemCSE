“Visceral Priorities” in Liberal Education: An Empirical Assessment
The concept of an ideal and liberating education suitable for free men is solidly rooted in ancient Greek tradition [6] and, as Hirst [5] claims, this concept has reappeared at crucial points in the history of education. Liberal education seems to be making such a reappearance these days, with the recent literature continuing to reflect the confusion traditionally found in discussions of the topic. Conrad and Wyer [2],.for example, suggest that the extent of the current debate reflects "a confusion over the proper approach to the problem." Ewens [4] cites a "tendency to confuse the practical functions . . . and the proper ends of liberal education." And Curtis [3] acknowledges "the confusion of purposes and goals that beset discussions" of liberal education.
