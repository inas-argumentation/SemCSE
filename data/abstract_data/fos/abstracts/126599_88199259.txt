The study of generation of dendritic cells from peripheral blood mononuclear cells by calcium ionophore and the intracellular signaling transduction
Objective To study whether calcium ionophore(CI) can introduce human peripheral blood monocytes(PBMCs) to dendritic cells(DC) and the signaling pathways. Methods PBMCs isolated from a healthy donor were cultured with CI(A23187) or rhGM-CSF plus CI for 40 h, or with rhGM-CSF plus IL-4 for 7 d. Partial of the PBMCs were cultured in different inhibitors for 30 min previously with W-7(10 μmol/L) or CsA(0.5 mg/L) or KT5926(1 μmol/L) followed by adding rhGM-CSF plus CI or rhGM-CSF plus IL-4. Then the morphological change of the cells were observed by phase contrast microscopy; surface markers on treated PBMCs were analyzed by flow cytometry; the proliferation of allogeneic T cells was detected by MTT colorimetry. Results PBMCs of the healthy donor cultured with CI for 40 h, or with rhGM-CSF plus CI for 40 h, or with rhGM-CSF plus IL-4 for 7 d, all had the typical morphology of DC. Simultaneously CD14 expression decreased, costimulatory molecule(CD80, CD86) expressions increased, and the proliferation of allogeneic T cells strengthened. Treatment with CI caused significant down-regulation of CD14 expression and up-regulation of CD83 expression, and displayed enhanced proliferation of allogeneic T cells. The morphological changes, surface marker expressions and the ability to enhancing proliferation of allogeneic T cells were inhibited by W-7 or CsA or KT5926 when culture with rhGM-CSF plus CI but not with rhGM-CSF plus IL-4. Conclusion CI has the ability to enhance marvedly transformation of peripheral blood monocytes to DC. The differentiation of PBMCs towards DC by CI may modulated by Ca 2+/calmodulin and regulation of multiple signal transduction pathways of its downstream.
