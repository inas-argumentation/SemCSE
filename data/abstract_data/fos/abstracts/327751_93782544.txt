Electrical transport properties of semiconducting rhenium silicide thin films on silicon(111)
Electrical transport properties of semiconducting rhenium silicide thin films are investigated. This silicide, which shows a commensurable fit with the (111) surface of silicon, has been epitaxially grown on such substrates by reactive deposition at 650 °C. A first kind of sample has been annealed at 750 °C under ultra-high vacuum. A second kind of sample has been annealed at 850 °C under partial pressure of hydrogen. Measurements of conductivity and the Hall effect have been performed in thin films prepared on highly resistive silicon substrates. The silicide appears as a p-type semiconductor for the first kind of sample and n-type for the second one. However, the intrinsic regime is almost reached near room temperature. Electron mobility for the second kind of sample is at least 200 cm2 V-1s-1 at ambient temperature and in excess of 1000 cm2 V-1s-1 at liquid-nitrogen temperature. The forbidden bandgap deduced from the thermal activation of carrier concentrations in the nearly intrinsic regimes is 0.28 eV. These facts indicate that the rhenium silicide thin films quality has been dramatically improved by the aforementioned thermal treatments in comparison to previous reports on thin films.
