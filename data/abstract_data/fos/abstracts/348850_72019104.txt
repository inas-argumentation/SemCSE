Part 6: How to collect and manage data successfully on a limited research budget
CLINICAL STUDY in renal nutrition, although often modest in size, can generate demands for organizing data on the scale of a much larger clinical study. Data may be collected from several sources: the laboratory, medical histories from patients’ charts, standardized tests and questionnaires administered to patients, and evaluations of patients from the clinical staff. Data from some sources, such as a medical history, may be collected only once per patient. Data from other sources may be collected periodically with different schedules. For example, laboratory tests may be conducted weekly, whereas clinical evaluations may be conducted once per month. How can the investigators of a small clinical study organize and manage data efficiently without the specialized staff and financial resources of a large clinical trial? In this contribution to the series, I will discuss how to adapt the principles of data management practiced in a large, well-funded clinical investigation to fit the resources of a smaller clinical trial.
