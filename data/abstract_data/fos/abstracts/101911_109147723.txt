Ultra-Low-Volume Spraying: Systems Evaluation and Meteorological Data Analysis
FOUR different spraying systems for aircraft were evaluated with two different settings on each system. The least variation in drop size was obtained from the Turbaero set at high rpm. However, 26 percent of the ground deposit was at or below the 50 \x drop size which is most susceptible to drift. The largest variation in drop size was from the 8008 nozzle (straight back) with only 2 percent of the calculated volume in drops at or below 50 \JL. Therefore, operators must decide on the system that gives the drop size spectrum best suited to control of a specific insect within the limits imposed by atmospheric cond-tions in the treatment area. With-in a range of 25 percent of the v.m.d. the range or spread in droplet sizes was proportional to the average droplet size and therefore could not be regulated independently with any of the four systems tested. Using six of ten possible atmospheric variables, 83 percent of the variation in calculated spray volume above the spray plane could be explained by the best fitting linear regression equation. How-ever, most of these variables would not be available to a pilot. The use of a temperature differential was as effective as the stability ratio in indicating ground deposits.
