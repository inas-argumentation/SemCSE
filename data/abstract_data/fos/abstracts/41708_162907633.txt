The Other portrayed by “Boucher’s polemical output was to prove as instrumental in the King’s murder as the dagger used by Jacques Clément” (125). And George Hoffmann contributes a stunning article on the most feared, hated, and vilified Other in that century of religious war and controversy: the Atheist. A richly documented examination of the meanings attached to the label, used so freely and with increasing frequency by the Christian apologists of the period, “In the Name of Atheism” is a masterful resolution of the querelle of Busson and Febvre. Inevitably, in a “themed” colloquium, there are papers that seem somewhat forced upon the topic (such is Terence Cave’s engaging discussion of “le mythe de Philomèle chez Rabelais, Ronsard et Shakespeare”) and those that circumvent the theme (such as Yvonne Roberts’s reassessment of the career of Jean-Antoine de Baïf, which exposes the fragility of humanist ideals before the religious and political realities of civil-war France). Both are impressive pieces, well worth consideration if one is not particularly “altéré d’altérité.” For those who are, the collection is splendidly suggestive, for the topic is rich and far from exhausted. But then, as Frank Lestringant maintains: “Qui peut prétendre en avoir fini une fois pour toutes avec l’autre?” (45). RICHARD A. CARR Indiana University, Bloomington
