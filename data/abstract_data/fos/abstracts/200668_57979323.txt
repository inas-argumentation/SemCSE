To Pee Differently Is Not All That Different 2: Care for Children After Learning CIC
Purpose Intermittent catheterization is part of the treatment of bladder function problems to minimize renal scaring. The most frequent complication of CIC is a urinary tract infection (UTI). Regularly parents and children have a lack of knowledge and skills regarding CIC. This can result in a wrong way of catheterization and UTI's. In the after-care situation not enough attention is paid to motivation and self-management of child and parents. Purpose is to develop and test an after-care project intended for children with a neurological disorder (mostly spina bifida) who perform CIC, to prevent UTI's. What is necessary for this after-care and how are the first experiences of children and their parents? Material and Methods An after-care route has been developed based on literature study. It is applied to twenty children who have spina bifida. Their experiences are evaluated by an interview. Results A booklet has been made which can be used as a help to overview everything regarding CIC and to promote self-management. The results of the test show that parents and children get enough information about catheters, medicines and UTI's. They appreciate to show the technique of catheterization to the nurse. The self-efficacy questionnaire encourages to think about the whole process. Conclusions To reduce the number of UTI's in children who are on CIC, it is necessary to pay more attention to knowledge, skills, motivation and self-management concerning CIC during the after-care. The booklet contains all these aspects. The first experiences in the test-phase were good. More research is needed to evaluate this after-care method.
