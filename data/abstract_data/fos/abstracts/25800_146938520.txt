Comment on “Acquiescence, Social Desirability and Inhibition Reflected by ‘Response Set’ Scales”
Webster ( 5 ) has recently stated in this Journal, ". . . arguments concerning whether the 'response set effect' is due to acquiescence (or denial), o r instead to social undesirability (or desirability) have seemed strange, if not trivial. . . . For it has long been obvious that these rwo 'dimensions' are functionally integrated into a larger syndrome within personality. . . . Furthermore, the correlations between . . . RS and Social Desirability scores are simply too high ( in the .80s after correction for attenuation) for these two characteristics (acquiescence and desirability) to be independent" (p . 789) .
