Ambient hydrocarbon and ozone measurements downwind of a large automotive painting plant.
A month-long study (August 1977) by Washington State University of ground-level and aerial air pollutant levels in the plume of solvent emissions from a General Motors Corp. assembly plant in Janesville, Wis., which annually emits an estimated 9200 tons of volatile hydrocarbons, measured against a background pollutant level one mile from the plant, showed that the ground-level nonmethane total hydrocarbons (NMTHC) concentrations in the plume were 2 to 2.5 ppM above the <0.5 ppM background levels; methane concentrations varied little in or out of the plume; ground-level in-plume NO/sub x/ concentrations were usually 5 to 10 ppb, with a maximum of 15 ppb; within one mile of the plant, 1121 jg/cu m of hydrocarbons were measured, 18% paraffins and 82% aromatics, the dominant species being toluene, C/sub 8/ alkanes, ethylbenzene, and the three isomeric xylenes; downwind in-plume toluene and xylene concentrations 11 miles from the plant exceeded background levels by a factor of 10 and 6, respectively; a large percentage of the organic emissions were oxygenated hydrocarbons; and on the three days that it was detected, maximum air in-plume ozone levels were 10 ppb above background levels.
