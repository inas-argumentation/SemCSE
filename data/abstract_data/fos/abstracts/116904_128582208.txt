Discussion of "The Dorothy Bentonite: an extraordinary case of secondary thickening in a late Campanian volcanic ash fall in central Alberta"
Lerbekmo (2002) recently described an occurrence of a Cretaceous bentonite horizon in south-central Alberta, which extends across an area of subcrop of nearly 10 000 km2 and is up to 13 m thick. The bentonite was emplaced in a single event and has all of the characteristics normally associated with the distal “co-ignimbrite” ash fall that accompanies the emplacement of voluminous ignimbrite deposits. Lerbekmo (2002) estimated that the volume of the unit was > 57 km3 and suggested that source of the bentonite lies 300 km to the southwest. In fact, as I show here, the volume of this unit is considerably greater than suggested and certainly > 83 km3.
