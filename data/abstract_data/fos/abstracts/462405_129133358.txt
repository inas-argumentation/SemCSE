Successional changes of middle Miocene diatom assemblages in the northwestern Pacific
Miocene diatom assemblages in four sections in Japan and at DSDP Site 438 off the coast of Northeast Japan have been examined quantitatively in order to determine floral changes through time and to compare with the quantitative results from the equatorial Pacific. The areal distribution of some diatom levels reveals diachronous distribution across the subarctic boundary by the effect of a “climatic wedge”. During the interval between 14.0 and 13.3 Ma, the middle Miocene diatom assemblages turned from warm-water assemblages to cold-water assemblages, and the number of first and last appearances of diatom species was highest at 13.8 Ma. The cold-water diatom Denticulopsis hustedtii increased abruptly in abundance at about 13.8 Ma. In the middle Miocene the cyclic peaks in the abundance of Denticulopsis hustedtii are recognized at 12.8 and 12.0–11.8 Ma, suggesting a cooling in the surface-water temperatures. During the late middle and early late Miocene, the pronounced abundances in both the cold-water diatom Denticulopsis hustedtii and the upwelling-related diatom Thalassionema nitzschioides are recognized and associated with the increased vigorous oceanic circulation and the increased upwelling caused by steepening of the latitudinal temperature gradient.
