Renal nutrition: Hawaiian style
T HE H A W A I I A N islands are located 2400 miles southwest of California and are considered the southernmost state of the United States. The islands were formed from volcanic eruptions of molten lava. The 7 major islands that make up the island chain include: the Big Island of Hawaii, Maui, Oahu, Lanai, Molokai, Kauai, and Niihau. About 80% of the total population reside on the island of Oahu. Honolulu, our state capital, located on the island of Oahu, is Hawaii's only metropolitan city. The Big Island of Hawaii is our youngest and growing island as a result of ongoing volcanic eruptions. The Native Hawaiians had much respect for their environment and life around them.1 They believed in maria, or a life force. Maria existed in all living things. Therefore, it was the belief that eating foods as natural as possible brought maria into their bodies. The traditional Hawaiian diet consisted of fish, po i (starch vegetable), ulu (breadfruit), and fruit. 1 Traditional Hawaiians were physically fit because of their diet and active lifestyle.
