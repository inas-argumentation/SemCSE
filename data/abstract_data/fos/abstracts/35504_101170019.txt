Determination of Erythromycylamine in Human Serum by High Performance Liquid Chromatography with Pre-column Derivatization
A high performance liquid chromatographic method using fluorescence detection is described for the analysis of erythromycyclamine,an active metabolite of dirithromycin in human blood.Erythromycyclamine is extracted from serum by methyl-tert-butyl ether and involves pre-column derivatization with 7-chloro-4-nitrobenzo-2-oxa-1,3-diazole(NBD-Cl) and reverse-phase chromatography on C18 column.The mobile phase was 0.01 mol/L potassium phosphate buffer(pH 6.0) and acetonitrile(80∶20) at a flow rate of 1.0 mL/min.desloratadine was used as internal standard.The standard curve was linear in the range of 0.010-1.245 mg/L of erythromycyclamine in human serum.The intra-day and inter-day precision studies showed good reproducibility with relative standard deviation less than 9% for all the analytes.The limit of quantification was 10 μg/L.This method has been applied to a pharmacokinetic study after administration of 750 mg dirithromycin in 18 normal subjects.
