Non-Linear Beam-Beam Effects in Present and Future Storage Rings Generalized Birkhoff Method on Conservative Mappings
A relevant application of the recent developments of non-linear dynamical theories lies in providing the description and the consequent calculation of the interactions among charged particles that either belong to a given beam or to different beams in collision with each other in storage rings. Indeed, present and future generation accelerators need a detailed reliable simulation of their behaviour which takes into account the dominant non-linear effects to achieve a desired degree of efficiency and luminosity. To this end and adequate mathematical description of the beam-beam interaction, in both versions weak-strong and strong-strong, appears necessary primarily. In this note we focus our attention on the weakstrong case and particularly on the proton machines leavine the electron machines to a future investigation, because of the radiation damping which plays a central role in these sys-tems. On the contrary in the proton-proton and proton-antiproton rings one may neglect this phenomenon, still acquiring a fairly realistic good simulation; more precisely we are going to work with dynamical conservative systems whereas the electron machines require the dissipative nonlinear theory. 1 The motion of a test-particle around its main reference orbit is analysed by a conservative mapping that follows from periodical kicks, due to the forces of the beam-beam collisions, that solicitate the otherwise regular betatron oscillations, if we disregard other non-linear contributions, random and deterministic, which are actually present but appear much weaker on experimental and theoretical basis. Another aspect that one has to consider in future rings is the effective spin polarization of the beams after long permanence in the machine (order of 109, 1010, 101 revolutions) in presence of depolarizing forces such as the beam-beam forces.
