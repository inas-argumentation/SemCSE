Distribution and persistence of trichlorfon in a forest environment
Trichlorfon, 0,0‐dimethyl‐2, 2, 2‐trichloro‐l‐hydroxy‐ethyl phosphonate, was sprayed over a mixed boreal forest in New Brunswick, at a dosage rate of 1.14 kg of active ingredient (AI) in 1.48 L/ha, using a team of three Grumman Avenger TBM aircraft. Samples of balsam fir, red spruce, red maple, soil and stream water were collected for residue analysis at different intervals of time after treatment. The initial residues in foliage ranged from10.8 to 17.3 ug/g fresh weight, but dissipated rapidly to 2.6 to 6.5 ug/g in three days. Residues in soil were about 3 ug per g initially, and dissipated to levels below the detection limit (0.05 ug/g) in about 2 weeks after treatment. The stream water showed an initial concentration of ca. 95 ug/L, but the AI levels reached below the detection limit (0.05 ug/L) in about two weeks post‐treatment. The data thus indicated a rapid dissipation of trichlorfon in different components of the forest environment.
