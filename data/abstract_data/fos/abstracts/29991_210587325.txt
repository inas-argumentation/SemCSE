Eighteenth-Century Economic History
The structure and narrative of the bookmake it an engaging read, particularly for graduate students and scholars of Yucatán and colonial Latin America. The use of micro-history also makes this work important for historians interested in methodology. The detailed minutiae of the case, however, might deter undergraduates. The last few pages of the final chapter (Chapter 8, “A Strange Turn of Events”), on nineteenth-century Yucatán, feel abrupt and out of proportion with the rest of the book—two pages to cover an entire century, while the eight-year murder case is explicated in over 200 pages.
