Development and Validation of a Fluid–Structure Solver for Transonic Panel Flutter
A partitioned fluid–structure coupling code for transonic panel flutter has been developed and validated. The Reynolds-averaged Navier–Stokes equations are solved numerically by means of an implicit finite volume method to account for nonlinear aerodynamics, as there are shock waves and a viscous boundary layer at the panel surface. An implicit finite element formulation of the structural equations, as well as a Galerkin solution of the von Karman plate equation are employed to solve elastic panel deformations with respect to geometric nonlinearities. A detailed validation process has shown good agreement with results from the literature for high subsonic and low supersonic Mach numbers. Thereby, a recent comparison between theory and experiment is confirmed. The validated solver is then used for further studies focusing on the impact of turbulent boundary layers on aeroelastic stability boundaries and instabilities.
