The neutrophil, a professional killer of bacteria, may be controlled by T cells
Neutrophil functions, including chemotactic responses and effector functions, seem to be increased by stimulated T lymphocytes and their products. In addition, T cells may act on mononuclear phagocytes, which in turn secrete products that augment neutrophil function. These events, in concert, may enhance the ability of these professional phagocytes to kill invading bacteria
