Experimental Analysis of Interference-Fit Joining of Aluminum Tubes by Electromagnetic Forming
This study investigates the main parameters that influence the strength of tubular component joints fabricated by electromagnetic compression, more specifically, interference fit by crimping tubes into mandrels. The mechanical parameters are the residual stress on the mandrel, the joining area, and the interfacial friction coefficient between the tube and mandrel. These parameters have a considerably complex relationship with electromagnetic compression process parameters, starting with the charging energy and distribution of magnetic pressure pulse that trigger the forming process, the initial gap between joining partners, the material components and geometry, and the shape and surface roughness of mandrel. This work investigates the interference-fit joining of AA6082-O aluminum alloy tubes with mandrels made of different materials (AISI 1045, AA6082-O, and AA6082-T6) and fabricated under different metallurgical conditions. The process is gradually analyzed by systematically considering the influence of other process parameters, such as the initial gap between joining partners, energy pulse, field shaper geometry and its resulting magnetic pressure, and mandrel material and surface roughness in order to fabricate joints with maximum strength. The derived results indicate, apart from the initial gap, the importance of the mandrel surface roughness and the field shaper geometry on the joint strength and its associated failure mechanism. These are presumed to be important contributions to the development and industrial implementation of EMF technology.
