In covering more familiar ground, the next chapter, “Provenzale and Opera in Naples,” reminds us of another obstacle to the composer’s reputation. Of six such works he is known to have written, scores survive for just two, Lo schiavo di sua moglie and Stellidaura vendicante. Chapter 6, “Chamber and Instrumental Music,” underscores another problem that hampers the study of Provenzale, that of dubious attributions. As Fabris notes, seven cantatas and five organ works fall in this category, further diminishing an already slender catalogue. Fabris returns to biography and social history in his final chapters. Chapter 7, “Hope and Disillusion,” traces Provenzale’s activities at other leading Neapolitan institutions as maestro to the city itself and to the Tesoro di S. Gennaro. “Disillusion” refers among other things to Provenzale’s experiences with the Real Cappella, to which he was not admitted until the death of a rival in 1680, and of which he never became maestro. Chapter 8, “Conclusion,” charts Provenzale’s posthumous legacy: his nine-voice setting of the hymn Pange lingua enjoyed wide diffusion throughout the eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries, long after his operas were forgotten. Fabris also provides details on the final years of the composer’s life. As in the preceding chapter, he publishes for the first time documents from the Archivio del Tesoro di S. Gennaro and the Archivio storico del Banco di Napoli. It is precisely here, though, that Fabris may leave some readers dissatisfied. As he notes in the preface, he transcribed approximately 600 documents from the Banco di Napoli archive in his dissertation: he offers but a fraction of them in the book. Perhaps the publisher is to blame: Ashgate, whose music list grows with astonishing speed, did not edit this title with great care, to judge by the various errors and inconsistencies it allowed to appear: for example, the date of Provenzale’s Motetti a due voci diverse is given as 1687 instead of 1689 (106); quotations from foreign languages are sometimes left untranslated (163, 165); the cantata “Amarilli che del fiero mio bene” is listed as “Amarilli crudel fiero mio bene” (187); and the publication of Fabris’s article “La musica sacra di Francesco Provenzale” is dated 1993 instead of 1998. Nevertheless, this is an important book, rich in information and detail. It is essential reading for all those with an interest in the music of Seicento Naples. ROBERT R. HOLZER Yale School of Music
