Autonomous and pragmatic models of literacy: Reading assessment in adult education*
In this report we examine current practices in reading assessment in the light of what Street (1984) has described as the autonomous model of literacy. In reviewing this model, we delineate two features that have been particularly influential in reading assessment: autonomy of text and autonomy of skill. We point out that such a model does not account for important aspects of reading and so propose what we call the pragmatic model of literacy, which accounts more adequately for how readers respond to fext. We use these two models to throw light on the reading comprehension component of a widely used test in the field of adult literacy, the Test of Adult Basic Education (TABE). We first analyze representative units from the old (1976) version of the TABE, showing how both the passages and the items reflect the autonomous model. We then analyze a comparable range of units from the new (1987) version of the TABE, which according to the test makers, represents a more communicative or, in our terms, pragmatic orientation to text. We show that, although the passages do encourage a pragmatic orientation, most of the items still require an autonomous orientation. As a result, there is often a marked discontinuity between passage and items. In certain instances, discontinuity is further reflected within the items themselves: The target response requires an autonomous orientation, whereas one or more distractors have a pragmatic appeal. Such distractors provide the kind of orientation that the passage calls for, even though they do not, strictly speaking, fit with the surface information that the passage provides.
