A Feminist Institutionalist Reconsideration of Karl Polanyi
This article reinterprets the work of Karl Polanyi in light of recent developments in feminist philosophy and social theory. Feminist thought within economics has largely taken the form of applying existing economic theory to the "case" of women, rather than entertaining the implications for economic theory of feminist scholarship. Feminist scholarship has achieved major advances over the last decade, so it is timely, and fills a major gap, to question economic theory from a feminist perspective. We believe that much of the existing corpus of economics is an obstacle to feminist theorizing and must be reconsidered. ' We have chosen to examine Karl Polanyi's work because his influence has recently grown in institutionalist circles,2 and because we find his views to be both promising and hospitable to feminist reformulation. Indeed, Polanyi's work has already been used in some important feminist scholarship outside of economics,3 so it is essential that the strengths and weaknesses of his economic accounts be considered. We will proceed by presenting a brief account of the historical and theoretical framework that we use as the basis for our reconsideration of Polanyi.
