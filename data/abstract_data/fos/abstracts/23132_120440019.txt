Token relativism and the Liar
Michael Clark (1999) considers some difficult cases for those solutions of the liar which treat sentence tokens rather than sentence types as the fundamental truth bearers; he argues that these difficult cases can be met, at an acceptable cost. In ? 1 below I expand on what is involved in taking tokens as fundamental, dubbing this idea 'token relativism', and detail a version of one of Clark's recalcitrant variants. The second section adds a further twist to generate an even more recalcitrant variant, one which I argue the token relativist cannot readily handle without self-refutation or retreat into an essentially Tarskian position. ?3 looks at the costs, in terms of revisions of standard notions of inference, which Clark accepts the approach must bear but argues that they are much more severe than admitted, at least in lieu of an independent account of the way in which some liar tokens 'malfunction'; ?4 similarly argues that the token relativist relies on an ad hoc rejection of one half only of the Tarskian T-scheme, with the final section concluding that the costs of token relativism outweigh any benefits.
