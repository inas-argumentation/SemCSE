Time sequence study of the cellular immune response to L-tyrosine–p-azobenzenearsonate in guinea-pigs
The cellular immune response in guinea-pigs to the monofunctional antigen L-tyrosine–p-azobenzenearsonate (RAT) was examined at weekly intervals following immunization. Peritoneal exudate cells (PEC) were tested by the migration inhibition factor (MIF) assay and lymph node cells (LNC) by both the MIF assay and antigen-induced stimulation of DNA synthesis in culture. PEC from nineteen out of twenty-seven animals were positive for MIF when tested over the 2–5-week period, while the majority of negative guinea-pigs (six out of eight) occurred at the 5-week point. Of twenty-eight animals tested, LNC from fourteen were positive for both MIF and antigen-induced proliferation, thirteen were negative for MIF but positive for proliferation, and only one produced MIF but failed to proliferate. The majority of LNC cultures negative for MIF were taken 3 weeks (four out of four negative) or 5 weeks (seven out of thirteen negative) after immunization. Thus, an apparent dissociation between MIF production and antigen-induced proliferation was observed in LNC responses to a given T cell-specific determinant. A similar dissociation between different cell compartments of the same animal was also apparent. The findings suggest that either different populations of thymus-derived cells, or the same population in different stages of differentiation, is responsible for these different manifestations of cellular immunity.
