Structure and metamorphism of the Otish Mountain area of the Grenvillian Foreland Zone, Québec: Summary
The area of study is situated in central Quebec, an area dominated physiographically by the Otish Mountains, between latitudes 51°30′ and 52°30′N and longitudes 69°30′ and 72°00′W. Geologically, the region lies along the southern edge of the Superior Province, adjacent to the Grenville Province. The area lies within the Superior Foreland Zone of the Grenvillian Foreland Belt in the terminology introduced by Wynne-Edwards (1972). The fault pattern, deformation, and metamorphism within this zone, related to the Grenville Front, are the subject of this paper. Detailed geological reports of the area on a quadrangle by quadrangle basis may be found cited in Chown and Caty (1973) and Fahrig and Chown (1973). Briefly, the region consists of three principal rock types. The area is sliced into numerous blocks by near-vertical strike-slip faults oriented 010 and 060. A discontinuous south-dipping thrust bisects the area from northeast to southwest and divides the area into subareas of different deformational character and metamorphism. The northwest subarea has undergone largely brittle deformation and little or no metamorphism, whereas the south-eastern subarea shows increasing metamorphism toward the southeast. This metamorphism is limited to fault zones; rocks within the fault blocks are relatively unaffected. The region offers an opportunity to compare the fault-induced metamorphic effects on three different rock types.
