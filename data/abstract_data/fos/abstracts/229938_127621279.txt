"Unmixing" of cesium-137, lead, zinc, and cadmium records in lake sediments
The inverse Berger-Health model to unmix sedimentary records of particle-associated tracers, originally used for {sup 18}O records of particle-associated tracers, originally used for {sup 18}O records in deep-sea sediments, is extended to include radioactive materials, environmental pollutants, compaction of sediments, and error analysis. The method is applied to the fallout tracer {sup 137}Cs, and to Pb, Zn, and Cd in sediment cores from Lake Michigan. The reconstructued {sup 137}Cs input records from northern Lake Michigan. The reconstructed {sup 137}Cs input records from northern Lake Michigan are in good agreement with {sup 137}Cs fallout data. The unmixed influx records of Pb, Zn, and Cd show excellent agreement with input records reconstructed previously by a frequency domain method, thus supporting the validity of the approach. Assuming that tracer concentrations as well as sedimentation and mixing parameters are known with sufficient accuracy, and that the forward model is correct, the ultimate limitation of this or any other reconstruction method lies in the finite depths of the physical sampling intervals.
