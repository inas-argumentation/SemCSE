Leading up to, during, and since the quincentenary of 1492, there has been an explosion of work on the encounters in the Atlantic world, many of which are cited in the bibliography but rarely enlighten the text. Not only does this book return to well-studied texts, but its blanket characterization of Native Americans as “primitive” or as “living in the stone age” is archaic. From an American perspective, this book recalls times when the Americas and its peoples were meaningful only to the extent that they reflected European perspectives, enlightened understanding of European attitudes, or satisfied European desires for wealth and knowledge.
