Comparison of Kirkpatrick's and Spence and Helmreich's Attitudes toward Women Scales
Kirkpatrick (1936) developed an 80-item Feminist-Antifeminist BeliefPattern Scale as an objective measure of attitudes toward women with respect to their involvement in economic, political, social and domestic spheres. Spence and Helrnreich ( 1972 ) found Kirkpatrick's scale unsatisfactory for their research purposes due to its "outdated nature" (p. 3) and thus constructed their own 55-item Attitudes Toward Women Scale. The present author has used both scales in unpublished research projects and thus was interested in the degree of relatedness between both scales. Two sections of a general psychology course (total N = 76, males = 37, females = 39) were administered both scales during one of their class periods. One section received the Kirkpatrick scale first and then the Spence and Helmreich scale; while in the other, the order of presentation was reversed. The means and sigmas for the Kirkpacrick and Spence and Helmreich scales for the total group, the males and the females were 60.22, 10.60; 56.59, 11.28; 63.66, 8.59 and 110.03, 25.18; 103.51, 26.13; 116.23, 22.57, respectively. As expected, the males scored significantly ( p L .01) lower, i.e., more traditional on both scales. The Pearsonian correlation coefficients between the two scales for the total N, the males and [he females were .87, .86, and .87, respectively. The substantially high positive correlations between these two scales suggest they seem to be capping relatively similar elements with respect to attitudes toward women's roles. Besides the over-all relatedness between these two scales, a next methodological step would be a factor analysis of their structural content to ascertain the dominant factors accounting for their shared variance.
