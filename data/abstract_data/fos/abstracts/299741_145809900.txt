Faculty Vitality beyond the Research University: Extending a Contextual Concept.
The objective of this qualitative and exploratory study is to identify individual and institutional/environmental factors that distinguish "vital" professors from a "representative" cohort of their colleagues. A steady stream of reports in the popular media [23] as well as higher education literature [6, 7] express concern over the state of faculty vitality nationwide. Worry is widespread that an aging, immobile, discipline-bound professoriate will not be able to provide state-of-the-art teaching and research necessary for the United States to remain "competitive." Fundamentally, this study tests whether the "faculty vitality" concept discriminates among professors in meaningful ways regarding their professional attitudes, practices, and achievements. It attempts to uncover factors that foster and sustain vitality among professors in small liberal arts colleges and builds upon faculty vitality research [10, 11] conducted at the University of Minnesota, a prototypic research university. It applies the "faculty vitality" concept in a distinctive type of higher education institution where the vital professor may be a stimulating teacher or a faculty leader but not necessarily a prolific scholar.
