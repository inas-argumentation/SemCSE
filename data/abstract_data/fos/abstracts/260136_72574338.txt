Renal Transplantation: Impact of Clean Intermittent Catheterization and Enterocystoplasties on the Frequency of Pyelonephritis and Renal Allograft Function
Purpose In children with a renal transplant it is believed that pts managed with an enterocystoplasty & CIC or managed by CIC alone have a higher incidence of pyleonephritis. As a sequelae of these infections it is hypothesized that the immunologic response is heightened and premature renal allograft loss occurs. This study was performed to compare and contrast the incidence of pyleonephritis and renal allograft function in children undergoing renal transplantation who are managed by enterocystoplasties & CIC, or CIC alone vs a control population. Material and Methods Children in the three groups were matched for the etiology of renal failure, the type of renal transplant performed, the immunosuppressive regimen used and the length of follow-up. Medical records were reviewed to determine the number of episodes of pyelonephritis. Renal clearance evaluations were performed at 1 & 6 months and then at yearly intervals post transplant.
