The Endogenous Flow of Credit and the Post Keynesian Theory of Money
The aim of this article is to show that there exists an alternative view on monetary matters, a view distinct from both the neo-quantitative view of the monetarists and from the so-called neoclassical synthesis, as represented by authors such as Edmond Malinvaul, James Tobin, or Paul Samuelson. I shall attempt to show that this alternative view is shared by economists from both sides of the Atlantic, and that their different contributions form a coherent whole. This alternate monetary theory I shall call "post Keynesian." It is part of this new post Keynesian paradigm, as presented by Alfred Eichner and Jan Kregel, said to describe genuine monetary production systems: "On the view presented here, that postKeynesian theory deals with a monetized production economy in Keynes's sense, there can be no analysis of money separate from the overall actions of the system" [Eichner and Kregel 1975, p. 1309, fn. 39]. As I understand it, the term "post Keynesian" describes those economists who attempt to carry on J. M. Keynes's creative attempt to break with tradition.
