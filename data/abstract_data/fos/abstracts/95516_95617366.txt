Photoreactions of the herbicide naproanilide and the effect of some photosensitizers
Photoreactions of the herbicide naproanilide [2‐(2‐naph‐thoxy)propananilide] were investigated both in an aqueous solution of acetone or ethanol and on the surface of glass by irradiating with UV light under laboratory conditions. The irradiation resulted in formations of the main photoproducts 2‐hy‐droxypropananilide, 2‐propenanilide and pyruvinanilide in the aqueous solution. The hydrolyzed products 2‐(2‐naphthoxy)propanoic acid and aniline were obtained as minor photoproducts. 2‐Naphthol formed from the cleavage of the ether linkage is considered to be further oxidized to the unidentified polar polymerized products which remained at the origin of the TLC. Photolysis of this herbicide in a deaerated aqueous solution was different from that oxygenated, giving the isomers, 2'‐ and 4'‐amino‐2‐naphthoxypropiophenone.
