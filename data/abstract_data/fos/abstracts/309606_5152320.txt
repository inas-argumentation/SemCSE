Commentary to: Association of BMI and pediatric urologic postoperative events: Results from pediatric NSQIP.
I found this study interesting e not only in its findings, but also in the author’s use of 30-day readmission as a measure of surgical complications. Thirty-day readmission has long been a standard measure of quality, but has recently become a hot topic. It’s been under fire since 2012 when it was linked to reimbursement for the medical conditions of acute myocardial infarction, pneumonia, and congestive heart failure by the Hospital Readmissions Reduction Program (HRRP). Several criticisms have been voiced: that the 30-day time period should not be applied to all conditions (one size does not fit all); that the measure includes readmissions for all causes as well as planned readmissions; and that readmission for medical conditions doesn’t correlate with other known quality measures. In 2015, the HRRP included the surgical procedures of total hip and knee replacement in the hospital readmission adjustment factor. The extent that readmission for surgical conditions is related to quality is now under scrutiny. Unlike readmission for medical conditions, readmission for surgical conditions has been found to correlate with hospital volume, mortality and processes of care [1], suggesting that it is associated with quality. But does readmission reflect surgical complications? A recent study in adults says yes e for the most part. Merkow et al. found the most common reasons for readmission are surgical site infection, small bowel obstruction and ileus. They concluded that surgical readmissions mostly reflect post-operative complications [2]. But the story does not end there. Socioeconomic status also influences readmission both for medical conditions and surgical procedures [3,4]. Income, insurance status, access to primary care follow-up, and social resources all play a part in both the patient’s recovery and a physician’s decision to readmit.When applied to the pediatric population, socioeconomic factors becomemore complexas theyextendnotonly to the patient, but also to the parents.
