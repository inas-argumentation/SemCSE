Exploring Social Issues.
The case is gaining popularity among social studies teachers everywhere as a means of bringing reality to the classroom. This method provides students with a selected series of real life events which necessitate application of concepts. Certainly the method is not new. Indeed it dates back many centuries, having been used by such outstanding teachers as Jesus (parables), Aesop (fables), and Grimm (fairy tales). Other case types include the novel, vignette, historical narrative, documents, and research data. Perhaps it is the wide variety of available case types that has contributed to considerable confusion among users of the method. This article will attempt to clarify the incident case when used in series in connection with decision-making processes. l In some subject areas case analysis may be based upon a single incident. In other areas a number of related inci­ dents may be provided, one at a time, for the purpose of bringing out a number of related concepts. If a single incident is to be used care must be taken to include necessary details. Each incident in a series is designed to encourage the processes of induction. Pertinent questions rue answered by the instructor or through additional case materials. The incident series merely provides added depth or insight over the single incident case.
