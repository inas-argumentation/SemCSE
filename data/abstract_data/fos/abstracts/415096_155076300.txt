Issue Positions of Democrats and Republicans in the U.S. House, 1998: A Research Note
Students of Congress have devoted considerable effort to assessing the relative ideological and issue positions of Democratic and Republican legislators. The significance of these questions is self-evident, as effective representation requires both that representatives from competing parties offer distinct positions and that those differences be sufficiently transparent for voters to hold a party's elected officials accountable for their actions. Most research in this area has used measures derived from party platforms and legislators' roll-call votes. As a complement to these past studies, this research note reexamines the issue positions of Democrats and Republicans in the U.S. House with data from a preelection survey of legislative candidates, Project Vote Smart's 1998 Congressional National Political Awareness Test (CNPAT).
