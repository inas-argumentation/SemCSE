Late Quaternary sediments and geomorphic history of north-central Vancouver Island
Materials from two glacial and two nonglacial intervals are identified on north-central Vancouver Island, British Columbia. The oldest Pleistocene unit, Muchalat River drift, consists of till and overlying glaciolacustrine silt. It has been tentatively correlated with Dashwood drift of the Semiahmoo Glaciation. An overlying single exposure of mudflow sediment in the Gold River valley contains wood dated at 40 900 ± 2000 years BP within the time span of the Olympia nonglacial interval. The Olympia nonglacial interval was characterized by a period of degradation in which Olympia-age sediments were deposited in transient sedimentary environments and subsequently eroded. Gold River drift includes Gold River advance deposits, Gold River till, and Gold River late glacial deposits, and was deposited during the Fraser Glaciation.
