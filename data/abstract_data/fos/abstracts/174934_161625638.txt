Dos Passos' "U.S.A.": A Critical Study, and: A Storyteller and a City: Sherwood Anderson's Chicago, and: Theodore Dreiser: Journalism. Vol. 1, Newspaper Writings, 1892-1895 (review)
Donald Pizer's Dos Passos' "U.S.A. ": A Critical Study makes a lucid and powerful case for reading Dos Passos' trilogy not only as a complex and rich literary work but as a modernist epic American novel. Like Whitman in Leaves of Grass, "Dos Passos was engaged in that distinctively American effort of writing a 'natural' and 'impersonal' epic of American life that was in fact a highly wrought and deeply personal work of art." Pizer reads briefly Dos Passos' early work as a gradual process of refinement, culminating in U.S.A.'s representation of American life that is at once personal, historical, and social. Aside from the incisive explications and fluid prose that mark Pizer's earlier studies, his study of U.S.A. offers two noteworthy contributions to Dos Passos scholarship: an analysis of the complex and demanding interrelatedness of Dos Passos' four modes of representation and an equally provocative situating of the problem of language at the center of Dos Passos' art.
