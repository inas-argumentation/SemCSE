Both versions achieved a remarkable popular success for this onetime cryptic and uncompromising literary avant-gardist. The text is the disarmingly simple account of the separation of a thirtyyear-old woman from her husband and the subsequent months of life alone with her son in a suburban villa. Handke restricts himself, in the manner of the nouveau roman, to an objective reporting of events and conversations, providing neither an authorial voice nor the inner voice of his heroine, Marianne.
