From Caste War to Class War: The Historiography of Modern Yucatán (c. 1750-1940)
As regional studies have moved to the forefront of research in Mexican history over the past decade, work on Yucatan' has been particularly well represented. Lured perhaps by the past glories of ancient Maya civilization whose ruins adorn the peninsula, its conquest by the Spaniards, the fierce resistance of the Maya to the Spaniards and later the Mexicans, and finally by the fabled riches of the henequen boom and its dramatic demise in the aftermath of the Mexican Revolution, anthropologists, economists, sociologists, geographers, and demographers have made important contributions along with historians.
