Intimate transition-metal contacts to UHV-cleaved silicon
Some n-type silicon single crystals have been cleaved on a (111) plane in UHV conditions (pressure approximately=2*10-10 Torr), and the work functions of the resulting atomically clean surface measured. Two transition metals of the 3d series (manganese and chromium) have been evaporated on these surfaces. The metal work functions and electrical properties, with emphasis on barrier heights ( phi b), of the resulting intimate metal-silicon contacts have also been measured without breaking the vacuum, using C-V and I-V methods. The results show that there is no significant difference between the Schottky barrier heights produced by Mn and Cr, i.e. phi bn approximately=0.53+or-0.02 eV for both contacts. For each transition metal, the difference ( phi b(C-V)- phi b(I-V)) approximately=0.06 eV is smaller than that obtained from any of the transition metal-silicon contacts previously studied. This result can be explained qualitatively in terms of Fermi level pinning by deep levels associated with 'cleavage-related' defects. The barrier-height value does not change with time and no simple correlation is apparent between it and transition-metal work functions. The total current is completely dominated by thermionic current at room temperature for the two diodes, while at low temperatures (T<or=-23 degrees C) both recombination and thermionic currents are important.
