Origin and significance of constant-size fenestrae associated with calcispheres from the lower carboniferous of the Gower peninsula, South Wales
Abstract Two types of vertical fenestrae of constant size are described from non-bioturbated host rock from the lower Carboniferous limestone of Three Cliffs Bay in the Gower Peninsula, South Wales. They are associated with four forms of calcisphere scattered in two bioturbated beds of 20 cm and 30 cm, respectively. The fenestrae are thought to be the decay voids of thread-like worms and the obvious burrows are believed to be the traces of such worms. It is suggested that the calcispheres, which only occur within the burrows, are the reproductive organs of these organisms.
