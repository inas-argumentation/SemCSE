Evans surveys “Authority” and ways Protestants and Roman Catholics viewed the issue of biblical authority and how these views were affected by the rise of biblical criticism. Says Evans, “The Protestant instinct was to treat the Scriptures as ‘given.’ It was as hard for Protestants to admit that God could have allowed errors and imperfections to appear there, as for the Roman Catholic church at the Council of Trent to accept the implications of the mistakes that had been found to litter the Vulgate” (400). Essays follow on theories of biblical interpretation as well as understandings of the nature and authority of scripture in early Lutheran theology, Reformed thought, and Roman Catholic and Orthodox theologies. Essays on the Bible’s use in the pulpit, on catechesis, and on liturgy and worship complete the section. Beyond the ecclesial struggles that focused on theology and use of the Bible in churches, essays in “The Bible in the Broader Culture” section include explorations of the Bible in political thought, the Bible and science, the Bible and the early modern sense of history, and the Bible in relation to literature, visual arts, and music. These topics show the wide appropriation of the Bible across a spectrum of human interests and ways the scriptures carried out significant roles to inform, shape, and influence ongoing developments in many fields. For example, Markus Rathey writes that “the Bible is no doubt the single most important source for texts in the music of the early modern period” (762). The volume is an indispensable resource for understanding developing approaches to the Bible as an ancient text. Also, as Cameron notes, “no age in the history of Scripture sawmore dramatic transformations in attitude to the Bible than the early modern period in Europe” (xv). These excellent essays cover both these dimensions of the history of the Bible.
