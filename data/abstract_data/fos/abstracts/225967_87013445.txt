ID: 92: Regulation of macrophage susceptibility to anti-CSF-1R antibody Emactuzumab treatment
Tumor-associated macrophages have been associated with disease progression and poor prognosis in various tumor types. Previously, patients with advanced metastatic disease have been treated with Emactuzumab, an antibody targeting the colony stimulating factor-1 cytokine receptor. Although treatment resulted in a strong reduction of tumor-associated macrophages in the vast majority of patients, the individual degree of tumor-associated macrophage depletion varied significantly. Hence we sought to identify factors that influence Emactuzumab-mediated killing. For this purpose, we used primary monocyte-derived human macrophages as a model system, and systematically investigated the impact of hypoxia, the macrophage polarizing TH2 secreted cytokine Interleukin (IL)-4 and macrophage-derived IL-10 on Emactuzumab-mediated killing. While hypoxia dramatically increased Emactuzumab-induced killing of macrophages under serum-free conditions, the presence of IL-4 but not IL-10 rescued macrophage viability. Notably, recombinant IL-4 treatment was associated with increased expression of macrophage mannose receptor 1 (CD206). This enhanced susceptibility of IL-10 polarized macrophages in a hypoxic environment was characterized by high expression of the Hemoglobin scavenger receptor scavenger receptor (CD163) and CSF-1R. Our findings suggest that Emactuzumab selectively reduces viability of CSF-1Rhi CD163hi while sparing CSF-1Rlo CD206hi macrophages in vitro. These results may help to identify patient subgroups expected to show less substantial depletion of tumor-associated macrophage after Emactuzumab treatment and may decipher if a correlation between the magnitude of tumor-associated macrophage depletion and clinical benefit exists.
