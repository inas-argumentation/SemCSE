Proton mobility and the structure of MoO2HPO4·H2O
Abstract The structure of MoO2HPO4·H2O has been determined by powder neutron diffraction and consists of molybdenyl groups coordinated to a water molecule and linked into double chains by hydrogen phosphate groups. These chains are held together by hydrogen bonds. Proton conductivity in a pellet of MoO2HPO4·H2O measured by an ac impedance method has a room temperature value of 1.5×10-5(ωcm)-1. The activation energy for this motion is 37.5±4kJ mol-1. Loss of water above 30°C causes a marked reduction in condictivity. This may indicate that the mechanism for proton transport involves the liquid in the intergranular region as is found in hydrogen uranyl phosphate and alpha zirconium hydrogen phosphate.
