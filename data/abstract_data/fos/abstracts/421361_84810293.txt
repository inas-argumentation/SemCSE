Acid Extrusion by the Ciliate Protozoan Tetrahymena pyriformis, and Activation of a Quiescent Na+/H+ Exchanger by CuSO4
In the extracellular pH (pHo) range 6.5–7.25, intracellular pH (pHi) in Tetrahymena pyriformis was 0.5–1 pH unit higher than expected if H+ were passively distributed across the cell membrane. Average H+ efflux from cells suspended in an inorganic salt medium containing glucose was 7.6 ± 0.61 nmol/mg dry weight/min ( ± SEM, n = 21) at pH0 6.8. H+ efflux was unaffected by sodium depletion, 0.4 mM amiloride (inhibitor of the Na+/H+ exchange system), 0.1 mM DIDS (inhibitor of anion exchange systems) and by 6 mM cinnamate (inhibitor of lactate transport). Addition of low concentrations of CuSO4 (about 5 µM) induces an amiloride-sensitive Na+ influx and an amiloride-sensitive and Na+-dependent increase in the H+ efflux. It is concluded that pH regulatory systems known from animal cells are inactive (or absent) in Tetrahymena. The Na+/H+ exchange system can, however, be activated with low concentrations of the heavy metal salt CuSO4, suggesting that this system is present, but silent under normal conditions.
