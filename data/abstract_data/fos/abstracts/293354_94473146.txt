Polyvinyl alcohol-heteropoly acid polymer electrolytes and their applications in electrochemical capacitors
Article history:Received 25 October 2009Accepted 3 May 2010Keywords:Electrochemical capacitorSolid polymer electrolyteHeteropoly acidPseudocapacitor A solid polymer electrolyte with a poly (vinyl-alcohol) (PVA) matrix and a mixture of H 3 PW 12 O 40 (PWA) andH 4 SiW 12 O 40 (SiWA) as proton conductor was developed. The proton conductivity of this polymer electrolytereached 0.013 scm −1 at room temperature with a very low activation energy of 3.9 kJ mol −1 at its optimalratio (when SiWA and PWA were mixed in equal proportions). The room temperature proton conductivitywas higher than that of pure PVA–PWA or PVA–SiWA electrolytes. The solid polymer electrolyte also showedgood stability over time and at varying levels of relative humidity. A pseudocapacitor using this solidpolymer electrolyte was demonstrated and showed good high rate performance and cycle life.© 2010 Elsevier B.V. All rights reserved. 1. IntroductionSolid polymer electrolytes are important enabling technologies forenergy storage devices, such as batteries and electrochemicalcapacitors (EC), which require high energy density, high powerdensity, and ﬂexible, multi-cell form factors. Desirable properties ofpolymer electrolytes are high ionic conductivity, good temperatureand environmental stability, as well as thin ﬁlm processability.However, the conductivity of polymer electrolytes is typically a feworders of magnitude lower than that of their liquid counterparts.Moreover, the ionic conductivity of many proton conducting polymerelectrolytessuchasNaﬁonanditsderivativesdependsstronglyonthesurrounding relative humidity (RH) and temperature [1,2], which isnot desirable for electrochemical energy storage devices that operateat room temperature (RT) and in an ambient environment. Particu-larly, the high sensitivity to water is a serious limitation for ECapplications, as a constantly low internal resistance is essential forhigh power and high rate applications regardless of the operatingenvironment. These shortcomings must be addressed for polymerelectrolytes to become viable materials for electrochemical energyconversion and storage devices.Heteropolyacids(HPA)aregoodcandidatesforsolidelectrolytesdueto their high proton conductivity in the solid state, making themattractive in fuel cells and EC [3–10]. However, drawbacks such as poorﬁlm forming capability, sensitivity to moisture, and water solubilityhave so far limited their applications as solid electrolytes.
