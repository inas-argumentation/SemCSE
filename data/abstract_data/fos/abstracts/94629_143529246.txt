Leisure policy in France
In this paper it is argued that, as in other areas of social policy, the State plays an ambivalent role in making provision for leisure. It is suggested that in France more coherent and more ideologically motivated leisure policies have been formulated than in Great Britain over the past half century, and the different phases of French leisure policy are reviewed. The paper shows how, basing its strategies on recommendations from the research it has generated, the State has acted to exercise control over leisure time and to manipulate the provision of leisure amenities. It may be claimed that, by bringing leisure into the political arena, the State has played an important role in shaping attitudes, expectations and the demand for leisure opportunities at the local and national level, although the conflict between social objectives and economic realities may sometimes inhibit full implementation of the policies formulated.
