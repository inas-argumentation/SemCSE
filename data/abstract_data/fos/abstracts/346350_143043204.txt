Serna has an extraordinary ability to tell entertaining stories about individuals in ways that reveal larger insights about the period: from the crossdressing stalker Marina Vega who traveled from Mexico to Hollywood in pursuit of Charlie Chaplin and then came back to Mexico to live as a man, to the illfated Honoraria Suárez, a Hollywood contest-winner who failed as a starlet but was remembered in Mexico as a national treasure. Chapter 5 explores the complex impact of racist aspects of US films and how Mexican officials tried to combat such tendencies in the context of modernization and nationalism. The book’s final chapter is especially ambitious, describing reception of these US films by Mexican communities north of the border. Serna convincingly contends that these films actually drew the migrants closer to their counterparts back in Mexico, giving them a deeper sense of mexicanidad.
