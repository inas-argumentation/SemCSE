Sex Specific Expression Patterns of Some Critical Genes During Morhogenesis of Genital Tubercle and the Effect of Exogenous Estrogen
Purpose Several genes are considered to be critical for the normal morphogenesis of the external genital organs and their defects lead to congenital malformations like Hypospadias. We examined the sex-specific ontogenetic expressions of these genes during patternning of fetal mice genital tubercles (GTs) and investigated the effect of in-utero estrogen exposure. Material and Methods Expression of several genes i.e. sonic hedgehog (shh), ephrin B-2 (efn-b2), ephrin B-2 receptor (eph-b2), fibroblast growth factor-8 (fgf-8) and fgf-10 were detected in mouse GTs from fetuses collected on gestational days (GD) 12, 14, 16 and 18, and from newborns using q-PCR analysis. Pregnant dams were exposed to ethinyl estradiol (EE) or corn oil as the control. Results Efn-b2 and eph-b2 expressions increased gradually in both sex during normal development of GT. Fgf-10 mRNA showed an initial increase in both sex until GD-16, later continued to increase in females and to decrease in males. However, both male and female GTs demonstrated a significant declining pattern in terms of shh and fgf-8 expression from GD-12 until birth. In utero EE exposure led to a 50.7% and 77.7% decrease in shh and fgf-8 expressions respectively (p= 0.002 and 0.001, respectively). A similar decrease after EE exposure were observed in eph-b2, but the levels of efn-b2 and fgf-10 (mesenchymal growth factor) were not affected. Conclusions Shh and fgf-8 genes are essential for the early phase of GT outgrowth. Because their expression can be altered by exogenous estrogens, they can be involved in the defected molecular pathways leading to feminizing conditions (i.e. Hypospadias) caused by endocrine disrupters. The previously published data that they are mainly expressed on the urethral plate of the GT supports this result. Finally, efn-b2 and fgf-10 are mainly expressed in later stages of GT development, but they are more resistant to estrogenic effects.
