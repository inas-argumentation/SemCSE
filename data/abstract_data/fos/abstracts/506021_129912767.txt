Major minerals, elemental and isotopic composition in modern temperate shelf carbonates, Eastern Tasmania, Australia: Implications for the occurrence of extensive ancient non-tropical carbonates
Eastern Tasmanian shelf sediments range from siliciclastics, mixed carbonates to pure carbonates. Carbonates consist mainly of skeletal bryozoa hashes with variable amounts of intragranular CaCO3 cement. Minerals detected by XRD are mainly mixtures of low- to high-Mg calcites and quartz with variable amounts of aragonite. Four factors account for 95% of total variance of mineral components and elemental composition and are related to elements in carbonate fraction (factor 1), positive relationship between Mn and Mg with latitude (factor 2), to calcite types from D-spacing variation (factor 3) and to longitudinal (i.e. distance from coast) variation of carbonate and siliciclastic contents (factor 4). Factorial plots indicate residue/carbonate plane with elements associated with them and that Mg, Mn and aragonite contents and calcite D-spacings are related to latitude and vary with water temperatures. 
 
Ca values reflect variable amounts of carbonate. Mg content indicates water temperatures of <10°C. Sr and Na are positively correlated and increase with both calcite and aragonite contents and the ratio of SrNa is ∼ 1, unlike high ratios found in warm-water carbonates. Mn concentrations are higher than in warm shallow marine carbonates and similar range of Sr and Na contents in both calcite and aragonite may be due to the formation of impure phases of CaCO3. The δ18O and °13C values are positively correlated and tend to fall between the trend lines of seafoor diagenesis and upwelling water. Positive correlation between δ13C and Sr (and Na) is due to both calcite and aragonite contents or due to the occurrence of vaterite. The δ18O values of whole sediments indicate water temperature from 14 to 8°C (normal seawater) or 10−4°C (upwelling water). These temperatures are slightly higher than those obtained from δ18O of eastern Tasmanian brachiopods. 
 
Originally calcitic ancient carbonates are abundant in stratigraphic sequences and can be better understood by comparison with modern temperature calcitic carbonates rather than with aragonitic modern tropical carbonates. Many of these ancient originally calcitic carbonates are of non-tropical origin as coeval tropical aragonitic carbonates occur elsewhere. Low SrNa ratios and high Mn contents are indicative of temperate origin. Heavy δ13C values can be used in calculating water temperatures. The non-tropical carbonates form at low CO2 levels in regions of upwelling waters, which maintain saturation of CaCO3 and provide nutrients for a luxuriant growth of fauna.
