North Mountain basalt from Digby, Nova Scotia: models for a fissure eruption from stratigraphy and petrochemistry
The early Mesozoic, quartz normative, North Mountain basalts in southwestern Nova Scotia (Digby area) form three units: a coarse massive lower flow (~190 m) bearing minor lenses of mafic pegmatite, a middle unit of thin amygdaloidal basaltic flows (~50 m), and an upper flow unit of massive phenocryst-rich basalt (~160 m). The two thick units show phenocrysts of orthopyroxene (bronzite) and (or) pigeonite, augite, and zoned plagioclase in a granular matrix of augite, pigeonite, and plagioclase. Variation diagrams and chondrite-normalized rare-earth-element patterns relate all chemical diversity between and within flows to removal and (or) accumulation of plagioclase and pyroxene phenocrysts (~1:1). High K, Rb, and Ba, appear related to assimilation of continental crust.
