On the genesis of male homosexuality: an attempt at clarifying the role of the parents.
The aim of the investigation was to help to clarify the question how the childhood relations that homosexual men had with their parents differ from those of heterosexual men. The sample consisted of 83 self-confessed homosexuals and 84 married men, and the investigation was carried out with the aid of a test which facilitates the recollection of early family relationships. The results confirm those of previous studies according to which homosexual men more frequently than heterosexual men had bad relations with their fathers, and had fathers who were ineffectual as parents and did not serve as models for their sons. The results do not agree with those of previous studies according to which homosexual men more frequently than heterosexual men were strongly attached to and over-indulged and over-protected by their mothers. Nor do the findings support the contention that the mothers of homosexuals were more often effectual as parents, or were more often taken by their sons as models than were the mothers of heterosexuals. Since the present findings support those of previous investigations which suggest that homosexuality is furthered by the lack of good relations between the child and his father, it has been concluded that more attention needs to be given than is usual at present to the role of the father in the upbringing of the child.
