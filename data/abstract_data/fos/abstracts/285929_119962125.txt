Pulsed Laser-Induced Shattering of Water Drops
Water droplets with radii ranging from 0.1 to 1.5 mm were irradiated by 10 jts pulses from a pulsed CO2 laser (A = 10.6 j*m) operating at intensities between 10 and 6xl0 W/cm. The absorption length (-10 jun) was much smaller than the droplet radii so that the laser-drop interaction was a surface phenomenon leading to overpressures on the irradiated front surface which exceeded surface tension forces by at least an order of magnitude. However, these high overpressures did not cause all droplets to shatter. There appeared to be a critical laser fluence of 0.8-2.5 J/cm required for shattering, essentially independent of radius. Above the critical fluence the drops were deformed into thin liquid films with subsequent breakup into smaller satellite droplets. For fixed pulse duration the shattering time decreases with increasing intensity and with decreasing initial drop size. Simple scaling and energy conservation relations are used to predict the radii of the satellite droplets and to estimate a critical impulse parameter.
