Secondary Reinforcement Effects in a T-Maze Test following Direct Placement Training
In a recent presentation of his theoretical views, Spence (1956) has held that the principle of secondary reinforcement as classically stated does not apply to the instrumental reward situation. That is, Spence would hold that such stimuli do not strengthen the habit strengths of preceding responses, but exert their effects on performance (,E,) rather than learning (,H,). These effects are mediated, according to Spence, primarily by the mechanism of the fractional anticipatory goal response (7,-s,) mainly as a determiner of the strength of the incentive motivational factor ( K ) , and, secondarily, by its associative con. . nections with the criterion response. The fractional goal response initially is classically conditioned to stimuli associated with receipt of reward which, in mazes and runways, occurs at the terminal end of the reaction chain. During subsequent trials, the goal response comes, by generalization, to be aroused progressively earlier in the response chain. When the above sequence has been accomplished, the ?,-so mechanism operates to enhance or degrade performance depending upon following experimental conditions. The present study was designed to determine whether or not new learning can be mediated entirely on the basis of reward training to a goal box of a particular brightness. This use of the direct placement procedure differs from the intent of the direct feeding technique employed in a recent study by Stein (1957). In that experiment, major interest was in the enhancement of running speed following certain direct feeding procedures. In both experiments, however, the assumption was made that the classically conditioned goal response and the criterion instrumental response are capable of being manipulated relatively independently of one another. From Spence's theory, it would be predicted that training trials involving nothing more than goal box feeding would be sufficient to establish the reward value of the stimulus characteristics of the box. Such reward or "reinforcing" value would presumably be related to the strength of r,-s, classically conditioned to the goal box.
