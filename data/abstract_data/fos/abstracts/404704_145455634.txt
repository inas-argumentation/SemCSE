Studies have revealed that, contrary to what was previously believed, students as early as the elementary grades are able to learn history at levels beyond the memorization of specific information. Indeed, researchers recognize that when presented with curricular and instructional approaches and materials that are conducive to understanding historical events, children can and do attain high levels of thinking about the past (e.g., Levstik and Pappas 1987; Lee and Ashby 2000; Vansledright 2000). A number of descriptions and definitions of understanding have emerged. During the 199Os, the Harvard Graduate School of Education conducted an extensive study of teaching for understanding in a range of disciplines including history. Boix Mansilla and Gardner (1998) identified the four dimensions of understanding: knowledge, methods, purposes, and forms.
