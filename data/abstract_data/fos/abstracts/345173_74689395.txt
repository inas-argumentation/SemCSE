A videotaped program for training pharmacists to conduct family planning counseling.
Previous studies of pharmacists involvement in family planning counseling showed a varying degree of involvement a community need for more services and a willingness of pharmacists to expand their role but a need for additional education and training. A videotaped program was developed for the latter purpose containing an information base needed for counseling a 3 pharmacist-patient vignettes allowing viewer participation in evaluating patient data and pharmacist performance. The program was administered to 2nd professional year classes at 2 pharmacy schools using an experimental and control group design with posttest only. 2-way analyses of variance of mean test scores revealed significantly higher scores by subjects in all experimental groups but nonsignificant differences between scores related to school of enrollment or sex of respondent. Results indicate that both cognitive information and counseling performance can be taught and evaluated using this technique. (authors)
