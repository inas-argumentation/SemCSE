Changes of Biomass and Nutrient Contents during Banana's Floral Bud Initiation and Differentiation
An attempt was made to study the changes of histological morphology, the biomass and nutrient contents in different parts of banana plant during the stages of floral initiation and differentiation through field experiment, microscopical identification on paraffin cut section, and chemical analysis of nutrient contents. According to the histological morphology of the banana plant at different vegetative stages, floral initiation and differentiation of the banana plant was obviously divided into three stages: floral initiation, inflorescence differentiation, and floral primordium differentiation. The plant biomass at the three stages of floral initiation and differentiation might be applied as basic morphological identification indexes for floral initiation and differentiation. The leaf nitrogen, phosphorus and sulfur contents rose to the peak at the early stage of the floral initiation but dropped rapidly from the floral initiation to inflorescence differentiation stages, whereas the leaf potassium, calcium and magnesium contents were just the opposite. All macronutrients in floral organs were changed steadily at the floral primordium differentiation stage. The leaves were found to be the best part for nutrient diagnosis as compared to other parts of the plant such as pseudostem and corm.
