Stability of Agreeing Response Set over a Period of Time
Agreeing response set and its influence on psychological tests has recently received increased atrention. Couch and Keniston (1960) have provided a comprehensive review and investigation in which they conclude that "agreeing response tendency is based on a central personality syndrome." They also present a short and quickly administered 15-item Yeasay-Naysay Scale which measures agreeing response tendency. Because of current interest in the response set variable, it seems probable that this short scale will be widely used in research. Already, it has been used ro investigate individual differences in the production of retroactive facilitation and inhibition (James & Stone, 1960). Considering the possible future use of this agreeing response set scale, there are practical reasons for knowing the stability of this scale's scores ovel a period of time. The purpose of the present study was to test the stability of this scale. In the Fall of 1960, 207 students in an introductory psychology class were given the 15-item Yeasay-Naysay Scale (Couch & Keniston, 1960). They were retested wirh the same scale three months later. The test-retest reliability obtained was highly significant (r = .65; p .lo). The obtained coefficient of reliability (.65) was somewhat lower than might be desired for this type of test. Using the generalized Spearman-Brown formula, a more desirable reliability coefficient (35 or higher) might be obtained if the scale were increased from 15 items to at least 45 to 60 items. However, inspection of the scatter diagram suggested that the reliability of the extreme scores would be higher than that of the total distribution.
