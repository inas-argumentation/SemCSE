Effect of Ventricular Injections of Ribonuclease on Learned Discrimination and Avoidance Tasks in the Rat
Behavioral studies relating ribonucleic acid (RNA) to performance dependent on memory have involved humans and lower animals. It has been reasoned that introduction of ribonuclease, an enzyme which breaks down RNA, would modify this RNA structure and the long-term memory which it stores. However, evidence is contradictory regarding its effect on intact tissue and regenerating tissue. Corning and John (1961) from their work on transected conditioned planarians reported that only the regenerating tissue appeared to be affected by the ribonuclease. This contrasts with unpublished observations by E. R. John, B. Wenzel, and R. Tschirigi (personal communication, 1963; cf. Corning & John, 1961) of a decrease in performance on pattern discrimination for food but no decrease in the conditioned avoidance response to visual or auditory cues when a ribonuclease solution had been injected into the lateral ventricle of cats. The purpose of the present study was to assess the effect of such lateral ventricle injections of ribonuclease on avoidance and/or discrimination tasks in the rat. In an effort to replicate the study of John, er al. using rats, parallel procedures were designed. Eight rats were trained to discriminate visual patterns and obtain food by pushing open a door in the pattern and to avoid shock upon the presentation of visual and auditory stimuli. Cannulas were then permanently implanted into the lateral ventricles of the brain and injections of the ribonuclease solution introduced through the cannulas. Analysis indicated that there were no significant differences in number of correct discriminations, number of avoidance failures, or avoidance latencies following ribonuclease injections. The fact that a high concentration of ribonuclease in combination with a volume of solution which approached the maximum for one injection, led to no significant decreases in either a learned pattern discrimination task or avoidance task suggests that such single injections of ribonuclease into the ventricular system do not cause losses in retention in rats within a short time span. Thus, our results do nor replicate those of John, et al., in which the effect was noted within a shorr rime after the injection, precluding the assumption that the ribonuclease interfered only with transmission to newly formed brain tissue. Our results with rats constitute negative evidence for such modification of RNA within intact tissue.
