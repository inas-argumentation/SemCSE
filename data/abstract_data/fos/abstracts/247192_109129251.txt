Mechanical stresses likely to be encountered in the manufacture and use of plastically encapsulated devices
Mechanical stresses are usually built into silicon device structures at virtually every stage of manufacture. Wafer preparation, oxidations, diffusions,~ metallization, die and wire bonding, encapsulation, curing and lead cropping can all introduce mechanical stress. In addition the sign and magnitude of the stresses can vary as a function of the processing conditions, the materials used and the overall design of the device and package. Even during use these mechanical stresses can either intensify or relax depending on the operating conditions of the device. Given that large variations in mechanical stresses can occur, it is useful to briefly review how they are introduced, how they affect device properties and reliability, what failure mechanisms are involved and how we can avoid or modify their most deleterious effects.
