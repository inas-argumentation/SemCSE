Why various types of donor can either enhance or reduce electron mobility in narrow-gap semiconductors
Under conditions of a fractional occupancy of impurity states, it is possible for the charges localized on the impurities to correlate their mutual positions. The electron mobility in such samples exhibits greatly enhanced values. The effect has been previously observed in HgSe:Fe. The authors study the case of HgSe doped with Fe (resonant donor nFe=2*1019 cm-3) and Ga ('shallow' donor 0<or=nGa<or=1*1019 cm-3, i.e. up to complete filling of Fe-donor states). By keeping the concentration of Fe constant and changing the concentration of Ga in the sample they can vary the parameter that rules the degree of spatial correlation, i.e. the ratio of the number of unoccupied states to the total number of donors. As the alternative method of tuning the discussed ratio, the hydrostatic pressure is also employed. The results of the conductivity and Hall effect measurements are compared with the short-range correlation model modified appropriately in order to account for the presence of randomly distributed Ga ions. A quantitative agreement is observed. Intuitively, additional donors, when fully ionized, should lead to a drop in the mobility. They show that conditions exist when co-doping with Ga can lead to a surprising enhancement of the mobility.
