Fauna from the Markley formation (Upper Eocene) on Pleasant Creek, California
INTRODUCTION Only recently have paleontologists in North America begun to appreciate either the length of time represented by the Eocene epoch, or the fact that faunal and stratigraphic sequences are less simple than early workers had believed. During the last decade, several distinctive Eocene faunal zones have been discovered on the West Coast, and several new facies of faunas already known have been found. The more important of these discoveries were summarized in a recent paper by Clark and Vokes (1936). The present paper describes a fauna from the Markley formation (Upper Eocene), recently discovered a few miles north of Vacaville, California, in the hills bordering the west side of the Sacramento Valley. This fauna is apparently equivalent to one found in the lower portion of the type section of the Tejon formation, which, in turn, is considered the equivalent of the Cowlitz formation of southwestern Washington.
