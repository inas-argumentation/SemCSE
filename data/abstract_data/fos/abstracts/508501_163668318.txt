The national interest in obtaining information about the Barbary corsairs in particular and Islamic civilization in general is evident in the concluding narratives of Piracy, Slavery, and Redemption: "A True Account of the Captivity of Thomas Phelps" (1685) and "A True and Faithful Account of the Religion and Manners of the Mohammetans" (1705) by Joseph Pitts. The former account, which has never been reprinted, is a straightforward narrative of Phelps' enslavement and escape with the descriptive detail and avowed truthfulness of a "plain-speaking Englishman." During the reign of the Catholic James II, triumphant Protestantism apparently receded in favor of assertive nationalism. As its title suggests, Pitts' treatise transcends the captivity narrative genre in the sheer magnitude of its descriptive detail, including the first English account of the Hajj. Because he fell into temptation and "turned Turk," Pitts is able to give his readers unprecedented access into the Islamic world. Upon his deliverance, he becomes that "other" world's interpreter, a role that was repeated through five editions of "Religion and Manners of the Mohammetans" before 1750 and its inclusion in several ethnographical anthologies in the later eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. The popularity of Joseph Pitts' interpretation of Islam, along with the cumulative texts assembled by Vitkus, begs the questions of their impact on British literature, politics, and perceptions, questions that scholars such as Joe Snader, Andrew Hadfield, and, most recently, Linda Colley, have begun to address. Like the genre of the captivity narrative itself, this collection transcends geographical and disciplinary boundaries, and researchers of such diverse topics as cross-cultural interaction in the early modern world, the formation of British national identity through external encounter, colonial and travel literature, western perceptions of slavery, and the early foundations of orientalism will find Piracy, Slavery, and Redemption to be both a useful and timely volume.
