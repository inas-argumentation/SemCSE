Artificial intelligence approach to palaeogeography and evolutionary trend analysis of Laurentian brachiopod fauna in the Rhynchotrema-Hiscobeccus lineage
Previous studies, based on the qualitative morphological description on the Late Ordovician brachiopod fauna hypothesized that Hiscobeccus, which is one of the diagnostic taxa in North American epicontinental brachiopod fauna, evolved from Rhynchotrema, a well-known rhynchonellide brachiopod, which lived in pericratonic regions of Laurentia. A quantitative approach conducting multivariate analysis, which was based on biometric characters of rhynchonellide brachiopod specimens from the Late Ordovician (upper Sandbian–upper Katian) in North America, supported this hypothesis that the younger and larger Hiscobeccus evolved from older and smaller Rhynchotrema during the Late Ordovician. Recently, artificial intelligence has found many applications in various fields especially in geosciences. However, the application of artificial intelligence in paleontology is new and can be used as a reliable method in data analysis and interpretation. The current study proposes the first report of employing an artificial intelligence model based on neural networks to map a set of quantitative morphological measurements of Rhynchotrema-Hiscobeccus lineage of North America to their locality and evolutionary trend. The results showed that the accuracy of the neural network approach in estimating the locality of the testing brachiopod samples is 81%.
