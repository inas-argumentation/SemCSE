Sources of Information in Educational Research Literature
This article examines some ways in which research information is disseminated to researchers and practitioners. The analysis shows an increasing reliance on journals and a continued tendency to rely on more recent literature (especially journals) in research reports. In addition, the article lists the 15 journals most frequently cited in the fifth edition of the Encyclopedia of Educational Research. A comparison of this list with previous studies indicates considerable shifts in the prominence of journals related to educational research since the third and fourth editions of the Encyclopedia. In addition to indicating trends in educational research literature, these results suggest potential areas of interest and emphasis for both the individual reader and researcher and for the compiler of general and research library collections in education. The results also suggest sources in which information might be disseminated most rapidly to the widest possible audiences.
