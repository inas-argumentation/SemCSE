College Students' Digital Identity: Perceptions towards Employment and Career
To succeed in a classroom, today's college students need to be confident users of twenty-first century skills and technologies. Additionally, webpages and online information are becoming a necessity for professionals and commerce. What are college students' perceptions of digital identities and their usefulness in future employment and career enhancement? Forty-four students were guided to create and construct a digital identity website and report perceptions and results of the creative project. This paper includes combined research results collected from two groups of graduate students for a one year time period. Findings indicate reports of improved use of technology, career enhancement, perceived appropriate digital identity content for employment, award recognition, and peer respect as a result of creating digital identities. Purpose of the Study To succeed in a classroom, today's college students need to be confident users of twenty-first century skills and technologies. Professionals in all fields build digital identities. The necessary skills, technologies, competencies, talents and philosophies all combine to make up a teacher's digital identity. Through websites, blogs, research trends, information online, electronic presentations they see future career plans and professional development increasingly dependent on advance technological knowledge and skills. (Malloy, et. al. 2014). A twenty-first century teacher should be able to understand and use pedagogical technological content knowledge; educational technology issues and trends, national educational technology standards and student outcomes (NETS); technology in lesson design; internet research; information management; software apps and learning games; social media; multimedia presentations; involvement of students in learning assessment and technology integration. In the creative construction of a digital identity, each one of these areas is addressed. The purpose of this study is to investigate students' perceptions of creating digital identities. Also explored are perceptions of the usefulness of digital identities with implications toward employment. Review of Literature A digital identity is as important to a technology-using educator as their face-to-face classroom presentation. A digital identity helps aspiring teachers build a multimedia resume show casing individual tech talents as well as showcase interactive technologies to create engaging lessons. A teacher's modeling enables students to also become digital citizens safely, ethically, and creatively in our information-based society. (Maloy, et.al. 2014). Technology facilitates inquiry-based learning. Researchers from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) stated that technologies enable teachers to "convey concepts in new ways that would not otherwise be possible ..." (Klopfer et al., 2009, p.4) November (2009) distinguishes between teachers who use technology to automate or informate teaching. Automating, or learning from indicates that technology is maintaining existing practices. Informate indicates learning with and means that technology is creating substantive change in schools. Automating also happens when teachers incorporate technology into their daily lives. Informating happens when a technology changes an activity by fundamentally redesigning or refining it. Teachers create learning happen in their classrooms using leadership skills. Choice and use of technology showcase leadership as they alter existing patterns of teaching and learning in schools (Christian, Johnson, & Horn, 2010). Collins and Halverson, (2009) state that integrating new technologies is the first step in making a teacher a technology-leading educator.
