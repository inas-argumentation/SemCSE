The Charles William Eliot Centennial
H IGHER and professional education in America owe their present standards to no one more than to Charles William Eliot. March 20, I934, marks the centennial of his birth. When he came to Harvard in I869, it was a small college with loosely appended professional schools. He left it forty years later a great university of which the professional schools were definitely and consciously a part. Some of the conditions which he strove to change are told in quotations from his writings. In i854, previous to his twentieth birthday, he wrote to his mother that his life work was to be in the field of education. Two years later while giving a course in chemistry in the Medical School, he was convinced that changes were needed.
