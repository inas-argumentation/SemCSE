36: Herpes simplex virus type 1 selectively blocks development of neuronal precursors derived from neural progenitor cell
Herpes simplex virus type 1 (HSV-1) is a common human pathogen and one of the leading causes of encephalitis (HSE). HSE patients that survive encephalitis often display severe and permanent neurological impairment. Our previous results in an animal model of HSE revealed HSV-1 infects cells in the subventricular zone (SVZ) where neural stem cells (NSCs) reside. We hypothesize the neurological sequelae observed in encephalitic patients is the result of altering the course of NSC differentiation as a consequence of HSV-1 infection of these cells. In order to test this hypothesis, mouse GFP-expressing NSCs were cultured and infected with different concentrations of HSV-1 (McKrae strain). NSCs were susceptible to HSV-1 as shown by plaque assay and immunohistochemistry. HSV-1 infection of NSC cultures resulted in the specific loss of the neuronal precursors (doublecortin+) with no significant changes in the percentage of astrocytes (GFAP+) or oligodendrocytes (GALC+). The addition of microglia or microglia/NSC conditioned media to the NSC cultures prevented the loss of neurons. Analysis of a number of transcription factors from these cultures identified phosphorylated Stat3 as correlative with protection. Suspension array analysis or ELISA of analytes including IL-1 α , IL-1 β , IL-6, IL-10, TNF- α , CXCL10, IFN- α , IFN- β , CSF, and IGF-1 detected IL-6, TNF- α , and CXCL10 in the conditioned media. The addition of neutralizing antibody to IL-6 was found to block the protective effect of the microglia in the NSC cultures. In vivo , HSV-1 infected mice presenting with encephalitis showed a 30–50% reduction in NSCs levels in the SVZ as determined by flow cytometry. We conclude NSCs are susceptible to HSV-1 which impacts specifically on neuronal survival without affecting the differentiation process of other NSC-derived cells. It is tempting to speculate that this loss contributes to cognitive impairment in the host.
