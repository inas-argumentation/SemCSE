Teaching Mathematics in the High School Setting.
With the No Child Left Behind federal mandate, teachers and school administrators are challenged to provide the best instruction possible in mathematics and reading. Students are required to take the state mandated tests in grades three through eight and in grade ten. The stakes are high in that students may not be passed on to the next higher grade level if a test is not passed in the required grades of testing, If the exit test for graduation is not passed, a student may not receive a high school diploma. The punishments for not passing a test then are indeed very stringent. The best curriculum possible needs to be aligned with the state mandated objectives so that each student may achieve optimally. Mathematics is one of two curriculum areas mandated for testing under No Child Left Behind (NCLB). It behooves the mathematics teacher to provide high quality instruction to all learners. All classifications of students be they mentally handicapped, English as a second language, behaviorally disordered, among others, are required to be counted in on taking the state mandated test. Social promotion is not to be emphasized but test results are, in order for students to be promoted to the next grade level. Measurable results are wanted for the student to demonstrate proficiency in mathematics, as well as in reading. The contents of this manuscript will be delimited to mathematics instruction. Penalties for Low Test Results There are penalties for low test results from state mandated tests. The student who fails a test on grade three cannot be promoted to grade four. Nor can a student receive a high school diploma if he/she fails the exit test. A school can be labeled as failing if the adequate yearly progress (AYP) test results are low. Each state writes their own test questions and sets the ayp. If a school is below the ayp for two years in a row, then that school is labeled as failing. A student may opt out of a failing school. He/she might then attend one that is not negatively classified. A further penalty for low achieving schools is that test results by school is published in the media. Parents may then see how well schools are doing in teaching students. If a school or school system is continually labeled as being in the failure category, then the state may take over with a new administration and teachers. This would be a drastic step with other procedures being emphasized first, such as much inservice education for teachers, summer school for students, as well as indepth tutoring. There will be a tightening up of the entire educational system. It behooves a state to provide pre kindergarten education for all students as well as parents becoming actively involved in informal teaching of students in the home setting. The mathematics curriculum cannot be left to chance but needs quality planning and implementation. The mathematics curriculum needs support from all in a community (Ediger and Rao, 2000). Planning the Mathematics Curriculum There is much planning that is essential in a good mathematics curriculum. Teachers need to evaluate each objective to be stressed, be it knowledge or skills ends. The objective for student achievement needs to relevant and important. It should incorporate vital facts, concepts, and generalizations. Skills ends should reflect quality student achievement in using what has been learned. Use needs to be made of all knowledge objectives achieved. Practical use as well as theoretical application must be emphasized. The objectives chosen locally should harmonize with the state mandated objectives.
