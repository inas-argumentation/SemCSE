The Dynamics of the Drug Market
Cocaine and heroin have been banned since 1916, marijuana since 1937, and many other drugs at least since Nixon's "War on Drugs"; yet they are all more prevalent than ever. Some drugs have become more widespread at the same time that the use of alcohol and tobacco has diminished. This paradox is rooted in the nature of the drug market and follows from the dynamics of supply and demand. The attempt at eradication not only has to fail; it actually tends to expand the market! Actually, the "War on Drugs" is not a war at all. However grounded it may be in morality and political expediency, and even though its chosen instruments are the police, the military, and the courts of law, it is an attempt to bring about an economic result namely, the shrinking and eventual disappearance of a market. This will only succeed if the policies chosen interact properly with the targeted market, and that depends on the market as well as the policies. In fact, the "War on Drugs" generates results nearly the opposite of those intended.
