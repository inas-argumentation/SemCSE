Reading and Laterality Revisited
1 A shortened version of this paper was presented to the International Symposium on Learning Disabilities, Miami, Florida, October 1973. 2 This research was supported in part by a grant from the Valentine-Kline Foundation. Teachers and researchers in the field of learning disabilities (LD) have encountered the concept of laterality since Orton ( 1937) presented his theory of hemispheric dominance and the strephosymbolia syndrome. A child was said to have mixed or crossed laterality when he deviated from the &dquo;normal&dquo; pattern-i.e., showed a consistent preference for one side of his body in eye, hand, ear, and foot usage. For instance, if a right-handed child used his left eye for sighting under binocular conditions, he was said to have crossed laterality.
