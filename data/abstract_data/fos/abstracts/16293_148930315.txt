Light too has added both structure and interest to his book. His findings are clearly laid out in nine engaging chapters. Many of the important ideas appear in different contexts, but this repetition does not detract from a well-written, carefully thought-out work. Subheadings within chapters and "logical lists," particularly when focusing on specific conclusions, strengthen the organization. Most readers will value the numerous anecdotes highlighting the sound of thoughtful student voices. Their compelling stories give life to the largely invisible research. If there is such a thing as a "feel good" academic book, Light has written it. The creative insights and advice are predicated on qualitative research on students' own observations and perceptions. Importantly, under them lies a sincere desire to make a genuine difference in students' lives. Light's book has wide applicability because the decisions made by students, by administrators, and by faculty can have profound impacts on the overall undergraduate experience. Light lambastes colleagues who fail to acknowledge the cumulative effect of these decisions, particularly decisions made by campus leaders. The worst thing one can do is admit good students and "get out of their way" (p. 2). Campus leaders, on the other hand, "should make a thoughtful, evidence-based, purposeful effort to get in each student's way" (p. 209). Light's final chapter summarizes proactive policies and practices that students say can improve their life and learning on campuses. These include establishing policies of inclusion, working consistently to develop a positive campus culture, encouraging attitudes of "good will," and capitalizing on the crucial first few weeks of the first year. To this list should be added: Questioning your assumptions and gathering data, as Light and his team are doing, on which to base decisions to strengthen all aspects of the undergraduate experience. Talk to students.
