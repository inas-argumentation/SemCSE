The tone of the book throughout thus tends to link failure in stopping "horizontal" proliferation with failure in stopping "vertical" proliferation (i.e., other arms races). This becomes particularly evident in Chapter 18 on the "failure" of the 1975 NPT Review Conference; he accepts very literally the grumblings of the diplomats of the "Group of 77" that the superpowers' failure to make meaningful progress at SALT was relevant to their own obligations to forgo nuclear weapons. It is not so clear that there is a true linkage here, or even that countries like Mexico and Nigeria and the Philippines really see a linkage. (Their diplomats of course have to pretend to see a linkage). The continued superpower arms race is bad, and continued nuclear proliferation is bad, but the two may be handled better separately than jointly. The author obviously does not see it that way. Liniked to this overall premise of the book, one thus sees less discussion than might be appropriate to the special nuclear proliferation temptations of Israel and of South Africa, and of South Korea and Taiwan, all countries close to the bomb, all countries likely to be totally indifferent to Soviet-American progress on SALT. The political ramifications of nuclear proliferation in these cases simply do not fit the unitary picture of arms control that Epstein endorses. The author similarly devotes relatively little discussion to the selfishly unilateral options that the United States and Canada and like-minded nations will now have for the forestalling of nuclear proliferation, through the coordination of the Nuclear Suppliers' Conferences, or the stifling of plutonium reprocessing schemes and new uranium enrichment processes. It is here where some of the tougher choices for an antiproliferation strategy will have to be made. A final kind of unitary bias is shown in the very title of the book. It is indeed true that every addition to the nuclear club is a setback for world peace and international sccurity. Yet the chance to stop number seven is not our "last chance," any more than was our mismanaged effort to stop India as number six. GEORGE QUESTER Cornell University
