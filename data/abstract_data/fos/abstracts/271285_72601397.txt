Longterm Outcome of Antidiuretic Treatment in Two Different Modes – A Multicentric National Analysis of 487 Enuretic Children
Purpose This national multicentric retrospective survey investigated whether structured withdrawal of desmopressin can improve longterm outcome in enuretic children. Patients and Methods In a retrospective survey 487 monosymptomatic enuretic patients from 181 primary care centres were documented. Treatment with desmopressin (0.2 or 0.4 mg) was either abruptly terminated or progressively withdrawn by lengthening the treatment intervals (structured withdrawal every 2nd, 3rd day and so on). 361 children could be followed-up at least one year after finishing treatment with 129 and 232 patients respectively in the two groups. Initial enuresis severity was comparable. Results The group with abrupt termination (initially 173 children) showed 51% response, 27% partial response and 22% no response at end of treatment. One year after end of therapy 72% of 129 patients were continent (65% without any treatment) and 44.2% of the initial responders at end of treatment remained continent, 18% relapsed. The expected maturation rate (15%) is increased to 58% (termination group) and 77% (withdrawal group). It might be assumed that desmopressin stimulates the body's own production of antidiuretic hormone.
