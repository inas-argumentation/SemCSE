Modification of Cluttered Speech in an Emotionally Disturbed Child

Operant conditioning techniques have been widelyapplied to the modification of emotionally ,disturbed behaviors. Within the realm of verbal behavior, these techinques have been successfully applied in the treatment of stuttering (Goldiamond, 1965), to reinstate verbal behavior in a mute psychotic (Isaacs, Thomas & Goldiamond, 1960), and to develop a verbal repertoire in autistic children (Lovaas, 1966). This paper will describe the application of these techniques to the modification of “cluttered” speech. The term “cluttering” is used to classify a rather diverse set of speech anomalies, such as letter and vowel substitutions, poor articulation, word omissions, and disruption in speech tempo. This type of speech deficiency appears quite often in normal as well as emotionally disturbed children. Weiss (1964) has speculated that both cluttering and stuttering result from constitutional differences which give rise to a delay in the development of speech. He believes that stuttering develops in individuals who have a history of cluttering. The evidence t h u s far, however, has not substantiated the notion that a history of cluttering is a necessary condition for the development of stuttering, although the two conditions often coexist. Freund (1953) has reported, for instance, that approximately 50 percent of children whose stuttering first appeared at puberty had a history of cluttering. Although cluttering may not be as aberrant as stuttering or stammering, nevertheless the verbal behavior is unintelligible.
