In one way or another it has been of concern to such fields as physical education, psychology; psychiatry, neurophysiology and optometry. In fact, some aspect of motor learning can be regarded as playing a very significant part in a!most any endeavor of human concern. The branch of motor learning under consideration in this report is learning through motor activity and, specifically, children's learning basic skills and concepts in reading and language through movement-oriented experiences. In the field of physical education, when we speak of learning through motor activity, we refer to things that children do in order to learn. Hence, as far as the physical educator is concerned, motor learning activities derive from basic physical education curriculum in the three broad categories of game activities, rhythmic activities and self-testing activities. For this reason we can also refer to motor activity learning as the physical education learning medium.
