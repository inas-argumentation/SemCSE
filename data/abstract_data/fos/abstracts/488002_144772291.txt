The Brazilian People: The Formation and Meaning of Brazil (review)
Throughout the book, the author evaluates the characters of people related to Pedro II, their weaknesses, their virtues, and their adequacy for the roles they played. For instance, Barman comments about Pedro IFs father, "By nature the emperor [Pedro I] was too ebullient, too erratic, and too emotional for his role" (p. 17). Even though this does not jeopardize the whole analysis, it gives the book a certain naivete, and it is regrettable that acknowledging the role of individuals in the Second Reign involved the assessment of personalities and naturalistic assumptions. Nevertheless, this should not obscure the fact that this beautifully-written biogra' phy, with a solid empirical basis, is now indispensable for the understanding of Pedro II and the micro-politics of his reign.
