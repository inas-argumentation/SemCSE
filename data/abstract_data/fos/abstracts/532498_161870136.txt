Surrealism, sex, revulsion, and pessimism dominate. After World War Two, Meral believes that Paris' importance for American writers is largely ended except for its continuing role with black and gay writers. Meral's volume details hundreds of books and thousands of references. In places it approaches a catalogue of references, each painstakingly developed yet each so limited and specific as to distract attention from the author's major points. Meral recognizes that Paris' importance as a theme in American literature is not major. Thus, this study's impact is also limited. Meral's insights are most helpful to me in characterizing the role of Paris in Henry James's works. At the other extreme, Paris during the "lost generation" era of the 1920s is shown as providing far less inspiration for American writers than expected.
