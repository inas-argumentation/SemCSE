The second weakness is stylistic. Although Castro makes a commendable effort to offer a readable narrative, his chapters refuse to forego the introduction-content-conclusion format of most monographs, so the text is a good deal more repetitive than it need be. These introductions also summarize Rolland’s private life, depriving the ensuing narrative sections of any suspense, surprise, or pathos. And there are quite a few clumsy, stating-the-obvious transitions (“Rolland was not alone in . . . ”). Still, a biographical study is definitely a sound choice for attracting readers to subjects they might otherwise avoid, from debates over tax policy to innovations in concrete, whose drabness belies their importance to the fulfilling of the promises of the Revolution.
