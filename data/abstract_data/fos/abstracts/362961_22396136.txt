The KATP Channel is Critical for Calcium Sequestration into Non-ER Compartments in Mouse Pancreatic Beta Cells
K<sub>ATP</sub> channel activity influences beta cell Ca<sup>2+</sup> homeostasis by regulating Ca<sup>2+</sup> influx through L-type Ca<sup>2+</sup> channels. The present paper demonstrates that loss of K<sub>ATP</sub> channel activity due to pharmacologic or genetic ablation affects Ca<sup>2+</sup> storage in intracellular organelles. ATP depletion, by the mitochondrial inhibitor FCCP, led to Ca<sup>2+</sup> release from the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) of wildtype beta cells. Blockade of ER Ca<sup>2+</sup> ATPases by cyclopiazonic acid abolished the FCCP-induced Ca<sup>2+</sup> transient. In beta cells treated with K<sub>ATP</sub> channel inhibitors FCCP elicited a significantly larger Ca<sup>2+</sup> transient. Cyclopiazonic acid did not abolish this Ca<sup>2+</sup> transient suggesting that non-ER compartments are recruited as additional Ca<sup>2+</sup> stores in beta cells lacking K<sub>ATP</sub> channel activity. Genetic ablation of K<sub>ATP</sub> channels in SUR1KO mice produced identical results. In INS-1 cells transfected with a mitochondrial-targeted Ca<sup>2+</sup>-sensitive fluorescence dye (ratiometric pericam) the increase in mitochondrial Ca<sup>2+</sup> evoked by tolbutamide was 5-fold larger compared to 15 mM glucose. These data show that genetic or pharmacologic ablation of K<sub>ATP</sub> channel activity conveys Ca<sup>2+</sup> release from a non-ER store. Based on the sensitivity to FCCP and the property of tolbutamide to increase mitochondrial Ca<sup>2+</sup> it is suggested that mitochondria are the recruited store. The change in Ca<sup>2+</sup> sequestration in beta cells treated with insulinotropic antidiabetics may have implications for beta cell survival and the therapeutic use of these drugs.
