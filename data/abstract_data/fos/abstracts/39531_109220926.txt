Air-Jet and Row-Crop Headers for Soybeans
ABSTRACT DATA are presented on the effects of variety, row spacing, and header design on losses during the harvest of soybeans. The Corsoy, Amsoy-71, Beeson, and Williams soybean varieties were grown in row spacings of 18 and 76 cm. A row-crop header was compared to a conventional floating cutterbar header and to a built-in, flexible cutterbar header. Both cutterbar headers were operated with and without air-jet guards, by turning the air supply on and off. The row-crop header was the most efficient one used on the 76 cm rows under the field conditions in 1976. On the built-in flexible cutterbar header, the use of the air jets reduced losses slightly, but on the conventional floating cutterbar header, air jets reduced losses 45 percent. The increases in yield for the 18 cm row spacing versus the 76 cm spacing were 4 percent for all varie-ties and 8 percent for the Corsoy variety.
