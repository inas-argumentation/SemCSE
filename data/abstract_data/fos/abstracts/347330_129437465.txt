Paleoecology of a middle Eocene mangrove biota (vertebrates, plants, and invertebrates) from southwest Texas
This is a paleoecologic analysis of a middle Eocene suite of fossils, the Casa Blanca fauna and flora. Specimens were recovered by excavation, screen washing, and core drilling in marginal marine deposits of the Laredo Formation, Claiborne Group, in Webb County, Texas, U.S.A. The associated fauna includes three invertebrate, eight chrondrichyan, six osteichthyan, one amphibian, ten reptilian, and numerous mammalian taxa. Plant megafossils and sporomorphs consist of one lycophyte, seven ferns, one conifer, and at least ten angiosperm species, including the mangrove-forming palm Nypa and members of a back-mangrove swamp forest and lowland tropical rain forest. Of special interest is the high diversity of the vertebrate fauna, unsurpassed in Paleogene Coastal Plain deposits, and the occurrence of the mangrove palynoflora in brackish water deposits, probably the first such deposits to be analyzed palynologically from the middle Eocene of the Gulf Coast. Biogeographic distributions of living relatives of the Casa Blanca biota are used to reconstruct the paleoecological conditions which existed in Webb County, Texas, during deposition of the fossil-bearing strata. These species indicate that the depositional setting was an estuarine Nypa mangrove-complex growing under tropical conditions. Intertidal waters, warmer than 24°C and with little wave action, had salinities close to 10%. Chelonian remains suggest that climatic conditions resembled those now found in coastal areas of southern New Guinea and northern Australia, or those prevailing in southeastern Mexico and northeastern Central America.
