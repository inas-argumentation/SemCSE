Sedimentological evidence concerning the paleoceanography of the cretaceous western north atlantic ocean
Black clays developed as a result of progressively intensifying intermittent anoxic conditions from the Early through Mid-Cretaceous. The preservation of primary sedimentary structures, of authigenic minerals such as pyrite and siderite, and of significant amounts of organic carbon testify to the stagnant conditions. Intermittent anoxic conditions prevailed in the deepest portion of the basin, resulting from vertical migration of an intrasediment anoxic interface. Middle Aptian variegated clays within the black clay sequence at several low-latitude sites suggest a widespread oxygenation event of short duration occurred there. Greater thicknesses of black clays accumulated in low latitudes, implying that surface or intermediate depth currents redistributed the sediments south-ward. Millimeter-scale laminae characterizing the Early and Mid-Cretaceous sediments represent seasonal sediment input where seasons during the Cretaceous had durations measured in years. Cenomanian variegated clays succeeded the black clay interval and represent deposition under totally oxygenated conditions. Thus the sluggish and stagnant conditions present in the Early and Mid-Cretaceous were followed by increased current activity. These currents were derived from more northern latitudes as Iberia was rifted from North America. Cooler temperatures presumably increased the oxygen capacity of the water and its ability to sink into the deep basin to relieve the stagnant conditions present. The Late Cenomanian through Late Campanian was a time of widespread marine transgression, resulting in the deep ocean becoming a sediment-starved basin. Clastics and carbonates accumulated on the shelves as the Calcite Compensation Depth (CCD) shallowed appreciably. The Late Campanian through Maastrichtian interval is characterized by calcium carbonate accumulation throughout much of the deep basin. A significant lowering of sea level, paralleled by a deepening CCD, caused this sedimentation pattern.
