Nonparticipation in Welfare Programs by Eligible Households: The Case of the Food Stamp Program
Implicit in most discussions of the welfare issue is the assumption that households that are eligible to participate in public transfer programs will indeed do so. The "welfare explosion" of the late 1 960s has been partially attributed to the fact that, as a result of the actions of various welfare rights organizations, eligible households became aware of the welfare benefits to which they were entitled and actively sought to acquire them.2 Continued high caseloads in the 1970s provided further evidence that our public welfare programs were effective in reaching their intended beneficiaries. This attitude has become so ingrained, in fact, that fears have been voiced that the welfare system has become overextended, and now reaches a number of people not truly in need of assistance. The available evidence, however, does not appear to support this view.
