The Public is Invited to Dance: Representation, the Body, and Dialogue in Gertrude Stein (review)
After reading Sea-Brothers, I am prepared to believe that "the sea's influence on American literature has been more profound and continuous than is presently accepted," but I doubt that it exceeds "even that of the inland frontier," as Bender claims. Still, this is a rich and exciting addition to the critical studies of sea literature already produced by Thomas Philbrick, Willard H. Bonner, Jeanne-Marie Santraud, Charles Lee Lewis, Robert K. Martin, and Merrill R. Davis.
