Rory Bester I n Distinction: A Social Critique of the Judgement of Taste (1984) and The Field of Cultural Production: Essays on Art and Literature (1993), Pierre Bourdieu unfolds the interrelated concepts of cultural and symbolic capital. Where cultural capital implies a series of codes of cultural knowledge and a level of competence in accessing and reading these codes, symbolic capital marks the consecration that comes from this cultural knowledge and the recognition thereof. In Bourdieu's own words, cultural capital, tied to the meaningfulness of consumption, involves "an act of deciphering, decoding, which presupposes practical or explicit mastery of a cipher or code. ... A work of art has meaning and interest only for someone who possesses the cultural competence, that is, the code, into which it is encoded." Recognition of the code brings empathy and appreciation.
