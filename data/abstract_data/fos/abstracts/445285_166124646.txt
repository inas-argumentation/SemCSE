Andrew Redden’s “Divine Messengers and Divine Messages” reinvigorates well-known images like Jacob’s ladder in the context of Latin America. Markus Friedrich’s “On Reading Missionary Correspondence” explains how vital letter-writing was to the “DNA” of the Jesuit mission. And Renate Dürr’s “Early Modern Translation Theories as Mission Theories” explains how José de Acosta’s experiences in Peru revolutionized his translation work. According to Dürr, De Acosta broke with custom, preferring “personal experience” as a guide to translation over “dead book-learning” (215). In the final essay, Susanna Burghartz demonstrates the need for approaches to the study of theology and media. In the printed works of Theodor de Bry and others, Burghartz notes that references to the Straits of Magellan are filled with “colonial knowledge, worldly wisdom, and moral-religious edification,” indicating how theology and media are intimately connected to the appropriate political, economic, and even geographic contexts (245). The volume, however, is not without some significant missteps. In the introduction, Strasser and Wild inaccurately characterize “the new medium of print” as “the flagging print business” in the years before Martin Luther’s break with Rome (4). Compounding this mischaracterization, the endnote speculates, “The Reformation may even have saved print from its premature extinction” (28). Elsewhere, Helmut Puff claims that Thomas Müntzer’s theology “marks the move away from medieval interpretations of the Mass as sacrifice to the new understanding of Mass as the locus of an encounter with the divine” (117). If this is true, what then is the doctrine of transubstantiation if not an encounter with the divine? These essays occupy a borderland between theology and communication that is vast and has until now been occupied mostly by studies of print and manuscript, with only recent works on music, liturgy, and other media. The vastness of this border amplifies the differences of the essays. Although they fit together uncomfortably, the essays are successful indicators of the robust fields on either side of the border and of how much fertile soil remains to be tilled.
