Adaptive Hybrid Surrogate Modeling for Complex Systems
This paper explores the effectiveness of the recently developed surrogate modeling method, the Adaptive Hybrid Functions, when applied in conjunction with different sampling techniques and different sample sizes. The Adaptive Hybrid Functions is a hybrid surrogate modeling method that seeks to exploit the advantages of each component surrogate. To explore its effectiveness, the Adaptive Hybrid Functions is applied to model four disparate complex engineering systems, namely: 1) wind farm power generation, 2) product platform planning (for universal electric motors), 3) three-pane window heat transfer, and 4) onshore wind farm cost estimation. The effectiveness of the following three distinct sampling techniques is investigated: 1) Latin hypercube sampling, 2) Sobol’s quasi-random sequence, and 3) the Hammersley sequence sampling.
