Hydrothermal synthesis and low temperature conduction properties of substituted ceria ceramics
Rare earth or alkaline earth oxide-substituted ceria (CeO 2 ) powders were successfully prepared via hydrothermal synthetic route. Using these powders after freeze-drying, highly dense ceramics of Ce 1− x M x O 2 with M = Ca 2+ , Sr 2+ , Sm 3+ , Gd 3+ and Y 3+ and x = 0.2 were prepared by sintering at much lower temperatures than 1700 °C, common for CeO 2 preparation by conventional ceramic techniques. For example, 20 mol% samaria-substituted ceria with 98% of the theoretical density was sintered at 1450 °C. The conductivities determined by an ac impedance method were approximately 100 times higher than that of yttria-stabilized zirconia (YSZ). The conductivity was optimal with Sm 3+ substitution ( σ (210 °C ) ~10 −6 S/cm). The analysis of the impedance spectra indicate that the observed impedance at the relatively low temperatures of measurement (300–600 °C) is predominantly dependent on grain boundary effects.
