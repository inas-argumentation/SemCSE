While they find only mixed support for the latter, they reject the former outright. This is followed by the last installment in the volume, a study by Frederico Jayme of Thirlwall's Law as it applies to Brazil. He finds support, leading him to the conclusion that "a policy of export promotion, combined with an import-substitution strategy, could be rational in terms of policy prescriptions, since both strategies lead to moderate BOP constraints in the long term" (p. 325). All in all, this is a nice collection. However, although the editors have done their best to select topics that are key in Post Keynesian economics, the necessarily narrow focus of each chapter means that it is likely that readers will find some fascinating and others tedious. This is because empirical work done properly requires that the author carefully document data forms and sources and explain in careful detail the method and specification. If this is a topic in which you are already interested, these tidbits are engaging and invaluable; otherwise, they are monotonous. But, as a collected volume, it is easy to pick and choose. Because many of the chapters are fairly technical, this volume is appropriate to economists and perhaps graduate students, but not undergraduates.
