Techniques in dosimetry and 3‐D treatment planning for stereotactic radiosurgery/radiotherapy
The goal of this work was to improve stereotactic radiosurgery(SRS)dose calculation and dosimetry techniques. Monte Carlo techniques were used to calculate and PTW diamond detectors were used to measure SRSdose distributions. A method of calculating dose distribution using sector-integration of Monte Carlo-generated pencil beam kernels is proposed. This technique can be used both for circular and irregular fields. It is especially useful in calculating the dosimetry for irregular fields, where the shape of the treatment field is determined during planning. Monte Carlo techniques were confirmed as reliable references for calculation of the dose distribution of SRS fields. Diamond detectors were found to be potentially ideal for SRS. They yield more accurate results than the commonly used silicon diodes, ionization chambers, film or thermoluminescencedosimetry. Bioeffect planning, as a rather new approach, was introduced in which treatment plans can be evaluated according to the bioeffect distribution rather than the dose distribution. Dose can be converted to biologically effective dose using the alpha/beta ratio from the linear-quadratic model. Estimates of TCP and NTCP can also be obtained using the Alpha, Beta values, dose-volume histogram data, and in the case of normal tissue the partial volume of the organ irradiated.
