Reagentless enzyme electrode for glucose-6-phosphate using a mediator-modified graphite electrode and macromolecular NAD+
An enzyme electrode for the reagentless determination of glucose-6-phosphate was developed. A cellulose membrane was used to retain a mixture of glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase and NAD+ linked to polyethyleneglycol at the N-6 position by an aminoethyl spacer. The reduced macromolecular coenzyme is reoxidized at a graphite electrode modified with 3,3′-bis(benzo[a]phenoxazine-7-ium,5-amino-9-(diethylamino))-1,4,N,N′-diamidobenzene at an applied potential of 0 mV vs SCE. The electrode was used for substrate determinations at steady-state in a flow injection system. The response was slightly non-linear from the detection limit, 10 μM, up to 10 mM glucose-6-phosphate. The life-time was limited to a few days because of loss of coenzyme.
