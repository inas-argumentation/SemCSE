Observation of immunogenicity of bivalent and tetravalent recombinant plasmids containing dengue viruses type 1 to 4 prM-E genes in mice
Objective To observe the immunogenicity of bivalent and tetravalent recombinant plasmids in mice and lay a foundation for developing polyvalent DNA vaccines. Methods Endotoxin-free plasmids DNA were extracted with kits from QIAGEN. Mice were co-immunized with bivalent or tetravalent recombinant plasmids and GM-CSF intramuscularly, and electroporation was immediately performed at the same sites post-immunization to increase DNA uptake. The mice were boosted on the week 2 and 4 post-prime. Antibody titers were determined by indirect immunofluorescence assay and neutralizing assay. And cellular immune responses were evaluated with stimulation indexes and levels of secreted IFN-γ of splenocytes. Muscles from immunized-sites were stained with HE to observe cellular infiltrates. Results Mice immunized with recombinant plasmids began to produce antibodies against dengue viruses type 1 to 4 on the week 4 post-prime. And on the week 14 post-prime, the neutralizing titers reached 1∶32. The stimulation indexes and IFN-γ levels from immunized groups were both higher than those from the control groups. GM-CSF could promote humoral immune responses, but had no effect on cellular immune responses. Conclusion The constructed bivalent and tetravalent recombinant plasmids could induce humoral and cellular immune responses in mice.
