Socio-Economic Attitudinal Correlates of Educational Viewpoint
EDUCATION, a pervasive government function, is likely to reflect liberal and conservative econom ic philosophies. Most previous investigations have assumed that liberalism-conservatism is a uni-di mensional socio-economic factor. Because Lipset (2) and others have shown that this assumption is untenable, separate scales were constructed in this study as measures of economic and social liberal ism-conservatism. Economic liberalism-conserva tism was defined with reference to disputes involv ing the distribution of wealth and power. Social lib eralism-conservatism was defined with reference to civil liberties issues. The Economic Scale is shown in Table 1 and the Social Scale is shown in Table 2. According to their responses to these two scales, respondents were characterized as conservative on both economic and social issues (CC), conservative on economic issues but liberal on social issues (CL), liberal on economic issues but conservative on so cial issues (LC) or liberal in both respects (LL). Each type of liberalism-conservatism has its own relevance for education, depending on which aspects of education are considered. Many educational is sues depend primarily on economic philosophy; oth ers depend primarily on willingness to encourage or tolerate dissent and general orientation concerning standards of morality. Accordingly, separate scales were constructed as measures of different t y p es of educational viewpoint. The sixscales, which constitute the dependent variables,were titled as follows: Equalization Scale; Moral Controver sies Scale; Methods, Standards, and Discipline Scale; Facilities Scale; Social Studies Controversies Scale ; and Breadth of Curriculum Scale. These scales are shown in Tables 3-8, respectively. All scales in the study are of the Likert-type, each hav ing six response alternatives.
