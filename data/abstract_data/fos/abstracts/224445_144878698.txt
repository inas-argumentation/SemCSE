Business as usual? Leisure provision for the unemployed.
Leisure provision for the unemployed is a topic of some controversy. On the one hand sociologists and psychologists claim that leisure is unlikely to yield the satisfactions traditionally fulfilled by work; on the other, public and voluntary sector leisure providers often feel that the unemployed need (or deserve) special forms of provision. This paper offers a basic description of the leisure participation patterns of the population at large, draws inferences about the likely participation patterns of the unemployed, and examines the range of leisure provision currently being directed at the unemployed by local authority recreation departments. The paper concludes by reporting on the effectiveness of some of the schemes provided to date.
