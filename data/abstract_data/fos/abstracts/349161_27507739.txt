Generalization of Coping Skills in a Group of Retarded Students
This study invesrigated the generalization of behaviors and skills required for adaptation to both school and home enviro~lents and shown by a group of mild-moderatesevere mentally retarded students. It is a perennial problem wherher behavioral gains made in an original treatment environment will be generalized to other social settings. The assumption was made that generalization is a function of the presence or absence of reinforcement or punishment contingencies in extra-classroom settings ( 1 ) . Students learn to discriminate between reinforcing and nonreinforcing environments. To ascertain a measure of generalization of adaprive behavioral skills between home and school, The Coping Inventory (2) was used. This is an observational instrument designed for use by teachers, paraprofessionals, and parents. The 48 items in the inventory are scored on a continuum from maladaptive ( 1) to adaptive (5). Behavior viewed as appropriate to the environment, as enhancing self-care, is considered adaptive coping behavior. Items are distributed among six factors, with respective interobserver reliability and split-half reliability coefficients ranging from .84 to .95 and .96 to 99. The subjects were 13 mild-moderate-severe mentally retarded students whose ages ranged from 11 to 20 yr. (M of 16 yr.). Each student was involved in vocational, community, domestic, and recreational/leisure skill training at a self-concained special school. The analysis of the data was by paired t-test comparisons of ratings by individual teachers and parent/guardians on each dimension. None of the t ratios reached 2.06, necessary for a significant difference at p .O5. Values for the respective faaon were: Self-productive (t = .82, df = 1, SD = .83, teacher M = 3.5, parent/guardian M = 3.31); Self-active (t ~1.08, df = 1, SD = .87, teacher M = 3.83, parent/guardian M = 3.57); Self-flexible (t = -.07, dj = 1, SD = 1.00, teacher M = 3.18, parent/ guardian M = 3.19); Environment Productive (t = -.46, dj = 1, SD = .81, teacher M = 3.73, prent/guardian M = 3.62) ; Environment Active (t = .75, df = 1, SD = 1.11, teacher M = 3.28, parent/guardian M = 3.52); Environment Flexible (t = .51, df = 1, SD = .71, teacher M = 3.51, parenr/guardian M = 3.40). The consistent ratings between 3 and 4 by teachers and parent/guardians indicate a relatively high effectiveness in coping skills for the group in different settings. It is recommended other special education programs include regular comparison of perceptions of teachers and parenc/guardians as a means of estimating accountabiliry of the programs.
