The paleoceanographic significance of the radiolarian didymocyrtis tetrathalamus in Eastern Cape basin sediments
Anomalously high relative abundances of the radiolarian Didymocyrtis tetrathalamus are found in deglacial sediments of the Cape Basin core RC13-228. These high abundances represent a unique kind of “no-analog” assemblage which are produced by sporadic influxes of D. tetrathalamus to the eastern Cape Basin sediments. These influxes require at least a seasonal reduction in the northward component of wind stress off southwest Africa, since D. tetrathalamus lives in the surface mixed layer. It is possible that Agulhas Current leakage into the South Atlantic supplies D. tetrathalamus to the Cape Basin seasonally in which case the record of this species in RC13-228 could monitor the flow of Agulhas water over the core site. Maximum accumulation rates of D. tetrathalamus in RC13-228 occur during times of minimum Southern Hemisphere summer insolation and lead minimum δ 18 O (minimum ice volume) by several thousand years. This signal is therefore at least partially independent of Northern Hemisphere ice volume forcing.
