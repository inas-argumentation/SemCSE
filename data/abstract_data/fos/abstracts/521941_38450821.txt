The effects of electric convulsive treatments on nitrogen, calcium, and phosphorus metabolism in psychiatric patients.
Summary Electric convulsive treatments were therapeutically administered to 7 psychiatric patients. Two patients were diagnosed as schizophrenic reactions, 5 as depressive mood disorders. Complete balance studies were done on the metabolic unit of the Payne Whitney Clinic before, during, and after the series of treatments. Determinations of nitrogen, calcium, and phosphorus excretion were done. Four patients demonstrated a gradual, progressive decrease in urinary and stool calcium loss, with an associated increase in calcium retention during the administration of electric convulsive treatments. Three patients showed no alteration in calcium metabolism. This experiment revealed no significant changes in nitrogen or phosphorus excretion. In those patients demonstrating calcium retention, the metabolic changes did not appear related to individual electric convulsive treatments, but rather to the overall series of 6–10 treatments. Moreover, these same patients demonstrated a more striking degree of clinical improvement than those in whom no calcium changes were noted.
