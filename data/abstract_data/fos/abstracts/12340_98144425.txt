High-Throughput Crystallography: The Challenge of Publishing, Storing and Using the Results
Crystal structure data have a fundamental importance that goes far beyond the original purpose of each analysis. However, as structural throughput increases, journals and databases record a rapidly decreasing proportion of the structures that are being determined, and this data loss has unfortunate scientific ramifications. It is recognised that preparing a publication is now relatively time consuming by comparison with experimental procedures, but ‘socio-political’ factors connected with the ownership of, and responsibility for, crystal structure data also contribute significantly to the current situation. The crystallographic databases, working in concert with individual scientists and journals, have a role to play in releasing this log-jam of structural results into the public domain. What is needed is an open debate about the issues, and about the possibilities for maximising public data availability.
