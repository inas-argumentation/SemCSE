Effect of Column Temperature and Flow Rate on Retention and Separation of Oligonucleotides by Reversed Phase Ion Pair Chromatography
The effect of temperature and flow rate on the separation of different patterns of oligonucleotides,which classified into n serial and n/(n-1) serial according to their length and base composition,were investigated by measuring the resolution of model samples in reversed-phase ion-pair chromatography.At 25 ℃-45 ℃,the increasing temperature contributed to the separation of n/(n-1) serial,and the three trends of resolution were observed for different n serial oligonucleotides.The almost linear decreased resolution was obtained for oligonucleotides with the same composition but different sequence.While under the flow rate of 0.3-0.7 mL/min, the best resolution was observed under 0.4 mL/min after calibration for both serials.The research shows that the influence of temperature works not only through the mass transfer of oligonucleotides,but also by changing secondary structure of different sequences to influence the space bulk of base.And also,lower flow rate contributed to the separation of two serials by the increasing mass transfer of biopolymers.However the effect of initial organic strength and elute program on retention and separation changing with the flow rate should be paid more attention.
