Comparison of Australian and United States Word Association Values
As part of the normative requirements for a larger research project, singleword free association responses to a sample of 53 stimuli from the test used by Russell and Jenkins (1954) were obtained from 343 first-year psychology students in the University of Queensland, Australia. The responses were obtained in one session. Administration and instructions were essentially those described by Russell and Jenkins ( 1954). The results provide some interesting comparisons with U.S. norms. ( 1 ) Of the 53 stimuli, 12 evoked primary responses different from those obtained in Minnesota. These stimuli are listed below, together with their primary responses, and the percentage frequency at which the latter occurred (Queensland data are given first) : bath (water, 34.1%, clean, 31.2), carpet (floor, 20.6, rug, 45.6), cheese (mouse, 22.6, crackers, 10.7), child (mother, 15.4, baby, 15.8), earth (sky, 22.2, round, 12.91, hand (finger(s), 32.0, foot, 25.3), quiet (noisy, 11.6, loud, 34.5). religion (God, 23.3, church, 28.3), trouble (worry, 10.4, bad, 8.9), whistle (train, 13.0, stop, 13.0), wish (desire, 13.8, want, 12.3), yellow (green, 11.4, blue, 15.5). ( 2 ) The same primary responses as were found in Minnesota, at a frequency not significantly different from that obtained by Russell and Jenkins, were evoked by the following 16 stimuli: afraid, boy, bread, butterfly, comfort, cottage, deep, green, hammer, lion, man, mountain, salt, spider, stem, window. ( 3 ) The remaining 25 stimuli also evoked identical primary responses in Queensland and Minnesota, but with a significant difference in frequency ( p = .Or) (McNemar, 1962, p. 60) . Significantly more US. students gave the dominant response to the following 20 of these stimuli (the percentage frequency of primary responses is given in parenthesis, with the Queensland figure first) : bitter (44.9 vs 64.7), black (60.2, 74.5), blossom (52.0, 66.7), city (23.3, 35.0), dream (26.19, 44.9), eagle (37.4, 54.61, girl (62.6, 69.3), hard (46.5, 66.91, heavy (36.6, 57.8), high (54.7, 66.9), king (60.1, 74.5), long (62.8, 75.2), needle (36.9, 46.0), priest (24.9, 32.5), scissors (41.3, 66.6), slow (43.5, 74.6), table (75.8, 83.3), tobacco (34.1, 51.1), whiskey (18.6, 28.2), woman (54.2, 64.1). Australian students gave a significantly higher-frequency primary response to only 5 stimuli: cold (41.2, 34.5), memory (19.7, 11.8), rough (51.9, 43.6), sweet (55.3,43.1), working (20.1, 13.1). Full normative data for the 53 stimuli are available on request.
