Induction of ovulation and pregnancy with gonadotropin-releasing hormone in the form of nasal drops. (Abstract only)
The value of gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GRH) in the treatment of anovulatory sterility with amenorrhea has been limited by its short duration of action necessitating repeated parenteral injections. We have examined the hormonal and clinical response to GRH in nasal drops in 6 amenorrheic women in order to evaluate its clinical usefulness. Follicular maturation was induced with human menopasual gonadotropins (HMGs) and the usual upper limit of urinary estrogen excretion (150 mcg/24 hours) for safe administration of human chorionic gonadotropin (HCG) was overstepped in each case. A daily dose of 7.5 mg GRH was then administered at 2-hour intervals for 6 hours on 3 consecutive days. 1 patient showed minimal response 3 responded with a lutinizing hormone (LH) discharge sufficient to induce clinical and hormonal evidence of luteinization and the remaining 2 patients ovulated; 1 of them became pregnant. We conclude that in amenorrheic women with responsive pituitaries GRH in the form of nasal drops can stimulate and maintain LH levels sufficient to induce ovulation following follicular maturation with HMG. The ease and simplicity of self-administration of GRH nasal drops is of practical importance for women requiring long-term therapy or as a substitute for HCG where HCG might cause ovarian hyperstimulation or following intentionally prolonged administration of HMG producing a higher degree of follicular maturation. The pregnancy induced following ovulation induction with GRH nasal drops appears to be the 1st of its kind reported.(Full text)
