Refractive-index measurements of paraffin, a silicone elastomer, and an epoxy resin over the 500-1500-nm spectral range.
With the increase in interest in the field of optical com­ munications, there is a need to know the refractive indices of materials used in conjunction with the optical fibers. Fur­ thermore, over the past few years as sources and detectors have become available, the operating wavelength for systems has progressively moved from the visible to IR to take full advantage of the lower fiber attenuation there. Thus re­ fractive-index measurements are required not only over the 800-900-nm window but also at 1300 and 1550 nm. There appears to be little information in the literature on refrac­ tive-index measurements at these wavelengths. To remedy this deficiency, experiments were carried out using an IR viewer in conjunction with a Hilger-Chance refractometer. The experimental arrangement is shown in Fig. 1(a). A tungsten filament lamp was used as a source, and a number of narrow passband interference filters (having halfwidths of 10 nm or less) were used to select specific wavelengths. The operating principle of the refractometer is as follows. Light from the source is condensed onto a slit in a small collimator, is refracted by the 90° V-block and specimen, and passes into a telescope provided with a reflecting prism (not shown) as the decollimating lens. The optical axis is thus rendered parallel to and rotates about the axis of a divided circle, the instrument in effect being a goniometer arranged in a vertical plane. The circle is engraved at intervals of 10 min of arc, and an optical system provides for simultaneous reading by a micrometer-microscope of diametrically opposite points on the circle, thus minimizing any error liable to be introduced by any slight eccentricity of mounting. Thus the angle of deviation θ, which is a function of the refractive index, may be determined very accurately. The eyepiece of the refrac­ tometer carries two short parallel lines in its focal plane, and the setting is made on a fine line which runs along the middle of the wide slit S [Fig. 1(b)]. The zero calibration of the refractometer was initially checked, and then the arrangement was set up with the first sample using a sodium vapor lamp as the source, thus enabling alignment to be made with the naked eye.
