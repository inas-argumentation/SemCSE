Correspondence between $\alpha$ inelastic cross sections off $^{24}$Mg and isoscalar monopole strengths
The isoscalar monopole (IS0) excitation is considered to be a characteristic transition mode for populating $\alpha$ cluster states of nuclei. The $\alpha$ inelastic scattering has been used as an ideal probe for the IS0 excitation. We aim to clarify the correspondence between the IS0 transition strengths and $\alpha$ inelastic cross sections, the B(IS0)-($\alpha$,$\alpha$') correspondence. Emphasis is put on the correctness of the interpretation of the $\alpha$ inelastic scattering as an IS0 transition process. Diagonal and transition densities of 24Mg are calculated with antisymmetrized molecular dynamics and a microscopic coupled-channel calculation based on a g- and t-matrix folding model is performed to obtain $\alpha$ elastic and inelastic cross sections off 24Mg at 386 MeV. Calculations with the distorted wave Born approximation and plane wave Born approximation are also carried out to see the effects of the channel coupling and nuclear distortion. The $\alpha$ inelastic scattering is shown to be significantly affected by the nuclear distortion as well as the in-medium modification to the nucleon-nucleon effective interaction. Nevertheless, the B(IS0)-($\alpha$,$\alpha$') correspondence tends to hold. However, when a 0+ state having a different intrinsic structure is considered, the validity of the B(IS0)-($\alpha$,$\alpha$') correspondence becomes worse. The coupled-channel effect is found to be non-negligible even at 386 MeV. The $\alpha$ inelastic scattering is not a process expressed by the IS0 transition operator. The robustness of the B(IS0)-($\alpha$,$\alpha$') correspondence stems from a constraint on the transition densities between 0+ states. The node of the transition density reflects the intrinsic structure of the excited state. The difference in the position of the node distorts the B(IS0)-($\alpha$,$\alpha$') correspondence.
