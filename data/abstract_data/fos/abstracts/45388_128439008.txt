Carbon and oxygen isotope event markers near the Frasnian-Famennian boundary, Luoxiu section, South China
Anomalous values of δ13C and δ18O suddenly appear in a bed below the top of the Frasnian Stage at the Luoxiu section in South China. The δ13C values are characterized by a multi-negative peak reaching a maximum of −6.6%. The complex peak occurs in a 20 cm thick bed suggesting that the event which produced the carbon isotope anomaly was of relatively short duration. The critical bed (Unit E) is referred to as an extinction layer as it contains no fossils, probably due to hostile depositional conditions. Moreover, δ13O values within Unit E display a positive excursion suggesting that the depositional environment experienced an estimated decrease in temperature of several degrees centigrade. Strata above Unit E are again fossiliferous indicating a rapid revival of normal organic activity. The 13C and δ18O anomalies represent strong geochemical evidence for a major global catastrophe at the Frasnian-Famennian boundary.
