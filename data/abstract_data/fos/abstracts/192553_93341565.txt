Contribution of the thermal treatment to the ionic conductivity of the one-dimensional superionic conductors AxGa8Ga8+xTi16$minus;xO56 (A=K, Rb)
Single crystals of one-dimensional superionic conductors A x Ga 8 Ga 8+ x Ti 16− x O 56 (AGGTO, A =K, Rb ) were thermally treated between 1100 and 1275°C in air for 0.5–72 h, and slowly or rapidly cooled to room temperature to enhance their ionic conductivity, because the ionic conductivity of as-grown crystals of KGGTO is especially small in spite of having tunnel walls that are smooth inside. The thermal treatment was carried out on the assumption that the blocks are excess oxygen ions in tunnels and may be removed by their evaporation. The contribution of the thermal treatment to the ionic conductivity was evaluated by a complex conductivity measurement from 100 Hz to 32.55 GHz and a curve fitting analysis using an equivalent circuit corresponding to the ion conduction model. The ionic conductivity of KGGTO in the low frequency region below ⋍ 10 GHz was enhanced by the removal of the blocks by the thermal treatment. The optimum treatment condition was at 1200°C for 2 h and the total and bulk ionic conductivities increased by ⋍ 10 4 and ⋍ 10 6 times as for as-grown crystals at 1 MHz, respectively. The ionic conductivity of RGGTO was not enhanced drastically by the same thermal treatment condition.
