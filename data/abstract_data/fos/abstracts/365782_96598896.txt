Determination of the ozone concentration in outdoor air near Delft, The Netherlands
Abstract The most widely used method for the analysis of atmospheric total oxidants is the neutral-buffered KI method, which is accepted as the reference method for ozone determination under laboratory conditions and also for calibration purposes. Because this method is not specific for ozone, several more specific ozone-measuring techniques have been tested and developed by the Research Institute for Public Health Engineering TNO at Delft. These include the chemiluminescent reaction between ozone and Rhodamine-B, the spectrophotometric indigosulphonic-acid method, based on the bleaching activity of ozonised air on a neutral-buffered solution of this acid in water, and a galvanic detection method which has been made highly selective for ozone. The manual spectrophotometric determination of ozone with di-(pyridyl) ethylene, which is also claimed to be specific, proved to be unreliable, while commercially-available Mast-meters are essentially total oxidant meters and not ozone detection devices. Ozone determinations in outdoor air indicate that smog-forming conditions occur in this part of The Netherlands. During 1969 and 1970, 25 days occurred during which ozone values of more than 200 μg/m 3 were recorded. Data of outdoor air measurements for oxides of nitrogen, hydrocarbons, and aldehydes support this point of view.
