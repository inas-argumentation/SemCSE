A review of electromagnetic investigations in the Kapuskasing uplift and surrounding regions: electrical properties of key rocks
Electromagnetic investigations of the Kapuskasing uplift show that the gross electrical conductivity structure of the present crust is subhorizontal (contrary to the lithology as defined by seismic experiments), with increasing conductivity with depth, a feature common to most continental crusts. The current upper crust of the Chapleau block includes zones of reduced resistivity; the near-surface expression of the Ivanhoe Lake cataclastic zone (  40 kΩ∙m), these include a subhorizontal layer at ~ 5 km and a subhorizontal to dipping layer at ~ 2 km. The deeper layer is interpreted as imaging deep fluids (porosities > 0.5%) postdating the uplift.
