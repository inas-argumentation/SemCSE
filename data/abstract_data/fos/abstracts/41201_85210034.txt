Biologically active interleukin 1 in human eccrine sweat: Site-dependent variations in α/β ratios and stress-induced increased excretion
Abstract Human eccrine sweat devoid of epidermal protein contamination was collected from palms, soles, and different sites on the trunk. Interleukin 1α (IL 1α) and interleukin 1β (IL 1β) content were analyzed for immunoreactivity by enzymo-immunoassay and immunoblotting and for bioactivity by the stimulation of prostaglandin E 2 (PGE 2 ) production in human dermal fibroblasts. The bioassay was validated by using blocking antibodies against IL 1α and β. All sweat samples were found to contain significant amounts of immunoreactive and biologically active IL 1. The immunoreactive forms were at 17 kDa as shown by immunoblotting analysis, indicating that they were mature (secreted), undegraded IL 1 peptides. Whereas IL 1α was detectable in sweat samples obtained from both truncal and palmo-plantar regions, IL 1β was only detectable in the sweat of palms and soles (IL 1 α/β ratio > 700 in trunk and 5.4 in palms and soles) indicating a site-dependant difference in the excretion of the two IL 1 molecules. IL 1 concentration was high in spontaneous (IL 1α, 3.7; IL 1β, 0.3 ng/mL) and pilocarpine induced sweat (IL 1α, 3.9; IL 1β, 1.2 ng/mL), and it was much increased during jogging and sauna (IL 1α, 22.6; IL 1β, 3.3 ng/mL). This does not appear to represent an excretory process aimed at clearing blood IL 1, but rather a stress-induced increased production of IL 1 by sweat gland cells.
