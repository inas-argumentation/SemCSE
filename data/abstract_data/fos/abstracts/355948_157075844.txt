Revolution, American Style: The 1960s and Beyond
Neither economists nor sociologists need another word for institutions. Little new is accomplished by adding the following variables to studies of economic growth and calling them social capital: political instability, civil liberties, property rights, political risk, civic communities, or generalized trust. As Ben Fine and Francis Green point out, "The idea of seeing social capital alongside physical capital and human capital as inputs in the production of goods or utility is merely to extend the fetishism of commodities beyond its traditional realm" (p. 87). Fine and Green want to escape reductionism to the individual, utility maximization and universal categories. Social relations are prized in themselves and not only means to the production of marketed goods and services.
