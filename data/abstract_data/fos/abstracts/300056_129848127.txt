Discussion of "Generation of heavy-mineral glacial indicator dispersal trains from a diabase sill, Nipigon region, northwestern Ontario"
The paper by Phillip Larson and Howard Mooers (2005) is an interesting attempt to document the glacial dispersal of one heavy mineral and then try to relate the mineral’s dispersal to their mathematical model for glacial indicator dispersal (Larson and Mooers 2004). The model then allows them to make conclusions about conditions at the base of the glacier, the erosion rates, and entrainment and deposition processes that occurred beneath the glacier. There is, however, information not apparently considered by the authors that could significantly alter several of the assumptions made and, therefore, some of the conclusions that were made in this paper. A few of my concerns, based, in part, on field observations in the area of Nipigon sills to the west of their study area, are mentioned as follows for their consideration.

Firstly, the study area is located to the east of Lake Nipigon, surrounded by areas that contain thick deposits of carbonate-rich till (Thorleifson and Kristjansson 1993). Several previous workers in this area have suggested that fast flowing glacial ice (an ice stream) deposited this material (Hicock 1988, Hicock et al. 1989, Thorleifson and Kristjansson 1993). These workers (Hicock 1988, Hicock et al. 1989, Thorleifson and Kristjansson 1993) argued that the carbonate-rich till formed a “low-resistance stratum for overriding ice streams either by deforming and (or) supporting high subglacial water pressures” (Hicock et al. 1989, p. 2249).
