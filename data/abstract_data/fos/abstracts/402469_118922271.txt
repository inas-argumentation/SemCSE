Two-body interaction. I. Inverse scattering problem, nonlocal potentials, and a conception of quantum measurement theory
The present paper generalizes preceding papers of the author [1]–[6] and opens a cycle of works concerning the general quantum-mechanical inverse scattering problem (for a given partial channel) and its solution in analytic form in a nontraditional setting: the scattering wave function in the momentum representation is recovered immediately from the scattering data. The solution we find for the problem in question is given by a family of phase-equivalent wave functions with explicitly controlled functional-analytic arbitrariness. This arbitrariness corresponds to the arbitrariness in the choice of phase-equivalent nonlocal potentials and demands including some additional concepts of quantum measurement theory to a complete description of quantum-mechanical systems. We discuss several related problems of quantum-mechanical probability distributions and their statistical interpretation. The mathematical apparatus of the approach thus developed is illustrated by the example of describing the nucleon-nucleon interaction in uncoupled partial channels. In conclusion we announce the topics of forthcoming works of the series.
