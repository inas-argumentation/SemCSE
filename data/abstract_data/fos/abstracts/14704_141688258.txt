Development of University Counseling Profession in Taiwan
This article was to elaborate the development of the university counseling profession in Taiwan through an intensive literature review. The university student body has been expanding radically in the last three decades, and the student population, as well as their problems, have become more diverse and complex. Thus, the importance of university counseling profession and the quality of UCC services have been identified in assisting students to cope with stress, to deal with psychosocial problems, and to facilitate development during their university journey. Eight main themes derived from the literature review: (1) historical evolution and current condition of university counseling profession, (2) UCC roles, functions and services content, (3) UCC administration and management, (4) students' utilization of UCCs and attitudes toward UCPs, (5) characteristics, problem types, and needs of students on university campuses, (6) UCC services meeting the needs of students on university campuses, (7) UCC challenges and obstacles, and (8) UCC prospects. UCC services became diverse, multi-channeled, and multi-dimensional to respond to student needs. UCPs should be flexible in professional roles as well as job contents, strategies, and implementation methods. The major challenge for UCC is to develop a native model of services with the perspectives of campus as a community. UCPs are playing the major role to enhance mental health services of university campus members. Key words: university counseling profession, university counseling professional ********** The higher education system in Taiwan has undergone major changes in the past two decades. The number of universities has tripled from slightly over 50 to nearly 170, and the enrollment has soared from 300,000 to more than 1.2 million (Ministry of Education [MOE], 2014). Across university campuses, student bodies are diverse and their problems are complicated (Hsu, 2005). University counseling centers (UCCs) fulfill the critical role in providing mental health services to their campus members. The existing literature in Taiwan mostly focused on the procedures, theories, and technical applications of counseling and/or psychotherapy treatment conducted by university counseling professionals (UCPs) (e.g., Chan & Yeh, 2012; Chang, Wang, Chen, & Yang, 2012; Hsu, 2014; Lin, 2005); it lacks a holistic, systematic, and comprehensive picture of UCCs. We reviewed the literature intensively and summarized in this article the significant themes on the development of university counseling profession in Taiwan. Our intention is four-sided: (1) to retrospect the past development of UCCs, (2) to reveal their current status, (3) to comprehend the challenges and obstacles they are facing, and (4) to elaborate their prospects. Historical evolution and current condition of university counseling profession In Taiwan, the counseling profession has been developed for six decades and has evolved from its infant stage to become a certified and licensed profession. Since The Education Conference of Executive Yuan in Taiwan set up counseling offices at schools in 1975, counseling professionals have assumed the major role on campus to facilitate mental health among students (Hong, 1990). In 1976, MOE stipulated the regulations requiring every higher education institution in Taiwan to establish its own UCC (Two, 1998). The Psychologist Act was adopted in Taiwan in 2001; as a result, the majority of the UCPs are now licensed counseling psychologists. Their job responsibilities include: (1) assess psychological function and development, (2) treat cognitive, emotional, and behavioral disorders, (3) treat disorders in social adaptation, and (4) provide psychological counseling and treatment for neurotic disorders (Taiwan Guidance and Counseling Association [TGCA], 2014).
