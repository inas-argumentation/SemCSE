Brief Report: Biochemical profile and ultrasound findings in irregularly menstruating females-The Indian Scenario
: Objective: Survey of postmenarcheal housewives presenting to the OPD with primary complaints of menstrual disorder for at least 3 months, classified as Oligomenorrhoea, polymenorhea, menorrhagia, metrorrhagia, to explore any possible relationship with biochemical findings and transabdominal pelvic ultrasound findings. Subjects and methods : 200 women aged 18-45 participated. Information collected and measurements performed included: a menstrual history, anthropometric measurements, clinical observation of acne and hirsutism, transabdominal pelvic ultrasound and biochemical analysis of a fasting blood sample. We evaluated the Ultrasound findings and looked for their correlation to menstrual irregularities and biochemical parameters Results: Oligomenorrhoea was the commonest menstrual cycle irregularity. Obesity and hirsutism was found to be the chief finding in 69% and 59% of the cases respectively. BMI in cases was significantly more than controls (p<0.05). Serum testosterone was significantly higher in cases compared to controls (p<0.0001). Cysts were the commonest findings on ultrasound. A positive correlation was obtained between cholesterol with age, weight and BMI and between weight and age in the entire group of cases. TSH levels in polymennorhea and metrorrhagia groups were lower than controls.
