Underrow Ripping and Irrigation Effects on Corn Yield
A 4-year study was conducted in Southeastern Virginia on a Norfolk loamy fine sand soil to evaluate the effect of underrow ripping and irrigation on corn yields. Corn yields for Pioneer 3369A variety in irrigated plots averaged 76% higher than in nonirrigated plots which averaged 6390 kg/ha (102 bu/a). Underrow ripping increased corn yields 15.1% (1240 kg/ha (19.8 bu/a)). Corn yields were increased considerably more by underrow ripping with no irrigation than underrow ripping with irrigation. An interactive effect on corn yield was observed between plant population and irrigation. Under water stress, corn planted at a higher population decreased in yield. Application of irrigation water was successfully scheduled using a water balance model.
