The Contribution of Overseas Savings to the Funded National Debt of Great Britain, 1750–1815
E xpensive wars occupied more than half the 65 years after 1750 and 2 increased the nominal British national debt 10-fold, so that by 1815 the debt was about twice the level of national income. Before 1800 there was no information available about the amount of debt held overseas although there was always considerable concern-rumours of a high proportion indicating some sort of danger to the country. About 50 years ago Wilson revived interest in the overseas debt.2 More recently, economic historians have been concerned at the extent to which domestic savings were diverted from productive investment to financing the wars; and consequently they have been interested in the extent to which savings from overseas reduced that diversion.3
