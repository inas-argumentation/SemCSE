The Hypertonicity-Induced Na+ Conductance of Rat Hepatocytes: Physiological Significance and Molecular Correlate
The initial event in the regulatory volume increase (RVI) of rat hepatocytes is an uptake of extracellular Na<sup>+</sup> that is then exchanged for K<sup>+</sup> via stimulation of Na<sup>+</sup>/K<sup>+</sup>-ATPase. While it was generally assumed that this Na<sup>+</sup> uptake is mediated by the activation of Na<sup>+</sup>/H<sup>+</sup> antiport and Na<sup>+</sup>-K<sup>+</sup>-2Cl<sup>-</sup> symport it could be shown recently that, in addition to these transporters, hypertonic stress also stimulates conductive Na<sup>+</sup> entry. In a quantitative study, it was found that the relative contribution of Na<sup>+</sup> conductance, Na<sup>+</sup>/H<sup>+</sup> antiport, and Na<sup>+</sup>-K<sup>+</sup>-2Cl<sup>-</sup> symport to the initial Na<sup>+</sup> import as well as to the RVI process (at 300 → 400 mosmol/l) is approximately 4 : 1 : 1. When the osmotic sensitivity of these Na<sup>+</sup> importers was tested (at 300 mosmol/l → 327, 360, 400, 450 mosmol/l) it became clear that Na<sup>+</sup> conductance is the prominent mechanism of RVI from 360 mosmol/l upwards whereas Na<sup>+</sup>/H<sup>+</sup> antiport is the most sensitive transporter with 65 % of its maximal activation at 327 mosmol/l already. Concerning the intracellular regulation of the Na<sup>+</sup> importers involved in RVI it was found that Na<sup>+</sup> concuctance as well as Na<sup>+</sup>-K<sup>+</sup>-2Cl<sup>-</sup> symport – but not Na<sup>+</sup>/H<sup>+</sup> antiport – are activated via PKC. With respect to the molecular correlate of the volume activated Na<sup>+</sup> conductance it could be shown that it exhibits a rather low affinity to amiloride (IC<sup>50</sup> = 6.0 μmol/l) and an overall sensitivity profile of EIPA > amiloride > benzamil = phenamil that, at first sight, would not speak in favor of a typical epithelial type of Na<sup>+</sup> channel (ENaC). Western-blot analysis and RT-PCR techniques, however, revealed that α-, β-, as well as γ-ENaC are, in fact, expressed in rat hepatocytes. Moreover, by use of an antisense-DNA based approach it could be shown that at least α-ENaC is part of the hypertonicity induced Na<sup>+</sup> conductance.
