Behaviours of Ore-forming Solutions in Seawater
If the "submarine exhalative" or "submarine volcano-sedimentary" hypothesis is really the case for the genesis of some strata-bound base metal and manganese ore deposits with the volcanic association (e. g. NIGGLI, 1925; SCHNEIDERHOHN, 1941 ; WATANABE, 1957), questions must arise as to the behaviours of the ascending ore-forming solution in seawater. A number of physical and chemical interactions will be involved in this ore solutionseawater system. However, apart from the chemical interactions, some of the most fundamental questions may be as follows. After its welling up onto the sea floor, does the ascending solution float up in the surrounding seawater, or rather form a stagnant pool on the sea floor ? Is there any limitation in mixing between the ore solution and seawater? If so, to what extent will the mixing proceed, and what influence will be expected in the resulting mineral deposits? These are the problems of which the author intends to give some clues in this article. The discussion is based on a very simple view that the behaviours of the ascending ore-forming solution and its derivatives in seawater are essentially determined by the density difference between them. Though many assumptions and approximations are introduced into the discussion, the results obtained seem to have some implications to the observed features in the "Kuroko type" strata-bound sulfide deposits.
