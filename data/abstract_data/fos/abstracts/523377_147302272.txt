Me and Borges
In Salt Lake City the writers come and go, and I have heard and met them, eaten and drunk with them, picnicked and played baseball with them, and served them as I could. A fan in the hot months, a real coffee cup, a trip up a canyon, a walk by a creek to see the ouzel in the dead of winter disappear below the ice and pop up again downstream singing, things that writers like to see. They are making a guest appearance, a summer conference, a oneor-two-day stand on the reading circuit for fame and profit. Sometimes they are looking for a job as we are looking for a writer, and the tensions are odd. A writer fully established may also be on the way down; a writer on the come may get a better offer at the next college. The business of getting a writer to come to a university is a serious recruiting game, a combination of bargaining, flattering, and nosing out the weak spots. The questions we ask are awful; we academics do not seem to know when to shut up. "Do you write during the day, or are you a night person?" Sometimes one department member is detailed to hustle off another particularly embarrassing one, keep him or her away for a while. The writers are not always that much better; they send us parody letters they would never send a publisher. A major figure from a standard anthology is he still alive? sends us endorsements from his old friends and adds that he really enjoys teaching Freshman English. Women writers with Black-Jewish-Native American ancestry list all the innovative yet traditional classes they can teach. Often we make hard offers to people who only want to pry a few thousand bucks
