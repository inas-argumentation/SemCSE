On Your Knees: Prayer in Undergraduate Romantic Relationship
Analysis of data from a 76 item survey on "Prayer and Romantic Involvement" completed by 286 respondents at a large southeastern university revealed that undergraduates who prayed more frequently were more likely to require that a person they dated not only would pray themselves but also pray with the respondent. Frequency of respondents' praying habits was also positively correlated with requiring that a future mate pray. In addition, those who prayed more often were more likely to have prayed to find a mate and prayed for help in resolving relationship problems with a mate. The theory of homogamy is suggested as an explanation for the findings. Implications include that prayer is an important value in the lives of these undergraduates and a discriminating variable influencing undergraduate dating and mate selection. This study examined the degree to which the frequency of prayer was a meaningful factor in romantic relationships, including requiring that a potential date and potential mate pray. Prayer has been the focus of considerable research including the relationship of prayer to psychological well being (Leondari and Gialamas, 2009), its use in marital therapy (Sullivan and Karney, 2008), and the personality profiles/demographic factors of persons most likely to pray (Francis and Robbins, 2008; Baker, 2008). In one of the few studies on prayer as it relates to one's romantic partner, Fincham et al. (2008) found in their longitudinal study that prayer for one's partner had a positive effect on relationship satisfaction by influencing one's commitment to the relationship and the future with the partner. Previous social scientific research on prayer has identified the relationship between prayer and demographics. For example, the Baylor Religion Survey, a national random sample of U.S. adults (N=1,721) found that women, African-Americans, and those with lower incomes prayed more often than males, whites, and those with higher incomes. Definition of Prayer and Research Questions While definitions of prayer vary, the authors regarded prayer as an exercise or ritual designed to communicate with the supernatural for the purpose of asking for divine intervention in one's situation. The two research questions driving this research were: 1. What is the "prayer profile" of undergraduates and 2. Does the frequency of prayer determine whether a person prays for a mate, insist that their partner pray, or end a relationship if a partner does not pray? Sample and "Prayer Profile" Data for this study came from a sample of 286 undergraduate volunteers at a large southeastern university who completed a 77 item questionnaire (approved by the Institutional Review Board) on "Prayer among Undergraduates." Respondents completed the questionnaire over the Internet. Demographic characteristics of the sample follow: Prayer Profile-Over three fourths (75.5%) reported that they prayed--35.3% prayed between 2 and 6 times a week; 21.7% prayed every day. About a quarter (24.5%) reported that they never prayed. Almost sixty percent (59.5%) believed that people who pray are happier. A prerequisite for dating or marrying someone is that they must pray "some of the time"--45.8% and 61.4%.respectively. Over half (52.1%) would end a future relationship if the partner was not supportive of their praying.
