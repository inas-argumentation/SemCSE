The Corporate State, Economic Performance, and Social Policy
In My Land is Dying, Harry M. Caudill made a poignant and powerful assault on strip mining and the wastelands it creates. "The society is still essentially feudal, still fundamentally composed of barons and serfs. The distinction is one of power. The industrialists-that is the destroyers and polluters-are the barons. The serfs are the millions of one vote citizens whose taxes subsidize and support the system." 1 These words are chosen to open this essay because feudalism offers an interesting and instructive "model" for understanding the economic society in which we now live. Our world is dominated by giant corporations which, in their shifting alliances and struggles for power, behave very much like the great baronies of the feudal past. The corporation, bolstered and only rarely hindered in its pursuit of power and profit by the state, has latched firmly onto the levers of control in the contemporary market economy. To some degree power may be shared with lesser groupings, such as trade unions, the universities, or the scientific and technical "estate," but such sharing is often more illusory than real.
