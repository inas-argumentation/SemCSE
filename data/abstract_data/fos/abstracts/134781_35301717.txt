Goal Behavior of Adolescent Addicts and Delinquent Non-Addicted Peers
Wikler ( 4 ) conceptualized the state of addiction as fulfilling a need for continuous activicy toward the unattainable but pressing goals of achievement and prestige enhancement, thereby underscoring the importance of understanding the addict's goal behavior. Deriving from this premise, the focus of interest of this study was to ascertain whether hospitalized addicts make u p a homogeneous group, irrespective of sex and ethnicity, in terms of their ability to judge critically their own performance and correct for discrepancies between an objective standard and their ability to attain it in accordance with effective ego functioning. Thc Cassel Group Level of Aspiration Test ( 1 ) was selected to tap goal behavior because it presents a clearly structured task. Ss were 7 8 newly admitted adolescent heroin addicts at Riverside Hospital upon completion of their withdrawal regimen. Six subgroups were identified on the basis of sex and ethniciry (Negro, Puerto Rican, and Caucasian). As a partial control, data were obtained from 50 male non-addicted delinquents from Riker's Island Penitentiary identified on the basis of ethnicity. The mean age for both groups was 19, with a range of 17 to 22. Ss had a minimum of eighth grade education, belonged to Hollingshead's ( 2 ) class IV or V with regard to social position and had Beta IQs ranging from 8 0 to 115. Goal behavior was evaluated in terms of goal-striving and goal-setting components ( 3 ) . Goal discrepancy scores were assumed to be a measure of goal-setting, and attainment discrepancy scores, a measure of goal-striving. Using a 2 X 3 factorial design for analyzing the goal-discrepancy and goal-striving scores of the addict subgroups, there were no significant sex nor ethnic differences. Comparison of the male non-addicted, incarcerated group with the male hospital, addicted group also yielded no significant differences for either measure. T o the extent that unrealistic goal behavior contributes to experiences of failure and to a diversity of behavioral consequences linked to the private implications of unsuccessful attempts at achievement, these groups could not be differentiated.
