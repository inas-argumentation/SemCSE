Duloxetine for pain symptoms in patients with major depressive disorder
Introduction: Duloxetine, a new serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor, is being promoted as an effective treatment for pain associated with major depressive disorder (MDD). The first randomized, controlled trial to specifically address this issue as the primary endpoint was published recently. Patients with MDD often present to their primary care physician with physical (including pain) rather than emotional symptoms. The physical symptoms associated with MDD have been correlated with greater functional impairment, increased healthcare utilization, and poorer response to depression treatment. Pain symptoms are among the most common physical symptoms in patients with MDD and include the head, neck, back, abdomen, joints, etc. In previous studies, duloxetine seemed to reduce pain levels in patients with MDD. However, these patients were not selected on the basis of presence or severity of pain, and reduction of pain was not the primary endpoint for treatment.
