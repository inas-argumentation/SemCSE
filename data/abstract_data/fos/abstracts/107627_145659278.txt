The University Department: Retrospect and Prospect
The academic department in the modern university is, like the university itself, the result of the interaction of many forces. And, although the department in something approximating its present form has existed only since about I900, it has become a potent force, both in determining the stature of the university and in hampering the attempts of the university to improve its effectiveness and adapt to changing social and economic requirements. Ever since departmentalization appeared, inquirers have looked for the reasons behind it. Knowledge of the historical development and of underlying factors does not resolve today's problems, but it does provide some perspective for understanding them. Furthermore, a mythology of departments has arisen which, to some professors, makes any questioning of either the autonomy or the necessity of departments an expression of doubt about the worth of organized knowledge, the significance of academic freedom, and the importance of maintaining standards in higher education.
