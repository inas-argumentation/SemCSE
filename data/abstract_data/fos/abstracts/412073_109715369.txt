Forest Prime Mover Dynamic Simulation Model Using Partitioned Screw Matrices
Atracked forest prime mover simulation model was developed to assist in the evaluation of prime mover's capabilities to handle a large, high rotational energy forest and residue reduction head currently under development by the US Forest Service Equipment Development Center in San Dimas, California. The reduction head weighs approximately 400 kg and stores 20 X 104 joules of rotational energy. Power consumption has been estimated at 245 kW/m of head width. It has been shown through use of the model that only vehicles of the very large horsepower class can safely handle this reduction head. A unique mathematical technique was used in the dynamic analysis leading to the development of this model. Partitioned screw matrices, specifically the use of a 6 X 6 partitioned screw inertia/mass matrix, were developed by Nicodemus (1979), for use with classical formulations of inertia force and dynamic equilibrium equations for simplicity, completeness, and generality, and were employed in this model. One problem associated with large mobile equipment systems revolves around the use of existing prime movers versus development of new prime movers to specifically handle new tools.
