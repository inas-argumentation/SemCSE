Central Bank Austerity Policy, Zero-Inflation targets and Productivity Growth in Canada
Should central banks fight inflation? The Bank of Canada surely believes so. While high levels of employment and economic growth are said to be the ultimate objectives, both the Canadian government and the Bank of Canada believe that "the best contribution that monetary policy can make to these objectives is to preserve confidence in the value of money by achieving and maintaining price stability" [Bank of Canada 1994, 43].1 For this reason, the Bank of Canada and the Department of Finance, under a Liberal Party government, agreed in a December 1993 joint statement to extend inflation targets, setting the 1995-1998 range between 1 and 3 percent, precisely the target that had been set for 1995 four years before.2 No other target is set in this joint statement. In addition, there is not even a passing reference to unemployment in all of the newly issued policy report of the Bank [Bank of Canada 1995c]. There is thus no doubt that inflation control is both the means and the ends for the central bank of Canada.
