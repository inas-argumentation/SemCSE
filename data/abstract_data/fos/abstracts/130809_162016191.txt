These include Juan Maldonado’s chronicle of the comunero uprising, De motu Hispaniae (ca. 1524), Alfonso de Valdés’s Diálogo de Mercurio y Caronte (1529), and Lazarillo de Tormes (1554). The chapter on Maldonado is quite interesting in elucidating this little-known work, although Pérez-Romero is often forced to read beneath what he sees as a superficial adherence to the official condemnation of the rebellion, to a secret sympathy as suggested by an emphasis on the protagonism of the urban and rural poor, who refused to be coopted by the nobles and merchants who repeatedly tried to redirect the uprising to their own advantage. Valdés is read as the most radical statement of an Erasmian-derived social theory, while Lazarillo is examined for its biting satire of the way the poor were treated in Spain. Like the Celestina chapter, these final two sections incorporate a good deal of summarizing to reach conclusions that are not radically new. Rather strangely, throughout his book the author ignores the most commonly examined element of social conflict in Renaissance Spain, the religious or “caste” conflict that followed the collapse of medieval convivencia. Rojas was most definitely a converso (a descendant of Jews who had converted to Christianity), Valdés was probably one too, and possibly Flores as well; furthermore, the period the author discusses included the founding of the Inquisition, the expulsion of Jews from Spain, and the conquest of Granada and forced conversion of its Muslims to Christianity, but Pérez-Romero chooses to overlook these issues. From his liberationist perspective, the author also dismisses all criticism that, as he sees it, “deny the social reality of literature . . . [and] support official ideology and institutions” (202). His most extreme statement of his position is in the first two chapters, in which he grounds his approach in the social writings of Noam Chomsky and a rather one-sided reading of Bakhtin’s book on Rabelais, while being quite dismissive on ideological grounds of many scholars with whose emphases or interpretations he disagrees. The extreme polemics of the initial chapters (and of comments throughout the book) are, in this reviewer’s view, quite unfortunate, for they suggest a refusal to engage in scholarly dialogue. The author is in fact scrupulous in citing and summarizing prior scholarship, and thus the acerbic remarks merely put off the unconvinced reader. IGNACIO NAVARRETE University of California, Berkeley
