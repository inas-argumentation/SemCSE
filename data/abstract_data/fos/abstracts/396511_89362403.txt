201: Interleukin-11 is the dominant IL-6 family cytokine during gastrointestinal tumourigenesis
The concept of combating tumour progression through pharmacological inhibition of growth promoting cytokines within the tumour micro-environment is emerging as a therapeutic opportunity that bypasses the difficulties of targeting intracellular signaling components, including transcription factors. To date, much focus has been placed on antagonizing Interleukin (IL)-6, with clinical trials for ovarian, renal, prostate, and breast cancers underway. While IL-6 is one of the most well recognized intrinsic connections between inflammation and cancer, surprisingly little is known about the role of the closely related cytokine, IL-11. Both IL-6 and IL-11 form a multimeric GP130/JAK signaling complex to activate the Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription (STAT)-3 pathway. Persistent STAT3 activation is a recurrent feature of many metastatic human cancers and is associated with poor prognosis. In a panel of human colorectal cancer biopsies, we have shown that IL-11 expression has a stronger correlation with elevated epithelial STAT3 activation than IL-6. We have genetically manipulated STAT3 signaling in a series of mouse models to demonstrate that IL-11 is a critical regulator of the network of inflammatory cells present in the tumour microenvironment, and ultimately provides survival signals for tumour cells. Using genetic inhibition of IL-6 and IL-11 signaling in mouse models, we reveal that IL-11 has a dominant role during the progression of sporadic and inflammation-associated colon and gastric cancers. Accordingly, we show that in these cancers, as well as in human tumour cell line xenografts, pharmacological inhibition of IL-11 signaling alleviated STAT3 activation, suppressed tumour cell proliferation and reduced the invasive capacity and growth of tumours. Our results introduce a new cytokine targeted therapy, and suggest that in many disease models that were previously considered to be IL-6 centric, the role of IL-11 should be carefully examined.
