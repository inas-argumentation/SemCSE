Clinically used PET (polyethylene-terephtalate) vascular prostheses (VUP Joint-Stock, Brno, Czech Republic) of 6 mm inner diameter were modified with fibrin and fibronectin assemblies deposited by layer-by-layer technique. Endothelial cells were harvested from inner jugular vein, cultured in vitro and seeded onto the lumen before implantation. Unseeded control graft was implanted in contralateral limb. Bypass graft explants were observed in confocal microscope. Results: Five animals were implanted for evaluation of bypass setting without cell seeding. Domestic pigs showed undesirable weight gain while performing cell culture. Five seeding procedures succeeded and one cell culture failure occurred. The seeding density achieved on vascular prostheses was 60±40 cells per cm. Mean period of doublestage seeding procedure was 50±16 days. Both seeded and control grafts occluded in two of the five seeding procedures; one graft thrombosed due to poor outflow tract and one seeded graft remained patent 22 days while control grafts got occluded. Conclusions: The most feasible model seems to be the bilateral aortointernal iliac bypass graft with external iliac ligature performed in a minipig. Pre-coating of fibrin/fibronectin does help with autologous cell seeding and culturing in the lumen of PET vascular prosthesis which may also enhance the bypass graft patency.
