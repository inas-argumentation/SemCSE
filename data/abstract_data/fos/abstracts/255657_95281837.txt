Modelling of polymer batteries
Abstract We analyze theoretical performance limits of secondary polymer batteries based on electroactive polymer electrodes and solid or liquid organic electrolytes. A simple expression is given relating the energy per weight and volume density to properties of the materials used. The analysis reveals that it should be possible to construct polymer batteries utilizing polymer electrode and electrolyte with higher energy density than present day Ni/Cd batteries. We also conclude that development of lithium intercalating polymer electrodes for thin film polymer batteries could be an attractive route if sufficiently high intercalation potentials can be found. This requires a small band gap polymer material.
