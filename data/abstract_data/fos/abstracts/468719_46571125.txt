A new science of health: salutology and the evolutionary perspective.
Traditional medical science is primarily concerned with pathological processes; with understanding disease, illness and sickness; and the causes and management of dysfunction. The starting point is a healthy organism, and medicine seeks to explicate departures from this state of harmony. Thus disease is usually conceptualized and classified in terms of the causes of pathological process—neoplasia, infection, trauma and so on. Yet, while this has proved a valid and fruitful viewpoint, the pathological perspective misrepresents the human situation in important respects. To the eye of an evolutionary biologist it is health, not disease, which requires explanation. The arrangements of matter associated with disease are much more probable than those associated with health— there are more ways of being dead than alive. Life and health require a high degree of adaptation and organization, and a constant input of energy. Health represents a triumph against the odds. The human organism is formed and develops by complex and fallible processes through a sequence of embryonic and fetal stages to a slowly maturing existence of relative autonomy and self-regulated homoeostasis, all the while being assaulted by endemic hazards, only to undergo eventual senescence, death and disintegration.
