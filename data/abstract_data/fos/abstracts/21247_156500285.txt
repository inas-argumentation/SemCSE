Effects of social capital on the demand for higher education
This paper is based on a survey of senior secondary school students,10909 in three provinces and one city in the Mainland and 3157 in Hong Kong,and analyzes the effects of social capital on the demand for higher education.Results show that student participation in social group activities and family education expectation have significant effects on the demand for higher education among students in both Mainland China and Hong Kong.Number of siblings,family tutoring,and parent-child communication have positive effects among students in the Mainland.The breadth of family social network and teacher-student communication are significant factors among students in Hong Kong.However,some factors that are generally expected to have positive effects are found to be statistically insignificant in this study.These include family social network and parent-school communication.
