As interesting as these details frequently turn out to be under Otten's reading, the readings themselves cannot allay the book's oft-revealed and unnecessary anxiety about tracing the history of their production. One would suppose that the historicizing materialism to which Otten pledges allegiance in the later chapters of his book would be especially vigilant to the fact that every deployment of the term "matter" or "the body" is a stroke against the kaleidoscope particularity of objects and their histories, devouring the incommensurate into the generic. "Matter" is rooted in the systematicity that Otten's materialism seeks, initially, to avoid. When it escapes a tight orbit around these powerful and obliterating terms, however, A Superficial Reading of Henry James is a refreshing voyage past the "center of consciousness" and toward the outskirts.
