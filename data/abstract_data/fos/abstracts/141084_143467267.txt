Governing the use of force: Does the UN charter matter anymore?
Over the past 50 years many have questioned the influence of UN Charter norms as an authoritative factor in constraining the resort to force. The political landscape post‐World War II has never mirrored the assumptions underlying the Charter paradigm. Yet, most analysts have still utilized a ‘top‐down’ approach beginning with the assumption that Charter prohibitions represent unquestioned standards. They begin with the applicable rule(s) then determine the extent of compliance/deviance. In contrast the ‘bottom‐up’ approach, based upon the methodology of customary law, attempts to infer standards from behavior. An examination of post‐World War II conflicts from the ‘bottom up’ perspective indicates only one clear rule: the use of force for pure conquest or subjugation is no longer permissible. Apart from this, the evidence indicates the international community will tolerate the unilateral resort to force for a variety of purposes when no other remedies exist
