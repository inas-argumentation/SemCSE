Divergence and Convergence of American-Soviet Interests in the Middle East: An Israeli Viewpoint
The Middle East has been an area of big power contests from time immemorial. Its written history and archeological discoveries bear ample witness to the wars between the empires of the North and the South. The prophets of Israel were not only imbued with ethical pathos, but no less with political wisdom. They exhorted the rulers and the nation to beware of big power entanglement. "Out of the North evil shall break forth upon all the inhabitants of the land," predicted Jeremiah (Jer. 1:14) in a warning still worth heeding. The contemporary geostrategic facts in the Middle East are not much different. Israel continues to lie on that narrow strip of land along the eastern shore of the Mediterranean that had always been the only landbridge between Asia and Africa, at times facilitating the peaceful flow of people, commerce, and ideas, and at other times serving as a battleground for contending armies. Egypt continues to live by the great river, the lifeline of the country's multimillenarian civilization. Ancient Babylon and Persia, both superpowers in their time, fought over the control of the Euphrates valley, as do Iraq and Iran today. Gone are the great empires of antiquity. Disintegration befell the Ottoman regime. The rule of France and Britain, its successor in the Middle East, was short-lived. But longlasting are the region's endemic rivalries.
