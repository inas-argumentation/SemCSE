In vitro allergy testing: Quality control and result reporting
i n 1966, the Ishizakas first established that a new class of immunoglobulins, immunoglobulin E (IgE), was responsible for the reaginic or skin-sensitizing properties of the serum from allergic individuals. 9 The almost simultaneous discovery of the first IgE myeloma made it possible to produce antihuman IgE antibodies. Anti-IgE antibodies, in turn, lead to assays for both the total quantity of IgE and for the quantity of IgE directed toward specific allergens. Subsequent studies have firmly established the role of IgE antibodies in the pathogenesis of allergic disease. 7 There is a close association between the atopic diathesis or genetic predisposition toward allergic disease and the tendency to produce IgE antibodies when exposed to antigens. Thus allergic individuals usually have increased total serum IgE concentrations and detectable quantifies of IgE specific for common allergens in their serum. 2,s,7
