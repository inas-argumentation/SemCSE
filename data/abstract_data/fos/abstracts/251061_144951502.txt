Test Performance and the Use of Optional Questions.
DuCette and Wolk reported a research which “clearly indicated that giving options in an essay examination … produces poorer test performance.” The contention of this paper is that the obtained difference in that study was an artifact of the scoring procedures. This is demonstrated by a study of percentage scores of a group of thirty-four students when their examinations were scored by the percentage score on 1.) all six answers, 2.) “opted” four answers, 3.) best four answers. The mean scores increased significantly at each step from method 1 through method 3, indicating that the significant differences in DuCette and Wolk’s study were due to scoring the non-option group on their best four answers.
