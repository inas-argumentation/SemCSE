In comparison to this examination, Railey's treatment of The Reivers as a virtual fairy tale born of the utopian longings of a natural aristocrat seems rather slight. Still, by this point in the book, Railey has convincingly stated his case. In general, critics who perform ideological analysis in Faulkner's studies play the same role that Railey identifies for Mink Snopes and Linda Snopes Kohl at the end of The Town—that is, pointing to alternative visions likely to be resisted by the established order. There is, after all, a pervasive concern that emphasis on ideology invariably detracts from understanding and appreciation of Faulkner. On the contrary, as Railey demonstrates in this significant study, defining the limitations of an artist is an important step toward recognizing the mighty resistance to larger forces so instrumental in producing great artists and artistic achievements.
