The Regents College Teaching Fellowships: New York State's Program for Increasing the Supply of Competent College Teachers
SECOND in the nation, both in the number of students enrolled in institutions of higher education and in the number of residents attending institutions of higher education, New York State has long been aware of the importance of qualified college teachers. In I958, a program of Regents College Teaching Fellowships was inaugurated as one approach to the problem of increasing the supply of competent faculty members. Recently, the program has been formally evaluated. Since New York's policy of awarding fellowships to prospective college teachers is the only one of its kind in the country, the state's experience with the program may be of considerable value to others interested in the role of state government in raising, or at least maintaining, the quality of college faculties. Several developments in recent years indicate the need for increased numbers of qualified college teachers. The most obvious of these is the progression of the large postwar generation from high school to college. In addition, advances in knowledge and the growing sophistication of American technology make higher education a necessity for increasing numbers of today's young people. In planning for the needs of higher education, then, a state must consider not only the growing numbers of young people of college age but also the increasing rate of college enrollment. These two developments alone would justify a state's concern for maintaining the quality of college faculties in the face of increasing demands from its citizens for higher education. Two other considerations, however, affect the state. First, the availability of high-quality institutions of higher education is of increasing importance to many types of industry. In order to obtain contracts for highly specialized products, industries need the resources of academic knowledge which the universities supply.
