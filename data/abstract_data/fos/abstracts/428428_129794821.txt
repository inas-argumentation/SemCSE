Les silicates de sodium du manga (Niger), Marqueurs de paleoenvironnement?
Sodium silicates and chert of similar appearance are often considered as environmental indicators: saline playas with shallow ground-water tables for magadiite, kanemite and makatite formation; post-evaporite environment for kenyaite and chert by leaching magadiite with fresh meteoric water. Sodium silicate nodules were sampled in the Manga are (eastern Niger) in interdunal depressions with variable ground-water table levels. The age of these silicate concretions is related to estimates of the leaching values. In the N'Guigmi depression near Lake Chad, with shallow ground water, the recent concretions are constituted only of magadiite included in organic-rich sediments. In numerous depressions of the Gazourmadje area, more distant from Lake Chad, the groundwater table is deeper (about 30 m). There, many sodium silicate concretions are included in white diatomite. Their constituents are magadiite and kenyaite together in the core and only magadiite in the cortex. This mineral distribution between core and cortex implies that kenyaite is not a secondary product but an authigenic mineral like magadiite. The leaching of nodules determines a decrease of sodium content in magadiite structure. The sodium leaching may even be total and then crystallisation of silicic acid occurs (acid often named H magadiite or silhydrite). The sodium release is strongly increased by organic matter. Sodium silicates appear to be useful indicators of ancient evaporitic environments, especially because of the disappearance of other sodium carbonate paragenetic minerals. In Manga, sodium silicates are too stable to give more information on post-evaporite evolution.
