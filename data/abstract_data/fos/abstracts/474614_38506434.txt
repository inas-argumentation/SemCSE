Phenomenological aspects of the alcoholic "blackout".
Detailed information about "blackouts" (amnesia for events occurring during a drinking episode) was obtained from sixty-four alcoholics by use of a structured interview. Two forms of memory loss seemed identifiable. One type of amnesia had a sharp onset and was seldom followed by recovery of memory. The other type lacked a definite onset and the person was unaware of having forgotten an event until reminded of it or recalling it, usually dimly, at a later time; a certain degree of recall was usually possible, and some subjects reported that subsequent drinking seemed to facilitate recall. It was hypothesized that the latter, "fragmentary" memory loss may be a "state-dependent" effect of alcohol, whereas the more discrete en bloc blackout may originate from a physiological disturbance in the brain for which alcohol may be a necessary but not a sufficient cause.
