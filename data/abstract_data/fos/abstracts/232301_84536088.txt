Microbial production and downstream processing of 2,3-butanediol
Abstract In the direct conversions of starch by Bacillus polymyxa a maximum of 38 g l−1 2,3-butanediol is produced with a yield of 0.28 diol per starch (g g−1). By preliminary saccharification of starch and then cultivation with Klebsiella oxytoca, a 2,3-butanediol concentration of 99–100 g l−1 is achieved with a yield of 0.5 diol per starch (g g−1). Klebsiella oxytoca converts high-test molasses to 2,3-butanediol in the same concentration and yield. The same diol concentration only at lower productivity can also be achieved by conversions of black strap molasses, provided it contains less than 2% salts. 2,3-Butanediol can be separated from bioprocess media with very good results by salting out using water-free potassium carbonate. After precleaning the medium, from molasses or saccharified starch conversion process, it was possible to separate 94–96% (w w−1) of the 2,3-butanediol present using 53–56% weight potassium carbonate. The concentration of the 2,3-butanediol in the diol phase resulting was in this case 97% weight. Salting out can be used to separate other diols also produced using microbiological methods.
