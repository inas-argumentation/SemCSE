Of Wonders and Wise Men: Religion and Popular Cultures in Southeast Mexico, 1800-1876 (review)
This book will certainly be a valuable teaching tool because it allows students to analyze and interpret primary sources; however, it has a few shortcomings. Feldman's attempt to locate and re-identify the mysterious and elusive Xocmo people is incomplete and at one point he contradicts himself in his assessment of them. In addition, some analysis via the endnotes would have been helpful to clarify Spanish misconceptions and mytho-history, such as the claims that K'ichee' sacrificed and ate their Kaqchikel captives. In fact, Feldman notes that an earlier reader had questioned Tovilla's historical veracity in the margins of the text, but does not expand on this himself. Despite these minor lapses, Feldman provides us a rare opportunity to explore relations between the Spanish invaders and Manche. What emerges is that there were no real winners in these struggles.
