Relationship between Firearm Availability and Primary and Secondary Murder
Lester (1988) has shown that the regional variation in firearm availability in the states of the USA is related to the use of firearms in homicide and to the over-d homicide rate. Smith and Parker (1980) used FBI data to calculate separate rates of primary murder (involving family and friends as victims) and secondary murder (involving strangers as victims) for each state of the USA in 1973. The present note explored the association between availability of firearms (as measured by the percentage of suicides using fitearms and the accidental death rate from firearms in each state) in 1970 (the closest census year) and the rates of primary and secondary murder calculated by Smith and Parker for the 48 continental states of the USA. The percentage of suicides using firearms was positively associated with the rate of primary murder (Pearson r = 0.49, two-tailed p < .001) but not of secondary murder (r = -0.01). Similarly, the accidental death rate from firearms was positively associated with the rate of primary murder (r= 0.39, p < .01) but not of secondary murder (r = -0.07). Availability of firearms appeared in this study to be associated with the rate of primary murder in the states of the USA but not with the rate of secondary murder, perhaps because the presence of a firearm has more impact on impulsive assaultive behavior.
