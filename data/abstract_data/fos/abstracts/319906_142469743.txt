While all the essays detail the impact of state policies, whether intended or not, on gender relations and rights, demonstrating the influence of gender on the state is much more difficult. Karin Rosemblatt argues successfully that the class compromise underpinning the Popular Front government in Chile of the 1930s and 1940s relied on the cooperation of working men who recognized that disciplining male behavior (for example, through marriage and antialcohol campaigns) could buttress class solidarity even as it benefited national economic progress. Collectively, these essays challenge future researchers to analyze more thoroughly the ways that gender is not only affected by but also contributes to the process of state formation.
