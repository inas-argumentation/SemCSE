TU‐G‐214‐02: Imaging the Biological Effect of Dose in the Liver Normal Tissue
The potential clinical value of in vivo functional and physiological imaging for assessment of dose response of the liver parenchyma has been explored in recent years. Conventional NTCP models are based upon physics parameters (dose, volume). While providing guidance for treatment planning and permitting safe delivery of higher doses than having previously been possible, they do not account for the broad range of individual patient biological radiosensitivity. Early monitoring of the biological effect of dose in the liver normal tissue via functional and physiological imaging can potentially select patients with pre‐clinical signs of hepatic function changes prior to the onset of symptomatic radiation‐induced injury. In addition, mapping regional distributions of hepatic function and its response to dose would permit re‐arrangement of the dose distribution to preserve functional subunits in the liver. Care must be taken, however, to understand the limits of these imaging techniques, including reproducibility, and to validate the biomarker with adequate endpoints. This lecture will provide an overview on functional imaging for developing a dose‐response model of the liver for individualized therapy. The limitations of these techniques will also be discussed. Learning objectives: 1. Understand functional imaging as a biomarker for normal tissue response in the liver; 2. Understand the influence of imaging parameters and image quality on quantitative metrics and uncertainty in the dose‐response model of the liver. 3. Understand clinical applications and limitations.
