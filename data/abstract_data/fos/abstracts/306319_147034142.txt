The Scientific Orientation of Engineering Education: A Broader and Deeper Training for Engineers
IN THE years since the Second World War, scientific and technical advances have brought about a fundamental change in the nature of engineering. The phenomenal rise of such new fields as automation, nucleonics, and electronics has greatly increased the demands upon the engineer's ability to assimilate and apply new concepts at extremely sophisticated levels. As a result of this, the basic principles of engineering education have been undergoing a searching re-evaluation. In its early days, engineering was considered to be an art rather than an exact science, and an engineer depended more upon empirical observation and rule of thumb than upon any scientific body of knowledge.
