Immunofluorescent patterns and specificity of human antinuclear antibodies.
Immunofluorescent antinuclear antibody tests were performed on selected sera using leucocytes and rat liver sections, and the patterns of staining compared. Antibody to DNA was detected by immunodiffusion, complement fixation and radioimmunoelectrophoresis. Specificity of reactions was determined by digestion of nuclear substrate with DNAse, RNAse and trypsin. 
 
Nuclear staining patterns were correlated with the presence or absence of antibody to DNA, clinical diagnosis, immunoglobulin class and titre of antinuclear antibody and the source of nuclear antigen used. 
 
Bright, shaggy fluorescence tended to occur most frequently in sera from patients with systemic lupus erythematosus containing antibodies to DNA, but was also observed in from one-fourth to one-third of sera when neither lupus nor antibody to DNA was present. No pattern of fluorescence was limited to any immunoglobulin class. Dilution of sera tended to alter the pattern observed in immunofluorescence tests, as did selection of nuclear substrate and qualitative factors including error. Sera with antibody to DNA were obtained from patients with rheumatoid arthritis, autoimmune haemolytic anaemia, chronic active hepatitis and most often, systemic lupus. Specific patterns of fluorescence did not correlate well with clinical diagnosis, immunoglobulin classes or specificity for nuclear antigens, although previously noted trends were confirmed. Moreover, results obtained in one test system correlated only moderately well with those found in another. It is suggested that the significance formerly ascribed to specific patterns of nuclear fluorescence is limited by technical variables and that morphological descriptions may not adequately indicate specificity for certain nuclear reactants.
