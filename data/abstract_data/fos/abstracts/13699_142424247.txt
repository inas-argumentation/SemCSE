Female Veteran Counselors Regarding Student Characteristics and Resultant Service Strategies in Taiwan.
The purpose of this study was to describe perceptions of female veteran counselors regarding student characteristics and resultant strategies for counseling services at three science/engineering research-oriented universities in northern Taiwan. This study adopted a qualitative method of phenomenology utilizing in-depth interviews for data collection. The twelve participants' ages ranged from 30 to 45 years old with an average age of 38.85. The themes that emerged from data analysis included participant perceptions of university and campus culture, student characteristics and major types of problems, and the focus of counseling treatment and service strategies. Counselors highlight diverse and multi-channelled counseling services as key to ever-changing students, and emphasize counseling services to match characteristics, leverage strengths, and complement weaknesses of students. In addition, counselors emphasize the importance of advocacy of counseling services and the establishment of a professional image and reputation for university counseling centers. Key words: student characteristics, university counseling center, counseling services, female veteran counselor ********** The number of students in higher education in Taiwan has increased from 300,000 to nearly 1.2 million in two decades (Ministry of Education, 2011). As the number of students on campus grows rapidly, counselors face radical challenges in the diversity of students and the complexity of their problems (Hsu, 2005). University counselors, responsible for mental health on campus, engage in close interactions with students. Their perceptions of the characteristics, lifestyles, problems, behavioral patterns, and subculture features of the student population have a strong bearing on the counseling theories, conceptions, and strategies adopted, as well as on the content and delivery methods of counseling services offered. Previous research has typically focused on client perceptions of counselor techniques (e.g., McNeill, May, & Lee, 1987), the counseling process (e.g., Kim, Ng, & Ahn, 2005), the treatment effects (e.g., Lin, 2001a), or a special type of counseling approach (e.g., Reese, Conoley, & Brossart, 2006), rather than counselor perceptions of client characteristics and the counseling services afforded. Counselor perceptions of client characteristics and/or student traits strongly affect the attitudes, thoughts, strategies, and skills adopted to deal with client problems and/or student needs. The present study describes the perceptions of Taiwanese veteran counselors regarding characteristics of the student body, and presents strategies counselors adopt to provide services tailored to student needs. This study investigates two questions: What are the characteristics of students that counselors perceive? How can counselors employ counseling strategies and skills tailored to the perceived characteristics of students? Literature review is delineated below. Characteristics and Problem Types of Undergraduate and Graduate Students in Taiwan Common problems Taiwanese university students share include emotional distress, career prospects, curriculum and learning adjustment, and attitudes toward life and learning (Huang, 1999). The adjustment problems of Taiwanese university students involve health, emotions, social interaction, family, extracurricular activities, and career planning (Hsieh & Hwang, 1998). Stressors for Taiwanese students include academic study, career or life goals, and interpersonal relationships (Wang, 2007). The most urgent needs of Taiwanese university students involve career planning, emotion management, self-exploration, and interpersonal communication (Fan, Lee, & Pan, 2001). In terms of graduate student problems and needs, academic stress is viewed as the top stressor among graduate students in Taiwan (Aiou, 2005; Huang, 2006).
