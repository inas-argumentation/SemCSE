An Empirical Method of Forecasting Radiation Fog and Clear-Sky Minimum Temperatures at Vancouver, British Columbia
Cases of night-time cooling under “clear sky” conditions are examined in particular at a seacoast location during the season of radiation fog. A prediction system is developed and incorporated into a working chart which has a twofold purpose as follows: to give the forecaster objective estimates of (a) the minimum temperature, (b) the mean pattern of temperature fall, and (c) the earliest and latest times of fog occurrence; to provide, by its use under all “clear sky” conditions, records of the expected and actual cooling curves at night along with hourly visibility, cloud cover and surface wind, in order that (a) the system itself may be further refined, and (b) the forecaster may have an hourly history of conditions on previous critical nights. The approach is completely empirical and is based primarily on the sunset values, either forecast or actual, of both temperature and dewpoint.
