Suppression of printout effect in photographic phase holograms.
It is now well established that phase holograms can yield much higher diffraction efficiencies than amplitude holograms. Several authors have also described techniques for producing phase holograms using fine-grain photographic materials. The procedure followed most often has been to bleach the silver image chemically, resulting in the conversion in situ of the grains of metallic silver into a transparent silver salt of high refractive index, usually a silver halide. An alternative procedure is to oxidize and dissolve away the silver image, leaving a phase image made up of the remaining, unexposed silver halide grains. While a surface relief image is also formed, due to local tanning of the emulsion layer during processing and changes in emulsion thickness, this is effective only at low spatial frequencies, and the reconstruction is mainly due to the variations in refractive index arising from the bleached grains. A major inconvenience with such phase holograms has been their tendency to darken or discolor when exposed to ambient light, because of the formation of printout silver. It has been shown that the stability against printout is essentially determined by the silver halide of which the image is formed, AgCl being the poorest in this respect, while AgBr is considerably better and Agl exhibits a very high degree of resistance. One technique for producing images consisting of Agl is to use a bleach bath containing KI. Alternatively, it is possible to convert an image consisting of AgCl, or AgBr, to Agl by soaking it in a solution of KI. The latter procedure is particularly useful in processes in which the phase image is formed from the original silver halide grains. However, while the techniques described in the literature work well with some emulsions, disappointing results have been reported with other ultra-fme-grain emulsions such as Kodak 649F. Poor results were also obtained by the authors with both techniques in preliminary experiments with Kodak 649F plates.
