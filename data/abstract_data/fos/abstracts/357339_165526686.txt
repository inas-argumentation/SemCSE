Surviving manuscripts of musical compositions must also be considered exceptional in terms of the perceived value that kept them preserved: from collectors they went to well-endowed libraries in London, Cambridge, Oxford, and one or two other cultural centers. On this reckoning, even Graham Freeman’s study of the transmission of lute music, it could be said, argues from the exceptional (preserved notated sources) to the ordinary (musical sounds made and passed on of which no material trace survives). Beyond consideration of the broadside ballad, there is very little arguing from the ordinary outward in this volume, which would have had to entail far more recourse to records at the level of parish, town, or provincial cathedral. If one excepts London’s Guildhall Library, only one county record office appears in the list of abbreviations and library sigla, and only one cathedral archive. We are still a long way from viewing early modern music in England as roundly as is its sister art in Records of Early English Drama.
