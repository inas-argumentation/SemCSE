Structure of Upper Cretaceous chalk benthic communities, southwestern Arkansas
Paleoecological investigation of chalk facies in the Saratoga and Annona Formations includes paleoenvironmental analysis as well as examination of benthic macro-invertebrate faunas. The Saratoga sandy chalk facies was deposited in a middle shelf environment (25–50 m depth), while the Annona chalk-marly chalk facies was deposited in a outer shelf environment (75–125 m depth). Each facies is characterized by a distinct community of benthic macro-invertebrate fossils dominated in occurence by epifaunal suspension-feeding bivalves and deposit-feeding echinoids. However, sandy chalk also contains moderately common infaunal suspension-feeding bivalves and gastropods, and rare infaunal deposit-feeding bivalves, while chalk-markly contains only rare infaunal bivalves and no reported gastropods. Differences in composition within and between the two communities apparently reflect original distribution of shelled taxa. Trace fossil preservation indicates that substrates in both facies were thixotropic. Functional analysis demonstrates that many taxa show adaptations for living on soft substrates. Differences in diversity within each facies may be owing to non-uniformity of substrate softness, because many suspension-feeders were unable to cope with the softest substrates and are therefore sparse in rocks apparently representing these substrates. Variations in substrate softness may have been caused by differing rates of bioturbation. Presence of infaunal suspension-feeding bivalves in the Saratoga, but relative lack in the Annona, may be primarily a manifestation of softer Annona substrates caused by generally finer grain size of Annona sediments. Several studies of chalk faunas have concluded that sparseness of originally aragonitic fossils was caused by secondary sea-floor dissolution or diagenetic processes. Results of this study demonstrate that primary organism-sediment interactions could have also played a significant role in causing the general lack of originally aragonitic fossils in Upper Cretaceous chalks.
