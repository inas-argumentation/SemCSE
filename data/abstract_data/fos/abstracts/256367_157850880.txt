Lead user innovations are important because they often lead to commercial products. The old-fashioned model of innovation, wherein producers find a need and fill it, is arguably inefficient. The vast majority of such innovations are commercial failures. Von Hippel cites studies placing the success rate of such innovations around 26 or 27 percent (p. 108). Working collaboratively with lead users is a promising alternative approach to innovation. For example, von Hippel and his fellow researchers studied the actual innovation process at 3M between February 1999 and May 2000. In that time, 3M was developing projects based on five ideas from lead users and forty-two ideas generated by more traditional means. The lead-user-developed project concepts "had projected annual sales in year 5 that were greater than those of ideas generated by non-LU methods by a factor of eight-an average of $146 million versus an average $18 million in forecast annual sales" (p. 139). This striking difference points to the potential value of identifying and collaborating with lead users. Von Hippel identifies a number of successful strategies for doing both. There are many reasons to commend this book. Von Hippel's overviewofliterature showing how intellectual property rights are outdated, out of line with real-world trends, and often harmful to social welfare is compelling. Other interesting topics include his discussions of open-source software, his examination of producers providing "toolkits" as a means of collaboratively innovating with lead users while helping steer the path of these innovations, and his speculation that lead users might offer their home countries an important competitive advantage. The broad scope and cutting edge hypotheses of this book point to the need for more corroborative research. For example, many of the supporting studies cited in Democratizing Innovation focus on trends in relatively small and esoteric industries. Nevertheless, the work as a whole is compelling. In keeping with the spirit of freely revealing innovative works, von Hippel has made a copy of this book available for download, under a creative commons license, at his website: http://web.mit.edu/ evhippel/www/ democ.htm. It is definitely worth a look.
