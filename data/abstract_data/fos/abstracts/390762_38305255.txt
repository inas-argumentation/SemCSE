Pharmacotherapy for Borderline Personality Disorder: A Review of the Literature and Recommendations for Treatment
Borderline personality disorder (BPD) is a highly complex and difficult to treat disorder that is characterized by severe dysregulation in multiple domains. Although psychotropic medications are commonly prescribed to individuals with BPD, there is no single psychotropic medication of choice in the treatment of BPD. Data gathered from randomized double-blind, placebo-controlled pharmacotherapy trials have been largely inconclusive in identifying preferred agents for particular domains of dysfunction. Even when used as an adjunct to psychosocial treatment, use of psychotropic medications for individuals with BPD is complicated by such factors as risk of suicide by overdose, noncompliance with medication regimen, "paradoxical" medication effects, and adverse side effects. We begin this chapter with an overview of BPD, including the prevalence of this disorder as well as clinical challenges that make this population of individuals notoriously difficult to treat. We next provide a review of the research literature of the efficacy of psychotropic medications for individuals with BPD, focusing mainly on outcomes from randomized, controlled clinical trials that utilized double-blind and placebo procedures. Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT), a cognitive-behavioral treatment developed by Linehan (1993a, 1993b) for severely dysfunctional individuals with BPD, is briefly described. We conclude with several specific recommendations and practical suggestions derived from DBT to consider for use with individuals with BPD who are using psychotropic medications.
