South Asian subcontinent not represented in international pharmacy education supplement.
Letter to the Editor: As part of American Association of Colleges of Pharmacy (AACP) President Victor Yanchick's initiative on the globalization of pharmacy education, AJPE decided to go global in a quest to merge the ideas from others on pharmacy education and soliciting pharmacy professionals from different countries/regions to educate North Americans and the rest of the Journal readership about pharmacy education in their respective countries. Indeed, it was a wise move to discuss pharmacy education from an international perspective and go along with colleagues from other countries. However, I was a little disappointed to see that AJPE's special supplement issue on international pharmacy education (2008, Volume 73, Issue 6) did not include any articles on pharmacy education in the South Asian subcontinent, comprising India, Pakistan, and Bangladesh. Perhaps India is the only country which provides the most international pharmacy graduates to the World and also a feeder country when it comes to supplying pharmacy educators and researchers. For the past few years I've been writing about the major issues in Indian pharmacy education and highlighting the challenges and deficiencies in the pharmacy curriculum in Indian pharmacy education. My latest letter to the editor was focused on the introduction of a new 6-year PharmD program in some Indian pharmacy colleges/schools that could possibly meet the worldwide expectations on pharmacy practice requirements. I would request the AJPE editors to consider publishing another issue focusing on pharmacy education in the Indian subcontinent, as well as the African continent and South American continent. 
 
 
Naushad M Khan Ghilzai, PhD 
 
LECOM School of Pharmacy, Erie, PA
