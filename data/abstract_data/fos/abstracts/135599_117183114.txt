A test based on mdl criterion for comparing increasing dose levels with a zero dose control
This paper considers a comparison of increasing dose levels of a substance with a zero dose control, when we can utilize the prior information that the treatment effects, if any, will increase as the dose is increased, In such a situation, we usually wish to determine the minimum effective dose which is the lowest dose level better than the control. We regard this multiple comparison problem as a model selection. In this paper the term "model" is used for any hypothesis that imposes a nondecreasing trend on effects of the increasing doses. We propose a test based on minimum description length (MDL) criterion, which is for selecting a model that minimizes the number of bits required to write down the observed data. The power of the proposed test is compared with those of some competing tests. The results show that the test has the power comparative to the competitors, and further, it can correctly determine the minimum effective dose with high probability.
