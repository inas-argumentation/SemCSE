Situational and Relational Aspects of Interpersonal Communication
We are currently engaged in a program of research aimed at developing a conceptual framework for studying and measuring interpersonal communication. Our approach in the initial phase of this research is conceptual; that is, we are exploring the dimensions people use implicitly in characterizing and comparing different kinds of interpersonal communication. Results from this phase will guide our future laboratory and field studies of &dquo;live&dquo; communication. Thus far we have found four dimensions of interpersonal relationships that are invariant over different experimental methods, stimulus sets, and subject groups (Wish, 1973; Wish, Kaplan, and Deutsch, 1973).
