The relationship between prenatal porencephaly and the encephalomalacias of early life.
The brain of a spastic idiot aged 2½ years at death showed extensive bilateral defects of the frontal and parietal lobes. In these regions the walls of the hemispheres were represented by a thin sheet of neurological tissue lying beneath the leptomeninges.This membrane was attached laterally to the temporal and occipital lobes where it was continuous with the molecular layer and thinned-out remnants of the cortex. Medially it fused with an arch of neural tissue, identified as part of the cingulate gyrus, which passed sagittally from the region of the anterior commissure to blend with the occipital lobes. The structure of the membranous parts of the cerebral wall was similar to what is found in hydranencephaly. The left optic tract and the corpus callosum were absent. Signs of an old necrotizing process were seen in areas of gliosis and calcification in the claustra, in the roof of the posterior horns of the lateral ventricles and in the left external geniculate body. There was also a diffuse sub-ependymal gliosis with small calcifications and a patchy granular ependymitis of the ventricular system. Micropolygyria was present in much of the preserved parts of the cerebral cortex and there was hyperplasia of the striatum. Abnormalities were also found in the calcarine cortex where on the left side the stria of Gennari was absent and on the right the superficial extent of the area striata was increased. These malformations were considered to be secondary to a destructive process affecting the cerebral hemispheres during the third month of foetal life.
