Statistical Monitoring of Clinical Trials: A Unified Approach
To some extent, the book follows the same path as early research on queueing theory, which was focused on obtaining exact expressions for performance measures. Novel additions include a few references on applications throughout the text, two detailed chapters on computational methods, and another chapter on simulation. Compared with other books on the subject, the book’s price is reasonable, but is certainly not its strongest point. The authors have adopted a few unusual features in its presentation. The presentation does not follow the typical technique of analyzing each queueing model to its fullest extent. Rather, the authors prefer to emphasize the applicability of a method (such as the formation of difference-differential equations for the steady-state distribution of queue length) by presenting various queueing models in which this technique can be used. Inevitably, this tactic breaks the continuity of the presentation, as one needs to revisit the same models, but it better demonstrates the various paths that one can follow when tackling a problem. This gives the book a rather pleasant flavor; rather than being presented with a recipe book where one can find all of the necessary formulas for each model, the reader is challenged to understand the various ways in which these results are derived. Another unusual characteristic concerns the material presented; for example, the chapter on Markovian queues includes a section on optimization. Although the material that is covered in such atypical sections is elementary, it nonetheless helps give the reader a more holistic view of queueing theory and its function. My main concern regarding this book is its appropriateness for self-study. A lot of relevant material is left to the reader as exercises, while typically only one example (usually the M/M/1 queue) is presented to the reader. For example, standard models, such as the M/M/∞ queue, or the moments of the busy period in an M/G/1 queue are left to the reader. On the other hand, the authors consider it worthwhile to elaborate on the transient behavior of the M/M/1 queue; small parts are left as an exercise and there is no appendix on Bessel functions, while some of their most trivial properties are used without any further comments. At some points, one gets the distinct impression that a graduate student would need the careful guidance of an instructor to get through the material. An instructor wishing to adopt this book as a course text would need to carefully select which sections should be presented, and in which order. One also may need to refer to other sources and probably would need to design some exercises (perhaps those that help the student master the material, rather than prove something not discussed in the text). On the other hand, an instructor certainly would benefit from adopting a few of the ideas in this book, such as spending some time on optimization, various computational methods, or simulation, and stressing the applicability of a method before overwhelming the student with the derivation of all conceivable performance measures of a model.
