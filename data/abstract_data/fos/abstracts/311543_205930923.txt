Professor Sueo Ueno - personal reminiscences
In early 1967, I met Dick Bellman at a meeting in Portland, Oregon. At that time I was preparing for graduate study, and wanted to come to USC to study optimization theory with Dick. But as a father and husband with a small child, I needed a job. So Dick suggested I apply to The RAND Corporation for a position as a programmer, which he would then endorse. Magically, this procedure actually worked, and in June 1967 I left Oregon to take-up residence in Santa Monica, the deal being that RAND would allow me time o during the day to attend lectures at USC. What a deal! On one of my ®rst days at RAND, Dick introduced me to Bob Kalaba and suggested that I spend part of my time working with Bob, providing programming support for studies then underway on problems of radiative transfer in the atmosphere. This turned out to be one of the turning points in my intellectual career, leading to more than three years of collaborative eort, several papers and my ®rst book, all done jointly with Bob. It also led to my meeting many of Bob's collaborators, including Professor Ueno. My memory is a bit fuzzy on the exact date when Bob introduced me to Sueo Ueno. But I certainly recall the circumstances. One day, I think it was in early 1968, Bob and I were going for lunch at a restaurant on Wilshire Boulevard, near the RAND building. Having agreed to meet in the RAND lobby to walk over to this place, I was a bit surprised when Bob turned up with this ``elderly'' Japanese gentleman, as well as a young Japanese woman. It turned out that the gentleman was Professor Ueno, while the lady turned out to be Harriet Kagiwada. Of course, I suppose that Professor Ueno was only in his late-40s at that time. But to a 25-year-old such an age seems hopelessly remote. And so it did to me then, though Professor Ueno looks much the same today ± more than 30 years later ± as he did then.
