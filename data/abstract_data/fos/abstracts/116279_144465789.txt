Bauer establishes four periods of material culture between the conquest and the global age—contact goods, civilizing goods, modernizing goods, and developing goods—without always explaining the theoretical basis for these distinctions. As an example of modernizing goods, he cites the clothing worn in balmy Rio de Janeiro by belle epoque dandies who paraded their modernity in the same worsted frock coats, vests, and trousers fashionable among stock jobbers in rainy London. Yet a similar perverse dress code exemplified the civilizing goods of a previous era, when colonizers carried wool to the Caribbean to maintain the hierarchical trappings of early modern European society.
