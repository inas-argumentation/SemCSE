The Influence of Social Class on Activity Preferences of Comunidades Eclesiais de Base (CEBs) in the Archdiocese of São Paulo
A number of interpretations have recently emerged which attempt to explain the nature and sociopolitical implications of Roman Catholic base Christian communities (CEBs) in Brazil. Most studies have tended to describe these small, informal lay groups as a predominantly lower-class phenomenon which is facilitating the self-liberation of the poor from the weight of centuries-old political and economic oppression. Despite the fact that this particular vision of the CEBs is currently gaining considerable popularity in both religious and social scientific circles, it offers, in our view, a somewhat distorted portrait of the phenomenon. In essence, what the literature suggests is that the groups are a socially and ideologically homogeneous collectivity, whose basic nature and sociopolitical thrust are inextricably linked with class-related factors.
