The Unionization of College and University Teachers: Comments to a Small Committee
L AST February, the Los Angeles Times carried a story by Harry Bernstein under the headline "Union Aims $ioo,ooo Campaign at Colleges-State Faculties Target of AFL-CIO Drive as Rally Protests Pay Cuts." The story begins with the statement that "a $Ioo,ooo fund has been pledged to launch an all-out drive to organize 5,ooo faculty members of California state colleges . . ." It seems appropriate at this time to explain why the state-college presidents should support attempts to unionize the state-college faculties. It is difficult to be open-minded about proposals which, we assume, involve radical departures from relationships we understand. In the past I have not been sympathetic to the establishment of an employeemanagement relationship between the members of the teaching faculty and the members of the faculty with administrative assignments.
