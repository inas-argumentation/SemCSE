Plutarco Elías Calles and the Mexican Revolution (review)
Although Calles was the principal architect of the Mexican postrevolutionary state from 1925 to 1935, he remains something as a cipher as a person. Worse, his political legacy has been largely defined by his foes. To remedy this, Jiirgen Buchenau's balanced, judicious, and concise biography ably synthesizes an impressive amount of scholarly literature, and mines overlooked primary sources—above all those of the superb presidential archive of Fideicomisio Plutarco Elias Calles y Fernando Torreblanco.
