If there is a fault to be found in Calibrations, a highly original, sociopolitically committed, and erudite book, it is the author's willingness to subordinate the aesthetic to the social in the final analysis. To introduce a rupture between the literary and social when elsewhere there is continuity is to needlessly vitiate the force of the book's avowed commitment to dialectical embedding. It remains for Nussbaum to make that claim for the role of the literary when she avers that the quandary of tragic characters is not preordained so much as it is produced by the struggle to find moral salience. As interpreters, we must do no less.
