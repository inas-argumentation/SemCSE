Colombian Transport and the Reforms of 1931: An Evaluation
URING THE quarter century from 1923 to 1948 the Republic of Colombia experienced a remarkable development. At the beginning of that period the country's economy was geared almost exclusively to the production of coffee for export. Its politics were domniiated by the Conservative Party whose program was largely enervated by intense local prejudices nurtured on bitter memories of civil strife and territorial dismemberment. The towering mountain ranges which enveloped the people limited their outlook and restricted their movement, while the plodding pace of the mule seemed to set the temnpo of their life. Prospects of an American indemnity for the Panama Canal' kindled new dreams, however, and during the 1920's and afterward concerted efforts were made to overcome the nation 's isolation, provincialism, and economic backwardness. Indemnity payments, easily obtainable foreign loans, and a booming coffee market helped create the largely artificial prosperity of the 1920's derisively known as the Danee of the Millions. But it also brought a mnore efficient national banking system, important fiscal reformi-s, pheniomenal growth of the coffee producing regions, exploitation of the nationi's virgin oil resources, and the inauguration of one of the first commercial aviation companies in the world. A split in the Conservative Party, coinciding with the onslaught of the great depression, enabled the Liberal Party to elect its candidate to the Presideincy in 1930, for the first time in the century. Colombialls hailed the sulbsequlent peaceful transfer of power as marking a long step toward their political maturity. Political stability and economic recovery characterized the administration of Enrique Olaya Herrera 1930-1934, as over eight hunidred new industries were created to help diversify the economy and significant reforms were achieved in all branches of public administration.
