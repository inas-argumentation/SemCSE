Numerical calculations of the transient loading of blunt obstacles by shocks in air.
This paper presents the results of the application of a numerical method for solving twodimensional fluid dynamics problems to the problem of transient loading of blunt obstacles by shocks in air. The numerical method, a time-dependent pure-Eulerian technique, is applied using a high-speed computer. Two different obstacles are considered under variable incident shock conditions: a rectangular block and a right circular cylinder. Both obstacles are aligned to the flow such that the incident shock is normal to their flat faces. In the case of the block, the calculated results of the transient pressure pulse on the front, top, and back sides are compared with experimental data. The agreement is shown to be quite good. For the right circular cylinder, the loading on only the flat face is investigated. The calculation results are in good agreement with analytical predictions of the overpressure. A consistent error of less than 10% in the calculated time duration of the pulse is estimated on the basis of analytic comparison. In most of the calculations, the equation of state of air is taken to be polytropic. One blunt cylinder calculation, which uses a more realistic equation of state, is included for comparison. The results of this comparison indicate that real air effects can be approximated quite well with the proper choice of 7 in the polytropic equation.
