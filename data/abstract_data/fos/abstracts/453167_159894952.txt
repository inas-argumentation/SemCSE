John Spurr, in a similar vein, argues that Dryden’s conversion to Catholicism did not materially benefit him during James II’s turbulent tenure on the throne. “‘I can . . . not forsake my Religion, because I know not what Church to go to, if I leave the Catholique’” (quoted, 253), Dryden wrote in a letter of 1699: the reflection applies, with equal justice, to his impolitic loyalty to the Stuarts after James II’s ouster. The essays collected in Zwicker’s Cambridge Companion to John Dryden are sophisticated, well-argued, and alert both to formal and political dimensions of Dryden’s texts. Three of the essays, however, are especially memorable. Christopher Ricks offers a dazzling reading of Dryden’s triplets in Absalom and Achitophel that is certain to change the way we attend to both the heroic couplet and the triplet in his poetry and his rhymed drama. In a wonderfully subtle and wide-ranging essay, Paul Davis treats Dryden’s translations of Ovid and Virgil, arguing that Dryden was uniquely poised to appreciate “the entire variousness of Augustan culture” (77). Finally, Paulina Kewes contributes a masterful survey of Dryden’s politics over his long, discontinuous career writing for the stage. Her extensive knowledge of Dryden’s dramatic corpus, combined with her refreshing unwillingness to fit it to one procrustean model, makes for a particularly valuable account of Dryden’s changing tactics in different political circumstances. The Cambridge Companion to John Dryden will prove to be a vital resource for seventeenth-century scholars. JENNIFER BRADY Rhodes College
