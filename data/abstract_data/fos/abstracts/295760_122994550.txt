On the Correction for Guessing on a Multiple-Choice Examination.
If a student answers correctly x0 out of k questions on a multiple-choice examination with n choices for each question, he is usually given a grade G= [X0- (k-X0) / =(n - 1)] out of k, where the reduction is justified as a correction for guessing. This paper presents an investigation of the distribution theory underlying this correction and other corrections for guessing. The probability distributions of the random variables involved are derived. We then find the assumptions and restrictions that should be imposed on these probability distributions so as to obtain the different corrections. The correction in G, based on random guessing of unknown answers, is compared with corrections based on “educated” guessing.
