Masculine Mystique, Feminine Mistake, and the Desire of the Analyst
The collapse of phallocratic ideals and the effects of the feminist and gay liberation movement on traditional gender stereotypes have allowed for an unexpected rapprochement between psychoanalysis and social change. Our new social ideals and the fantasies they necessarily produce have left behind the dream of finding the other half in the other sex. It is a given that love and desire are not a perfect match. Our new erotic vicissitudes have brought to a greater level of awareness the irreducible distance that separates the subject from the realm of his or her fantasy. Sexual freedom in that sense has only made more apparent what Freud used to call the bedrock of castration and what Lacan has translated by the famous aphorism 'there is no sexual relation.'
