Another special presentation was by Alia Al-Ghunaim, the architect of Bayt Abdullah Children’s Hospice, who discussed the design and philosophy of the first children’s hospice in the Middle East. Bayt Abdullah is a new concept in hospice design, bringing all the elements of palliative care, family support, children’s play areas, entertainment and education together in an environment created specifically for these very special children and their families. The conference ended with a film of the groundbreaking ceremony for Bayt Abdullah, which had been held just a few days before the conference started. One thousand schoolchildren attended the ceremony on the 20,000 m 2 site where Bayt Abdullah will be built. Close to the sea in Sulaibikhat Bay and bordering Kuwait’s Ministry of Health, Bayt Abdullah will be the final stage in the development of a fully comprehensive paediatric palliative care service in Kuwait. A specialist team of doctors, nurses, child life specialists and nutritionists have already come together as Paedpals, the paediatric home care team, giving children with life-limiting and lifethreatening conditions, and their families, an alternative to returning to hospital for their medical care.
