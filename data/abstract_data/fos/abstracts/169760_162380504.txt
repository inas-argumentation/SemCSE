Fictions of Capital: The American Novel from James to Mailer (review)
It has been said that Theodore Dreiser wrote novels by layering slices of business next to slices of sex. Fictions of Capital, by Richard Godden, is organized on the same model: slices of economic history alternate with slices of literary criticism. Like Dreiser's fiction, too, this book is flawed but ultimately worthwhile. Godden spreads on a thick coating of jargon. Critiquing capitalism, he blithely ignores Marxism's economic failure and political collapse. He is, however, a master of close reading and carefully constructed arguments.
