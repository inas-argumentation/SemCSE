A Shortened Version of the Burns Perfectionism Scale
The Burns Perfectionism Scale (Burns, 1983) is a 10-item scale to measure neurotic perfectionism ( Hamachek, 1978). Although the preliminary reliability and validity data are promising (Lamm, 1985) ,"ore data are needed. In particular, at face value, it appears that one of the items measures a more healthy than unhealthy attitude; Item 5 says "If I try hard enough, I should be able to excel at anything I attempt." The purpose of the present study was to see if the internal consistency could be improved by eliminating Item 5. The Burns scale was completed by 119 women and 31 men ( N = 150) in the waiting room of a family practice medical center of a small midwestern city. The mean age of the subjects was 31 yr.; all but one subject were Caucasian. The item-total correlation for Item 5 was .20; the other itemtotal correlations were all above .48. T h e interns1 consistency of the original scale was .76; when Item 5 was removed, the internal consistency was .81. The Pearson correlation between the original and shortened scale was .9S. For the original scale the mean score was 17.16 (SD: 6.41) and for the scale minus Item 5, the mean was 14.33 (SD: 6.28). In conclusion, the shortened scale has higher internal consistency than does the original scale; and the validity of the shortened scale is cornpareble to that of the original. In further research, it is suggested that the shortened scale might be used. Most importantly, test-recest reliability and other validity data are needed for the shortened scale.
