A recombination model for transient and stationary stress-induced leakage current
Charge-separation experiments performed at a variable time after stress and on n + - and p + -polysilicon p-MOS devices show that recombination in oxide defects is the mechanism leading to low-voltage stress-induced leakage current (SILC). Accordingly, a new model for the SILC is developed, which considers both trap-assisted tunneling and electron–hole recombination in oxide defects. Simulation results are in good agreement with transient and steady-state data for both electron and hole SILC in different MOS devices. According to this model, recombination is mainly responsible for low-voltage leakage current, which represents a severe limitation for flash EEPROM scaling.
