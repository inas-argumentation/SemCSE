The German-American Connection in the 1980s
German-American friendship is a "factor of world stability which everyone can and must reckon with" proclaimed the foreign minister of the German Federal Republic thirty-five years after the German surrender in World War II.' It was a familiar theme. For more than three decades West German and American opinion leaders have celebrated harmony between their countries, and public opinion polls have borne them out.2 Just recently, in spring 1980, the West Germans showed themselves true to their words by joining the American trade embargo against Iran and by following Washington's call for a boycott of the Moscow Olympics. They drew, however, a line on American demands for significant restrictions on their flourishing trade with the Soviet Union. It is clearly not an unbounded friendship and should not be mistaken for a love affair. West Germans and Americans respect each other, but feelings of mutual affection are not as evident as in Anglo-American relations. The association between the United States and the German Federal Republic is more like a business partnership for mutual profits, involving sober cost-benefit calculations based on separate interests.
