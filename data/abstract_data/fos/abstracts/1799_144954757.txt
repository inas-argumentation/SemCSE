An Analysis of Principal-Teacher Communication Patterns under Conditions of High Covert and Low Covert Perceptual Disagreement
The purpose of this study was to investigate in a field setting principal-teacher communication task behavior under conditions of high covert perceptual disagreement and low covert perceptual disagreement. This manuscript reports the design and results of an exploratory empirical study. The results indicated that principal-teacher communication behavior was significantly different between the two conditions for four of the seven variables analyzed. This finding supports the hypothesis that superiors tend to communicate with subordinates in a disconfirming pattern under conditions of high covert perceptual disagreement.
