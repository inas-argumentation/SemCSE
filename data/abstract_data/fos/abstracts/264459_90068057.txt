Sweeteners and sweet taste enhancers in the food industry
Since the end of the twentieth century, obesity has been one of the greatest public health concerns worldwide (Disse et al., 2010), with consequences beyond esthetic issues, as it is considered a risk factor for many different diseases (Fujiwara et al., 2012; Ribeiro & Santos, 2013), such as cardiac conditions, hypertension and diabetes. To limit the prevalence of diseases related to the excessive consumption of sugar, researchers have worked on the isolation of naturally occurring compounds and development of synthetic sweeteners. These sweeteners are able to replace sugar totally or partially, but are rarely as satisfying as the full sugar alternative because they fail to trigger physiological satiety mechanisms (Raben et al., 2002; Swithers et al., 2010).
