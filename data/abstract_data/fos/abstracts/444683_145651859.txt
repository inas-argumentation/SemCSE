Socioeconomic Status and Mode of Presentation as Factors in Children’s Concept Attainment
In attempting to determine the dependence of children on concrete experiences as indicated by Piaget’s Theory, three levels of stimulus abstractness for conservation and discriminative response tasks were developed. Tests at these three levels, concrete, film, and abstract, were administered to 360 second and fourth grade children partitioned on socioeconomic level. The results indicated a strong grade effect for both tasks, a mode effect on conservation with the abstract mode scores lowest, and a mode effect on the discriminative response tasks with the abstract mode scores highest, and a socioeconomic effect for conservation in favor of high SES. There were Grade x Mode and Mode x SES interactions for the conservation test and a Grade x SES interaction for the discriminative response test The lower scores on the abstract form of the conservation test indicated the greater reliance of children on concrete experiences.
