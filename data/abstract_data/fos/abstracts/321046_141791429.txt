Waking from the Dream: Mexico’s Middle Classes after 1968 by Louise Walker (review)
Historians have engaged Burkholder's main argument. Michel Bertrand downplays the watershed character of the 1750s in terms of creole/peninsular conflict, because arriving officeholders continued to be quickly absorbed into local society. Horst Pietschmann maintains that during roughly the same period feuding visions of a "federative" empire versus a nascent nation state superseded the creole-Spanish controversy. Nonetheless, Burkholder makes a compelling case by drawing on his own massive research and synthesizing the current historiography. His fascinating study, positioned at the intersection of identity, justice, and politics, should interest all early-modern students and scholars.
