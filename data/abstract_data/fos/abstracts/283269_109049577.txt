Milking and feeding chores in a confinement-stall barn.
DETAILED time-and-motion studies of milking and feeding chores were conducted in 34 confinement-stall barns in Wisconsin. Both pipeline and buckets with transfer station systems were included in the study. A milking chore simulation model was developed using the results of the in-barn studies. The time spent machine-stripping the cows was varied to observe its effects upon the timeliness of the rest of the milking chores. Milkers were modeled using two, three, and four milking units per person. The simulation results indicated a person could never use more than three units unless he had a helper to prep the cows, and that he must limit his machine stripping time to less than 10 s in order to per-form all of the milking chores in a timely manner. The results of the feeding time studies showed that feeding time is determined more by how the feeding system is managed than by the type of feeding system.
