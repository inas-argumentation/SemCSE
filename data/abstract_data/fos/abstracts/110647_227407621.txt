Design of a Spintronic Emitter based on Acoustic Excitation of Terahertz Spin Waves.
Narrowband terahertz (THz) radiation is crucial for high-resolution spectral identification, but a narrowband THz source driven by femtosecond (fs) laser has remained scarce. Here we predict, using phase-field modeling, that fs-laser-induced picosecond acoustic pulse can excite THz spin wave of higher frequency in a metal/dielectric/magnetoelastic heterostructure, which generates the narrowband THz pulse via magnetic dipole radiation. Using Al/MgO/FeGa as an example, we demonstrate the emission of a THz pulse with a strong peak of narrow linewidth (~0.05 THz) at 0.73 THz from the magnetoelastic FeGa film when irradiating the Al film with a fs laser. Principles of tuning the peak frequency, linewidth and the radiation power by heterostructure design are established. Our results also suggest that ultrafast acoustic pulses can enable long-distance (up to millimeters) transport of THz exchange spin waves which by themselves only propagate a few nanometers, thus offering new opportunities for THz magnonic devices as well.
