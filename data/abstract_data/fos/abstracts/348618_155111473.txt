Minor P Doping to Effectively Suppress IMC Growth in Solder Joints with Electroplated Co(P) Metallization
Electrodeposited Co(P) layers with three different P contents (0.5 wt.%, 0.8 wt.%, and 2 wt.%) have been coated on Cu substrates by adding minor amounts of sodium hypophosphite to the Co plating bath. These hcp-Co(P) coatings exhibited highly preferred (002) orientation. Solid-state interfacial reactions of 6-μm-thick Co(P)/Cu with pure Sn were conducted at temperatures of 160°C to 210°C. In the Sn/Co-0.5 wt.%P/Cu reactions, a uniform intermetallic compound (IMC) layer of CoSn3 was formed and its thickness increased linearly with aging time. Most importantly, the IMC growth was significantly inhibited compared with that of the Sn/Co/Cu reaction without P doping. When the P content was increased to 0.8 wt.%, the formed phases contained not only CoSn3 but also metastable CoSn4, and the suppression effect became stronger. The IMC growth rate at 180°C was decreased by 95% in comparison with the pure Co case. The growth rate with Co-2 wt.%P was between that observed for the 0.5 wt.% and 0.8 wt.% P compositions, so the IMC growth kinetics of the Co coatings with 0.5 wt.% and 0.8 wt.% P compositions were systematically investigated. Minor P doping in the Co deposit greatly suppressed the IMC growth.
