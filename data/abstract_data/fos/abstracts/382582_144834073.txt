Two major purposes were formulated: 1) First, an attempt was made to study the effects of the donoras level of resources on the recipient’s self-perceptions. Although some past research has dealt with the effects of bbe level of the donor’s resources on the recipient’s perceptions of the donor (e.g., Gergen, Ellsworth, Maslach, & Seipel, 1973), no data is yet available concerning the possible effects of this variable on the recipient’s self-perceptions. Nevertheless, research (Fisher and Nadler, in press; Morse, 1972) and theorizing (Blau, 1964) in the area of recipient’s reactions to being helped, suggest that under some conditions the receipt of help may effect the recipient’s feelings of self-worth. In view of the above, the present research attempted to specify the effects of the donor’s level of resources on the recipient’s self-perceptions. 2) The second purpose of the present study was to specify the link between receiving help and subsequent self-help efforts. Concerning this relationship, it may be that conditions which lead to feelings of cooperation and sharing also lead to greater dependency and less self-help. On the other hand, and in line with previous research on threat to self-esteem (e.g., Coopersmith, 1967), aid which threatens the recipient’s self-esteem may lead to a greater selfhelp effort in order for the recipient to regain positive self-regard.
