Modelling the Effects of Income Growth and Discretionary Change on the Sensitivity of UK Income Tax Revenue
In Hutton and Lambert (ig80 a), henceforth HL, an exact formula for calculating the elasticity of the income tax was given and applied to the United Kingdom for the period I973/4 to I977/8. The formula ((i) in HL) was of the form y = I + E + 2, where El relates to the marginal rate structure and 2 to allowances. During a period of rapid growth in nominal incomes, the elasticity y was found to vary considerably from year to year and it was noted that, since Z2 was the dominant term, the scale of allowances must play a large part in determining the sensitivity of total tax revenue to growth in incomes. Since these allowances are determined annually by the government, and income tax revenue forms about 30 % of total tax revenue, it is worth exploring further the effects of discretionary change upon the elasticity. In this paper we develop a simplified model of the UK income tax which enables the variations in elasticity from year to year to be broken down into components identifiable with income growth and discretionary changes in allowances, marginal rates and thresholds. The structure of the paper is as follows. First, a brief comparison of the UK and US income taxes points clearly to the particular features of the UK tax which are responsible for the relative instability of the UK elasticity. Then, a simple analytical model is proposed which incorporates these features and provides the means of assigning relative importance to the various determinants of change in the elasticity. By applying the model to data for I973/4 to I977/8, we find that income growth and discretionary change in allowances were indeed the principal (and offsetting) effects on the elasticity during this period, and all other factors were of relatively minor importance. Appendix I contains the technical results, and in Appendix II the potential of the model for answering similar questions about changes in per capita income tax liabilities is indicated, and a simple measure of fiscal drag is obtained.
