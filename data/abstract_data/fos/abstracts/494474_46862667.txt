Concerted action of histone methyltransferases G9a and PRMT‐1 regulates PGC‐1&agr;‐RIG‐I axis in IFN&ggr; treated glioma cells
HighlightsSp‐1 and NF&kgr;B serves as negative regulator of RIG‐I in IFN&ggr; treated glioma cells.IFN&ggr; decreases occupancy of Sp1 and NF&kgr;B on RIG‐1 promoter.RIG‐I inhibition increases expression of de‐differentiation markers.IFN&ggr; induced PGC‐1&agr; regulates RIG‐I expression.Histone methyltransferases G9a and PRMT‐1 effect RIG‐I and PGC‐1&agr; expression oppositely. ABSTRACT IFN&ggr; induced de‐differentiation markers are negatively regulated by retinoic acid inducible gene (RIG‐I) in glioma cells. In addition to RIG‐I, IFN&ggr; treatment increased H3K9me2; histone methyltransferases (HMTs) G9a and protein arginine methyltransferase‐1 (PRMT‐1) levels. While G9a inhibition further increased IFN&ggr; induced RIG‐I, PRMT‐1 inhibition abrogated IFN&ggr; elevated RIG‐I levels. IFN&ggr; induced Sp1 and NF&kgr;B served as negative regulators of RIG‐I, with decreased occupancy of Sp1 and NF&kgr;B observed on the RIG‐I promoter. A diminished H3K9Me2 enrichment was observed at the NF&kgr;B but not at Sp‐1 binding site. IFN&ggr; induced PPAR gamma coactivator‐1 alpha (PGC‐1&agr;) positively regulated RIG‐I; with PRMT‐1 and G9a affecting PGC‐1&agr; in a counter‐regulatory manner. These findings demonstrate how concerted action of HMTs aid PGC‐1&agr; driven RIG‐I for the sustenance of glioma cells in a de‐differentiated state.
