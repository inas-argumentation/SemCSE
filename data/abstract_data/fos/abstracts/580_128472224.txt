Petrogenesis and paleoenvironment of limestone lenses in upper Devonian red beds of new south wales
Upper Devonian fluvial or lacustrine red bed deposits contain lenses of algal limestone fragments, and autochthonous calcareous tubes of possible algal origin. The algal limeclasts are composed of vari-textured micrite, and contain cavities filled with sparite mainly. The sparite occupied the voids prior to erosion of the clasts. Many of the micrite limeclasts underwent recrystallization without having affected the sparite cavity fillings. It is not possible, therefore, to determine whether recrystallization occurred before or after erosion and transportation of the limeclasts. The presence of recrystallization does not indicate, therefore, that the limeclasts have been derived from an older limestone source. On the contrary, the total petrologic and stratigraphic information, discussed in some detail, suggests an intra-environmental origin for the limeclasts, i.e., they are genetically classifiable as intraclasts. Based on size, morphology and genesis a number of different types of micrites and sparites are recognizable. The paragenetic relationship between these carbonate types, and some non-calcareous components, is complex and of at least two generations.
