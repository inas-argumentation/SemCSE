Paleoceanographic record of the last glacial/interglacial cycle in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden
Hydrographic conditions in the Red Sea are strongly controlled by regional climatic conditions and the restricted water exchange with the Gulf of Aden (Indian Ocean). In turn, intermediate and deep water masses in the Gulf of Aden reflect the nature of Red Sea outflow. Planktonic and benthic foraminiferal faunas, and the lithology and mineralogy of Red Sea and Gulf of Aden sediments were studied in order to evaluate changes in water mass conditions in the two basins during the last 18,000 years. The results indicate that changes in the local climate and circulation regime during this period resulted in significant hydrographic changes in surface and bottom waters in the Red Sea and intermediate waters in the Gulf of Aden. During the last glacial maximum, high aridity and glacio-eustatic sea level lowering resulted in surface and bottom water salinities in the Red Sea which were considerably higher than at present. In the Gulf of Aden, glacial surface salinities and temperatures remained similar to Holocene values, while surface water productivity may have been lower. Surface salinities in the Red Sea decreased after 18,000 years B.P. as the connection between the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden was reestablished. However, bottom water salinities remained high until at least 11,000 years B.P. Pluvial conditions during deglaciation enhanced the density difference between surface and bottom waters in the Red Sea, and resulted in a decrease in the oxygen content of the bottom waters. Hydrographic conditions in the western Gulf of Aden reflect a change in the properties of outflowing Red Sea water during deglaciation.
