Conduction and charge analysis of metal (Al, W and Au)?Ta2O5/SiO2?Si structures
Metal–insulator–silicon structures containing 10 nm thick insulating films obtained by thermal oxidation of Ta films deposited on Si were experimentally studied in terms of a previously developed comprehensive model for I–V characteristics combined with a modified C–V method. The influence of the metal (Al, W and Au) on the leakage and the oxide charges was studied in detail. It was found that the following two contributions in the oxide charge are present: (1) Qif = −(0.2–0.7) × 1011 cm−2, of the SiO2 interfacial layer, and (2) Qtp = (3–4) × 1012 cm−2, of the Ta2O5 and the Ta2O5/SiO2 interface. A third contribution from the charge located in Ta2O5 near the metal surface, Qg = 4 × 1012 cm−2, occurs only in the case of metal with a high work function (Au) and can be explained by the discharging of positive traps from a thin layer adjacent to the metal surface into empty states of the metal valence band. Poole–Frenkel conductivity of the Ta2O5 bulk was found to be almost the same for Au and W while for Al it is almost two orders of magnitude higher. The top electrode providing simultaneously the lowest leakage and the lowest flatband voltage is that of W.
