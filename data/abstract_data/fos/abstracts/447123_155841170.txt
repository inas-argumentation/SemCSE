Advising the President on Enrolled Legislation: Patterns of Executive Influence
The decision to approve or disapprove bills and resolutions passed by Congress is an important presidential function, one that brings the executive into the final stages of the legislative process. This decision is not perfunctory. From Washington's administration to 1978, there have been 2,379 vetoes with 610 since the Truman administration alone. Only 90 vetoes have been overridden by Congress. There have been various studies of presidential vetoes. Their number, their form, their rationale, and even the constitutional power to veto itself have been subject to scrutiny.1 Few studies, however, have concentrated on the process by which the president makes his decision,2 and none has analyzed that process
