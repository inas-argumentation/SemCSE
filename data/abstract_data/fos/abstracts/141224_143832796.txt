A semiotics of violent actuality: encoding football fan behaviour during Euro'92.
This paper analyses coverage of a number of incidents of football fan violence on Swedish television during the 1992 European Championship. Concentrating mainly on the technical characteristics of the video footage used — coming mostly from amateur or surveillance sources — it argues that they illustrate an emerging semiotics whereby poor technical quality, far from being a disadvantage, has come to be regarded as a guarantor of actuality. The paper also examines how this actuality footage was framed both by discourses circulating in the leading Swedish tabloids, and above all by its combination with footage from other sources — including professionally shot footage — by Swedish television. The article concludes by suggesting that this semiotics can itself be seen as part of a larger signifying structure whereby television presents itself as a non-hazardous way of enjoying large-scale sporting events.
