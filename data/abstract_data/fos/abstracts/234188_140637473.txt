Reply to comments on “Palaeoecology and palaeogeography near cochrane, Alberta, Canada, just after the last major high stand of glacial lake Calgary”
Abstract When columbus sailed across the Atlantic, conventional wisdom suggested that the earth was flat. However, today Dr. Jackson and ourselves enjoy the benefits of the fact that the earth is round. Thus it was fortunate that Columbus ignored the mass of contemporary literature to test the current ideas of that age. There are good examples of lengthy debates in the geological literature where some parties were reticent to accept the evidence, e.g., the “100 years war” over the origin of granite. In the end, the evidence must be accommodated in the paradigms since the opposite is impossible. Jackson has not explained the significance of his arguments to North American Quaternary research. If Harris and Ciccone (1983) and Harris (1985) are right, then the problem of where the stagnant ice masses of Prest et al. (1968) fit into the history of deglaciation is solved. If they are wrong, then their significance remains to be explained. At any given time, there is a limited body of evidence and facts available, and this changes with time. Over the last twenty years, the available facts that have been accumulated point to the conclusions put forth in Harris (1985). Harris and Ciccone (1983) were merely adding more data. It is appreciated that these conclusions offend Dr. Jackson and that they are not reflected in the previous literature, but we think that the available evidence is sufficiently consistent, abundant and compelling that we must adjust our paradigms accordingly. Should new evidence be forthcoming which alters the balance of the evidence, then we shall be happy to adjust our paradigms, but until that time, it would seem unethical and unreasonable not to acknowledge the present balance of the evidence as we find it.
