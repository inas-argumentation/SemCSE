Study on Genetic Diversity of the G Protein of Subgroups A and B Human Respiratory Syncytial Viruses Isolated in Beijing,China
To achieve the data of genetic diversity and molecular epidemiology of human respiratory syncytial virus(HRSV) in Beijing, nucleotide sequencing of the C-terminal region of the G protein gene was performed on 63 isolates obtained from Beijing Children's Hospital in year 2003 and 2004. The circulation pattern and genetic variability of both HRSV group A and B isolates circulating in Beijing were described. The results showed that 96.8% (61/63) Beijing HRSV isolates belonged to group A and 3.2% (2/63) belonged to group B in year 2003 and 2004 indicating that group A and B HRSV cocirculated in Beijing in year 2003 and 2004, predominating with group A HRSV. Phylogenetic analysis of partial G gene nucleotide sequences of Beijing 61 group A strains and 2 group B strains revealed that all 61 group A isolates clustered into one assigned genotype, GA2, 2 group B isolates clustered into one previously identified genotype GB3, which demonstrated that GA2 genotype was the predominating epidemic genotype in Beijing in year 2003 and 2004. The nucleotide sequence and amino acid homologies were 87.8%-100% and 77.9%-100% respectively among 61 Beijing group A isolates, and 94.7% and 88.1% respectively between two group B isolates, Beijing04-11 and Beijing04-66. There were several different HRSV GA2 genotype cocirculations causing HRSV epidemics of different HRSV transmission chains in Beijing in year 2003 and 2004. HRSV group B Beijing04-11 strain contained a 60bp-nucleotide duplication in the C-terminal region of the G-protein gene, which was similar with Ken/29/03 from Kenya and isolates from Buenos Aries in 1999. This was the special case for characteristics of HRSV polymerase prone to copy the duplicated region, possibly according to this way to help HRSV escape preexisting immunity. This is the first time study on the genetic diversity of human respiratory syncytial viruses isolated during the epidemic period from children in Beijing, China. These data provided a better understanding of the patterns of HRSV strains circulating in Beijing and the data of genetic diversity of epidemic HRSV is useful for development of diagnostic reagents and vaccine production.
