Theon's tale: does a Cambridge change result in a substantial change?
Theon is a proper part of Dion, who is a whole man, a person; Theon is all of Dion minus the latter's left foot. A well-known puzzle is created when we ask what happens to Theon if Dion's left foot is severed from his body. Is it a plausible view, for example, that Dion and Theon will exist thereafter as numerically different objects that are made of the same matter, sharing all of their qualities and occupying exactly the same spatial positions at the same points in time? And if not, which of the two should be said to have been destroyed in this operation? Examining the principal solutions to this puzzle, Michael Burke [3] suggests that each of them conflicts with some common-sense assumption.
