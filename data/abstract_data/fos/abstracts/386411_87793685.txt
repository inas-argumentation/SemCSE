P059 Tumor cell derived type I IFN stimulates tumor immunosurveillance, suppressing metastasis to bone
Introduction Breast cancer is the most common type of non-skin cancer among women. Breast cancer associated morbidity is not commonly due to the primary tumor but rather the concomitant metastasis–the invasion of tumor cells to tissues such as bone. Bone is the most common site of breast cancer metastasis and once this has occurred, it is usually incurable. This severe morbidity is largely attributed to our lack of understanding of the mechanisms of bone-specific metastasis. To investigate the metastatic process we utilized the unique syngeneic 4T1.2 model of spontaneous metastasis [1] , [2] to bone and compared the transcriptional profile of tumor cells purified from primary mammary tumors and matched bone metastases. This differential transcriptome comparison revealed a substantial set of genes suppressed in metastases were highly enriched for promoter elements for the master type I IFN regulatory factor, Irf7, as well as Irf7 itself. In this study, we show for the first time that suppression of the type I IFN pathway in breast tumor cells, not immune cells as usually described, is critical to the development of bone metastases. Furthermore, we reveal the immune cell targets that are responsible for IFN-driven metastasis suppression. Methods We restored type I IFN signaling in mice bearing 4T1.2 tumors by enforced tumor cell expression of Irf7 or direct IFN administration. We then explored the consequences of altered type I IFN signaling on metastasis and immune activity in this model. In addition, we utilized type I IFN receptor null mice (Ifnar-/-), NOD SCID IL-2rγ-/- mice (lacking a functional immune system) and mice specifically depleted in certain immune cell populations, to demonstrate the critical function of the immune system in Irf7 induced metastasis suppression. Results The restoration of Irf7 gene in tumor cells significantly reduced bone metastases and prolonged metastasis free survival in the 4T1.2 murine model. Irf7-driven suppression of metastasis was reliant on host type I IFN signaling because this suppression was diminished in mice deficient in the type I IFN receptor or immune responses. In addition, systemic interferon administration greatly prolonged survival and reduced metastasis. Metastasis suppression was specifically correlated with increased CD4+, CD8+ T cells and NK cells in the peripheral blood and was reversed by depletion of these effector cell populations. Conclusion We report for the first time that tumor cell intrinsic type I IFN immune pathways stimulate host tumor immune surveillance to suppress bone metastasis. Loss of tumor cell IFN signaling predicts increased risk of development of bone metastases. Our data prompt future research into the therapeutic potential of type I IFN pathway-targeted immunotherapies in breast cancer patients. In addition, dissection of the molecular markers in primary tumors that may determine which patients are most likely to respond to such therapies and benefit from prophylactic treatment.
