Effects of drying conditions on impact fracture resistance of selected corn varieties
KERNELS of three corn varieties were sheared in a compound pendulum apparatus to identify differ-ences in impact strength due to genetic characteristics and drying conditions. Kernels were dried from an initial 25 percent moisture content, wb, to final moistures of 21, 18, 15 and 12 percent. Values of shear energy per unit area, maximum failure shear stress, and impulse per unit area were calculated from deceleration traces on a storage oscilloscope. Although all three parameters increased with final moisture content, failure shear stress, with the smallest coefficient of variation, best characterized the impact strength of corn kernels. Shear strength of all three varieties decreased, but not uniformly, as drying air temperature increased. One variety, however, exhibited a significantly greater resistance than the other two.
