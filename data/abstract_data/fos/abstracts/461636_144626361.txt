Strength of GSR and Avoidance Conditioning as a Function of CS Intensity
In an earlier study o'f the relationship between intensity of the conditioned stimulus and strength of conditioning (Kirnmel, 1959), maximum GSR conditioning was obtained using a 1,000 cps tone of 35 d b (re: .0002 dynes/cm.'), which was the lowest intensity used in that study. The present study was designed to examine more closely the intensity range below and just above 35 d b to locate the maximum more precisely. To extend the range of applicability of the present findings, an instrumental response was added to the siruation along with the GSR, and the CS-UCS interval was reduced from 4 sec. to 450 msec. The instrumental response used was finger-withdrawal from a key via which the electric-shock UCS was delivered. The duration of the tone was 900 msec., with the shock beginning 450 msec. after the onset of the tone and terminating with it. Finger-withdrawal terminated boch stimuli and, if it occurred before the shock had started, avoided the shock. This procedure has been shown to produce conditioning of boch the instrumental response ( W i d e n s & Platt, 1954) and the GSR (Wickens, Cross, & Morgan, 1959).
