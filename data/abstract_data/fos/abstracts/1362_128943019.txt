A preliminary model for subaqueous-glacial and post-glacial sedimentation in intra-continental basins
The physical and chemical characteristics of subaqueously deposited glacial sediments are described and referred to processes before, during and following sedimentation. Relative to tills, glacial marine deposits are depleted in Fe, as a result of deposition in comparatively reduced environments. Fe depletion in many ancient diamictites may be attributed to subaqueous deposition, but only after petrologic study. Post-glacial formation of siliceous shales, cherts, dolomites, and bedded barite is attributed to: 1. (1) concentration of Si in the water column of basins marginal to glaciers through dissolution of silicate minerals in glacier milk; 2. (2) precipitation of carbonates on warming of saturated basin waters during deglaciation; and 3. (3) sedimentation of BaSO 4 in association with post-glacial marine transgression and increased biologic productivity.
