A sauropod skeleton associated with theropod teeth in the Upper Jurassic of Thailand: remarks on the taphonomic and palaeoecological significance of such associations
The association of a partial sauropod skeleton with carnosaur teeth is described from the Late Jurassic Sao Khua Formation of northeastern Thailand. This association is reminiscent of similar instances reported from the Upper Jurassic of western North America and East Africa and can best be explained by dismemberment of a sauropod carcass by carnosaurs. This type of evidence does not indicate whether the sauropod was killed by carnosaurs or whether the carnosaurs merely acted as scavengers. The sauropod skeleton/carnosaur teeth association can be used as a taphonomic indicator of a lack of post-mortem transportation. This in turn can lead to palaeogeographical conclusions, as exemplified by the case of the Damparis dinosaurs (Late Jurassic of France), the occurrence of which indicates in situ immersion.
