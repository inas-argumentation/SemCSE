Reflecting on the Common Good: Harold Rugg and the Social Reconstructionists
t the start of each semester, one A instructor faces her new students in the class Methods of Teaching Social Studies in the Secondary Schools and asks two questions: What is social studies? and What is the role of the social studies teacher? Her students are usually startled and somewhat amused by these questions until they actually begin to wrestle with answering them. It is only at this point, late into their teacher preparation program, with only this methods class and student teaching standing between them and their very own classrooms, that they have been asked to reflect on defining their academic discipline and their role as future social studies educators. In fact, this past semester, when the instructor asked her eighteen students to define social studies, not one of them, to the instructor’s dismay, included the concepts of citizenship or democracy in their original definitions.
