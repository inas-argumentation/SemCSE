Lineage classification of Chinese {\sl Listeria monocytogenes} isolates based on the partial sequence of the {\sl actA} gene
Objective To investigate the feasibility of actA gene as a marker for lineage classification of Listeria monocytogenes(Lm) and the genetic and evolutionary relation between Chinese strains and US strains. Methods A total of 40 Lm isolates were obtained from food (n=32) and sewage (n=6) samples as well as from two human listeriosis cases that occurred in China. All isolates were characterized for molecular subtype by DNA sequencing of the 597?bp 3′ terminal region of the virulence gene actA. Sequence data were used to classify the 40 Chinese Lm isolates into sequence types and phylogenetic lineages and to compare the sequence types of the Chinese isolates with those of isolates from the US. Results The Chinese isolates could be separated into two genetic lineages with 14 and 26 isolates belonging to lineages Ⅰ and Ⅱ, respectively. Lineage Ⅱ could further be subdivided into two clusters (ⅡA and ⅡB). Lineages Ⅰ and Ⅱ were identical to the two lineages previously described among US Lm isolates. A total of 14 actA sequence types could be differentiated among the 40 Chinese Lm isolates; two specific actA sequence types were found from both Chinese and US isolates. Conclusion DNA sequencing of 3′ terminal region of actA is an effective method for the fast subtyping and lineage classification of Lm. Lm clonal groups found in China are very similar to those found in US. Many Lm strains may thus represent globally distributed clonal types.
