It is hard to avoid relating Singer's book to Commons' Legal Foundations of Capitalism. Singer demonstrates conclusively that no simple rule can replace judgment and that the ultimate criterion of judgment is some desired social configuration. On the other hand, he does not deal, as did Commons, with the effect of social class on the shape of society desired by different people-especially by judges. Nevertheless, Singer's book is important and well worth reading.
