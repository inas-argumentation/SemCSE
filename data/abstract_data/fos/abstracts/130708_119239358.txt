On the dark matter haloes inner structure and galaxy morphology
In the present paper, we extend the study of Del Popolo (Mon. Not. R. Astron. Soc. 408:1808, 2010) to determine the slope of the inner density profile of galaxy haloes with different morphologies.We study how galaxy morphology changes the relation between the inner slope of the galaxy halo density profile, α$\alpha$, and the stellar mass, M∗$M_{*}$, or rotation velocity, Vrot$V_{\mathrm{rot}}$. For this, we use the model of Del Popolo (Astrophys. J. 698:293, 2009) in combination with observed data from the Romanowsky and Fall (Astrophys. J. Suppl. Ser. 203:17, 2012) sample of elliptical and spiral galaxies, the Local Group sample compiled by McConnachie (Astrophys. J. 144:4, 2012), and the simulation results by Cloet-Osselaer et al. (Mon. Not. R. Astron. Soc. 442:2909, 2014).We find that the slope α$\alpha$ flattens monotonically, from α≃−1$\alpha \simeq -1 $ at Vrot≃250km/s$V_{\mathrm{rot}} \simeq 250~\mbox{km/s}$, to α≃0$\alpha \simeq 0 $. After Vrot≃25km/s$V_{\mathrm{rot}}\simeq 25~\mbox{km/s}$ the slope starts to steepen. The steepening happens in the mass range dominated by non-rotationally supported galaxies (e.g., dSphs) and depends on the level of offset in the angular momentum of rotationally and non-rotationally dominated galaxies. The steepening is a consequence of the decrease in baryons content, and angular momentum in spheroidal dwarf galaxies.We finally compare our result to the SPH simulations of Di Cintio. Our result is in qualitative agreement with their simulations, with the main difference that the inner slope α$\alpha$ at small stellar masses (M∗≲108M⊙$M_{*} \lesssim10^{8} M_{\odot}$) is flatter than that in their simulations.As a result, the claim that finding a core in dwarf galaxies with masses slightly smaller than ≃106M⊙$\simeq 10^{6} M_{\odot}$ (as in the Di Cintio, or Governato, supernovae feedback mechanism) would be a problem for the ΛCDM$\Lambda\mbox{CDM}$ model which should probably be revised.
