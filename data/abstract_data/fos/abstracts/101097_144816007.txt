Early Retirement: Some Questions and Some Options.
Current interest in early retirement arrangementsand both administrator and faculty groups are interested in itarises from the combination of higher education's fiscal and budgetary problems, the surplus of young Ph.D.s, and mounting concern about the impact of these two phenomena on college staffing. An individual's interest in early retirement, especially his attitude toward the idea, is colored by where he sits. Some faculty members might welcome an opportunity for early departure from the tensions and rapid change on campus; others might resent any move on the part of an institution that suggested an earlier retirement age policy. One man's carrot is another man's stick. In addition to these human conditions, the financial conditions are also variables. Institutions on the brink of bankruptcy may have no choice but "retirement" of people in their early 40s as well as those in their early 60s, people with tenure as well as those without it.
