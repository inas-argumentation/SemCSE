"To Rescue the Germans Out of Sauer's Hands": Benjamin Franklin's German-Language Printing Partnerships
WHEN BENJAMIN FRANKLIN DREW UP A CONTRACT to make David Hall the managing partner of the Philadelphia printing house in 1748, he promised not to become involved in other publishing ventures for the eighteen-year duration of the contract. However, he violated that covenant.1 Franklin's reasons for doing so emanate from his political involvements, his moral convictions, his civic loyalties, and his desire to leave his ideological stamp on German immigrants, who constituted a segment of colonial society Franklin thought particularly needful of moral education.
