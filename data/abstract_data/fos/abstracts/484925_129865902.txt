The Ordovician–Silurian boundary on Cornwallis and Truro islands, Arctic Canada: preliminary data
Four sections of the lower part of the Cape Phillips Formation, two outcrops on northeastern Cornwallis Island and one outcrop and one drill core from Truro Island, Northwest Territories, Canada, provide significant new data on the Ordovician–Silurian boundary. They show evidence of continuous sedimentation through the boundary interval and a continuous sequence of graptolite zones, including the bohemicus and persculptus zones, which have not been previously found in Arctic Canada. Strata yield graptolites, including uncompressed specimens, and conodonts through most of the sections. The ordovicicus conodont Zone occurs within the pacificus to lower persculptus graptolite zones. The nathani conodont Zone contains a "transitional fauna," a mixing of species typical of the preceeding ordovicicus Zone and those generally regarded as Silurian indicators.
