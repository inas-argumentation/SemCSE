The SEC's New Chief Accountant Sets Some Clear-Cut Goals
I think the use of market values for assets and liabilities is very desirable, especially for financial institutions. Walter P. Schuetze, the Securities and Exchange Commission's new chief accountant, is a precise man. He chooses his words carefully and speaks in measured phrases. He does not, however, mince words. In a Journal interview shortly after he took office in January, he clearly defined his top priorities upon assuming his position. When asked about the profession's problem areas, he says, "I do not think the users of financial statements have been well served over the last 10 to 15 years. As a result, I think financial accounting and reporting have lost some of their credibility, and we need to improve their relevance." Schuetze also has firm ideas on how financial reporting can regain its credibility. He says, "I know where I want to begin the process of improving financial accounting and reporting. We need to get debt and equity securities marked to market. Financial statements that use historical cost to value stocks and bonds simply have outlived their usefulness. Users who would benefit from current value accounting for debt and equity securities include shareholders and potential shareholders, employees and potential employees, creditors and potential creditors, and customers. "We also need to measure loan impairment by using discount rates tied to current market interest rates. If we can reach these two goals during my term in office, we will have made great strides toward restoring the relevance of financial statements." ONLY THE BEGINNING Schuetze is fond of saying, "The profession needs to crawl before it can walk and walk before it can run." He considers the use of current values for debt and equity securities the crawling part and believes it will be much more difficult to apply current values to fixed assets, such as property, plant and equipment, and to intangibles. He says, "I think getting to market value on fixed assets is a long way away. We should concentrate on debt and equity securities and on measuring loan impairment." Along these lines, Schuetze expects the American Institute of CPAs special committee on financial reporting to communicate frequently with his office as the committee evaluates the nature and extent of information that should be included in financial reports. FINDING THE DATA Schuetze realizes that marking all debt and equity securities to market will not always be easy. While noting that prices for many securities can be found in the morning newspaper, he acknowledges that "getting a fair value for debt securities not traded in robust markets is not easy."
