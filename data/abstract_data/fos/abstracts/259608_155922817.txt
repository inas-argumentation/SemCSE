Annamaria Viterbo: International Economic Law and Monetary Measures: Limitations to States’ Sovereignty and Dispute Settlement
Annamaria Viterbo’s book is a complete and relatively easy to read legal history of post-1944 international law as it pertains to the effective economic policy space of sovereign states. It is, therefore, an excellent guide to the legal-institutional environment of international trade and finance in terms of both the letter and intent (as interpreted by the courts) over the last several decades. As such, the text should reside as a reference on the shelves of all those engaged in economic research on the matter. Having stated what the text is, it is worthwhile to briefly discuss what it is not. It is not a book on either theoretical or empirical economics. It does not discuss historical or ongoing operations of the state within the discussed legal environments. For example, there is no discussion of the various international efforts to save the Bretton Woods system during the 1960s and early 1970s. Similarly, there is no economic (theoretical or empirical) analysis of the added policy space post-Bretton Woods. Finally, there is no discussion of the history or personal motivations behind various international legal arrangements.
