Making Mistakes: Error and Learning in the College Presidency
The literature on the college and university presidency focuses on presidential roles [6, 15, 16], presidential tasks and responsibilities [9, 30, 48], and the social and organizational contexts in which college presidents work [32, 33]. However, we know very little about how presidents take on their roles, identify and assume their responsibilities, and learn about their work places [18], and with few exceptions [10], we know even less about the difficulties they face and the mistakes they make in doing so. In this article I examine the selfreported errors of college and university presidents and analyze them from the perspective of organizational learning. The concept of organizational learning may be considered at the level of the individual, such as the client, the service provider, or the manager [ 1, 28, 46], or the collectivity, such as the formal or informal group [2, 12, 41] or the organization itself [27, 34].
