Interdialytic Fluid Gains in Diabetic Patients Receiving Hemodialysis Treatment
• Objective: The objectives of this study were (1) to compare fluid gains of diabetic patients with those of age- and gender-matched controls and (2) to determine if type of diabetes is a factor in higher interdialytic fluid weight gains. • Design: The interdialytic fluid weight gains of diabetic and nondiabetic subjects were monitored for 1 month. • Setting: Study subjects were selected from those patients receiving HD therapy at the Northwest and Elliot Bay Kidney Centers in Seattle, WA. • Patients: A sample of diabetic and nondiabetic HD patients was chosen. Diagnoses of polycystic kidney disease, congestive heart failure, and advanced liver disease excluded patients as subjects due to the abnormal tissue and fluid weight gains that accompany these conditions. Forty-six diabetic HD patients were matched with 53 nondiabetic HD patients for age (within 10 years) and gender. • Measurements: Interdialytic fluid weight gains (IWG) of these 99 subjects were monitored for 1 month. • Main results: The diabetic group exhibited no significant differences from the nondiabetic group in IWG expressed as a percentage of dry body weight nor in the average IWG or the number of times that the IWG exceeded 1.5 kg. Eighty-eight percent of the diabetic subjects and 86% of the nondiabetic subjects exceed the fluid gain guideline of 1 .5 kg. • Conclusions: The researchers conclude that diabetic HD patients do not gain more fluid between dialysis treatments than nondiabetic HD patients. The fact that the majority of HD subjects, regardless of diabetes, gained more than the recommended 1.5 kg between dialyses warrants more attention. Therefore, future research should review the guidelines for interdialytic fluid gain to ensure both medical appropriateness and assistance in patient compliance with regard to fluid restriction. © 1993 by the National Kidney Foundation, Inc.
