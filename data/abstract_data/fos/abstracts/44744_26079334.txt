Special Issue on Architectures and Protocols for the Future Internet
We are delighted to present this Computer Networks special issue on ‘‘Architectures and Protocols for the Future Internet.’’ We believe this area of networking research is particularly important and exciting since it addresses the very foundations of today’s and tomorrow’s Internet. The Internet architecture has been remarkably successful in allowing a planet-scale internetwork to form. However, the Internet has been losing its original simplicity and transparency as new classes of applications, business models, security mechanisms, scalability enablers, and operational and management requirements have given rise to point solutions that extend the architecture without regard to its original design principles. Consequently, the research community has been actively looking for new approaches to evolve or supersede the Internet architecture. Substantial efforts by academia, industry, and network operators in Europe, the Americas, and Asia have resulted in promising proposals to address the limitations of the current Internet architecture. This special issue contains eleven original, high-quality papers that present, analyze and discuss state-of-the-art research results on Internet architectures and network protocols. These papers present both revolutionary ‘‘clean slate’’ and evolutionary ‘‘dirty slate’’ architectures for the Future Internet, with a particular focus on architectures based on splitting locator and identifier roles in naming, as well as related topics.
