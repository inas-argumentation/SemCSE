Late Quaternary sedimentation in the eastern Arctic Basin: Stratigraphy and depositional environment
The Late Quaternary history of the Arctic Basin is poorly known because most sediment cores seemed to have accumulated very slowly (based on magnetostratigraphy on the order of one to a few millimeters per thousand years). Two short sediment cores from the Eurasian Basin of the Arctic Ocean have now been dated by means of O- and C-isotopic ratios. The O-isotope stratigraphy suggests that these sediments accumulated much faster (on the order of one to four centimeters per thousand years) than in the central Arctic Ocean. The cores penetrated sediments belonging to isotope stages 1–3 and allow a detailed description of the depositional environment under the Arctic ice cover during the last ice age and the ensuing climatic warm interval.
