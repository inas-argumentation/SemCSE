Attitudes of Norwegian and American Children Toward Mildly Retarded Children in Special Classes
1This article is based on the author’s doctoral dissertation. Appreciation is expressed to Drs. Martin B. Miller, Herbert Goldstein, and Irv Bialer for their helpful suggestions. It is well known, both in Europe and the United States, that public institutions for mentally retarded people are of higher quality in the Scandinavian countries than in the United States. It is therefore generally assumed that attitudes toward the retarded are more favorable in Scandinavia than in the United States (e.g., Lippman, 1972). Yet, there are few data to support this assumption. Jordan (1969) found that Danish people did not express more favorable attitudes toward physically disabled persons than did Americans.
