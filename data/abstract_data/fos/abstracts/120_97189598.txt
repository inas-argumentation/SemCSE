Effects of soil-applied fluoride and lead on growth of loblolly pine and red maple
Additions of sodium fluoride or lead chloride at concentrations from 2 × 10 −4 to 2 × 10 −2 M reduced the growth of loblolly pine and red maple seedlings in pots of two forest soils. Lead chloride treatments also increased anthocyanin content of red maple leaves. Seedlings grew better on Whitestore sandy loan than onGGeorgeville silt loam, but there was little difference between soil types in influencing responses to fluoride or lead. Growing temperature also affected growth responses and anthocyanin content and interacted to some degree with treatment and soil variables.
