A controlled physiological, clinical and psychological evaluation of chlordiazepoxide.
Forearm blood flow, used as an objective physiological index of anxiety, has been found to be a useful addition to the available methods of drug assessment. In a double-blind controlled trial, chlordiazepoxide (Librium), in a mean dosage of 45 mg. per day, was found to be significantly superior to placebo on eight physiological, clinical, and psychological measurements. These were: resting and basal forearm blood flow, observer and self-ratings of global improvement, observer- and self-ratings of depression, Clyde Mood Scale factor of "Dizzy", and stress anxiety self-rating. The method of simultaneous clinical, physiological and psychological measurement is suitable for assessing many types of treatment in a very detailed way. The Clyde Mood Scale and Semantic Differential are valuable for quantifying subjective changes, and deserve wider use.
