Competition in the Defense Industry: An Economic Paradox
Recent public attention of government activities have centered around issues of inflation, the size of the federal budget allocated to defense, ecology and social reform. However, some of the most fundamental problems encountered by the government are the determination of its defense policies and its effectiveness in carrying them out. The United States being a major world power has no choice but to keep a strong military position and since most Americans believe in maintaining an adequate defense system, there is a continual debate concerning the government's level of efficiency in sustaining an efficient military-industrial complex. Judging from some recent literature,' the old question of how to accomplish an effective defense procurement program has not been dealt with decisively. The purpose of this paper is to show that the federal government in striving for procurement efficiency of defense products has created a situation of inadequate competition and industrial concentration by overregulation and inefficient administration. In order to explore these competition ambiguities further, perhaps a cursory examination of the present environment will give the reader cognizance of the current level of competition in the government-industry relationship.
