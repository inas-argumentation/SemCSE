RURAL MIGRATION TO URBAN SETTINGS: EDUCATIONAL AND WELFARE PROBLEMS*
Eighteen centuries ago, Marcus Aurelius wrote in his Meditations: tfAs for life, it is a battle and a sojourning in a strange land ..." I am often re? minded of that simple and perceptive line when I think about the great American migration to the city of those who come from simpler rural environments so poorly prepared to cope with the complexities of living an industrial urban life. Many of these are, indeed, strangers in a strange land. The problems they face and the problems they are, are by no means solely of their own making. Nor can they rely solely on their own resources to solve their difficulties and to suffice in their own search for the good life.
