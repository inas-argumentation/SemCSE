An enhanced saturation attack and its mitigation mechanism in software-defined networking
Software-Defined Networking (SDN) enables network operators to easily and quickly manage, configure, and optimize network resources via decoupling the control logic from forwarding devices. Such a separation, however, brings multiple potential bottlenecks that can be exploited by attackers to degrade network performance or even disrupt network availability. In this paper, we delve into an enhanced saturation attack, table-miss striking attack, and prove that it is more cost-efficient and powerful than the traditional saturation attack. In response, we propose SDNGuardian, an efficient and protocol-independent defense framework for SDN/OpenFlow networks to mitigate table-miss striking attacks. It stands between the controller platform and other controller applications, and protects the network by leveraging four functional modules: preprocessor to obtain the packet sensitive fields that trigger the controller to issue flow rules; attack detector to notify the signal about the occurrence of the attack; traffic filter to identify the attacked ports and filter traffic based on frequency; rule sweeper to eliminate malicious rules in the switch flow table. All designs of SDNGuardian conform to the OpenFlow, requiring no modification in protocol and additional devices. We implement a prototype of SDNGuardian in the Ryu controller and evaluate its performance in software testbed environments. Experimental results show that SDNGuardian can efficiently mitigate the table-miss striking attack and protect network infrastructure resources with negligible system overhead.
