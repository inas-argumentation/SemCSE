The Effect of Budgeting Time on the Achievement of Freshmen Normal School Girls
A large number of American people in gen eral, as well as the young people who enter colleges and normal schools each year, seem to be thoroughly convinced that all people are born free and equal. After careful con sideration, the falsity of this supposition be comes apparent. Differences in quantity and quality of bodily structure, health and ambi tion, capacity and aptitude for learning, and knowledges and skills developed are conspic uous in all individuals. "The most important educational problem, however, does not con sist in determining more facts about these dif ferences in native endowment or devising more practical and reliable methods for their measurement, but in finding a way of helping each student learn to make the best possible use of the talents and the energy and the time which he possesses."1 "The heaviest responsibility carried by any person is that of investing the twenty-four hours a day which are allotted to him."2 If young people can be taught to realize that achievement in any line of endeavor is vitally conditioned not only by capacity and aptitude but also by how they use each period of the twenty-four hours, much of their time and energy might produce more satisfying results. A large number of studies concerned with the effect of intelligence, praise and censure as an incentive, reading ability, rivalry, extra curricular activities, as well as other factors have been made. Comparatively few studies have dealt with the effect of a daily schedule for study, recreation, eating, personal care, and rest on scholastic achievement. Book3 found that after giving instruction to students on how to make a plan for the use of all available time, the percentage of efficiency for one group rose from 76 to 96 per cent, and in another group it rose from 84 to 98 per cent. He states that "efficient planning and the ability to carry out one's plan is, in fact, the most important single factor in successful work of every sort."4 Bird,5 Headley,6 Kitson,7 KleinSmid,8 Mor gan,9 Payne,10 Shreve,11 Werner,12 and Whipple13 have recognized budgeting of time as a significant factor in obtaining results from studying, but report no experiments to check this statement. The questionnaire method has been em ployed by a number of investigators to deter mine how the twenty-four hours of the day were used by college and high school students. In a study made at the University of Idaho, Goldsmith and Crawford14 found that the average amount of time spent on studying was three hours per day.
