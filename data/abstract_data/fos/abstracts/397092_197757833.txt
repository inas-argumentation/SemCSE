Slavery and Historical Capitalism during the Nineteenth Century ed. by Dale Tomich (review)
The histories of state formation and capitalism in the United States, Cuba, and Brazil are interwoven into the meteoric rise and fall of slavery in these countries after 1780. Very early in the history of the professionalization of the historian’s craft, locally based scholars of the US South, Brazil, and Cuba had to explain slavery to provide a national narrative. Thus, the largest and oldest cumulative volume of slavery studies in the Western Hemisphere, perhaps with the exception of the British Caribbean, is focused on the nineteenth century in the US South, Brazil, and Cuba. The conceptualization of “Second Slavery,” as a distinct phase in the relationship between slavery and capitalism, is a successful reinvention of this scholarship by Dale Tomich and an international group of scholars whose contributions are gathered in this book.
