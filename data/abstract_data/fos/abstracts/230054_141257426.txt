Great Expectations: Tips for a Successful Working Relationship with Your Thesis Advisor
There are several publications that provide detailed information for doctoral students regarding the ins and outs of completing a dissertation. Many students at this level of academia have already written a master's thesis and are acquainted with the path that must be trod in order to produce a document that will capsulate their research and findings. For master's level students, however, there is little in print that speaks directly to them regarding challenges they may face and tips they may use in establishing a successful working relationship with a thesis chair and committee members. This paper outlines specific suggestions for graduate students embarking on writing a thesis that will not only strengthen the faculty-student working relationship, but assist the student in completing this work in a timely fashion. ********** As students near the completion of their required course work, many see the process of writing a thesis as a long and lonely road. No longer do you have the frequent association with peers that you had while attending regular classes. Work and other non-school related activities begin to consume more of your waking hours. As you focus your priorities towards writing the thesis, it is also important to recognize the impact that your thesis chair can have on this portion of your education. A decision that Bolker (1998) calls, "one of the most important decisions you will make" (p. 19). Your advisor may be one who is always available to talk with you and who reviews your written work quickly, providing valuable feedback on each draft submitted. On the other hand, you may experience great difficulty even locating your advisor or finding a time that the two of you can meet together. You may have submitted an initial draft of a chapter weeks ago, but still have no idea when feedback will be forthcoming. Through these types of experiences, you begin to realize that your advisor can either be your biggest supporter or a significant roadblock in writing this thesis. Learning how to facilitate this faculty-student relationship is central to you completing your degree. Unfortunately, it is not one that usually develops naturally or by chance. For this relationship to be successful, you must be aware of a few expectations that advisors have of their graduate students. Armed with this information, you can put yourself in a position to develop effective communication skills and create the means for reducing frustration and barriers that may derail the successful completion of a graduate degree. This article is intended to provide graduate students writing their thesis with some tangible tips for a successful working relationship with their faculty advisor. Expectations of Advisors Select a relevant, manageable, and feasible project. One of the first expectations your, advisor will have is that you have chosen a research topic and project that is relevant to your professional development and will be of interest to those in your discipline. Your study should build on an existing body of knowledge and it should seek to answer questions generated by previous research. Selecting a relevant topic will also aid you in finding current articles for your review of literature. Upon completion of your thesis, you may choose to share your findings with others in your discipline through presentations at regional and national conferences and written publications. In addition to being relevant, your topic and project should also be manageable. This means that you have narrowed the focus of the research questions you are asking or the hypotheses you are testing so that they are specific and clear. The variables in your study should also be clearly defined and measurable.
