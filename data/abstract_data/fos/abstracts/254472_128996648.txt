The large Liassic bivalves: Symbiosis or longevity
The theory that the large size of some pelecypods of the Lithiotis facies (Gray Limestone Formation of the Veneto region, North Italy, Middle—Upper Liassic) is connected with the presence of unicellular symbiotic algae, usually promoting the Ca 2+ metabolism in the host skeletal structures, is rejected. The Lithiotis facies, corresponding to a lagoonal environment within the carbonate platform (inner and middle shelf) in the Veneto region, is particularly significant due to the abundance of bivalve molluscs, whose aragonitic shell material and microstructures are preserved, as well as growth periodicities and rates. Lithiotis Gumbel 1871, Cochlearites Reis 1903 and Lithioperna Accorsi Benini 1979 are the large bivalves of the Lithiotis facies; they often reach 40 cm in height and a thickness of 1.5 cm for each valve. A possible symbiosis results to be unreliable; this is supported by growth rate analysis: in fact, it has been verified that the large size is simply the consequence of a “long life”. The Ca 2+ metabolism is not accelerated; the monthly and annual growth rates are very close to those of present-day bivalves living in intertidal and subtidal environments.
