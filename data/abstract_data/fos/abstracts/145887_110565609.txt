Field Performance of a Grape Trash-Removal System—1977
AN experimental mechanical system for removing trash from mechanically harvested grapes was field tested for the third year. The system consisted of a cluster breaker to strip grapes from the rachis and a vibrating chain belt to separate the trash from the grape product. Seven of eight grape varieties appear compati-ble with this separator, but the eighth resisted stripping. Rain and field slopes were found to adversely affect separator performance. Under normal field conditions product recovery ranged from 78 to 98 percent in the first 46.5 cm of chain belt travel, petiole recovery ranged from 20 to 35 percent (by count), and total trash recovery ranged from near zero to about 20 percent. Installation of this separator on mechanical harvesters for grapes does not appear to be feasible, but a similar multistep separation system at the processing plants might be feasible.
