There is a small wooden box from Heiningen, perhaps the oldest extant item of its kind, one that late sixteenth-century conservators thought to be a jewelry box. The familiarity with the paramenta of the medieval rites was lost. Kruse gives a detailed description of the artistic designs of the box, including images of paten and chalice and Vogelsprachen (words surrounding rondelles with images of birds) communicating the works of mercy, and thereby identifies the box as a container for objects used in the Mass (either the Hosts or purificator towels). The recovery of the meaning of the pieces and an appreciation of the books and other objects in their original preReformation context is a theme of the book. An eclectic arrangement of topics and codicological desiderata notwithstanding, the highlighting of the use and later preservation of liturgical and devotional books and art, offered in an amply illustrated volume, makes this a valuable addition to research and university libraries.
