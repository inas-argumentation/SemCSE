Electronic anisotropies in the nematic phase of FeSe
The appearance of high-temperature superconductivity in the Fe-based superconductors is likely to be linked to the variety of other ordered states found in their phase diagrams. Certain systems, including FeSe, exhibit an unusual `nematic' phase where fourfold symmetry of the lattice is spontaneously broken while translational and time-reversal symmetries are preserved. In this phase the system undergoes a weak symmetry-breaking distortion of the lattice, but displays much more sizeable anisotropies in measurements which probe electronic properties, such as resistivity and quasiparticle interference. However experimental access to the underlying symmetry-broken electronic structure is often hampered by the formation of twin orthorhombic domains in the sample. Here we report a high resolution angle-resolved photo-emission spectroscopy (ARPES) study of single crystals of FeSe under mechanical strain, which promotes the population of only one orthorhombic domain at low temperatures. These "detwinned" measurements reveal a remarkable anisotropy: of the two electron pockets expected, only the peanut-shaped pocket directed along the longer $a$ axis is visible. These observations constitute one of the most dramatic manifestations of nematic order.
