The deontological conception of epistemic justification and doxastic voluntarism
According to the deontological conception of epistemic justification as endorsed by most traditional epistemologists, one is justified in holding a belief if and only if one is in the clear, or epistemically responsible, in holding the belief. William Alston criticizes this conception and any theory of epistemic justification based on this conception. The crucial disability that Alston attaches to the deontological conception is doxastic voluntarism.
