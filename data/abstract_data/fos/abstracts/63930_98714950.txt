Behavior of transition metal disulfides intercalated with sodium as solid solution electrodes
The effects of sodium intercalation in TiS 2 , TaS 2 and NbS 2 have been studied using high temperature sodium cells which utilize beta″-alumina as a solid electrolyte. The stability of this electrolyte in the voltage range in which intercalation and deintercalation occur makes it possible to study these processes without interference from background reactions and electrolyte cointercalation. Our cyclic voltammetry and galvanostatic experiments show that ternary compounds having sodium concentrations near unity can be obtained by electrochemical titration. Sodium intercalation is found to be reversible in TiS 2 and TaS 2 over the entire concentration range, while NbS 2 can be reversibly intercalated to a lesser extent. Intercalation is observed to proceed in steps, which may be associated with changes in the stacking order of the disulfides slabs.
