Christians, Blasphemers, and Witches: Afro-Mexican Ritual Practice in the Seventeenth Century (review)
There is a good deal more to be said of this impressive study—of the way in which the details proliferate like kudzu in some sections, so that the argument is obscured; of the insightful reflections on Mexican historiography; of the author's approach to microhi story. Readers will gain much from a close attention to two volumes, which should interest not only specialists in Mexican history, but also Latin Americanists more generally, historical anthropologists, and scholars of early modern religious sensibility.
