Boxer’s personal connections and memberships in academic societies yielded “additional opportunities to lecture and to publish” (p. 322). In the United States, Lewis Hanke opened many doors; in Brazil, the media mogul Francisco de Assis Chateaubriand became a financial patron. The best of Boxer’s scholarship dates from his tenure at King’s College, when he “became recognized as the premier historian of the Portuguese empire” (p. 342). After retiring from King’s College, Boxer taught at a variety of American universities, including Indiana University, where his coveted library now resides. Those who knew Boxer will certainly enjoy reading this book. Others will be fascinated by his “uncommon life,” as well as by the “aura” that Alden weaves around him. This celebration of the man goes well beyond Alden’s profound respect for Boxer’s publications and library. Alden’s description of Boxer’s unconventional marriages, his love of parties, his famed hospitality, his London “club,” and his wartime service all reflect the awe that Alden feels toward this charming Englishman of the last century who always traveled first class.
