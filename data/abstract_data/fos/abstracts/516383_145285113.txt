Amy Oliver finds in film and fiction about the Allende and Pinochet eras reasons why Chile and its struggles have remained prominent in global memory since 1973, a conclusion shared by Jeffrey Mindents' comparative analysis of the documentary films of Patricio Guzman. Art bears witness in ways that maintain and multiply memories, but Kristin Sorenson concludes from her study of the reception of the moving documentary on the disappeared, Fernando Is Back, the trauma that Chileans suffered during the Allende and Pinochet eras can inhibit their ability to respond to art that challenges their memories and politics. The prominent Chilean human rights lawyer, Fabiola Letelier closes the book with her conference keynote speech stressing the importance of active citizenship, human rights principles, social equality and international solidarity in the ongoing struggle for justice.
