The development of elaborative skills: Teaching basic writing students to make the commitment to audience and topic
The purpose of this study was to develop an instrument based on cues speakers use to support each other during the verbal elaboration of a topic in conversation and test ik effect on the development of writers’ abilities to elaborate during revision. In our search for cues that can facilitate elaboration during revision, we first looked at spoken discourse with the following questions in mind: What do competent speakers and hearers do in order to elicit the necessary information far the event? Could some of the strategies used by competent speakers and hearers, in order to elicit elaboration from the speaker, also be used by basic writing students? The study comprises two park aiming to answer these two questions. In the first part of the study we developed an audience-response device for the procedural facilitation of elaborative revision in composition instruction. It consisted of examples of the types of audience comments that prompt the elaboration of information in natural discourse. In the second part, the device was implemented in a group of 12 students in a university course for basic writing. Two research questions were addressed in the second phase: Will the texts of the students who use the response device reveal an increased extent of elaboration and an improved holistic quality of writing compared to students who did not use the device? Will these changes be maintained when the device is removed? The findings of the study indicated that the group of students who revised their texts by means of the device revealed significantly better development in writing than the control group according to the two criteria mentioned previously. In addition, our in-depth analysis of the first and second drafts written by 3 participants of the experimental class revealed that, first, they learned to commit themselves to an absent audience, and second, to their topic. This suggests a developmental route for acquiring elaboration skills during revision that should be further investigated. This study was developed on the basis of three interrelated premises: (1) communicative competence in writing manifests itself in the ability to elaborate one’s ideas; (2) procedures for activating elaboration exist and can be facilitated during revision; and (3) these procedures can be based on cues speakers use to support each other during the verbal elaboration of a topic in conversation.
