The G. R. G. Conway Collection in the Library of Congress: A Checklist
THE LATE George Robert Graham Conway (d. May 20, 1951) not only lived most of his mature life in Mexico, he also devoted time and considerable effort to locating and publishing rare materials pertaining to her colonial history.' Born in England and educated there as an engineer, Conway early found a unique nexus whereby he could combine his British background with his adopted patria mexicana as he proceeded to collect and study the histories of various sixteenth-century Englishmen who suffered at the hands of the Holy Office. His widening interests came to embrace the Jews who came before the Inquisition as well as the fate of certain leading families who figure importantly in the annals of Philippine history. His rich library contained not only manuscripts but also rare imprints and maps. To systematize his studies he had Inquisition records in the Archivo General de la Nacion transcribed and translated; where there were other records to supplement his principal source, he ordered copies or transcripts to be made. By the late 1920's Conway estimated that he had expended well over ten thousand dollars on this project alone. The Conway contributions to the Library of Congress began as early as 1926, when he sent a copy of his privately printed An Englishman and the Mexican Inquisition 1556-1560.
