The Benefits of Adult Learning: Continuing Education and the Development Process in the State of Kuwait
This study purposed to examine the contribution that continuing education (CE) as an educational sector can make to the development process in Kuwaiti society with its economic and social dimensions. The analysis focused on the individual and collective benefits that adult learners experienced as a result of their participation. Also considered are the attributes and aspects of CE provision that help generating these benefits. The study employed questionnaire as a quantitative method to obtain data from 366 adult learners enrolled in different CE courses and programmes. Findings showed that joining CE is a beneficial experience to both learners and society. In this study, social benefits emerged as significant, creating opportunities for social resources development, whereas opportunities for human capital resources to grow were found to be limited. The study argues that it is necessary to re-conceptualise the policies and practices of CE in order to facilitate a system of CE premised on notions of social prosperity as well as economic gain. To this end, the study makes a number of suggestions for the improvement of CE consistent with the political and socioeconomic context in Kuwait. Introduction At the moment, societies all over the world are witnessing different features due to the globalization phenomenon which makes human development a challenging process. These include the diffusion of information technologies, the need for more skilled individuals to keep abreast of development and change, cultural and linguistic diversity and the need to work towards a democratic society (Jarvis, 1995). Under such circumstances, people during the span of their adult lives need to update and broaden initial skills and stocks of knowledge that become obsolete. Also, rapid social change means that they need to go on learning in order to cope with and adjust to changes in social norms and modes of living. In this context, LLL, of which adult education (AE) and continuing education (CE) are part, has become an increasing necessity for all countries and not just an aspiration (Alsunbu12000). This belief has been presented in various national and international papers such as OECD (1996), UNESCO (1997), ALECSO (2000), World Bank (2003). The main theme of these papers is that success in realizing LLL is seen as key for human development. Similar to countries around the world, Kuwait is in the midst of transitions. At the moment, despite its considerable economic growth compared to its small population, Kuwait is witnessing some major demographic, economic and societal changes that make development a difficult task. Amongst these features are the ever-increasing dependence upon an imported workforce, the need for more skilled and technical national labour, the increase in spare time due to the high living standards, the increase in the life expectancy of Kuwaiti individuals and the small economy which depends on oil revenue as the only major source of national income (Alhasawi, 2000). This is in addition to what has been disclosed by Tony Blaire who headed the Report "Vision Kuwait 2030"; he states that : 'the population is set to more than double by 2030, 4/5ths of Kuwaiti citizens are employed in the public sector.., the education and health systems have higher levels of expenditures than the EU average but much lower outcomes' (Alqabas 2009). All of these are critical indicators that call for various methods to be exploited properly and effectively to tackle these challenges and one of these methods is CE (Almodhaf 2000). The assumption underpinning this study is that CE in Kuwait was established in the 1970s and early 1980s to meet certain requirements at that time; as these requirements have changed, this sector needs to be modified to meet the new societal and economic needs resulting from the continuous global change (Almodhaf 2000). In this regard, CE departments in Kuwait have to reassess their policies and their practices in this diverse continuum of challenges. …
