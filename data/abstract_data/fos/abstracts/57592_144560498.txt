The Validity of the Goodenough Draw — A — Man Test in Iraq
IRAQ IS faced with the problem of rapid develop ment, bringing with it a severe need for educated manpower, such as engineers, doctors, teachers, and technicians. The school population has expanded tremendous ly. Children are attending schools in ever increas ing numbers. Among the problems created by this condition is the need for psychological tests in gen eral, and intelligence tests in particular. Efficient use of human resources requires that children be grouped, that counseling be done, and equally im portant, that vital psychological and e d u c a t i o n al studies be carried out. Little has been done along these lines, and as grades are the only means of as sessing pupils at the moment, it would appear that some tool for obtaining an estimate of pupil intelli gence is the sine qua non of further progress in this area.
