The Brunn Minkowski Firey theory. I. Mixed volumes and the Minkowski problem
The Brunn Minkowski theory is the heart of quantitative convexity. It had its origins in Minkowski's joining his notion of mixed volumes with the Brunn Minkowski inequality. One of Minkowski's major contributions to the theory was to show how this theory could be developed from a few basic concepts: support functions, Minkowski combinations, and mixed volumes. Thirty years ago, Firey [8] (see Burago and Zalgaller [4, SS24.6] extended the notion of a Minkowski combination, and introduced, for each real p 1, what he called p sums. It is the aim of this series of articles to show that these Firey combinations lead to a Brunn Minkowski theory for each p 1.
