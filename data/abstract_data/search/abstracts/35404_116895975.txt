Big Data: A Revolution That Will Transform How We Live, Work, and Think
BIG DATA: A REVOLUTION THAT WILL TRANSFORM HOW WE LIVE, WORK, AND THINK by Viktor Mayer Schonberger and Kenneth Cukier Eamon Dolan Books, Boston, 2013,242 pp. ISBN: 978 0 544 00269 2I am fascinated by the concept and environment of big data. In simple terms: "Big data is not about trying to teach a computer to think like humans. Instead, it's about applying math to huge quantities of data in order to infer probabilities." (p. 12) Part of my fascination is that big data utilizes mathematical concepts and topics that I find interesting: modeling, data analysis, network analysis, artificial intelligence, and machine learning. In addition to shifting the modeling and decision making paradigms, it is the ultimate endorsement of the power of empirical modeling. In my perspective of mathematical modeling, big data propels empirical modeling way ahead of explicative modeling. And while this does not necessarily mean that scientific theory is dead, it does explain why many scientists, business leaders, and decision makers are into the big data collection mode: because by collecting all the data (not just a sample) and then testing many, if not all possible, models, the resulting information or conclusion is very reliable and robust. As the authors indicate, in the big data paradigm, "more trumps better" (p. 39) This leads them to conclude that "with information, as with physics, size matters" (p. 11) But it leaves us with the dilemma that by using big data methodology, we are able to determine what we should do but not why we should do it. In this book, the authors explain that resulting limitation and its consequences very well.This book carefully explains what big data is and what it is not: "big data is about predictions" (p. 11) and "the real revolution is not in the machines that calculate data but in the data itself and how we use it" (p. 7) And the question that is always considered is "How big is big?" Google is able to capture and store over 3 billion queries daily, so its data is big in just one day. And when Google looked at correlating locations of queries about flu symptoms with locations of HINI flu outbreak, it used billions of queries and 450 million different mathematical models to determine the best 45 search terms that would produce the best prediction of where the flu was occurring in real time, taking just minutes or hours, instead of the traditional and less reliable method that takes at least several weeks using the CDC reporting system. That makes this kind of data collection and processing both big and fast. And, according to the authors, things are continually getting bigger and faster.
