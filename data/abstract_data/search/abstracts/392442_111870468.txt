Healing Gardens in Hospitals
Following negative reactions to US institutional hospital design prevalent since the 1950s, a changed attitude, termed patient centered care, emerged in the 1990s. Therapeutic outdoor spaces, often called healing gardens, began to appear. Fifteen design guidelines some based on empirical evidence, others on common sense are described and illustrated. These guidelines encompass what we know at this time regarding the best way to design healing gardens in order to bring about positive health outcomes. Precedents currently being drawn upon by designers of healing gardens in the Western world are reviewed including the approach of creating patient specific gardens based on the medical diagnoses of those who will use them. Gardens specifically created for adult and child physical rehabilitation, for the treatment of depression, Alzheimer's disease, burns, HIV/Aids, and cancer are described and illustrated. Many areas that still need to be researched are summarised.
