Student evaluation of a professional studies lecture series delivered to final year veterinary undergraduates
Professional life skills' training is considered to be an important element of the veterinary curriculum by educators across the globe. Within the Bachelor of Veterinary Science programme at the University of Queensland, opportunities for the development of professional attributes exist throughout the curriculum, with dedicated professional studies courses delivered in Years 1 and 5. The final year course covers a range of topics related to the professional roles and responsibilities of veterinarians and the variety of non technical abilities relevant for practise. In 2007, this course included lectures, small group tutorials and a self directed research project. Student acceptance of content is thought to be a potential limitation for teaching within the discipline of professional studies. Therefore, a key objective of this study was to investigate student perceptions of the importance and relevance of topics covered within the lecture series. Student responses to a questionnaire were analysed using descriptive statistics and Pearson's correlation coefficient. Results showed that students appreciated the importance of content covered, believed that the lectures helped them to understand their veterinary role and responsibilities, and were, on the whole, enjoyable. Age, gender and intended field of practice was not correlated student perceptions.
