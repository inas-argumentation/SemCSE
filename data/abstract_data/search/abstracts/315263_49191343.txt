Radial discharging thermal characteristics of a small domestic oil storage tank
Discharging radial thermal profiles of a 40 litre domestic oil storage tank are presented. Low (0.6 L/min) and high (1.7 L/min) flow rates are used to discharge the oil storage tank. Radial temperature distribution results for both flow rates show that the temperature slightly increases radially from the tank inner wall to a maximum value which occurs at around 80 mm from the tank inner wall. After this increment, the temperature decreases as the radial distance increases. The higher flow rate eliminates the initial radial temperature rise at the bottom of the storage tank seen with the low flow rate due an increase in the axial heat transfer rate. Radial stratification number profiles show that radial thermal energy flow increases as the fluid flows down axially from the top to the bottom of the storage tank. The higher flow rate results in more axial thermal heat transfer at the expense of increasing radial thermal energy dispersion in the middle and bottom sections of the storage tank.
