The Price Perceived Quality Relationship: Evidence from U.S. Cheese Markets
We examine how price impacts consumer perception on product quality and therefore choice probability. A mixed logit model with behavioral conditions is developed to decompose the price effect into two channels: (i) a pure price effect where price affects the cost of obtaining the product, and (ii) a perceived quality effect where price affects the perceived quality of the product. The objective is to answer the following questions: (i) what is the marginal effect of price on the choice probability through the channel of price perceived quality relationship? And (ii) how does the impact on perceived quality change over repeat purchases? We utilize the Nielsen household panel and retail scanner data of the U.S. cheese markets from 2012 to 2014. The results suggest that with a $1 per pound increase in cheese price, the probability of choosing the product would on average decrease by 2.7% which consists of a 2.3% increase due to perceived quality effect. For about 10% of the products, the ratios of the perceived quality effect to the total price effect are greater than 1.9. This indicates a large heterogeneity of the price impact on perceived quality. In addition, we find that the perceived quality effect decreases after repeat purchases. We also discuss the implications on marketing strategy, government policy, and competition analysis.
