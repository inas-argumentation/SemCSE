Pulp chamber penetration by hydrogen peroxide following vital bleaching procedures.
The impact of fluoride is being felt with a decline in caries incidence over the past 25 to 30 yr. Restorative dentistry is focusing a great deal more on cosmetic applications, among which is bleaching of stained vital teeth. Although studies have shown that vital bleaching procedures can severely inhibit pulpal enzymes, deleterious effects have been minimal. This study attempts to measure the quantity of H 2 O 2 that reaches the pulp during a bleaching treatment. Roots of extracted maxillary anterior teeth were amputated approximately 3 mm apical to the cementoenamel junction and pulpal tissues were removed. A buffer solution was placed into the pulp chamber and the labial surface was exposed to 1, 10, or 30% H 2 O 2 at 37degC for 15 min. Hydrogen peroxide uptake by the pulpal buffer solution was determined colorimetrically.
