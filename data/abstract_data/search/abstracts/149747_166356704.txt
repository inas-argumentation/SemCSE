Privatizing Federal Spending: A Strategy to Eliminate the Deficit
The author presents a logical strategy for reducing federal spending by fostering attractive and feasible private sector alternatives for those who currently depend on federally sponsored programs. Dr. Butler proposes a series of practical measures for managing national assets more economically, and hence reducing the deficits, while still achieving public purposes. Among his recommendations are: Selling public housing to those who now rent; selling, or even donating, federal wilderness lands to qualified environmental groups like the Sierra Club and the Wilderness Society; turning over the postal service and other subsidized systems such as Amtrak to the private sector, eliminating the huge tax burden of operating these highly unprofitable services and improving efficiency virtually overnight; and the essence of privatizing is the notion of investing true ownership of an asset in a group of people other than "the nation." This group, in turn, has an incentive to manage the asset more economically. As a result public purposes are achieved at a lower cost to the government and thus to the taxpayer.
