Electrode potentials and interface plasmons in the metal/gaseous electrolyte (i.e. plasma) interphasic region
Abstract In a vacuum arc between two electrodes, the current across the electrodes is conducted through the plasma a mixture of ions and electrons at a high energy level. Essentially, this plasma behaves as a gaseous electrolyte providing the conducting path across the two electrodes within the arc. For the case, e.g. of metallic electrodes, two metal plasma interfaces thus arise the arc current enters the negative electrode i.e. the cathode) and leaves the positive electrode (anode) the cathodic interface becomes the site of highly energetic events involving erosion, ion bombardment, evaporation and ionization etc. of the metallic cathode. The metal plasma interface at the cathode behaves like a metal electrolyte interface with a characteristic interfacial potential drop (called the cathode fall) for every metal. This interface has been approached here in two ways: 1. (1) on the basis of an electrochemical model of the interfacial region; 2. (2) by treating the interface as a boundary between two plasmas (namely, the solid metal and the gaseous plasma) and deducing the resonant frequency of the plasma wave, and thence the interfacial plasmon energy and the associated potential drop, across the metal plasma interface. The two approaches are shown to yield comparable values for the cathode fall for the thirty two metals for which data are accessible. It is brought out that this cathode fall is nothing but the electrode potential value appropriate to the situation which, in turn, is of the same magnitude as the interfacial plasmon energy.
