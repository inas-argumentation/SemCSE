You're Fired: Some Airlines are Giving Economy Class the Pink Slip in Order to Boost Revenue
This article discusses how some airlines are cutting back or eliminating economy class customers in order to increase their yields. A leader in the movement is British Airways, which went against industry wisdom in the late 1990s by eliminating most of its cheap seats in favor of fewer seats with premium prices and amenities. The airline made over its cabins to offer lie flat beds and suites. It went from losing 21 million pounds in 1999 to making profits of 114 million pounds a year later. It has posted solid profit increases in the years since, with the exception of the September 11 period. Other legacy carriers are following suit, as ticket prices continue to fall relative to inflation, and consumers become more price sensitive, making it difficult for the larger, older airlines to compete on price alone. Qantas' A380s have 19 percent fewer seats than Airbus set as the marketing number of 555. Instead, it has more business class and more premium economy seats. Other airlines are doing similar changes to cabin configurations. Others, such as United Airlines, are offering last minute upgrade options at check in or even at the gate. Offering premium economy seats appeals to consumers willing to pay more but who are not interested in deluxe tickets that can run four or five times the cost of coach. Seat configurations allowing customers to purchase three across for privacy and more room are also being examined As the economic downturn worsens, more expensive business tickets may be replaced by the premium economy seats.
