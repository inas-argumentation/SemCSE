Sighted automation and fine resolution imaging
This paper discusses aspects of fine resolution imaging and associated data processing systems. The work of the authors was driven mostly by the military problem of automatic target acquisition and recognition, and this paper places some emphasis on the authors' contributions to synthetic aperture radar (SAR) developments from the 1950s through the 1970s. In addition to a discussion of the "SAR Story," the paper provides a perspective on the general role of fine resolution imaging in sighted automation. The following three important technical breakthroughs are included: (1) coherent optical data processing for SAR and more generally for holography; (2) coherent processing of pulse Doppler radar data during motion through resolution cells (decades in advance of related techniques for medical imaging) and (3) morphological image processing for automatic pattern recognition and for matched filtering of signal or, rather than plus, noise/clutter.
