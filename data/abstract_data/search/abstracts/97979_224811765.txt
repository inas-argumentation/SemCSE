This hypothesis was confirmed by the detection of a significant increase of microvessel and arteriole density in the contracted medial side of the relapsed idiopathic clubfoot (determining the functional heterogeneity of endothelial cells would be beyond the scope of this article) The increase in microvessel density was accompanied with a significant increase in the concentrations of angiogenesis related proteins in the contracted medial side of the relapsed idiopathic clubfoot namely VEGF, VEGFR 2, beta catenin and active betacatenin, together with the increase in the mRNA level of the 3 angiogenesis related genes namely VEGFA (Hs00900055_m1) KDR (gen for VEGFR 2) (Hs0091 1700_m1) and PECAM1 (gen for CD31) (Hs01065279_m1).1 These unique and significant findings of the changes in the composition of extracellular matrix proteins of the affected tissue in the relapsed clubfoot supported previously published data by our team that also confirmed upregulation of the collagen types III, V, and VI; in addition to the TGFb, TGFbip, and asporin overexpression.2 These areas of proangiogenic and fibroproliferative pathways were mentioned for the first time in connection with clubfoot.1,2 In our article, we described the methodology of the quantification of microvascular and arteriole density.1 We counted all the vessels with CD31 positive endothelial cells without muscular wall in the tissue sample. Arterioles were identified by the detection of their internal elastic lamina with the use of orcein and hematoxylin staining. We also counted all arterioles in the tissue sample, which were completely surrounded by dense irregular connective tissue. The relative number of microvessels and arterioles were expressed by their number per 1 cm. The methodology of microvascular density assessment is a recognized and widely used histological method, mentioned for example by Marien et al.3 The limits and benefits of this method were summarized in the review of Hlatky et al.4 Although microvessel density may not be an indicator of antiangiogenic treatment efficacy, it is assumed as a useful prognostic indicator.4 The aim of our study was to assess a difference in the vessel quantification between 2 different tissues, not to assess a subtle dynamic of microvessel quantification in time. The functional heterogeneity of endothelial cells, lining venous and arterial vessels in the contracted side of the relapsed clubfoot, was not mentioned and was not the aim of our research. As Grizzi et al5 mentioned, angiogenesis is a complex dynamic process and we would like to continue our research to also elucidate the functional heterogeneity of endothelial cells in clubfoot.
