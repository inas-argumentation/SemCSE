Long distance test of Bell's inequality using polarization entangled photon pairs in a 1550 nm band
We observed violation of Bell's inequality using polarization entangled photon pairs in a 1550 nm band. Accidental coincidences were included as detected. A 10.5 km fiber was used as a quantum channel for entanglement sharing. Quantum entanglement is the most important characteristic in quantum mechanics, and violation of Bell's inequality is the evidence of its non locality. Polarization entangled photon pairs are an essential resource for quantum teleportation [1 2] super dense coding [3 4] and quantum key distribution [5 7] A wavelength of 1550 nm is the most suitable for long distance transmission and sharing of entanglement. Spontaneous parametric down conversion is the most accessible method for obtaining such photon pairs; however bulk crystals suffer from low conversion efficiency and poor fiber coupling. Four wave mixing based on fiber nonlinearity is suited for fiber coupling [8 9] However, spontaneous Raman scattering dramatically increases the number of accidental coincidences, making it difficult to violate Bell's inequality. In this letter, a fiber optic two photon interferometer with two periodically poled lithium niobate (PPLN) waveguides is used for the generation of polarization entangled photon pairs in a band around 1550 nm [10] A 10.5 km dispersion shifted single mode fiber was used as a quantum channel, to share polarization entanglement between two remote parties. We observed violation of the Clauser, Horne, Shimony and Holt (CHSH) inequality [11] a particular form of Bell's inequality, without subtracting accidental coincidences in coincidence detection. Figure 1 shows the experimental setup. A light beam (772.5 nm) pumps each PPLN waveguide at ~300 mW (W1 and W2) The waveguide material is a congruent z cut lithium niobate (30 mm in length) Both facets are anti reflection coated for 772.5 nm and 1545 nm wavelengths. The fiber coupler (FC) used has a coupling ratio of 50/50. Two fiber optic polarization controllers (PC1 2) are adjusted to obtain the largest nonlinearity. At the outputs of the waveguides, long pass filters (LPF1 2) block 772.5 nm light beams. Polarization maintaining fibers (PMFs) are aligned such that a polarizing beam splitter (PBS) transmits photon pairs from W2 while reflecting those from W1. A voltage driven phase shifter (PS) is used to stabilize the interferometer [10] A short pass filter (SPF) is used with a cut off wavelength of 1550 nm, and two 1 nm band pass filters (BPF1 2) are centered at 1535 nm and 1555 nm, respectively.
