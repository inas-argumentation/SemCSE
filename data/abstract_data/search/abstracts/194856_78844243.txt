In addition, approximately 3113 pages are devotedto pharmacokinetic principles, a bit lightfor a thorough understanding; the paragraph devoted to plasma protein binding doesnotdiscuss theeffects of alterations of binding, wherethis issuebecomes important. Thispatternpervades thebook: mechanistic pharmacology is thoroughly andinterestingly treated,whileoptimization of therapyis essentially absent andadverseeffectsaredescribed without information on minimization or management. The text is logically arranged and authoritatively written froma diseasestandpoint, but it is inadequate froma therapeutic pointof view, and readers should useit onlyas background in lieuof morethorough therapeutics texts. Basicscience research is discussed well, but clinical trials aregenerally notmentioned. Whenindividual drugsare discussed, they are compared on the basisof pharmacologic, ratherthan system level, effects; clinical outcomes andadverse drugeffects are largely absent. It is herethat somepharmacist authorship couldimprove thebook'scompleteness andclinical utility. Some of theauthors betrayunawareness of clinical pharmacology despite a deepmechanistic pharmacologic understanding; forexample, the authorof theautism chapter refersto famotidineas an antihistamine. ComparIson with Other Related Books: This work can replace textbooks of medicine andneurology for understanding pathophysiology and biochemistry, but it will not surpass medicine and neurology texts fordescriptions of symptomatology or diagnostic criteria. Similarly, it is superior to standard pharmacology texts, butcannotreplace therapeutics texts for discussions of therapeutic choices,optimization, or managementof adverse outcomes. Reviewer'sSummary: The bookis useful either as a whole by those entering the neurology psychiatry fieldor as a reference for particular disorders. However, readersshouldprobably spendampletime on the firstchapter(Neural Principles) thisdiscussion is morethorough than thatoffered by mostmedicine or therapeutics texts,and a command of thischapter willgivereaders of therapeutics texts as wellas users of subsequent chapters in thisbooka morethorough grounding in neural pathwaysandneurotransmitters thancanbe hadasidefromspecialist texts. Clinical pharmacists willfindthechapter on Pharmacotherapeutic Principlesunnecessary. .The bookwill takephysicians withlittleknowledge beyond the traditional, mechanistic, basicpharmacology courses a giant stepfurther andwillenlarge pharmacists' basicscience understanding of drug actions. For the clinicianof eitherdiscipline, however, this book mustbe supplemented by readings in therapeutics texts thatdealwithindividualization and optimization of drug therapy, as its claimof "from bench to bedside"is not met. Yet, it will be a usefuladdition to the libraryof thosewitha serious interest in thisfieldand,if purchased with its limitations in mind, willnotsit idleon theshelf.
