Effect on hamster caries by purine derivatives vanillin and some tannin containing materials. Caries in relation to food consumption and animal growth.
Abstract In hamster experiments the following substances were tested for caries inhibitory effects: the purine derivatives theobromine, caffeine and xanthine, the phenolic aldehyde vanillin and the tannin containing materials tannic acid, which is a hydrolysable tannin, and mimosa and quebracho extracts, which are condensed tannins. Each substance was tested at various concentrations. A cariostatic effect without impairment of growth was found for xanthine at 0.2% for vanillin at 0.05 and 0.1% for tannic acid at 0.01, 0.02 and 0.05% for mimosa extract at 0.05% and for quebracho extract at 0.2% concentration. At higher concentrations the tannin materials lowered the growth rate. Theobromine and caffeine at 0.2% concentration also inhibited caries but at the same time they impaired the growth. The interpretation is thereby made difficult. Arguments are put forward for the view that the disturbance of growth would have a caries increasing effect and that thus the caries inhibiting effect per se really should be higher than appeared from the experimental results. In the control groups of the various experiments there was a significant increase of caries with lowered food consumption as well as with lowered growth. The hypothesis is put forward that in the smaller animal the natural tooth cleansing is less effective because the jaws, the tongue and the lips are smaller, the muscles are weaker and the salivary glands produce less saliva. The application of this point of view to the phenomenon of increased caries at lower age is discussed.
