Comparative study and modelling of machinability characteristics of single and double tempered AISI H13 steel during hard part finish turning using brazed tungsten carbide tip
Abstract The machinability of a material is highly influenced by the surface quality and material removal rate during the machining process. In any machining process, it is cardinal to have a thorough study about how the process parameters affect the machining quality. AISI H13 tool steel is a widely used as a die material at elevated temperatures in industries. A better surface quality of this dies will definitely be efficacious in ensuring product quality and smooth production lines. Thus, this study focuses on the comparative study of the effects of cutting speed, feed rate and depth of cut on surface roughness and material removal rate (MRR) on the hard part finish turning of single tempered and double tempered AISI H13 steel. Initially, annealed workpiece was hardened and tempered at a specified temperature. The machining was done using brazed uncoated tungsten carbide tip as the cutting tool material. Taguchi's L 25 orthogonal array was implemented for conducting the experiment with the aim of achieving minimal surface roughness (Ra) and maximize the material removal rate (MRR) by determining the optimal combination of input parameter for the desired response. To study the significant effect of the process parameters and the individual percentages contribution on the response parameters, analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed. Thus, the effect of two type of heat treatment on the machinability characteristics of the material was studied. Also, artificial neural network (ANN) models were developed for all the response parameters i.e. surface roughness and MRR which depicted propitious predicting potential for determining the required responses at any combination of cutting speed, feed rate and depth of cut.
