Studies on the formation of transfer ribonucleic acid ribosome complexes. IV. Effect of antibiotics on steps of bacterial protein synthesis: some new ribosomal inhibitors of translocation.
The effect of some antibiotics on several steps of protein biosynthesis was examined. Thiostrepton and siomycin A inhibited translocation through interference with ribosomal function; they also inhibited Phe tRNA binding to ribosomes and GTP hydrolysis dependent on ribosomes and factor G. Micrococcin inhibited translocation, but did not inhibit either the associated GTP hydrolysis or Phe tRNA binding to ribosomes. Althiomycin appeared to inhibit transpeptidation, although the inhibition was substrate dependent: it inhibited peptidyl transfer to C A C C A(Phe) but did not inhibit either acetyl phenylalanyl puromycin formation or GTP hydrolysis dependent on factor G and ribosomes. Bottromycin A2, carbomycin, chloramphenicol, griseoviridin, and lincomycin inhibited acetyl phenylalanyl puromycin formation, but not GTP hydrolysis dependent on ribosomes and factor G. It appeared that inhibitors of transpeptidation and some inhibitors of translocation (micrococcin for example) can uncouple GTP hydrolysis from functional translocation.
