Association between multiple cardiovascular risk factors and atherosclerosis in children and young adults
Atherosclerosis leading to cardiovascular disease is an important public health concern. Autopsy studies carried out on teenage adults during the Korean and Vietnam wars have demonstrated that atheroma is present in this age group but how soon do atherosclerotic changes start and what influences their development? Bogalusa is a town in Louisiana, USA, and the Bogalusa Heart Study is a long term epidemiological study of cardiovascular risk factors from birth to the age of 38 years. Seven cross sectional studies, each of 3 500 children, have been performed since 1973. Five follow up studies of volunteers from these cross sectional studies have also been done, with approximately 14 000 volunteer participants in total. Height, weight, blood pressure, serum lipid profile and cigarette smoking status have been recorded at each assessment. Autopsies have been carried out on volunteers who died most deaths were caused by accident or homicide to allow inspection of aortas and coronary arteries for the presence and extent of fatty streaks and fibrous plaques. Rather alarmingly, fatty streaks were almost universal irrespective of age whilst fibrous plaques increased in prevalence from about 5% at 2 15 years to about 60% at 26 39 years. The proportion of arterial intima having fatty streaks and plaques was found to be increased in the presence of risk factors such as raised serum lipids. For example, an average of 0.5% of intimal surface was affected by plaque formation in the absence of risk factors but this increased to 7.5% in the presence of three or more risk factors. A strong public health campaign aimed at the young has been advocated, recommending immediate action in favour of a suitable diet and against cigarette smoking. Bon appetit'l
