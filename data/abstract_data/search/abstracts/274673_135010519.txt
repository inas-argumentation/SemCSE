Enhanced Efficiency Fertilizers Effect on Yield, Nitrogen Use Efficiency and Greenhouse Gas Emissions in Aerobic and Puddled Rice (Oryza sativa L.
A field experiment was conducted during kharif 2013 at college farm, College of Agriculture, PJTSAU, Rajendranagar, Hyderabad. The soil was sandy clay loam in texture with 34.89% clay and slightly alkaline reaction with low electrical conductivity. The soil was medium in organic carbon content, low in available N, medium in available P and high in available K. Rice variety MTU 1010 was cultivated in split plot design with puddled and aerobic methods as main treatments and subplots treatments were T1control i.e. without addition of any N fertilizer; T2 nitrogen in the form of prilled urea (PU) T3Neem coated urea (NCCU) T4 Karanj coated urea (KCU) T5 Vitex negundo leaf extract coated urea (VCU) The treatments were replicated thrice and the plot size was 6 x 4 m. Recommended dose of 60 kg ha P2O5 and 60 kg ha K2O were commonly applied for all the treatments. Half of the N (60 kg ha) was applied as basal dose. Ferrous sulphate 0.5 was sprayed in aerobic rice as per normal package of practice. Gas flux from the soil was collected using closed chamber technique. Gas samples were taken from head space immediately after sealing at 0, 30 and 60 min intervals. The gas samples were analysed immediately in gas chromatography (Bruker 450) to prevent diffusion losses of gas. An incubation experiment was also conducted on the same soil at two moisture regimes i.e. field capacity and submerged conditions with six treatments viz. T1control i.e. without addition of any N fertilizer; T2 nitrogen in the form of prilled urea (PU) T3Neem coated urea (NCCU) T4 Karanj coated urea (KCU) T5 Vitex negundo leaf extract coated urea (VCU) T6 Castor oil coated urea (COU) in which nitrogen mineralization and GHG emissions were monitored at regular intervals till 102 DAI. Application of enhanced efficiency fertilizers significantly improved plant growth parameters and inturn grain yield over prilled urea applied plots under both puddled and aerobic rice cultivation. In aerobic rice, application of prilled urea showed significantly higher yield (4.02 t ha) than the plots without nitrogen fertilizer application (2.87 t ha) and significantly lower yield than all nitrification inhibitors. Significant difference among the nitrification inhibitors was not observed though highest grain yield was recorded in KCU (5.48 t ha) followed by VCU (5.13 t ha) and NCCU (5.08 t ha) The percentage increase in grain yield was 36.3, 27.6 and 26.4 with the application of KCU, VCU and NCCU, respectively over PU. The results suggest that the three nitrification inhibitors used in the present study were equally good and one can choose any one of them depending upon the availability of plant materials under aerobic condition. In case of puddled rice, application of prilled urea recorded significantly higher grain yield (4.52 t ha) than control (3.09 t ha) and significantly lower yield than nitrification inhibitors. Application of KCU registered significantly higher grain yield (6.35 t ha) than NCCU (5.68 t ha) and VCU (5.55 t ha) and were on par with each other. An increase in grain yield percentage of 40.5, 25.7 and 22.8 was realised by application of KCU, NCCU and VCU, respectively over PU. Under limited water resources, one can choose aerobic rice with addition of coated urea which resulted more yield than puddled rice with prilled urea application. Hence, water can be saved without losing grain yield and also reduces global warming potential (GWP) Significantly more uptake of N, P and K were observed in puddled rice than aerobic rice. Increased N, P and K uptake was also found with coated ureas application than prilled urea under both methods of rice cultivation. In aerobic rice all the coated ureas (Natural nitrification inhibitors) recorded 8.5 to 16% increase in agronomic efficiency over prilled urea (11.9% The highest agronomic use efficiency was observed in KCU followed by VCU and NCCU. Similar results were obtained with puddled rice also with an increase of 8.5 to 16% in agronomic efficiency over prilled urea (11.9% Aerobic rice cultivation was more economical with B:C ratio of 1.96 than puddled rice cultivation (1.5) The highest B:C ratio of 2.5 was found aerobic rice in combination of KCU followed by VCU (2.3) and NCCU (2.2) than prilled urea (1.7) The peak N2O fluxes were observed one weak after the application of N fertilizer to the crop. The cumulative N2O emission was significantly more in aerobic rice (0.95 kg ha season) than puddled rice (0.72 kg ha season) ie. 39.4% higher aerobic rice than puddled rice. Enhanced efficiency fertilizers viz. VCU, KCU and NCCU were found to be effective in reducing N2O emission by (55.3 and 47% (44.4 and 44.1% and (50.7 and 31.9% in aerobic and puddled rice, respectively over prilled urea. The peak daily flux of CH4 was observed at 50 DAT in puddled rice and 42 DAS in aerobic which coincided with active tillering stage of crop in both methods of rice cultivation. The cumulative methane emission was increased by 52% under puddled condition than in aerobic rice. The nitrification inhibitors mitigated methane emission under puddled condition application of VCU resulted in the lowest methane flux whereas under aerobic condition application of NCCU showed that lowest methane emission. Global warming potential of puddled rice (1486.28 kg CO2 eq. ha) was more than aerobic rice (894.09 kg CO2 eq. ha) Switching to aerobic rice instead of puddled rice reduces greenhouse gas intensity (GHGI) by 45% Under aerobic conditions NNI were more efficient in reducing GHGI than under puddled condition. At field capacity NH4 N content was lower and NO3 N was higher during entire incubation period compared to submergence. Significant correlation between reduction in NH4 N production of NO3 N was observed under both moisture regimes. Further incubation studies proved that the urea hydrolysis was delayed by one weak by enhanced efficiency fertilizers and they are effective in retaining NH4 N in mineral pool at higher concentration for longer period by reducing nitrification and denitrification. The total N2O emission was higher at field capacity than submerged condition in incubation studies. The nitrification inhibition was highest with NCCU at field capacity and under submerged condition highest nitrification inhibition was with KCU.
