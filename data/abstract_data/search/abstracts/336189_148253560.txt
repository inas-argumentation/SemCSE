Devaluing the Disruptive and Culture Implications for Stigmatization
This research examines how individuals who have unusual characteristics in American and Korean cultural contexts are evaluated by others. Research on perceptions of uniqueness (Kim Markus, 1999) and on cultural tightness (Gelfand et al. 2011) suggests that individuals who have an unusual characteristic (a disease or food sensitivity, being vegetarian or left handed) may be more stigmatized in East Asian than in American cultural contexts. Two studies directly assessed this proposition, and a third study began to examine the mechanism through which this occurs. The data suggest that individuals with unique characteristics are rated less positively in Korea than in the United States and that requesting special accommodations is particularly problematic in Korean cultural context. Implications for individuals with unique characteristics and for cross cultural conflict are discussed.
