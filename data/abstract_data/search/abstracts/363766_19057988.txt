Prediction of successful trial of labor after cesarean the benefit of prior vaginal delivery
Abstract Objective: To determine predictive factors for vaginal birth after cesarean section (VBAC) Methods: A retrospective cohort study of all women with singleton pregnancies and a prior single low transverse cesarean section (CS) who attempted vaginal delivery in a tertiary hospital (2010 2014) Pregnancy outcome of women with VBAC was compared to those who failed vaginal delivery. Sub analysis for women with no prior vaginal deliveries was performed. Pregnancies with non cephalic presentation, estimated fetal weight >4000 g and any contraindications for vaginal delivery were excluded. Results: Of the 40 714 deliveries, 1767 women met inclusion criteria. Among them 1563 (88.5% had a VBAC and 204 (11.5% failed. There was no significant difference between the groups regarding maternal age, comorbidities and pregnancy complications. Predictors for VBAC were (odds ratio, 95% confidence interval) interval from prior CS (1.13, 1.04 1.22, p=0.004) previous VBAC (2.77, 1.60 4.78, p 0.001) prior vaginal delivery prior to the CS (3.05, 1.73 5.39, p 0.001) and induction of labor (0.62, 0.40 0.97, p 0.03) For women with no prior vaginal birth, only birthweight was associated with VBAC (0.99, 0.99 1.00, p 0.02) Conclusion: While different variables may influence the rate of VBAC, the predictive ability of VBAC for women with no previous vaginal deliveries remains poor.
