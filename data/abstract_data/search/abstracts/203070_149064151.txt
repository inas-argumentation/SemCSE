Toward a Multidimensional Framework of Media Engagement: Conceptualizing Consumer Experience and Connection with Media Content in a Digital Environment
With the continued advancement in communications technology, today's consumers are faced with a media landscape characterized by a growing array of interactive multimedia devices, multiplatform and streaming media consumption, constant social media interactions, and a smartphone centric lifestyle. The complexity of media consumer behavior has increased tremendously by this multiplicity of devices, platforms, and contents, as well as the multitasking habit. The popularity and integration of social media into media platform functionality (e.g. YouTube video sharing and the Weather Channel Facebook community) further adds to the dynamic of the media consumption act. Recent research also suggested that consumers are now more likely to discover content in a continuous manner, and integrate media into their daily lives through social media, problem solving, social action, and entertainment (AP NORC, 2015) In other words, consumers increasingly focus on how combinations of media platforms play a role in their daily lives, rather than how the use of individual media delivers different gratifications. All of these developments mean that media companies now have more opportunities to connect with the audience. That is, there is a tremendous increase of quantity in contact points. Unfortunately, this also leads to serious fragmentation in both audience and attention. To succeed in such an environment, media companies now need to achieve a goal of "quality" instead of "quantity" when interacting with their audiences. The proposed study examines the concept and process of "engagement," which offers a "quality" evaluative approach in the study of consumer experience and connection with media content. The term "engagement" has been interpreted as connection, attachment, emotional involvement, and/or participation in the past (Brodie, Hollebeek, Juric, Ilic, 2011) There is an increasing number of engagement studies in recent years from the perspectives of consumer and brands, audience and media, and user and technology (Hollebeek, Glynn, Brodie, 2014; Napoli, 2011; O'Brien Toms, 2008) Many approaches have been adopted to examine the process of engagement. Overall, it is agreed that engagement is a multidimensional concept that involves three dimensions: cognitive, affective, and behavioral. While Vivek, Beatty, Dalela, Morgan (2014) proposed that conscious attention, enthused participation, and social connection to be the three phase progression of engagement, Hollebeek (2011) suggested that immersion (engrossed in) passion (love or adoration) and activation (willingness to spend time interacting) were the key elements of engagement. This study will conceptualize a media engagement process rooted in this cognitive affective behavioral tradition. Specifically, the study will propose a framework that explains the stages of media engagement starting from immersion to emotion, illumination, and then finally the motivation to act. It is a process from seeing the content, reacting to it, further ruminating it, and finally forming an action or intent based on the content consumed. The proposed framework depicts an ultimate progression but not all content would successfully go through all the engagement stages. The first phase, immersion, defined as a state of high concentration, is proposed here as the basic level of engagement when attention is a key factor. Emotion is the next phase of engagement, and one that we believe to be most critical for the success of media engagement for branding purposes. Many studies have found emotionally arousal elements to attract consumers' attention effectively (Teixeira, Wedel, Pieters, 2012) Duff and colleagues (2015) analyzed consumers' facial expression when watching advertising and found that delightful facial expression was correlated with higher rating of the ad. Moving from the basic affective aspect of engagement, the illumination stage of engagement presents a more direct and stronger connection between the media and audience through content. We proposed that such connections might be achieved through an informational/cognitive process, or profound engagement with an affective message that results in a deeper, transformational impact on individuals at the cognitive level. This level of engagement is a necessary condition for audience actions or any branded related behavior outcome. Illumination forms the basis of motivation to act. Finally, motivation to act can be considered as the final desired outcome of engagement. In a business context, motivation to act can be measured by usage/purchase intention or volitional word of mouth (WOM) recommendation (Sinthamrong Rompho, 2015) The study will also address how the proposed continuum of media engagement can be empirically examined by a triangulation of methods, including survey, implicit measures like facial coding and eye tracking, and qualitative methods. In addition, the paper will discuss the antecedents and effects of media engagement, especially in the context of brand outcome when the engagement occurs in a media environment containing brand messages.
