Rodolfo Fattovich, 1945 2018
I first met Rodolfo some sixteen years ago in 2002 as a first year student in the graduate programme of the Department of History Archaeology Unit at Addis Ababa University, Ethiopia. At the time, I was so inspired and honoured by meeting him because he had just been assigned to be the co supervisor of my MA thesis. Before this encounter, I had only known him from his writing and from the many times that my Ethiopian lecturers had mentioned him. I enjoyed working on my thesis with Rodolfo, appreciating his deductive reasoning, his capacity for linking facts and data and his prompt suggestions of literature that could answer my questions or inform me further about subjects related to African archaeology and heritage management. Luckily, Rodolfo then became my major supervisor when I joined the University of Naples L'Orientale for my PhD. Rodolfo was a meticulous field researcher. The evolution of early states in the north of the Horn of Africa and in Egypt (Fattovich 1997a, 2010) the development of early urban centres in the region, population contacts between ancient settlers on both sides of the Red Sea (Fattovich 1997b) the study of ancient landscapes and socio cultural and economic developments in the lowlands of Ethiopia and Eritrea (Fattovich 2006) are but some of the many topics with which he dealt intensively. His studies were always based on detailed chronological reconstructions following a culture historical approach and he was in the forefront of the debate regarding the definition of the term 'Pre Aksumite' in Ethiopian archaeology, something that has been controversial as it does not clearly indicate its prehistoric limits. Together with David Phillipson, Rodolfo also coined the term 'northern Horn' to refer jointly to Ethiopia and Eritrea, something well illustrated in his article on "The northern Horn of Africa in the first millennium BCE: local traditions and external connections" (Fattovich 2012) In Ethiopia, the Italian archaeological mission led by Rodolfo conducted research for over 35 years at the site of Bieta Giorgis adjacent to the World Heritage Site of Aksum. These continuous and uninterrupted missions have given us well documented data on the political, economic, cultural and religious past of the ancient people who settled in the northern Horn of Africa and since 2010 they have resumed work in the same area at another very productive site, Seglamen. The Italian Archaeological Expedition in Eastern Sudan (IAEES) was also initiated under Rodolfo's directorship as far back as 1980, organising a series of investigations at and around Kassala in northeastern Sudan that uncovered continuous settlement by a variety of population groups between the sixth millennium BC and the first millennium AD.
