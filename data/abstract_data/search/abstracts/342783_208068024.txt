Its classification has been controversial. Many entities of T cell or NK cell derivation do not have a specific immunophenotype. Clinical features are of major importance, being sometimes more relevant than the precise cell of origin. Angiocentricity appears to represent a peculiar finding in these lymphomas. In most of the cases the T cells contain the EBV transcripts EBER1. Gastrointestinal Tcell lymphoma seems to represent a clinicopathological entity within this group of lymphomas. We are reporting the case of a previously asymptomatic 5 year old boy presenting with spontaneous bowel perforation leading to extensive (1 meter) small bowel resection. At the same time hemophagocytic syndrome (HPS) was detected. Gross examination showed dilated and congestive bowel loops with numerous shallow erosions, ulcers, and two perforations. Microscopic study revealed a mediumsized, angiocentric, diffuse and multifocal, transmural T cell (CD45RO CD8 CD4 CD56 surface receptor EBV lymphoma with ulcers containing hemophagocytic histiocytes. Notably, scant polymorphonuclear leukocytes were noticed. B cells (CD20 were also scant and restricted to small lymphoid aggregates. RT PCR for TCR gene rearrangement regions detected clonality for V9 and V10/11 regions. Intervening mucosa was normal or showed partial villous atrophy due to lymphomatous infiltration. The patient underwent two more surgical resections of proximal small bowel due to perforations in the immediate postoperative period, the surgical specimens showing the same lesions. Extensive clinical and imaging work up did not show any other lesion. The HPS remitted after surgery. The boy is now under clinical follow up. Initial studies did not reveal any underlying primary immunodeficiency. The case seems to represent an example of what has been called "enteropathy associated T cell lymphoma like lymphoma without enteropathy," a condition not previously reported at this age. Avendano Universidad de Chile, Hospitales "Roberto del Rio" y "Exequiel Gonzalez C" Servicio Medico Legal, Santiago de Chile, Chile DOI: 10.1007/s10024 001 0051 0 Lower respiratory tract infection (LRTI) is the most common cause of hospitalization of infants in Chile. The role of viruses as a cause of death in this group of infants and in unexpected home death (UHD) is unknown. The objective of our study was to establish the cause of death in both groups, as well as to investigate the presence of viruses (respiratory syncitial virus [RSV] adenovirus [AdV] influenza [IV] and cytomegalovirus [CMV] which could be involved under this setting. We conducted complete autopsy as well as viral isolation and immunofluorescence of tracheobronchial aspirates in infants dying in their first year with diagnosis of LRTI (n 34) and UHD (n 100) at 2 pediatric hospital in Santiago de Chile and at the Medico Legal Department, between April 1988 and January 2000. In LRTI the cause of death was pneumonia in 28. Viruses were detected in 18/28 (64% AdV 9, RSV 8, PIV 1. Pneumonia was present in 28/28, the lesions being characteristic for the detected virus in all cases due to AdV and in 6/8 due to RSV. Of the 100 UHD cases, 83% were diagnosed as SIDS, 10% as bacterial pneumonia, 3% as congenital heart disease, and 3% as sepsis. Viruses were found in 9 cases of SIDS (10% RSV in 3, PIV in 4, and AdV in 2. Associated risk factors were prematurity and ventral positioning. During 1999, death from LRTI diminished from 20 to 7 cases, and AdV was never detected. The finding of viruses in SIDS seems to be incidental. The low frequency of viral infections in UHD contrasts with their high prevalence in LRTI. The aim of this study is the morphological and morphometrical analysis of the BMEA to establish a possible correlation between the basal membrane thickening and poor fetal outcome in the perinatal period. Ninety placentas were studied with H E and PAS methods. The morphometrical analysis was performed with KS 300 equipment. EM examination was performed on normal and basal lamina thickened cases. Out of 90, 11 (12.2% were found to be normal, whereas 79 (87.7% showed alterations and poor fetal outcome. The thickness of the BMEA was significantly greater p 0.030) in cases with alteration (6.6 4.6 m) than in the normal group (3.9 2.7 m) In 39 (43.3% placentas there were inflammatory exudates in various placental compartments. The BMEA thickness was significantly p 0.01) greater in the cases with chorioamnionitis (9.5 4.9 m) and villitis (6.6 3.8 m) cases. Clinically, maternal alterations cases were identified in 33 (33.6% and the BMEA was significantly greater p 0.027) in the suspected maternal infection cases. Garrahan" Buenos Aires, Argentina DOI: 10.1007/s10024 001 0054 x Budd Chiari syndrome is not frequent in childhood. It includes a group of conditions inducing hepatic venous outflow obstruction. Three different clinical presentations are recognized: acute, chronic, and fulminant. Liver biopsy is an important diagnostic method to evaluate the severity of the disease and to aid treatment decisions. We report the clinical, echo angiographic, and histological findings in the pretransplant biopsies and liver explants of two girls (34 and 25 months) with BuddChiari syndrome. The patients presented with painful hepatomegaly, abdominal swelling, ascites, and deep venous thrombosis in the inferior left limb in one case. US showed heterogeneous hepatomegaly and hypertrophic caudate lobe in both cases. The angiography showed portal hypertension in both, stenosis of the inferior vena cava in one, and thrombosis of the right and middle suprahepatic veins in the other. Paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria, myeloproliferative disorders, factor V Leiden mutation, antiphospholipid antibodies, and presence of anticoagulant transient lupus were all excluded by laboratory studies. In both patients the histology showed lobular fibrosis, severe sinusoidal dilatation, and hepatocellular atrophy, suggesting chronic Budd Chiari syndrome.
