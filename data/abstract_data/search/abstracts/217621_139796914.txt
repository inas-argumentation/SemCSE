The effects of temperature on friction and wear mechanisms during direct press hardening of Al Si coated ultra high strength steel
Abstract Direct press hardening is a non isothermal sheet metal forming method which combines forming and heat treatment in a single process. However, due to the high temperatures during the forming phase, tool wear is severe and friction is high. In this paper, hot strip draw tests are utilised to assess the influence of the forming temperature on the coefficient of friction (COF) and active wear mechanisms during sliding of Al Si coated press hardening steel (PHS) strip in contact with uncoated tools under typical hot forming process conditions. The COF is found to be temperature dependent during initial sliding against a virgin tool surface. Whereas, for 10 consecutive strip draws, COF is only temperature dependent for the first samples over the temperature range from 400 degC to 750 degC. This would be due to the tribolayers which form in the tool sheet contact during the test series.
