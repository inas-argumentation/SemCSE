Corporate Taxation and Bank Outcomes: Evidence from U.S. State Taxes
We examine the association between corporate taxation and bank outcomes, including lending growth, leverage, liquid asset holdings, and risk taking. To study these relations, we employ a sample of single state U.S. commercial banks and exploit cross sectional and inter temporal variation in U.S. state income tax rates, a setting that allows us to better isolate the effect of taxation from other correlated factors. Overall, we fail to find that changes in income tax rates impact bank activities on average. However, we find compelling evidence that the tax rate has significant effects on specific types of banks and during periods of economic downturn and credit risk uncertainty, highlighting potentially significant policyrelevant implications for regulators and policy makers. Our collective findings demonstrate how corporate income taxation affects bank outcomes, such as lending and leverage, which in turn affect the capital available for both non bank corporations and individuals
