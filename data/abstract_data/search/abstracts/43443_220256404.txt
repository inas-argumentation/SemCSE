INTRAPARENCHYMAL EPIDERMOID CYST CLOSE TO THE BROCA'S AREA AWAKE CRANIOTOMY AND GROSS TOTAL RESECTION.
BACKGROUND Epidermoid Tumors (or Epidermoid Cysts) are benign, slow growing, congenital and rare lesions, that represent approximately 0.2 1.8% of all intracranial tumors. Intraparenchymal ECs are very rare lesions that may account for 1.5% of all intracranial epidermoid tumors and the frontal lobe involvement was found in 39.2% of intraparenchymal ECs. We present a case of an awake craniotomy procedure to pursuit a maximal safe gross total resection of a rare intraparenchymal EC close to Broca's area in a bilingual patient. CASE DESCRIPTION A 45 year old man presented with a generalized seizure episode. Initially treated with levetiracetam what led to renal failure. Imaging findings demonstrated an intraparenchymal left frontal EC with peripheral coarse calcifications at the Broca's area. As the patient was bilingual and had a normal neurologic examination, we performed a left frontal awake craniotomy under local anesthesia with the purpose to mapping both languages, using the motor task and a test for language monitoring, alternating a naming task in Portuguese and English and a semantic task in Portuguese. A gross total resection was achieved with no neurological deficits. Histopathological examination confirmed the diagnosis of an epidermoid cyst. After 1 year, the patient still seizure free. CONCLUSIONS The awake surgery proved to be a useful tool for complete resection of the capsule even in a very eloquent language area. In bilingual patients with benign intra axial lesions, an intra operative mapping should be performed for all the languages the patient is fluent in to avoid postoperative neurologic deficits.
