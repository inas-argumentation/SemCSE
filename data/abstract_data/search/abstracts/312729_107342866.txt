CRACK AND SEAT METHOD OF PAVEMENT REHABILITATION
The Illinois Department of Transportation, in cooperation with the Federal Highway Administration, conducted a study to evaluate the effectiveness of the crack and seat method of pavement rehabilitation. Between 1983 and 1988 six projects were constructed and monitored for the study. The subject pavements were observed prior to and during construction, and were visually inspected biannually after construction to monitor the occurrence and progression of reflective cracking. In addition, deflection tests were run biannually after construction with a falling weight deflectometer. This article describes the construction procedure, the location and details of the six projects, the performance of the test sections, and the project results. This is followed by a brief discussion of the results and conclusions from the study. Based upon the observations made on the six experimental sections, several recommendations are made: a 100 ft test section should be designated on each job; a light spray of water should be applied to the test section after cracking to highlight the crack pattern; certain precautions must be taken when cracking through an existing asphalt overlay (these are outlined) guillotine type hammers of heavy, guided, free falling drop weights capable of producing full width, full depth cracking, are recommended for the cracking process; traffic can safely be maintained adjacent to the pavement breaker and also allowed on the cracked pavement prior to seating and the cracked and seated pavement prior to overlaying; one to five one way passes per lane with a 35 ton rubber tire roller should be sufficient to seat the cracked pieces; use of the crack and seat process on reinforced concrete pavements is not recommended at this time; and overlay thicknesses should be carefully designed on the basis of traffic, subgrade support, and material properties.
