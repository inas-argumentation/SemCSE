Nanoscience in the era of global science and global change Cooperative, quantitative, and focused on benefit to humanity
Nanoscience has emerged as a powerful new discipline that unites and empowers conventionally disparate scientific and engineering disciplines with a common perspective. Our foundation is centered around the twin goals of discovering and understanding the fundamental scaling laws for the behavior and properties of matter as it is controlled on the nanoscale, and on developing the ability to synthesize artificial nanoscale building blocks in a controlled manner. The early successes of this approach were rapidly followed by the development of a wide range of new applications of these materials. Personally, I have found it very rewarding to be involved in the development of colloidal quantum dots as one of the major building blocks of nanoscience, in the early harnessing of these in biological imaging that benefits medical research, and in color displays that save energy and render the images of our modern world more beautiful. I am excited to be part of the community quest for a much higher efficiency solar energy harvester than today's Si technology, while keeping cost low. If we are successful, we will help humanity to have access to energy in a way that is sustainable and renewable and that can be accessed personally as well as on a large scale. I am deeply grateful to the nanoscience community in China for honoring me last year at your annual symposium. When I was asked to contribute an article to this special issue of the journal Nano Research, I considered carefully how I could contribute best with just one brief publication to the nanoscience community in China. This perspective is dedicated to the early career researchers in nanoscience in China. I wish to share my thoughts with you about the world you are entering, the world you will in many ways define, and the opportunity and even responsibility you have to harness your skills to address global problems in a cooperative way. Yours is the first generation to grow up in a world where we have understood fully that human activity has the power to influence the ecosystems of our planet on a global scale. The challenges of global climate change, widespread species extinction, and the persistence of poverty, are all issues that urgently call upon the world of science to engage, and for scientists to imbue their work with a focus on seeking solutions that will benefit humanity and protect our ecosystems. We have many reasons to be optimistic about this quest. Foremost among these is the strengthening of the global science culture and the spirit of cooperation in science across boundaries not only of scientific disciplines, but also across cultural and geographic boundaries.
