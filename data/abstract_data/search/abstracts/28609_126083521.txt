Mass transfer, physical, and mechanical characteristics of terebinth fruit (Pistacia atlantica L. under convective infrared microwave drying
This research was investigated to the thin layer drying of terebinth fruit under convective infrared microwave (CIM) conditions with initial moisture content about 4.28% (g water/g dry matter) The effects of drying different conditions were studied on the effective moisture diffusivity, activation energy, specific energy, shrinkage, color, and mechanical properties of terebinth. Experiments were conducted at three air temperatures (45, 60, and 70 degC) three infrared power (500, 1000, and 1500 W) and three microwave power (270, 450 and 630 W) All these experiments were carried out under air velocity of 0.9 m/s. The effective moisture diffusivity of terebinth was obtained as 1.79 x 10 9 to 15.77 x 10 9 m2/s during drying. The activation energy of terebinth samples was measured to be 12.70 to 32.28 kJ/mol. To estimate the drying kinetic of terebinth, seven mathematical models were used to fit the experimental data of thin layer drying. Results showed that the Midilli et al. model withR2 0.9999,kh2 0.0001 andRMSE 0.0099 had the best performance in prediction of moisture content. Specific energy consumption was within the range of 127.62 to 678.90 MJ/kg. The maximum shrinkage during drying was calculated 69.88% at the air temperature 75 degC, infrared power of 1500 W, and microwave power 630 W. Moreover, the maximum values of the DL* (15.89) Da* (12.28) Db* 0.12) and total color difference (DE= 17.44) were calculated in this work. Also, the maximum rupture force and energy for dried terebinth were calculated to be 149.2 N and 2845.4 N.mm, respectively.
