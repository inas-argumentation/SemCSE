QUEST INTO PEPTIDE MOLECULES: UNSEEN IMPORTANT TARGETS IN ORGANIC GEOCHEMISTRY
Scientific objectives. Protein is most abundant nitrogen compounds in living systems making up over 50 on a dry weight prokaryotic biomass. Before breaking down to amino acid monomers (e.g. Goodfriend et al. 2000) or building up processes as a molecular evolution (e.g. Meierhenrich 2008) peptides are key intermediates and metastable molecules between protein polymers and amino acid monomers. The importance of peptide molecules is now widely accepted in biochemistry, therefore, direct qualitative detection and quantitative determination are required for providing a new insight of unseen aspects in organic geochemistry. Dipeptides are the smallest peptides consisting of two amino acids, hence, theoretically 400 dipeptides are possible combinations. To refine the original molecular information through an accurate analytical procedure, we have developed the method as underivatized formula, not as derivatized molecules. For the chromatographic separation of underivatized dipeptides, we used an improved method using an ion pair liquid chromatography electrospray ionization mass spectrometry (LC/ESI MS; Agilent Technologies Inc. and/or a corona charged aerosol detector system (LC/CAD) We used a reversed phase Hypercarb column (4.6 x 150 mm, particle size 5 mm; stationary phase, porous graphitic carbon, Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc. and a guard column. The advantage of determination of underivatized formulas are high through put and high recovery of the analytical procedure without derivatisation.
