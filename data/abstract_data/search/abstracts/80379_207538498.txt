Medical Improv: A Novel Approach to Teaching Communication and Professionalism Skills
Two residents are speaking to each other in gibberish, and 2 others are struggling to translate this exchange into Englishwhich is wonderful, because it means that they are successfully doing an improvisational theater exercise called Foreign Movie. The constraints of this exercise focus the gibberish speaking trainees on what they can say through body language and vocal inflection and focus the translators on what they can hear through nonverbal communication. Throughout the scene, inevitable disjunctures between intended and received messages generate laughter and insight. These physicians are doing an exercise usually reserved for actors because they're engaged in a novel training method called medical improv. Medical improv is the adaptation of improvisational theater principles and exercises to enhance such medical skills as communication, teamwork, and cognition. A medical improv course adapts theatrical improv exercises to health care in 3 ways: Some exercises are done exactly as they are in theater, followed by a medical debriefing that highlights their applicability to the health care context; some exercises are modified in a way that makes them applicable to the health care context; and new exercises are created using the principles of improv to target specific medical communication goals. Patient centered communication has been shown to improve clinical outcomes, chronic disease management, and patient satisfaction (1, 2) and communication breakdown has been correlated with serious medical errors (3) and malpractice suits (1) A review of meta analyses and systematic reviews incorporating 222 studies concluded that experiential strategies like role play are the most effective ways to teach communication skills (2) Medical improv is one such learning strategy. It also has unique elements that could make new contributions to communication and professionalism training. In contrast to role play, which replicates the complexity of a full medical encounter, medical improv isolates the building blocks of communication and professionalism skills and practices them in focused drills. For example, learners in one exercise practice recognizing and naming another speaker's emotion, a skill that can later be integrated into a more complex exchange. Another exercise, in which learners pass and receive verbal information in complex patterns to practice clear speaking and careful listening, might also teach skills immediately applicable to safe patient handoff. Medical improv fills a training gap, providing an opportunity to develop and practice the components of communication skills before integrating them in the dress rehearsal of role play and simulation or performance with real patients and health care teams. Medical improv also functions as a form of cross training. In contrast to role play and simulation focused on medical encounters, medical improv exercises often use nonmedical prompts and scenarios. This method gives medical learners the freedom to experiment with new approaches to old goals before implementing them in context. The exercises often generate the side effect of laughter. This aspect makes the training fun, but medical improv is not about teaching comedy. It is about learning to quickly connect with others to work collaboratively, creating emotionally honest, unscripted interactions. This method intentionally aims to establish a safe, lighthearted learning environment to facilitate risk taking, then invites learners to tackle difficult skills and tasks like adaptability, spontaneity, deep listening, building trust, and teamwork. Another strength of medical improv is its potential to increase physicians' comfort level and skills with uncertainty, ambiguity, and surpriseall aspects of medical practice. It provides clinicians with tools to go off script, which does not mean throwing away the goals of the encounter or skipping critical information. Instead, it is an advanced clinician's ability to spontaneously change the way information is typically ordered or delivered in response to a particular patient's needs (4) Medical improv might help clinicians interact with patients and colleagues in a more genuine, humane, and flexible way. Preliminary evidence suggests that medical improv deserves further study to determine whether it is an effective training method. Eighty three of 87 medical students at Northwestern University who took a 10 hour medical improv seminar reported that they believed it could make them better physicians (5) and 23 of 27 medical students who took an elective 8 hour improv course at Johns Hopkins designed to respond to the gap that currently exists in interview skills training programmes believed that the concepts addressed were very much or tremendously relevant to patient care (6) A pharmacy school that introduced 12 hours of improv into its communication course reported improved scores on standardized patient examinations (7) Experienced physicians have also begun noting how improv training helps their practice, describing it as a powerful, instructive, and enjoyable tool for acquiring the attitudes and skills that empathic, successful physicians need (8, 9) These initial reports mark medical improv as a rich area for future outcomes research with standardized patients and randomized groups of trainees to assess whether it measurably changes skills and behavior. Medical improv was first developed with undergraduate medical students but can also be used in graduate and continuing medical education. Efforts to develop specialty specific milestones for the Accreditation Council for Graduate Medical Education core competencies are under way, and medical improv might contribute to a language and framework for assessing trainee proficiency in communication and professionalism. Further work might explore new curricula with residents and experienced clinicians on such topics as interdisciplinary teamwork, communication during end of life care, behavioral change counseling, empathy training (4) reducing diagnostic errors, physician wellness, and ethics consultation. Medical improv is an exciting new training approach because it may address urgent needs in patient care and, as one student course evaluation put it, The laughs help the learning process (5)
