Helping Adolescents Make it Home.
The author argues that literature of nature teaches students about life and home. ach semester, I face a new group of tenth graders from across the country who have come to The Outdoor Academy of the Southern Appalachians tostudy in the woods. In the valley beneath Looking Glass Rock, I and my colleagues teach these young people how to learn about and appreciate this area of the Blue Ridge so that, when they return to St. Louis, Baltimore, Nashville, Orlando, and Albuquerque, these new skills can be focused on their landscape and their people. For any number of reasons, these students are frequently disillusioned with where they live and speak of their semester away as a reprieve from whatever it is family problems, peer pressure, concrete and smog they wish to escape. The objective of the Outdoor Academy is to teach these students that making a home out of a place takes effort, and that connecting oneself to the land and community can be a strong antidote to the teenage despair they express. As an English teacher and as an individual living in a culture permeated by what Barry Lopez calls "the most insidious of human anxieties, the one that says, you do not belong here, you are unnecessary," I am grateful to the ecocritics for calling attention to interdependence with the land as a vital cultural and spiritual issue (1989, 150) Lawrence Buell, author of The Environmental Imagination: Thoreau, Nature Writing, and the Formation of American Culture (1995) perceives a shift in critical theory from the traditional elements of character, theme, and plot, to a concentration on "the sense of place" (Parini 1995, 52) I propose integrating these issues of place and belonging in a concrete and immediate way into the high school English curriculum. Nature writers such as Barry Lopez, Wendell Berry, Gary Snyder, and Scott Russell Sanders are authors I rely on in the classroom because they deal directly with the difficulty and importance of finding oneself at home in a twentieth century universe.
