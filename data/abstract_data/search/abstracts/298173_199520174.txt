Atomistic Structural Analysis of Cathodic LiCoPO 4 Thin Films for Lithium Ion Secondary Batteries
Rechargeable lithium ion batteries have been developed for use as microbatteries in portable electronic devices for more than two decades. They are also being developed for use in larger scale applications such as fully electric vehicles and hybrid electric vehicles because of their high energy density and high power density. Successful development of solid state batteries based on multilayer configurations of electrodes and electrolyte requires a thorough understanding of the influence of microstructure and atomistic structure on battery performance. For example, cathode substrate and cathode electrolyte interfaces are known to affect charge/ discharge rates, cyclability and battery lifetime. Olivine structured orthophosphates LiMPO4 (M Mn, Fe, Co and Ni) are attractive cathode materials for replacing the conventional cathode material, LiCoO2, because of their high redox potential for Li ion intercalation of ca. 4.8V vs. Li/Li and high theoretical specific capacity of around 170 mAh/g [1] Olivine type LiMPO4 contains distorted MO6 and LiO6 octahedra and PO4 tetrahedra, where each PO4 tetrahedron and each MO6 octahedron is bonded by edge sharing, and lithium ions can migrate through one dimensional tunnels along the [010] direction. This low dimensionality for Li ion transport makes it imperative that the microstructure of LiMPO4 films be carefully controlled, especially the crystal orientation, to provide optimal stable battery performance. Recently, high angular annular dark field (HAADF) and annular bright field (ABF) techniques by scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM) have been developed, which provide atomic resolution detail when the microscope is equipped with an aberration (Cs) corrector. These techniques make it possible to identify Li ion columns within the crystal structure, so that microstructures of battery materials can be analyzed with unprecedented precision [2] For example, HAADF and ABF imaging of LiMn2O4 powders and thin films have been used to examine how the film/substrate lattice misfit affects the degree of thin film epitaxy [3] Direct observation of atom columns revealed that epitaxial LiMn2O4 films form atomically flat and coherent heterointerfaces with different single crystal substrates, but that in each case the crystal lattice is distorted with a compositional gradient in the film near the interface [4] In this study, LiCoPO4 cathodic thin films were fabricated and their crystal structure and microstructures and orientation relationship between film and substrate were analyzed. The thin films were fabricated from [Li Co P O] precursor solution by spin coating onto cleaned Au/Al2O3 (0001) substrates. The precursor films were then transferred to a single zone tube furnace and heated at 350 degC for 1 h and then annealed at 700 degC for 1 h. X ray diffraction (XRD) measurements of the heat treated films were performed with Cu Ka radiation using a Rint2000 (Rigaku, Tokyo, Japan) diffractometer operating at 50 V and 300 mA. STEM observations were performed using a JEM 2100F microscope equipped with a Cs corrector (CEOS GmbH) The peak intensities of XRD patterns of the thin films were different than those of the polycrystalline powder. In particular, (210) (101) and (020) diffraction peaks were much higher for the thin film than the powder.
