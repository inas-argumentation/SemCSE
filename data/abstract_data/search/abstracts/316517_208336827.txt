Scandinavian Journal of Immunology 2019 A Norwegian Winterreise and increased impact factor
Unlike the sad and love deprived youngster in German poet Wilhelm Muller's 'Winterreise' (set to music in 1927 by German composer Franz Schubert) immunologists walking up to Dr Holm's Hotel in Geilo to register for the 45th Scandinavian Society for Immunology meeting displayed happy faces. The early days of April offered mountains still covered in snow and some conference participants, including this journal's Editor In Chief, brought own skis (others rented on spot) to enjoy the last winter days of the year, in between lectures. Dr Holm's hotel was built in 1909 by Dr Ingebrigt Christian Lund Holm, an innovative physician specializing in respiratory diseases. Undoubtedly, many of Dr Holm's patients suffered from infection by M tuberculosis, and in his hotel, these patients could rest, breathe fresh mountain air and recover rapidly from disease. Increased public knowledge, better sanitary conditions and antibiotics has lowered the prevalence of M tuberculosis infections since Dr Holm's days, but still, 10 million people are infected, and 1.5 million killed by this disease annually.1 The Scandinavian Journal of Immunology regularly publishes new research in the tuberculosis field, on vaccines,2 disease pathogenesis3,4 and relation to HIV infection.5 Dr Holm's legacy, and the use of his sanatorium as a conference venue, therefore ties nicely into present day immunology and into our journal's objective to provide cutting edge new knowledge in human immune related disorders. The Scandinavian Journal of Immunology has a long tradition to support the annual meetings of the Scandinavian Society for Immunology, at which we take part in scientific discussion, invite article submissions and conduct strategic discussions. We also publish the abstracts from each meeting. In this issue, you can find the abstracts from Geilo in a separate section, covering many aspects of modern immunology. A few months after the Geilo meeting, the 2018 impact factors were released, and we note with satisfaction a factor of 2.563 for the Scandinavian Journal of Immunology, which should be compared with 2.314 in 2017. On behalf of myself and all editors, I wish to thank our contributors, readers and reviewers for your trust in the Scandinavian Journal of immunology. I look forward to continue a new year together with you!
