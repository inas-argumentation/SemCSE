Joint strength of Al Mg Si alloys for automobiles by advanced welding technologies
In the field of automobile manufacturing, characteristics required for the joining of aluminium are: the obtainability of good joint quality; the superiority in terms of costs and productivity, for example in higher welding speed; freedom in terms of design, for example in higher weldability with various shapes of joints; the freedom in terms of execution, for example in higher weldability with greater root gaps and so on. On the other hand, the welding techniques for aluminium have advanced rapidly. For example, the development of laser welding with high energy density and friction stir welding which is a non fusion welding method has progressed significantly. With the laser/MIG hybrid welding (Fig. 1) which has the advantageous characteristics of both laser and arc welding, there is even an example of application to the joining of aluminium components for automobiles. In addition, tandem MIG welding (Fig. 2) has been applied to service welding which makes it possible to conduct welding faster than with usual MIG welding, by using a torch with two insulated contact tips which generate arcs simultaneously with the wires fed by two welding machines to form a single molten pool for welding. Since these welding methods are low heat input welding methods and do not have much thermal effect on the base material, extrusion and the ability to recycle are very good. They are expected to be applied to much of the welding of Al Mg Si group alloys which are considered for use in automobile manufacturing and make it possible to obtain joints of high quality owing to much less softening attributable to welding heat. By applying the welding methods for structural aluminium which have been well discussed in recent years, for example, laser welding,friction stir welding, laser/MIG hybrid welding and tandem MIG welding, butt welded joints of Al Mg Si were actually produced to investigate the static tensile characteristics of joints and to compare them with those in the conventional methods, that is, MIG welding and TIG welding in terms of the extent of the thermal effect on the base material. This article reports the results of the investigation into how much the heat softened zone was recovered by post welding heat treatment.
