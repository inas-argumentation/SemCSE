2011 (Spine. 2011;36(24):2084 2088) a simple retrospective study examining whether applying vancomycin powder around the surgery site prior to closure reduced infection rates. A total of 1732 patients underwent posterior spinal instrumentation fusions at one institution by 3 surgeons. One group consisted of 821 patients from 2000 to 2006 receiving standard preand postoperative IV cephalosporin prophylaxis for 24 hours, while the second group consisted of 911 patients from 2006 onward that received the standard IV prophylaxis but also had 1 gm of vancomycin powder mixed into the iliac crest bone graft as well as 1 gm directly applied to the exposed surgical site, including dura, muscle, hardware etc. Patients were followed with serum and surgical site fluid (via drain output) vancomycin levels for 3 days after surgery in the second group to assess for systemic absorption in 178 patients. Patient demographics were mainly consistent between the 2 groups with respect to levels of fusion, type of fusion, indication for surgery and comorbidity status and smoking status. Deep surgical site infections occurred in 2.6%of patients with standard IV cephalosporin prophylaxis and in 0.2% of patients with standard IV cephalosporin prophylaxis and vancomycin powder application. Serum vancomycin levels were only detectable in 20% of patients having received the powder treatment on POD 1 with only 6% having detectable levels after POD 1. Intrawound levels of vancomycinwere high onPOD0 to 1, reducing significantly by POD 3. No additional complications such as higher pseudoarthrosis, cerebrospinal fluid leak, radiculitis, or renal toxicity were noted in the powder treatment group. This single institution, retrospective longitudinal study nicely suggests how a $12 per patient cost can potentially reduce surgical site infections by over 10 fold. Though there are the usual shortcomings of a study of this type and design, the potential of such an easily accessible application to reduce infections in spinal fusions is too important to criticize. This kind of low cost, low complexity intervention is refreshing in an ever growing environment of escalating costs and increasing scrutiny.
