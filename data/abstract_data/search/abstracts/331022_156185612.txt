Polis Guards and Sentiment Purification:On the Definition of Tragedy in Aristotle's Poetics
The definition of tragedy is the most controversial puzzle in Aristotle's Poetics. In fact,the biggest obstacle to comprehending Aristotle's poetics is established by the modern western academic circles. The modern western scholars regard Poetics as the literary theory in the contemporary sense,believing that Aristotle deals with the problem of modern drama theory. Starting from a linguistic analysis of its original meaning,the paper aims to restudy the definition of tragedy. By referring to Aristotle's Politics and Rhetoric as well as Plato's Republic in particular,it displays the significance of political philosophy that lies behind Aristotle's definition of tragedy. It concludes that the sentiment purification in tragedy implies the cultivation of temperament among the statesmen.
