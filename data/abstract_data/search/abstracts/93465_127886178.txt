Ab initio phasing
The background and use of dual space direct methods (also known as Shake and Bake) for the ab initio phasing of small proteins as well as the phasing of heavy atom substructures of large proteins is described. Basic concepts include normalized structure factors, multisolution procedures, random trial structures, phase refinement formulas, peak picking techniques, alternation of phase improvement in reciprocal and real space, and recognizing solutions. Two independent computer programs which implement the Shake and Bake algorithm, SnB and SHELXD, are compared and typical parameters are given. Other topics discussed are the use of Patterson information to get better starting phases, avoiding false minima, the effects of data resolution and completeness, special features of space group P1, and refinement strategies.
