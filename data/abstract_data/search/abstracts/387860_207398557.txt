The role of complex networks in behavior epidemiology: Comment on "Coupled disease behavior dynamics on complex networks: A review" by Z. Wang et al.
Outbreaks of disease can trigger spontaneous behavioral response of individuals to consider prevention measures (mainly including medical cure and non pharmaceutical intervention) which usually in turn influence the diffusion of epidemic, namely, forming the interplay between individual behaviors and epidemic dynamics. During the past decade, understanding such coupled disease behavior dynamics in population has become a critical tool for predicting the disease evolution and designing effective prevention strategies [1 3] Moreover, it has been well known that contact network topology plays a vital role in the spread of epidemic, it can even determine the outbreak threshold and the size of the disease [4,5] As the recent review showed [6] if the coupled disease behavior dynamics is incorporated into contact networks (where a vertex represents an individual and an edge between two vertices corresponds to the contact via which epidemic transition can take place) it becomes of particular importance to explore the influence of prevention behavior on public health in complex contact networks. Motivated by this seminal idea, the role of spatial network and its various underlying mechanisms, in the couple disease behavior framework have attracted great attention, both theoretical and experimental. For example, Funk et al. found that, in homogeneous mixing population, the coupled dynamics of epidemic and awareness resulted in a lower size of the outbreak, and it did not affect the epidemic threshold. However, in a networked population, the behavioral response could completely stop disease spreading, provided that the infection rate was below a threshold [7] Dwelling on the "susceptible infected recovered model (SIR) with information driven vaccination" in homogeneous random network and heterogeneous scale free network, Ruan et al. found that the epidemic diffusion would be effectively suppressed in both networks if the information creation rate and the information sensitivity were relatively large [8] In more details, the recent focus on complex networks has been extended from static networks to dynamic networks (like adaptive networks [9] and time varying networks [10] and from single networks to multilayer networks [11] (like interdependent network [12] interconnected network [13] and multiplex network [14] It is thus natural that the study of coupled disease behavior dynamics on these novel network topologies becomes a rapidly developing realm.
