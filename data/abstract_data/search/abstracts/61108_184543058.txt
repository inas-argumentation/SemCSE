Book Review: Skin Resurfacing
Peripheral nerve problems should be understood by all surgeons because of the common potential for nerve injury during a surgical procedure from laceration, stretching, or compression of a nerve. Even desiccation of a peripheral nerve exposed to the air for too long a period without physiologic solution may result in temporary or permanent damage. These types of injury are markedly significant in the cosmetic surgery patient who has had an elective procedure with resultant loss of sensation or muscle movement as a result of the operation. This includes brachial plexus injuries from abduction of the arms on the table, as well as ulnar nerve injuries from lack of padding around the elbow area during surgery. Even minimal traction on a nerve in susceptible patients can result in a prolonged neurologic problem. This can substantially reduce the patient's satisfaction with the cosmetic surgery and may result in litigation. This book is a comprehensive text on peripheral nerve problems involving all areas of the body. Chapter 1 is a commentary on peripheral nerve injuries over 45 years. The development of microsurgery has increased the technical accuracy of nerve repair as well as microvascular free flap transfers and muscle transfer, and methods of electrophysiologic testing have been improved. There is continued investigation in the area of tropic and trophic influences on nerve regeneration. Part 2, on innervation patterns and diagnostic techniques, covers sensibility testing, trauma involving key motor and sensory nerves of the face, clinical motor function testing of the upper and lower extremities, and peripheral nerve injuries of the lower extremity. Diagnostic nerve blocks are described, as are special diagnostic studies, magnetic resonance imaging, and the decision pathways for the therapist's management of peripheral nerve injury. Part 3 is on pain. It covers the effect of extremity blood flow on pain and cold tolerance, continuous peripheral epineural infusion for the treatment of acute pain, indications for use of a pain clinic, spinal cord and peripheral nerve stimulation for management of peripheral pain, painful neuromas and postneurectomy pain, and surg~cal sy~pa thectomy and sympathetic blocks. Anatomic overview is dealt with in part 4, and suture techniques are discussed in part 5. The next few sections are on trauma from missiles, lacerations, traction, and injections; brachial plexus trauma and reconstruction; and compression lesions.
