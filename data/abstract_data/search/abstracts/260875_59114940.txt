The healing power of worship in a liturgically deprived African community in South Africa
African culture was not recognised in the past as having its own wisdom, insights and values that informed the lives of the Africans. The growth of the African culture appeared to have been arrested. It was a matter of either civilising Africans or assimilating them into Western culture (Linda, 1997:39) Moreover, white missionaries attempted to stamp out any vestiges of Africans' indigenous culture and religion upon their conversion to European Christian denominations. This article attempts to reflect on the healing power of worship in a liturgically deprived society. Firstly, this article inquires who the liturgically deprived community in South Africa is, and what the meaning of 'liturgically deprived' is. Secondly, it looks at the garment Christianity wore when it were exposed to the African culture and thus liturgy. Thirdly, it searches for an antidote to the problem of liturgical deprivation. Fourthly, it analysis the healing power worship might have on the liturgically deprived, and lastly it handles the practical implication of the healing power of worship on the liturgically deprived.
