The FEBS Journal past and present
2013 In 2013, The FEBS Journal leapt into the digital realm as it moved to online only publication. This was the largest volume in many years, with 518 papers published. 149 of those papers were published in one of the journal's five special issues, on Protein Phosphatases: From Molecules to Networks; Proteoglycans: Signalling, Targeting and Therapeu tics; Catalytic Mechanisms by Biological Systems; Myogenesis; and Signalling: Frontiers in Cell Signa lling and Ca Signalling and Transport in Health and Disease. These Special Issues resonated with the community: reviews in these issues were among the most highly accessed or downloaded papers all year [1 4] Signalling [5,6] and drug discovery [7 9] also emerged as strong areas of interest to our readership. In looking through our download and citation data, we find it interesting that so called 'primer' articles that provide an overview of an important or emerging topic are highly downloaded [10 12] These pieces are clearly useful to our readership and serve as an important reminder that citations are not everything. Indeed, our Altmetric data bear this up. Although the papers with the highest Altmetric scores in 2013 tend to be highly cited [1,13,14] there are many papers with robust Altmetric scores that receive respectable but decidedly normal numbers of citations [15,16] Much has been written about the pox that is the journal impact factor and the single minded pursuit of those four (in)significant digits, and we welcome the arrival of these alternative metrics as a way to see what our readers find useful, interesting and important. It is also fun to interact with our readers and authors on Twitter @FEBSJournal) so please do stop by and say hello! 2014 Seamus Martin joined the journal as Editor in Chief on 1 January 2014, and immediately poured his seemingly boundless energy and enthusiasm into continuing to improve the content and visibility of the journal. By the end of the year, he had confirmed nine new members of the editorial board, launched the flagship Sta te of the Art Review series, and implemented a series of new editorial policies. Key among these policies was a limit on supplementary information. The explosion of supplementary information has been a burden on authors, who are now routinely expected to prepare essentially a second full paper that is then relegated to the supplement; and readers, as in many cases supplementary information is not appended to the main manuscript text, but instead languishes in the deep dark crevasses of publishers' websites as uneditable PDFs. The journal amended its policy on supplementary information to limit it to movies, 3D structures and images, and large datasets or lists of reagents/primers that would otherwise not fit in the manuscript. These guidelines are communicated to authors in each decision letter and are strictly enforced. Accordingly, the per cent of primary research manuscripts that contain supplementary information dropped from 67% in 2013, to 48% in 2014 and 31% in 2015. Changes were also made to standardise the issue size and number of special issues that the journal publishes in a year. In 2014, we published just two special issues, on Enzyme Catalysis and Allostery: A Century of Ad vances in Molecular Understanding and Celebrating the International Year of Crystallography. Each issue of the journal is now capped at a maximum of 15 papers. At the same time, a new initiative was introduced to better promote the strong papers published in the journal, by selecting one paper in each issue as the Editor's Choice. These papers are made freely available, prominently positioned in the journal's table of contents and featured in a special section of the website; in skimming through the past selections, it's impressive to see how the journal has evolved and grown.
