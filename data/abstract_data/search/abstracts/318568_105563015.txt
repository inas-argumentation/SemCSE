Nitrogen and oxygen isotope enrichment factors of nitrate at different denitrification rates in an agricultural soil
ABSTRACT Quantitative evaluation of denitrification by the dual isotope approach, which measures the stable isotope ratios of nitrogen (d15N) and oxygen (d18O) in nitrate, has been hampered by the wide range of values reported for the ratio of enrichment factors for 15N and 18O (15e and 18e, respectively) during denitrification. The objectives of this study were to determine 15e and 18e values at different denitrification rates under controlled conditions, and to infer possible mechanisms by which the 18e/15e ratio is influenced under different conditions. Column experiments were conducted at 25, 15, and 10degC, which enabled determination of 15e and 18e at different denitrification rates, in the absence of nitrate replenishment from ammonium oxidation and other sources. The values of 15e and 18e ranged from 11.8 to 14.9%0 and from 8.4 to 15.9%0, respectively, with 15e less sensitive to changes in the denitrification rates. The resultant 18e/15e ratio, ranging from 0.70 to 1.17, was close to the values reported for sediment incubations, and larger than those for groundwater systems. These results are consistent with the explanations that 18e/15e value itself is close to unity during denitrification, and that at smaller denitrification rates, concurrent reactions including re oxidation of nitrite to nitrate lead to smaller apparent fractionation of 18O and smaller 18e/15e ratios. This suggests that while linear relationships between d18O and d15N give a strong evidence of denitrification, apparent 18e/15e values are site specific and depend on the ambient conditions. In evaluating denitrification in such systems, we suggest the use of 15e in preference to 18e because 15e is less sensitive to denitrification rates.
