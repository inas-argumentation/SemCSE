Guest Editorial on the Jasmine Beckford Inquiry
Reading the Jasmine Beckford Report was for me a particularly painful experience since frequent references were made in it to the Maria Colwell Inquiry, of which I was a member. There are many similarities in the two stories, more marked than in the twenty or so other child abuse Inquiries which have been published in the last decade. In both there was a 'tug of love' dimension with foster parents critical of social workers; in both, there was a manifest failure to see the child sufficiently often and properly to appraise her physical state, in particular to use her weight to check her well being. As in so many other Inquiries, serious deficiencies in inter professional communication were apparent; for example the Colwell and Beckford Reports sadly reveal that what should have been safe places day nurseries and schools did not serve to protect Maria or Jasmine. As I read the Beckford analysis, I found myself wondering whether, had I been a member of that Inquiry, I would have written a minority report about Jasmine Beckford as I did about Maria Colwell.
