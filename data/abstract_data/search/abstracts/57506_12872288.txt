Minimally invasive retrograde endourological technique for obstructed ectopic ureter.
Congenital distal ureteral obstruction is commonly due to ureteral ectopia with or without ureterocele. We report ureteral dilation using a minimally invasive retrograde endourological technique as definitive treatment for an obstructed ectopic ureter. CASE REPORT A female newborn with the VACTERL syndrome (vertebral defects, and atresia, cardiovascular anomalies, tracheoesophageal fistula, radial and renal anomalies, and limb defects) presented with bilateral hydronephrosis, hypoplastic right heart, sacral agenesis and imperforate anus. Physical examination, ultrasonography and mercaptoacetyltriglycine (MAG3) scintigraphy revealed a dilated right ureter with evidence of distal obstruction (fig. 1, A and B) Cystoscopic evaluation shortly after birth demonstrated a dilated single system ureter inserting into the bladder neck. Due to the impending need for open cardiac surgery, retrograde ureteral dilation was performed to relieve the obstruction. Over an Amplatz super stiff wire the ureter was progressively dilated to 16Fr (fig. 2) Postoperative radiographic evaluation included ultrasound, which showed complete resolution of the hydronephrosis (fig. 1, C) Voiding cystourethrography performed 3 months after surgery revealed no reflux. Although MAG3 demonstrated accumulation of contrast material in the upper collecting system, prompt drainage was noted following the administration of furosemide (fig. 1, D) The patient has been free of infection and hydronephrosis during the first 15 months of followup.
