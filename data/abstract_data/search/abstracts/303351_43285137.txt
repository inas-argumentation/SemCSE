The Autonomic Nervous System: Anatomy, Physiology and Surgical Treatment
This is the most complete work on the subject that has appeared in English. Those familiar with Dr. White's numerous contributions to the study of the autonomic nervous system will expect to find in it the careful appraisement of each problem, the complete clinical studies, and the dispassionate judgment of the results of operations that have characterized his various papers. They will not be disappointed in this expectation; and, in addition, the book is marked by breadth of perspective, and by a judicious sifting and weighing of the already enormous literature on the subject. The contents are divided into three parts. The first of these deals with the anatomy and physiology of the autonomic nervous system, its general physiology, the physiological action of drugs and hormones, the physiology of visceral pain, and methods of study. The account given is everywhere adequate, and its selective nature is an index of the clinical experience of the author. Each chapter is followed by a list of the most important papers on the particular aspects considered, and these are well chosen and up to date. The chapter on methods of study will be particularly useful to those whose experience in dealing with disease of the sympathetic system is limited; it will enable them to set about the investigation of their patients purposefully and eclectically. Part II, in the author's own words, deals with "those conditions in which it is possible to modify abnormal activity on the part of smooth muscle and glands by destruction of their autonomic nerves," as well as the intricate subject of visceral pain. It follows the commendable plan of indicating both suitable and unsuitable types of case for operative intervention, and the author's opinions are illustrated by admirable case reports from his own practice, as well as by surveys of the practice of others. The reviewer finds Dr. White's outlook on this important matter to be exceedingly balanced; his advice is always considered and, except in certain cases in which he will have the support of those of experience, free from the dogmatism and empiricism that so often flourish in a new branch of surgery. The third part of the book deals with the operative surgery of the autonomic system. Although the descriptions are short, they contain the gist of the matter, and where this is necessary may be supplemented by reference to individual papers quoted in the bibliography appended to each section. The illustrations form the least appealing feature of this section: they have something of the anatomical diagram, and something of the accurate drawing, without achieving the simplicity of the one or the accuracy of the other. The book is lucidly written, and facts, hypotheses, and theories are clearly distinguished. Indeed the style unornamented and terse lends additional weight to the author's statement of his views. The volume is well produced, and typographical errors have been avoided. It is a book that will command a wide surgical public, not only among those who are seeking information, but also among the experienced who value an honest and unbiassed opinion.
