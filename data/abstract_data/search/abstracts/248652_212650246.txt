Is an Australian's home their castle? The challenges of ageing in place
In 2010, the Australian Association of Gerontology (AAG) chose housing as its 'Hot Topic' Following the Association's activities during that year, AAG produced a position statement, published in the Journal,1 that highlighted five research priority areas, aimed at optimising the availability of a range of age friendly accommodation, to meet the diverse and changing needs of older Australians (Figure 1) A decade later, AAG's Housing and the Built Environment Special Interest Group (SIG) reflected on the extent to which these priorities have been addressed. The current Editorial presents these reflections and draws attention to key research published in the Journal during this period. It cites social policy articles published in the Journal that draw attention to the importance of housing for older people. The decade from 2010 to the present was used to frame the search. A simple search using 'hous* identified 437 items. The search was refined in PubMed to focus on the concepts of ageing in place, independent living and social planning. Of the 69 articles retrieved, titles and abstracts were assessed to remove non relevant papers. For example, papers that considered housing stock as an investment/asset were excluded. It may be pertinent to return to this topic following the Commonwealth Government's Retirement Income Review. The remaining articles were categorised, and the Virtual Issue accompanying this Editorial highlights the role that housing and the local community environment play in enabling independent living. The challenges of creating an environment to enable ageing in place and the influence of housing tenure upon stable accommodation are both considered. The Editorial also notes the role housing and the local environment play in enabling social connection and a sense of community. The following sections provide a critical overview of the Virtual Issue content: the need for home modification, the influence of living arrangements on well being, and developing agefriendly communities, including the importance of co design.
