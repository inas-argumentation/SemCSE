The Creation of Man and The Creation of the King
The story of the creation of mankind in Gen 2 3, with the placement of the pair in the Garden of Eden and their subsequent expulsion from the garden, has raised numerous problems of literary analysis and evoked a wide range of responses in biblical scholarship. Many attempts have been made at reconstructing the primitive form of the myth, or myths, that lie behind the present literary account. But these efforts have been frustrated by the fact that until now no very close parallel has been found for the story of Gen 2 3 as a whole, even though many suggestions have been offered about the various details of the story. This is in marked contrast to the flood account in Genesis where a very similar story exists in a number of versions of the Babylonian flood epic. In the classic version of the Atrahasis Epic the flood even follows an account of the creation of mankind but the Babylonian treatment of creation has little in common with the Genesis presentation. This has not prevented some scholars from trying to read the biblical account as a version of the Babylonian epic but with little success. Nor is there any greater success in looking to the Ugaritic myths or among the rieh lore of the classical world. In this general absence of any extensive parallels scholars have resorted to a number of different strategies to try to reconstruct the myth of man's creation based upon the many possible biblical allusions to man's origin and primordial Status vis a vis the deity.
