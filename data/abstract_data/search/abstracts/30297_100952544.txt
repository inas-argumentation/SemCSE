Cool plasma for large scale chemical free microbial inactivation of surfaces
A new process for microbial inactivation of food surfaces is based on microwave generated plasma. Microbial inactivation results reported in the literature so far are exciting, but most were obtained from laboratory plasma devices which cannot be easily scaled up. A one metre long microwave cool plasma demonstration unit uses magnetrons commonly found in microwave ovens. Using this design, plasma lines of various lengths can be used to build plasma arrays for large scale, chemical free, microbial inactivation. In preliminary trials, Bacillus subtilis was inactivated to better than a 5.2 log reduction on a glass surface in 6 s exposure to microwave plasma and Staphylococcus aureus to a 3.7 log reduction. The future challenge is to achieve microbial inactivation on food products, wet surfaces or uneven surfaces such as paper.
