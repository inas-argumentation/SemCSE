Introduction to the Information Technology Revolution in Military Affairs
Contemporary military theory has evolved around a thesis about the recent fundamental transformation in the nature of war. Known in professional circles as an Information Technology Revolution in Military Affairs (IT RMA) this thesis served as an intellectual foundation for defense innovations on both sides of the Atlantic. The IT RMA became an umbrella term for the family of military visions, doctrines and concepts, such as effect based operations (EBO) and network centric warfare (NCW) and since the mid 1990s has turned into an integral part of the professional military lexicon worldwide. In many ways, an intellectual history of the IT RMA encapsulates the development of modern military theory. This special issue analyzes developments in contemporary military thought and focuses on the ideas generated within the American defense milieu during the 1990s, and further disseminated to military communities across the globe. Several unique features of the RMA make this military innovation particularly interesting for scholars and students of strategic studies. The intellectual history of the IT RMA is quite puzzling. The Soviets, the Americans and the Israelis, three pioneers of the field, approached this innovation in dramatically different ways. While in the American and Israeli cases, the cultivation of the technological seeds preceded the maturation of the conceptual ones, in the Soviet case theoretical activity preceded technological procurement and combat experience.
