Melanoma is fatal skin cancer, whose appearance is quite similar to nevus, hence diagnostic accuracy even by expert dermatologists is estimated 75 84% Studies on automated melanoma discrimination have been investigated, however it is difficult to design and extract effective features to build an accurate classifier as well as an appropriate segmentation of tumor area from surrounding skin as a pre processing. In this study, we applied a deep convolutional neural networks (DCNN) for automated melanoma discrimination. Since DCNN automatically extracts and obtains effective image features as a part of its training and performs accurate with them, abovementioned troublesome tasks becomes unnecessary. We used a set of 861 dermoscopy images, in which 157 melanomas and 704 nevi and trained our DCNN. Our system achieved a sensitivity (SE: melanoma detection accuracy) of 82.8% and a specificity (SP: benign detection accuracy) of 90.4% under the 5 fold cross validation test.
