"We Need Radical Gameplay, Not Just Radical Graphics" Towards a Contemporary Minor Practice in Computer Gaming
As Jesper Juul demonstrates in chapter 5 of Half Real: Video Games Between Real Rules and Fictional Worlds (2005) in computer games there are always at least two stories going alongside each other.1 The story of the game, what could be called the "internal" narrative, might be wrapped in a layer of fiction in the manual or in the introduction, or on the back of the box, but is at the bottom line run within the game by its source code. Alongside this runs the story of the playing, a narrative that is pieced together out of action, experience, frustration, and imagining as the player makes his or her way through the game itself.
