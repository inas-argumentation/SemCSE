Multi Domain Design Optimization of dv/dt Filter for SiC Based Three Phase Inverters in High Frequency Motor Drive Applications
High slew rate of the line voltage <inline formula><tex math notation="LaTeX"\boldsymbol {dv}\boldsymbol {dt}/tex math>/inline formula> has been a concern for power inverters based on the emerging wide bandgap switching devices, such as silicon carbide (SiC) <sc>mosfets</sc> Particularly, for SiC based power inverters feeding electric machines interconnected with long cables, there could be more severe insulation stress on the stator windings in electric machines, due to the higher d<italic>v</italic>/d<italic>t</italic> output from the SiC inverters. Compared to the conventional lower voltage electric transportation applications (e.g. 270 V dc) higher dc bus voltage of 500 600 V can dramatically reduce cable weight and systematic copper losses, hence improve the power density and efficiency of power converters. However, high dc bus voltage further increases the <inline formula><tex math notation="LaTeX"\boldsymbol {dv}\boldsymbol {dt}/tex math>/inline formula> level in the converter ac line voltages. Driven by the necessity of developing SiC inverters with 500 V dc bus in electric transportation applications while attenuating the <inline formula><tex math notation="LaTeX"\boldsymbol {dv}\boldsymbol {dt}/tex math>/inline formula> stress to a low level, this paper presents a multi domain design approach for d<italic>v</italic>/d<italic>t</italic> filters that comprehensively considers the constraints in electrical, magnetic, and thermal domains. Experimental results based on a 75 kW SiC inverter are provided to verify the efficacy of this design approach.
