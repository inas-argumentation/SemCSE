Emotion Regulation Difficulties
Although there has been a history of conceptualizing eating disorders (EDs) from a cognitive or cognitive behavioral perspective, recent years have seen a substantial increase in empirical attention to emotion related constructs in EDs. A considerable body of evidence suggests that disturbances in emotional functioning are common in individuals with eating disorders. In particular, co occurring mood and anxiety disorders are frequently seen in those with EDs, as are elevated levels of negative emotionality and various personality characteristics associated with extreme intensity or lability of emotional experiences. Consistent with the evidence of emotional functioning difficulties in EDs, a variety of emotion related theories of ED psychopathology have been proposed. For instance, escape theory posits that binge eating serves to reduce awareness of aversive self perceptions and associated emotional distress via a narrowing of attention to the immediate sensations and stimuli associated with a binge eating episode. In a recent risk and maintenance model proposed for bulimia nervosa, state based (i.e. depletion of selfcontrol) and trait based (i.e. propensity for rash action when distressed) processes are emphasized as risk factors, with the initially impulsive nature of bulimic behaviors transitioning to a more compulsive presentation during the course of the disorder. Several emotion related theories of anorexia nervosa have also been proposed. One model emphasizing fear of weight gain as a core feature of anorexia nervosa focuses on the etiological role of fear conditioning processes. Further, the emotion avoidance model proposes that the apparent link between affective symptoms and ED psychopathology can be accounted for in part by efforts to avoid emotional states. Given the salience of emotional functioning disturbances in EDs, emotion oriented ED treatments have also emerged, including Integrative Cognitive Affective Therapy, Emotion Acceptance Behavior Therapy, and a modified version of Dialectical Behavior Therapy.
