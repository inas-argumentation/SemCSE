The Taming of the Thames Reading the River in the Seventeenth Century
When the Thames River froze, as it did periodically during the early modern period, it reminded Londoners of their reliance upon it for access to national and international trade networks. In this essay, Joseph P. Ward discusses poems, broadsides, and other texts generated during times of frost and the ways in which they reflected the social and cultural tensions in the metropolis. For some Londoners, the frozen river became the setting for fun and games, while for others the disruption of trade, as well as the severe cold itself, meant economic hardship. Those with an interest in the workings of providence saw God's warning to the hardhearted in the river's frozen surface, though some other observers were more reluctant to see divine intervention at work.
