Risk Assessment, Risk Management, and Communication: Methods for Climate Variability and Change
Water resource engineers and planners have long encountered risk. A methodology for assessing risk is well established and engineers are familiar with means for managing risk. While the process is described in a variety of ways, in general it can be described as hazard identification, risk assessment, and finally as development of a risk management strategy. Growing recognition of the uncertainties that arise due to climate variability and change and nonstationarity of the hydrologic record raise the importance of little studied aspects of risk management. We present the concepts of residual risk, surprise, and performance during failure as key aspects of risk management that are frequently overlooked. A process described as climate consequence management is presented as an approach to risk assessment and management that is designed for the special case of climate risk and opportunity. Decision scaling, the tailoring of climate information for use in decision making, is an innovative aspect of this process. Finally, research is advocated in the area of developing new methodologies for managing risks in water resource systems.
