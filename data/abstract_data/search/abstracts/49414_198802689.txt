Feedback in E Learning and Academic Performance in Higher Education Institutions in Sub Saharan Africa: An Evidential Synthesis Protocol
For about three decades, e learning has been touted globally, even though it is quite a new idea in Sub Saharan Africa. In e learning, feedback can be either synchronous or asynchronous. However, the effectiveness of e learning teaching approach, and its acceptability to students, is uncertain. Again, limited systematic reviews have been conducted regarding online study, student feedback and its relations to academic performance. Thus, this systematic review is to find out the various feedback interventions available within e learning environments; how it improves learning and academic performance among students in higher education institutions (HEIs) and to construct a framework, that sustains the feedback stream in e learning. We will adopt the PICOS framework developed by the Cochrane Collaboration in 2011. We will search and extract data from Google Scholar, ERIC, Online African journals, and ProQuest. Selection criteria will be based on empirical studies conducted in higher education institutions. Papers that are eligible will be assessed by two members of the team independently, and using an assessment guide to ensure that the selection criteria are reliably applied by all the investigators. This review will qualitatively adopt data and investigator triangulation procedure to ensure trustworthiness of the review. The methods, strategies adopted and their effects on e learning will be evaluated and analysed by the authors.
