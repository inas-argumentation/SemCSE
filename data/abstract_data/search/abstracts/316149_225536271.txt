Synthesis, characterization and biological studies of Schiff base metal complexes derived from 4 aminoantipyrine, acetamide and p phenylenediamine
Abstract The largest collection of Schiff base metal complexes have been synthesized and characterized with 4 aminoantipyrine and its derivatives with some aldehydes and ketones. The Schiff base ligand (L) is prepared from 4 aminoantipyrine, acetamide and p phenylenediamine. Metal salts used to synthesize these complexes are Co(II) Ni(II) Cu(II) and Zn(II) acetates. The elemental analysis results are in accordance with the proposed formula assigned to these complexes. Mass spectrum of the ligand and its metal complexes were recorded. The molecular ion peaks confirm the stoichiometry of the Schiff base ligand and metal complexes. In IR spectra, the imine band is shifted to lower wave number in the complexes. UV spectral studies suggest square planar geometry for Co(II) Ni(II) Cu(II) complexes and tetrahedral geometry for Zn(II) The grain size of the metal complexes was estimated by Scherrer equation using powder XRD. In the present study, the ligand and its metal complexes are found to be nano crystalline in nature. The synthesized complexes show higher photo catalytic activity under UV spectra using methylene blue dye. Thermal decomposition is in covenant with proposed formulation of the complexes. Irreversible redox behavior of the complex was identified by cyclic voltammetric analysis. Molecular structures of the synthesized ligand and metal complexes have been optimized by DFT computations. DNA binding and DNA cleavage studies of the compounds were investigated. Antibacterial analysis were also done against four bacterial species, viz (B. Substilis, S. Aureus, E. Coli and K. Pneumoniae) and two fungal species such as (A. Niger and C. Albicans) Cytotoxicity of complexes has been tested against L929 fibroblast cell line. Anticancer activity of ligand and its complexes are evaluated using SK MEL 28 cell line.
