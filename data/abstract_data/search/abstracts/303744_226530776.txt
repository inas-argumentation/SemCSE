Ursodeoxycholic and tauroursodeoxycholic acids as antiapoptotic agents: modulation of Parkinson's disease
Ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) and tauroursodeoxycholic acid (TUDCA) are US Food and Drug Administration approved hydrophilic bile acids for the treatment of certain cholestatic liver diseases. Importantly, their use has been expanded to noncholestatic liver diseases and even to nonliver diseases. In recent years, numerous experimental and clinical studies have revealed the efficacy of UDCA/TUDCA as potent antiapoptotic agents for the treatment of numerous neurodegenerative disorders including Parkinson's disease (PD) This review aims to appraise current knowledge on the potential use of UDCA/TUDCA in the prevention and recovery of PD. Indeed, many previous studies have revealed the neuroprotective effects of these bile acids against the different experimental models of PD. Moreover, two small clinical trials are currently ongoing to investigate the safety and efficacy of UDCA on PD progression. Forthcoming large clinical trials will hopefully better elucidate the efficacy of repurposing UDCA/TUDCA as potential disease modifying treatments for patients with PD.
