Geographical visualization for security risk and mission impact assessment
The increasing number of security incidents, due to failures or cyber attacks, being experienced by critical infrastructures is motivating researchers to investigate the different issues associated with this problem. Among them, more attention has been devoted to the means and methods to present complex cyber security risk analysis results in an understandable and relevant manner. For some operational domains, including both critical infrastructure protection and military operations, to achieve a clear understanding of the impact of cyber risks due to potential or ongoing incidents it is essential to develop monitoring systems that exploit geographical data about the network under control. This paper describes a Visual Analytics solution for cyber security monitoring that encompasses the tight relationship between geography; network and system data (e.g. computers, network appliances, etc. and the organization's business or operational mission. The goal of the proposed solution is to increase the security officer's situational awareness of both the actual network risk level and the potential impact on the business or mission. This is achieved by clearly illustrating the relationships and the responsibilities that exist between the system and the mission levels. As a case study, the proposed solution is instantiated on the infrastructure of ACEA, a company providing critical infrastructure services, including power distribution and water purification, to cities in central Italy with millions of end users. This paper is an adaptation of a previous work [30] to the NATO context. 1.0 INTRODUCTION The security risks due to failures or cyber attacks (denoted in this paper as cyber incidents) of critical infrastructures is gaining increasing attention: a non exhaustive list includes the identification of business dependencies on supporting systems, the correlation of configuration, status and events from multiple sources, the automated assessment of risk, and the automation of risk response, including proactive and reactive actions. Situational awareness plays a central role in cyber defence and refers to two (at least) complementary aspects: the understanding of the network status, in term of nodes' level of risk, level of service, etc. and the consequences that the actual network situation has on the organization's ability to carry out their mission or business purpose. While a common agreement exists on using visualizations for dealing with situational awareness [11] what elements need priority in visualization and which is the most effective representation constitute still challenging research areas. Moreover, most of the proposed solutions deal only with the network structure and nodes' level of risk, without effectively dealing with the mission or business impact. In order to improve situational awareness, it is essential to deal with issues rising from the cardinality of the network nodes, its large spread on the geographical level, the presence of different hierarchical layers that exist at both topological and geographical levels, and the need to combine quick awareness overviews together with local (geographical and topological) views. Moreover, network information should be correlated with the organization's mission using a business mission impact model in order to assess the impact that compromised nodes have on organization's processes; this information is crucial to help the security officer defining and executing mitigation actions with the right priority of intervention, in order to minimize the impact of a cyber incident on the operational level of organization's processes. This paper describes the Visual Analytics solution that is under development within the PANOPTESEC European project [1] for monitoring the ACEA infrastructure. Most of the content has been published in [30] here the authors report an adaptation of the presented solution to the NATO context. The key novel features of the proposed solution are: the seamless integration of the geographical and topological hierarchical layers. the continuous relationship between the analyst's focus and the context, both at geographical and topological levels; the representation of cyber incident risk at different scales, with an automatic cluster/voronoi based optimization of the nodes visualization; the integration of the network topology and geography with the organization's mission and processes statuses; a novel visualization able to quickly raise the user's attention on the fragment(s) of the network associated with the affected organization's processes. For cyber defence, Situational awareness is a key concept for operators needing to intervene, mitigate risks, and determine the impact to organization's mission [31] As well summarized in [11] cyber situational awareness and visualization are tightly connected, and the use of Visual Analytics environments for monitoring and reacting to cyber incidents is a common solution from both academia and industry. The application of visualization to cyber security dates back at least ten years (see, e.g. [3] [21] However, a common pitfall is the usage of complex visual representations that are hard to grasp, e.g. [12] This issue can be avoided by involving the end user in the development cycle, defining clear goals that will drive the visualization design. Following this guidance, our system is based on a geographical representation, and enables inspection of the status of many resources in a scalable way. In the current literature different approaches exist to address the problem of visualizing cyber security data; e.g. [9] provides a comprehensive overview of network visualizations: in particular, among the others, low dimensional visualizations of networks mapped onto geography. In the particular sub field of cyber security visualizations that make uses of geographical representation, [5] presents a system for inspecting geographical and time dependent logs of coordinates. The authors chose to aggregate the points based on similarity while our approach uses a mix of similarity aggregation and efficient spatial aggregation. In [15] challenges about situational awareness for computer networks are presented, like awareness, confidentiality, integrity status of the command and control, intelligence, logistics, communications. Of particular interest is the integration of both geographical and logical representations for IT devices. Our solution provides an aggregate representation of these different properties to monitor and protect critical infrastructure, augmented with mission impact information. [18] presents a system that aims at integrating geographical, temporal, and logical views, while [14] presents a system that coordinates geographical visualizations with other security data: however, the mapping and aggregation are just computed in other views and then mapped statically on the current visualization, without the tight coupling and interactivity that are present in our solution. Concerning the visualization of security, risk, and vulnerabilities, [22] presents solutions for computing trust (availability, detection and false alarm trust values) also if the solution proposes geographical visualization, it provides only zoom mechanism, without exploiting hierarchies or linking aggregate data to geographic areas. [19] presents a solution for merging geographical and logical topology, in which the geographical information is not tied to the network topology. Similar to our work, this proposal allows for inspecting large networks by clustering nodes; however, our solution preserves the geographical information of the hidden network using Voronoi diagrams. The "impact assessment" of a compromised resource on the organization's mission is a key contribution of our work and one of seven major points that constitute the definition of situational awareness present in [2] In particular, in both [32] and [16] the importance of estimating impact of cyber threats to actual mission is assessed: the mission context can be in the military field [24] and critical infrastructures [14] Similar high level network status presentation is done in [10] however it uses a more abstract visualization paradigm that does not directly relate to the network topology. ViSAw [23] presents a visualization environment for assessing the impact of detrimental events to the organization's mission: however, the proposed visual solutions are more tied at an analytical evaluation (scatter plot, tabular chart) and are less integrated in an organic view like the solution proposed in this paper.
