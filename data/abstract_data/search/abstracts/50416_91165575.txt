Thermodynamic responses of ammonia oxidizing archaea and bacteria explain N2O production from greenhouse vegetable soils
Abstract Greenhouse vegetable soils are characterized by high N2O emissions, but the functional importance of potential ammonia (NH3) oxidation (PAO) and nitrite (NO2 consumption (PNC) by ammonia oxidizing archaea (AOA) and bacteria (AOB) is poorly understood. Inhibitors of 1 octyne and 2 pheny l 4,4,5,5 tetramethylimidazoline 1 oxyl 3 oxide (PTIO) in combination with potassium chlorate were used to examine the importance of AOA and AOB in NO2 production and consumption, to evaluate the soil specific effects on N2O production under six long term fertilization greenhouse vegetable soils across mainland China, and to predict their thermodynamic responses in a temperature gradient of 5 45 degC using the square root growth (SQRT) and macromolecular rate theory (MMRT) models. The ammonia oxidizers driven PAO and PNC exhibited a strong response to temperature with maximum rates attained at 35 degC or 40 degC by AOA compared to 30 degC or 35 degC by AOB. Concomitantly, compared with ammonium amendment, NO2 addition stimulated N2O production by approximately 2 4 fold for AOA and 2 9 fold for AOB at 20 40 degC in four of six soils.
