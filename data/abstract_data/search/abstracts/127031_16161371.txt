Limits of CMOS Technology Scaling and Technologies Beyond CMOS
The scaling of CMOS transistors has driven the tremendous growth of the semiconductor industry for the last four decades. However, most experts are saying CMOS is reaching its limits. This paper discusses the technologies that may postponing the scaling limit and the technologies that may replace CMOS when the limit is reached. I.INTRODUCTION n 1965, Gordon Moore wrote a paper entitled " Cramming more Components onto Integrated Circuits " where he first proposed that transistor density on chips would grow exponentially [1] This became known as Moore's law. Remarkably the industry has kept pace with this exponential growth for that last four decades. There is however a problem with exponential growth: it rapidly approaches infinity, while physical systems generally cannot run to infinity. As Moore himself pointed out in 2003, " No exponential change of a physical quantity can, however, continue forever. " [2] This raises the question, what needs to be done to maintain this growth in the near term and what will take its place in the long term? This paper examines briefly what issues are preventing CMOS scaling from approaching infinity. Then we examine some material and structural design changes that are being proposed to enable the transistors to continue to scale a little more. Finally we take a brief look at what technologies may one day supersede the MOSFET transistor and what obstacles they currently face. II.LIMITING FACTORS IN CMOS Since the 1970's people have been predicting the end of CMOS [3] Despite these predictions, the monetary benefit of growth has driven massive research, which has overcome all previous barriers [2] However, many experts are now claiming that the industry is reaching limits that no amount of research can push past. 1. Minimum dimensions that can be fabricated 2. Diminishing returns in switching performance 3. Off state leakage So far the primary limitations to chip scaling have been lithographic issues. Simply, how small can a transistor can be fabricated?
