An Overview of the Synthesis, Characterization, and Applications of Carbon Nanotubes
Abstract The outstanding chemical and physical properties of carbon nanotubes (CNTs) have been considered in multiple fields, both in science and in industrial applications. There is a range of applications going from additives in polymers, nanocomposites, and lithium batteries to hydrogen storage. The use of CNTs expands the limits of electronic, energy, and engineering, among other fields of science and technology. The manufacturing of products containing CNTs is now a reality that began with their discovery in 1991. Since then, multiple techniques for their mass production have been explored in order to maximize their potential applications. This chapter focuses on different aspects related to CNTs. It starts by presenting the description and features of CNTs. Then, it explains diverse routes to produce CNTs, the techniques for their characterization and, finally, their applications in rubbers. Concerning the synthesis of CNTs, diverse processes are detailed, such as arc discharge, laser ablation, and chemical vapor deposition (CVD) in its three variants: CVD itself, floating catalyst CVD, and fluidized bed CVD. The choice of the best route depends on diverse factors such as the type of CNTs (single or multiwalled) their quality, length and diameter, and type of substrate used, among others. The main characterization techniques used for the morphological and structural analysis of CNTs are explained. The end of the chapter presents a review of CNT applications as nanofillers in polymers, especially in rubbers.
