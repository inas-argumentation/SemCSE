Effect of Pre Cooking and Particle Size Distribution on the Pasting and Functional Properties of Trifoliate (Dioscorea dumetorum) Yam Flour
Aims: The effects of pre cooking and particle size distribution on the pasting and functional properties of trifoliate yam flours were evaluated. Study Design: 2 x 2 x 5 factorial experimental design was used for this study. Place and Duration of Study: The study was carried out in the Department of Food Science and Technology, Osun State Polytechnic, Iree, Osun State, Nigeria, between April 2011 and March 2012. Methodology: Freshly harvested trifoliate yam (white and yellow cultivars) were peeled, washed, diced, dried, milled and packaged while the precooked flour was cooked for 10 min, dried, milled and packaged. The flour was separated into different particle sizes using sieves 150, 212, 315, 425 and 500 mm. Pasting and functional properties such as bulk density, water absorption capacity, swelling power of the flour samples were evaluated. Results: Larger percentage of raw flour samples were recovered on sieve size 315 mm and 500 mm respectively while pre cooked flour samples were retained on the sieve <150 mm and 315 mm respectively. The peak viscosities of the pre cooked flours were lower than the raw flours and these decreased with increase in particle size. Pre cooked yellow trifoliate yam flour had higher bulk density value than the raw yellow flour at 212 mm but no significant difference (p 0.05) exists between them. The highest water absorption capacity (4.9 mL H2O/g) was observed in the pre cooked yellow trifoliate yam flour at 315 mm size sieve. Pre cooked flour of white trifoliate yam had higher swelling power (9.86) at 150 mm particle size and 80oC. The swelling power of the raw and pre cooked flours increased with increase in temperature. Conclusion: The pre cooked trifoliate yam flours with 150 315 mm particle sizes produced flours with higher pasting and functional properties.
