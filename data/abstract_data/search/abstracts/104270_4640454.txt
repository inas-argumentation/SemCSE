The vitamin K antagonists (eg, warfarin) have been the standard of care for many years, reducing both stroke and mortality rates by 64% and 26% respectively, compared with placebo/control. Approval and market release of direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs; also referred to as non vitamin K antagonist oral anticoagulants) such as dabigatran, rivaroxaban, apixaban, and edoxaban, have changed the landscape of thromboprophylaxis substantially. The pivotal clinical trials found DOACs to be at least noninferior to warfarin for the prevention of stroke or systemic embolism in patients and even offer additional stroke and mortality risk reduction by 19% and 10% respectively, relative to warfarin. The superiority of DOACs over warfarin (a significant decrease in overall stroke and mortality rates) was driven mainly by significantly fewer hemorrhagic strokes in patients receiving DOACs. These drugs were associated with lower major bleeding, and less intracranial bleeding compared with warfarin. However, high dose DOACs were associated with a significant increase in gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding, specifically for dabigatran at 150 mg bid, rivaroxaban, and edoxaban, in their respective trials. Both doses of dabigatran were associated with a higher incidence of dyspepsia, but there were no differences in GI bleeding for dabigatran at a dose of 110 mg bid (or apixaban, as well) when compared with warfarin. These trials have now been complemented by large postmarketing observational data, comparing various DOACs against warfarin or against each other in relation to effectiveness and safety. In this issue of Gastroenterology, Abraham at al report on the GI safety of DOACs in a large, population based study. Using propensity matched cohorts of patients receiving DOACs (dabigatran, rivaroxaban, or apixaban) they found that GI bleeding occurred more frequently with rivaroxaban than dabigatran (hazard ratio, 1.20; 95% confidence interval, 1.00 1.45) whereas apixaban was associated with a lower risk of GI bleeding than dabigatran (hazard ratio, 0.39; 95% confidence interval, 0.27 0.58; P< .001) or rivaroxaban (hazard ratio, 0.33; 95% confidence interval, 0.22 0.49; P< .001) Event rates increased for all DOACs in patients aged 75 years, but apixaban was found to have the most favorable GI safety profile both overall and among all age categories; safety was least favorable for rivaroxaban.
