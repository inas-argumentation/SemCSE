Effects of an online and video based learning environment on pre service teachers' self efficacy beliefs, attitudes towards inclusion and knowledge of inclusive education during practical school experiences
We examined the impact of an onlineand video based learning environment on preservice teachers self efficacy beliefs, their attitudes towards inclusion and their knowledge of inclusive education before and after a teaching practicum. While pre service teachers in the control group (CG) received a conventional practicum supervision (n 48) pre service teachers in the intervention group (IG, n 41) self reflected online about challenging situations in teaching inclusive classrooms and received video based peer feedback. All students had a significant increase of self efficacy with regard to handling classroom disruptions and improved their knowledge of inclusive education after the teaching practicum. However, there were no differences between students in the CG and the IG. Implications of these finding are discussed.
