Applications of a Model of Goal Orientation and Self Regulated Learning to Individuals with Learning Problems
Publisher Summary This chapter discusses the applications of a model of goal orientation and self regulated learning to individuals with learning problems. Self regulated learning concerns the application of general models of regulation and self regulation to the issues of learning, in particular academic learning that occurs in school or classroom contexts. An important aspect of models of self regulation is that individuals regulate toward a goal, thereby implicating the motivational system. Goal setting is most often assumed to occur before starting a task, but goal setting can actually occur at any point during performance. Learners may begin a task by setting specific goals for learning, goals for time use, and goals or criteria for eventual performance, but all of these can be adjusted and changed at any time during task performance as a function of monitoring, control, and reflection processes. Cognitive monitoring involves the awareness and monitoring of various aspects of cognition and is an important component of what is classically labeled as metacognition.
