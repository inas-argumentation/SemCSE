Nanocarbons: Preparation, assessments, and applications in structural engineering, spintronics, gas sensing, EMI shielding, and cloaking in X band
Abstract The chapter deals with the synthesis and applications of nanocarbons such as graphene like nanocarbons (GNCs) reduced graphene oxide (rGO) and nanocarbon spheres (CNS) The nanocarbons were integrated with nitrogen (N) tellurium (Te) and cobalt (ferro) and analyzed by numerous characterizations. The approach for synthesis was facile and modifications were significant. The prepared nanocarbons were implemented for their usage in structural engineering, spintronics, electromagnetic interfering (EMI) shielding/cloaking, and gas sensing. We begin with a general introduction. Second, the synthesis of biphase, sp2 sp3 bonded, a few atomic layers, and disordered GNC networks is described. Third, GNCs/epoxy nanocomposites show substantial improvements in mechanical, physical, and thermal properties. Notably, in the mechanical aspect, the fracture toughness (KIC) and critical strain energy release rate (GIC) were found to increase in flexural strength and modulus at 0.01 wt% which was attributed to excellent GNC dispersion. Fourth, the spin transport, magnetic correlations, and spin bath properties for GNCs/N GNCs were studied. Electron spin resonance (ESR) analysis showed enhancement in spin spin and spin lattice relaxation, whereas vibrating sample magnetometry (VSM) studied magnetic correlations exhibited a reduction in ordering parameters by N. Chemical and transport details are presented. In the fifth and seventh sections, mitigation in the X band transmission mode shielding parameters for GNCs/polyurethane and CNS are, respectively, presented by studying complex permittivity, AC and DC conductivity, skin thickness, transmission (S21) effective transmission loss (SET) shielding effectiveness, etc. In the sixth section, the molecular spin parameters for rGO due to the Te adatom were revealed, showing substantial reactivity with oxygen that improved the C/O ratio. It also affected the Te C spin splitting to alter the dynamical force constant at C C and C C, consequently generating mechanical stress within the rGO network. Optics based gas sensors were studied for CNS. The metamaterial aspects of a ferro CNS split ring resonator are presented in section eight. The chapter consists of nine (conclusion) sections and presents discussions on 60 graphical results and 10 data tables with 200 cited references that are the most relevant. Details are presented.
