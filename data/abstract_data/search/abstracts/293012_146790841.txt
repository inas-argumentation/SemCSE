Typology of Bahasa Indonesia and English Code Mixing: A Case Study in Facebook Group of English Department Students of Sampoerna School of Education
The feature of Facebook group has shaped another virtual speech community. When the participants of a Facebook group are bilingual speakers, two or more languages will inevitably come into contact. This study aims to classify patterns and analyze feature within those patterns in the use of Bahasa Indonesia and English that appear in utterances based on Muysken's typology of code mixing. A set of corpus in Facebook group of bilingual speakers were used in this study. The data were collected by using table adaptation of Muyken's typology of code mixing. Result shows that there are three patterns found in this study: insertion, alternation and congruent lexicalization. Each pattern almost has equal portion of occurrence. The most frequent features in each pattern are single constituent, nesteb aba, and content word within insertion; non nested aba and embedding in discourse in alternation; and non nested aba and homophonous diamorphs inside congruent lexicalization. This study also strengthens conclusions that the use of jargon in utterances would likely generate the occurrence of code mixing. Several restrictions of the research result in the suggestion of the use of ethnographic approach while discussions on theoretical and practical issues toward the findings invite careful observation and particularized concern.
