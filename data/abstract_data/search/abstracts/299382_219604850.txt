Editorial: risk assessment for chronic hepatitis B patients in the immune tolerant phase both definition and selection matter
The natural history of chronic hepatitis B (CHB) particularly in patients who acquire the infection via perinatal transmission, can be conceptually categorised into different phases by serological, virological, biochemical and histological features.1 Patients who were characterised by HBeAg reactivity, high level of viraemia, normal alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and no or minimal liver inflammation or fibrosis were thought to stay in the immune tolerant (IT) phase,2 although the concept of "true immune tolerance" has been challenged by molecular and immunological examinations.3 Regardless of the controversy over nomenclature,4 a critical issue from the clinical perspective is whether antiviral therapy is indicated for patients with the features of IT phase.5 In the absence of randomised trials that clarify therapeutic effectiveness in these patients, quality data from observational research that delineates the clinical outcomes without antiviral treatment are essential to inform practice. In the multicentre cohort study by Lee et al,6 the cumulative incidence of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) at 10 years of follow up was 1.7% among 946 Korean patients (mean age, 36.8 years) who presented with the IT phase characteristics. Meanwhile, it was estimated that 70.7% of the study population would leave the IT phase over 10 years and most of them (n 378 of 470) changed to HBeAgnegative chronic hepatitis. The authors further demonstrated that a high serum gradient of viral DNA (above 107 IU/mL) and a low level of ALT were associated with the likelihood to remain in the IT phase. These results indicated that the 10 year risk of HCC was low in young CHB patients free of significant liver inflammation or fibrosis despite having high viral replicative activity, and implicated that antiviral therapy might thus be obviated or deferred. As the authors pointed out, their findings contradicted a similar study also from Korea, in which the 10 year cumulative incidence of HCC was 12.7% in the untreated IT phase patients.7 This striking difference might result, at least in part, from the deliberate consideration of restricting eligibility according to liver fibrosis status in the current study.
