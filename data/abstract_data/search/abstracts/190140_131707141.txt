The First Democracies: Early Popular Government Outside Athens, Eric W. Robinson book review
Greek history in antiquity is very often perceived, and not by students alone, as the history of Athens; and the history of Athens is as often regarded as simply a history of democracy, itself an institution that passes as unique to the Athenians and their polis structure. It is therefore refreshing to come across a volume that appears to be devoted precisely to a study of democracy beyond the agora of Athens and even the frontiers of Attica, yet to a period in which Athens is held up to be the progenitor of popular participation in government as a result of Solon's reforms. It was clearly not alone in experimenting with constitutional development, even if, because 'Athens was the most famous democracy of the ancient world, it is often assumed that both the concept and the constitution itself originated there' (p. 9)
