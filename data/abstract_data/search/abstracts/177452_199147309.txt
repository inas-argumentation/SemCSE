The created legend of the Lost Battalion was not the same as the very human and complicated experiences of these four men, none of whom thrived after coming home. Like many other veterans, they were damaged by the war, and they had difficulty adjusting to civilian life. The most poignant example was Whittlesey, who received the Medal of Honor but never wanted to talk about his time leading the Lost Battalion. Shortly after attending the 1921 unveiling of the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier at Arlington National Cemetery, he committed suicide. Lengel's attention to the aftermath of war fits the focus announced in his subtitle the book is not a detached account of troop movements in a battle, but a personal story of men in war.
