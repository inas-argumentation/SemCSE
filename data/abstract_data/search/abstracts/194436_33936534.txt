Influence of fat structure on the mechanical properties of commercial pate products.
Five commercial brand pates were characterized by examining their texture, microstructure, fatty acid composition, melting profile, and polymorphism. Pates evaluated at 4degC showed much higher hardness values compared to when tested at 22degC. Pates with higher fat content and higher saturated fatty acid and triacylglycerol contents were found to be harder. Smaller fat globules were also found to be correlated to higher hardness values. Increases in solid fat content were correlated to an increased hardness at 4degC vs. room temperature but could not explain differences observed at a specific temperature. Powder X ray diffraction studies demonstrated that while the fat extracted from one of the pates crystallized in a b polymorphic form; while embedded in a pate protein matrix, it was crystallized in a b' polymorphic form. This implies an effect of the food matrix on fat crystallization and structure and an interaction between fat and other components present in the food matrix.
