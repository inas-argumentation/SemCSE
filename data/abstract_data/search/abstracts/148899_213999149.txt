Auckland, New Zealand
Abstract Auckland offers a case study of where, why, and how policy changes have caused parking supply and prices to adjust toward a more efficient level. Since the 1990s, the city has trailed and extended new approaches to parking management, including removal of minimum parking requirements from business and mixed use zones and introduction of priced parking to manage on street parking demand. Auckland's policy approach has emphasized (1) complementary strategic policy settings for managing the supply and price of parking and (2) the use of low cost trials to gather evidence and build the case for further changes to policy.
