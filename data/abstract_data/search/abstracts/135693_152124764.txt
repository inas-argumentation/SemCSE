The Sublime Perversion of Capital clarifies how Uno's work bridges Capital and these recent studies from outside economics. Likewise, it teaches us how Uno's work as well as Marx's intersect with Foucault's. In fact, both Foucault and Uno worked out their thoughts on the commodification of labor power or, biopolitics, through the examination of prewar and wartime Germany's attempt (in Uno's case, Japan's, too) to form a regional economic sphere in response to the expansion of "free trade" beyond their borders. Today, whether it is in the Middle East or Africa, no place can be properly analyzed without consideration of similar attempts to build regional autonomies (e.g. China's "One Belt, One Road" in the midst of hyper globalization. Given these historical connections, Walker's book also helps us write what Foucault called the history of the "present" particular to our time the kind of history alongside which the author places Uno's genjy o bunseki, or "conjunctural analysis." Soon, English translations of Uno's Ky ok oron [Theory of crisis] and Keizai seisakuron [Theory of economic policy] will be available and will supplement Walker's book by providing detailed accounts of the role of crisis in the production of labor power as commodity and the relation between the agrarian problem, feudality, and finance capital's distortion of pure capitalism as the "world of principle." Moreover, these new publications will foster the sort of international and interdisciplinary collaboration for the study of Uno's work that has already begun to take place. Walker's book does much to clarify the relevance of Uno's work for both historical research and studies of the present moment; it occupies a central place in the on going "Uno Renaissance."
