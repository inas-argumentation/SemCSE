The Relationship between Work Family Conflict and Work Family Enrichment of University Professors
Work family interface has two primary experiences: work family conflict and work family enrichment, which are being studied by science. There are numerous studies on work family conflict (Greenhaus Beutell, 1985) however, recently the positive experiences of work and family have begun to attract the attention of researchers (Greenhaus Powell, 2006) Both, conflict and enrichment have bidirectional sense, which means that work can positively or negatively affect to family andvice versa (Frone, 2003; Grzywacz Marks, 2000) Empirical evidence supports the idea that conflict and enrichment are different phenomenas, from conceptual and empirical perspective, but may or may not be related. For example, the absence of work family conflict does not necessarily mean the presence of work family enrichment, and vice versa (Carlson et al. 2006; Grzywacz Marks, 2000; Hanson et al, 2006).The relationship between positive and negative impact of workfamily interface is an interesting phenomenon to study. Considering that research on work family enrichment is recent, the analysis of its relation to the conflict is even newer. Here is the novelty of this work, where we will analyze the possible relationship between these constructs and the impact on organizational outcomes, such as job satisfaction and organizational commitment.The purpose of this paper is to test an empirical model in order to establish whether a relationship exists between work family conflict and work family enrichment of university teachers. These findings could be important inputs to design and implement human resource policies on work family balance, in order to improve faculty's job satisfaction, and consequently, their commitment with the organization.Theoretical Background and Research Model Work Family Conflict and Work Family EnrichmentThe conflict between work and family is defined as a form of conflict in which the pressure of the roles that are assumed in work and family are somehow mutually incompatible (Kahn et al. 1964) Work family conflict is the extent to which the demands and responsibilities in one of the two roles (work or family) interferes with the demands and responsibilities in another role (family or work) Some people who assume both roles experience no conflict between them, while others do it, extremely. In addition, Gutek et al. (1991) and Frone et al. (1992) demonstrated that work family conflict (work interfering with family) and family work conflict (family interfering with work) act differently, they are clearly separable and relatively independent of each other.Work family enrichment is defined as the extent to which experiences in one role or function, improve quality of life in another role or function (Greenhaus Powell, 2006) Specifically, enrichment occurs when resources (skills and perspectives, flexibility, physical and psychological resources, social capital resources and material resources) obtained in a role, improve performance in another role, either directly (instrumental path) or indirectly through their influence on positive affect (affective route) Work family enrichment is considered one of the most significant dimensions of work family balance (Allis O'Driscoll, 2008) it focuses on the positive effects from work to family (work family enrichment) and from family to work (family work enrichment) Both enrichments have shown to be positively related to individual's mental health (Aryee et al. 2005) and organizational results, such as job satisfaction (Beutell Witting Berman, 2008; Hammer et al. 2005) and organizational commitment (Wayne et al. 2006; van Steenbergen et al. 2007) However, studies on work family enrichment are still scarce to confirm their results (Barai Bhargava, 2010) In that sense, this research intends to be a scientific contribution from the organizational analysis perspective, to the literature on the study of this phenomenon.
