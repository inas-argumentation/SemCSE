Education in Gerontology and Geriatrics Comes of Age
We are pleased to introduce the readers of this journal to a group of articles that presents innovative strategies for the education of health care professionals about aging. Some of these articles address specific target learner audiences, whereas others address multiple audiences and stress interdisciplinary cross fertilization, one of the premier hallmarks of geriatrics expertise. Describing interventions that use varied external funding sources, the articles depict novel ways to promote and disseminate approaches for improved care. This issue includes the development of a program using theater scripts, an analysis of interdisciplinary student aging interest groups, the comparison of pedagogical techniques for learning geriatric competencies, a consortium for the enhancement of psychosocial learning, and a program to integrate aging relevant content into medical curricula. Each contribution uses different measures of success to determine if goals were achieved, and each article focuses on promoting and disseminating the application of feasible methods for the enhanced care of older patients, including Websites and e newsletters for teachers and learners. Altogether, these approaches showcase the lively, practical, and thoughtful educational initiatives that are increasingly evident in education and clinical practice. The demographics of aging in the United States of America demand fundamental changes in how and what we teach health professionals about care of the elderly population, and equally important, how we translate health care reform measures into practice. These articles respond to the growing need for such efforts in geriatrics and gerontological education by demonstrating ways to apply innovative and effective approaches in the classroom and clinical settings. They build on the careful accretion of carefully acquired basic and clinical research focused on aging. The creation of this sturdy knowledge base upon which practice recommendations are formulated and implemented is a welcome advance, of which the field should be justly proud. This advance has been a long time coming.
