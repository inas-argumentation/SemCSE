Review: collaborative care improves depression and anxiety symptoms in adults

To view please visit the journal online (http:/dx.doi.org/10.1136/eb 2012 101139) CO M M EN TA RY Worldwide, neuropsychiatric disorders account for nearly 40% of disability. Chief among these are anxiety disorders, the most prevalent mental disorders internationally, and depression, projected to rise from the fourth to second leading cause of global disease burden by 2030. In the developed and developing countries alike, most people with depressive or anxiety disorders never receive treatment. For those who receive care, treatment most often occurs in primary care settings, and is often inadequate. Because effective treatments too often do not reach the people in need, outcomes are poor. Collaborative care models emerged as scalable solutions to overcome existing deficits in primary carebased treatment of depression and have since been applied to a variety of chronic psychiatric and medical conditions. These models are among the most researched health service interventions. In collaborative care, a multidisciplinary primary care based team provides longitudinal care using a structured approach to treatment and proactive follow up, usually facilitated by a tracking system to monitor a defined patient population. Typically, the team includes a primary care provider and a care manager, most often with support from a psychiatric consultant. Care is population based, evidence based and based on 'stepped care' or the intensification of treatment for people who do not improve with initial treatments. Archer and colleagues synthesised the results of 79 RCTs (24 308 participants) of collaborative care that represent a wide variety of settings, participants, medical comorbidities and treatment modalities. The science is clear: collaborative care is effective across a broad range of healthcare settings globally. It increases the delivery of guidelineconcordant pharmacotherapy and improves both short term and long term outcomes for depression and anxiety. Improvements were sustained up to 24 months, a notable result because many trials provided services lasting only 6 months. Lack of appropriate funding mechanisms hinders the adoption of collaborative care. The time has come for healthcare systems and policymakers to act to address the under treatment of common mental disorders, a global public health tragedy, by incentivising its widespread implementation.
