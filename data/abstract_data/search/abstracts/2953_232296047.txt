TGF b1 activated cancer associated fibroblasts promote breast cancer invasion, metastasis and epithelial mesenchymal transition by autophagy or overexpression of FAP a.
Cancer associated fibroblasts (CAFs) play an important role in the initiation, metastasis, and invasion of breast cancer. However, whether autophagy acts as a tumor promotion mechanism by inducing epithelial mesenchymal transition (EMT) is still controversial and remains undefined at the mechanistic levels. In this study, we investigated whether autophagy or FAP a is required for the invasion, pulmonary metastasis and EMT of breast cancer cells and underlying mechanism. We employed an in vitro model of NIH3T3 fibroblasts treated with H2O2 and confirmed that TGF b1 could convert fibroblasts into CAFs through autophagy under oxidative stress in the tumor microenvironment. Modulation of autophagy by rapamycin, 3 methyladenine or ATG 5 knockdown regulated the expression of CAFs markers, suggesting a role of autophagy in the tumor promotion mechanism of TGF b1 induced CAFs activation. Furthermore, we established an indirect co culture model and a mixed xenograft as a corresponding in vivo model. We demonstrated that TGF b1 activated CAFs promote tumor invasion, pulmonary metastasis and EMT, which act through autophagy and overexpression of FAP a in both models, while autophagy inhibitor 3 methyladenine blocked these effects induced by TGF b1 activated CAFs. Moreover, the co localization of LC3b and EMT marker vimentin in mixed xenograft also revealed that TGF b1 activated CAFs promote tumor growth, pulmonary metastasis, and EMT program partly through autophagy. In addition, knockdown of FAP a resulted in reversed EMT and abolished tumor invasion and pulmonary metastasis induced by TGF b1 activated CAFs. Taken together, we conclude that both autophagy and FAP a are required for breast cancer cell invasion and metastasis. Targeting autophagy or FAP a rather than both can serve as a potential approach to improve the prognosis for human breast cancer.
