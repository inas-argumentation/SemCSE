Social relations contributing to teachers' professional capabilities and integrity: for the best of student learning
The papers in this issue of Teachers and Teaching: Theory and Practice elaborate the challenges and dilemmas as well as resources and strengths in teacher learning and professional development among student teachers in teacher education, teachers in different career phases in multiple professional contexts and even in the process of considering leaving from the profession due to unwillingness to compromise in professional matters. The studies focus on teachers and their immediate contexts, but they all reflect broader systemic and educational policy factors that influence teachers and their professional work. In one way or another, they emphasise the importance of professional community for teachers throughout their careers in order to be at least capable and competent for the work of teaching. The studies reported are based both on qualitative and quantitative data sets, and even mixed methods designs. Throughout the papers in this issue the teachers' capabilities knowledge, beliefs, practices and professional actions in classroom with students and their relevance to student learning and in relation to current and future needs are emphasised. Teacher capabilities both in terms of their values, conceptions, practices and classroom action are always current issues in teacher research due to their dynamic nature and reciprocal relationships with the surrounding society and its changing needs. The basic instructional core questions still remain the same: how to promote student learning in the best possible way? What kinds of approaches should teachers favour in their own teaching in order to succeed in facilitating student learning? What characterises effective teaching? Related to these questions, trying to define and create optimal conditions for teacher learning are key questions for teacher researchers and educators at the moment. They aim to find and bridge the gaps between values, conceptions, classroom observations and pedagogical practices. They try to support teacher learning, especially in terms of social relations and professional community with mutual engagement, joint enterprise and shared repertoire (cf. Wenger, 1998) Through functioning professional community (Vangrieken, Dochy, Raes, Kyndt, 2015) authenticity and integrity as core characteristics of the teacher profession, they may contribute for the best of students. In this issue, various approaches linked to teacher's professional core capabilities in different phases and contexts of teacher career are investigated and questioned explicitly or implicitly. In the first paper, 'Mapping the social side of pre service teachers: connecting closeness, trust, and efficacy with performance' the degree of pre service teachers' social and cognitive conditions in relation to their performance in a high stakes mathematic teaching performance event are considered by Yi Hwa Liou, Alan J. Daly, Esther T. Canrinus, Cheryl A. Forbes, Nienke M. Moolenaar, Frank Cornelissen, Michelle Van Lare and Joyce Hsiao. They have framed their study by utilising social capital theory and self efficacy, emphasising both the individual cognitive and social aspects related to student teacher learning and performing when striving to become professional teachers. The importance of student teachers' close and tight peer relations and networks as well as their contribution of student teachers' higher level professional performance is especially emphasised.
