Of particular interest is the use of new analytical techniques that show what the pots were used for. Encrustation analyses uncovered evidence of the use of corn and beans, along with native rice and a few other plants, although the pots seem to have been used mainly for cooking fish. The data show a remarkable "Late Woodland surge in the use of domesticated plants" (p. 87) illustrating that people were cooking and possibly growing corn in the region by about A.D. 200. Flotation analysis shows the wide range of plants that were gathered. Appendix B, "Plants Used by Various Chippewa Groups" (pp. 105 109) provides a comprehensive list giving Ojibwe as well as botanical and common English plant names. Dental wear patterns, along with discoveries of animal remains, balance the picture, however, indicating that an abrasive, high protein diet held dominance over plant based consumption (p. 95) The book covers the stories of the old ones in comprehensive detail and much more. It should appeal not only to scholars and experts in the field but also to general readers because Syms provides a sound and accessible introduction to what archaeologists do and how they do it. He is attentive to indigenous perspectives and to what local elders tell archaeologists about the artifacts and their work. The archaeologists were told that "nothing happens without a reason" (p. 11) When a burial is discovered, "it is because the Old One has allowed himself/herself to be discovered" (p. 11) Archaeologists have "the double responsibility of reporting on the heritage knowledge that is shown by the Old One," consulting with First Nations communities to exchange perspectives and insights, while also sharing historical and scientific findings with the general public as a whole (p. 11) The research that goes into such work is interdisciplinary. Readers are introduced to numerous analytical tools and techniques, such as the means of dating ancient remains, the uses of mitochondrial DNA, and ways to determine the sex, age, and health history of individuals of past centuries. Also introduced are, the tracking of the origins of their bone and stone tools, with the latter suggesting trade or travel contacts with some rather distant points such as the Knife River area of North Dakota. The text could have used editorial work to eliminate considerable repetition; and an index would have been helpful. However, the large format of the book and the ample illustrations and maps, as well as the substantive content, make for a most accessible and attractive contribution to Manitoba archaeology.
