Book Review: The Empty Ocean
Weir shows that despite the exigencies of war, basic research continued that would in the postwar period transform the very nature of ASW and ocean scientific investigations. The prewar accidental discovery of the deep sound channel, was confirmed during the conflict. Yet wartime efforts to utilize the discovery that sound underwater travelled at vast distances were scuttled by conservative naval officers and scientists, who did not initially realize the true potential of their discovery. As Weir explains, during the Cold War, the development of nuclear powered submarines, forced the US Navy to seek an even closer working relationship with ocean scientists. Using the deep sound channel, scientists developed SOSUS, the fixed underwater passive listening system, which tracked Soviet submarines throughout the Cold War. At the same time the navy funded expensive basic scientific investigations, such as the development of the deep diving submersible, the Trieste. Weir's book, therefore, should be read by anyone interested in the history of ocean science, ASW, and scientific military cooperation. It is a pioneering study which will likely prove the jumping off point for numerous other more detailed examinations in the early history of ocean science.
