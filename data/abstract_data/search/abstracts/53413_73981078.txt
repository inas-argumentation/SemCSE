Relationship between socio economic factors demographic characteristics and nutritional status of pregnant lactating and weaning mothers.
22 villages in 3 contiguous blocks of Mahabubnagar district of Andrha Pradesh India were selected for study in order to examine the various factors that determine nutritional status of pregnant lactating and weaning rural mothers. Random procedures were adopted for village selection. The women were married and their mean current age was 26. Nutritional deficiency was found higher among older than among younger women and in each of the age categories the higher the parity the higher was the nutritional deficiency. A causal model was proposed that postulated that socioeconomic status demographic factors and family structure determine fertility behavior and nutritional status. Older women were found to be less restrictive in their norms regarding food taboos during lactation than younger women. When other variables were considered women in nuclear families had more pregnancies than women in joint families. Women who reported more food taboos during lactation reported a larger number of pregnancies even when age was held constant. Independent of the effect of number of prgnancies older women displayed higher levels of nutritional deficiencies than younger women. Even when other factors were held constant women from lower socioeconomic status had higher levels of nutritional deficinencies than women from higher levels of socioeconomic status. The study revealed that although cultural norms regarding food and pregnancies may have an impact on nutritional status nutritional status will fail to improve unless the standard of living is increased.
