Data Augmentation and Deep Learning for Hate Speech Detection
Due to the stark increase of hateful content on the internet, hate speech classification has become a subject of growing interest for industry and academia. Many machine learning methods hit natural boundaries to their effectiveness due to high class imbalance in most publicly available datasets. This is driven by the fact that abusive content is only a fraction of the total online content. This paper addressed this issue by taking a two pronged approach towards improving hate speech classification using deep learning methods and data augmentation. We initially conducted a comparative study optimising a multi step deep learning architecture for speech embeddings and network topologies, looking at a combination of convolutional and recurrent neural networks. In our second step, we augmented the text data using 1) synonym replacement of the word vector with the highest cosine similarity to vectors from a large pre trained word corpus and 2) a generative recurrent neural network which creates new samples of a pre defined class. On a task specific level, our results showed a strong improvement of hate speech classification, outperforming the current state of the art by 4% accuracy and up to 28% hate speech class recall after using data augmentation. As a wider contribution to supervised NLP tasks, we validated the effectiveness of the developed data augmentation techniques on other datasets, achieving an average of 4 6% improved accuracy. This research highlights three major takeaways for supervised NLP and hate speech detection research going forward. Firstly, the alignment of speech embeddings in multi step approaches is important, especially for larger NLP systems. Secondly, deep learning models using a hybrid architecture of CNNs and RNNs are a lot more effective than traditional machine learning methods for this learning task. Finally, we showed that data augmentation also matters for natural language processing and should be used in presence of class imbalance. To do so, research on the three simple but tough to beat baselines presented in this paper should be extended. The results can be applied to build more robust supervised NLP applications when class imbalance is a problem.
