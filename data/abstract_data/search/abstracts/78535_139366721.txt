Damping mechanism model for fatigue testing of a full scale composite wind turbine blade, Part 2: Application of fairing
Abstract Aerodynamic fairing is one of the most efficient methods for increasing oscillating amplitude during resonance type fatigue testing of a composite wind turbine blade. The attachment of an aerodynamic fairing near the blade tip can decrease the modal damping ratio by about 36% resulting in an increase of oscillating amplitude by about 56% Despite being a superior method, there has been no method able to handle the fairing effect systematically, so blade test engineers have maintained the status quo. This study evaluates the effect of the aerodynamic fairing based on the damping mechanism model developed in Part 1. After attaching an aerodynamic fairing on the three different blades in Part 1, their modal damping ratios were measured and then compared with the modal damping ratios of the blades without aerodynamic fairing. The effect of fairing length was also analyzed based on the damping mechanism model. The results of this study give credence to the damping mechanism model which considers fluid inertia, caused by a delayed response of flow development encompassing the test blade, to be the main contributor of damping.
