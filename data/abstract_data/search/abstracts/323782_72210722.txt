Virtual ethics committee, case 2: can we restrain Ivy for the benefit of others?
The team on the John Smith Unit (a unit devoted to care of the elderly) would be grateful for the Ethics Committee's view on the following. One of our female patients, Ivy (not her real name) has become increasingly confused and disorientated. The main effect of this is that she has started to wander about the ward, and recently this has become more serious as she has started to interfere with other patients. Typically, this varies from sitting on their beds, to rummaging in their lockers, to removing drinking cups and other items from their bedsides and putting them in the toilet. But on one occasion, she tried to push away a mobile drip stand whilst another patient was still attached to it. Ivy is unable to wander out off the ward because the door is locked at all times, with swipe card access. We have been managing Ivy in a variety of ways, primarily by giving her more nursing attention and trying to keep her more occupied, but on occasions resorting to sedation. Clearly, sedation is not in her best interests, but is necessary for the safety and comfort of other patients, particularly when the ward staff are extremely busy and when there are staff shortages. We read with interest the protocol now being used in ICU, which favours physical restraint over chemical restraint in certain cases. We would like to consider a version of physical restraint in Ivy's case. We feel that it would be inappropriate to tie her to a bed or chair but would like to place Ivy in a tilting chair. Because Ivy is both quite frail and not very tall, she is unable to get up and out of the chair when it is tilted back. She is unable to fall out of the chair because it has high sides, and it is also well padded so she would not be harmed by any attempts to get up.
