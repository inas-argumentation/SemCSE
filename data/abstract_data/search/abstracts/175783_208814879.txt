Comparison of As, Ni, Zn, Cd, and Pb removal using treatment agents at mine area
This study investigates As, Ni, Zn, Cd, and Pb removal onto Fe oxide minerals, limestone, black shale, and dolomite from mine wastewater. Natural materials or waste products with a high capacity for heavy metals can be obtained and employed of with little cost. For investigating the neutralization capacity, the change in pH, Eh, and EC as a function of time was quantified. Of the treatment agents, concrete showed that the high neutralization efficiency for abandoned mine drainage (AMD) and maintained the pH value above 11. The adsorption of heavy metals by the samples was influenced by compositions of treatment agents. The experimental results of desorption revealed that when the adsorption or precipitation occurs, there was no significant releasing from all treatment agents. In this study, the results suggest that concrete can be used successfully in the treatment of mixed metalcontaminated wastes at mine area.
