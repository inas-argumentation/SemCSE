Management of elderly patients with skin cancer.
Skin cancer is the most commonly diagnosed type of cancer in the United States with over 4million cases in 2012 The lifetime risk of developing skin cancer is estimated at 1 in 5 A recent study conducted by the CDC estimates an astounding cost of $8.1 billion per year for skin cancer management Recent reports have also focused on the rising "financial toxicity" of individual patients burdened with steeply increasing out of pocket costs In the context of the increasing incidence of skin cancer and rising costs, the study conducted by Bouhassira, et al. highlights the need for more effective prevention and management strategies for skin cancer in the elderly. In their retrospective study of 241 geriatric patients over the age of 75 undergoing surgery for skin cancer, 20% of patients had one or more significant surgical complications including dehiscence, infection, and hematoma. Multivariate analysis did not reveal any significant risk factors, however, with the exception of male sex. Histologic type and positive margins on initial resectionwere associatedwithhigher complication rates. In critically evaluating this manuscript, it is important to note the patient population that is comprised of a higher risk cohort of patients may not be generalizable to all elderly patients with skin cancer. Specifically, all cases in this study were taken from a multidisciplinary consultation clinic at a tertiary care University setting in France specialized for skin tumors.
