Association of Gestational Weight Gain With Maternal and Infant Outcomes: A Systematic Review and Meta analysis
Gestational weight gain (GWG) in excess of Institute of Medicine (IOM) guidelines has been associated with adverse pregnancy outcomes including preeclampsia, postpartumweight retention, and offspring obesity. Prevalence of obesity and excess GWG are becoming more prevalent from 40% in 2013 to 2014, with more than 50% of obese pregnant women gaining gestational weight greater than IOM GWG recommendations in a US study from 2002 to 2008. Thus, the impact of GWG on pregnancy outcomes is important to all pregnant women and their providers. Given this background, the current systematic review evaluated the associations between GWG above or below IOMguidelines (gain of 12.5 18 kg for underweight women [body mass index {BMI} <18.5 kg/m] 11.5 16 kg for normal weight women [BMI 18.5 24.9 kg/m] 7 11 kg for overweight women [BMI 25 29.9 kg/m] and 5 9 kg for obese women [BMI =30 kg/m] and maternal and infant outcomes. The studies included in the meta analysis were limited to articles from 1999 and onward, published in English involving singleton gestations in women 18 years or older, and had sample sizes of 500 women and above. Body mass index was defined byWorld Health Organization categories, and GWGwas defined as the difference between the final weight prior to delivery and the prepregnancy weight. Primary outcomes were small for gestational age, preterm birth, and large for gestational age, and secondary outcomes were macrosomia (indicated by birth weight >4000 g) cesarean delivery, and gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) There were 1,309,136 women (from 23 different studies) included in this meta analysis. The overall results showed that 47% of women had GWG greater than IOM recommendations, and 23% had GWG less than IOM recommendations.Women who gained more weight than the IOM guidelines were at increased risk of cesarean delivery (adjusted odds ratio [AOR] 1.30; 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.25 1.35) and their neonates had an increased risk of macrosomia (AOR, 1.95; 95% CI, 1.79 2.11) In contrast, women who gained less weight than the IOM guidelines had an increased risk of having a small for gestational age neonate (AOR, 1.53; 95% CI, 1.44 1.64) In summary, gaining weight outside the IOM guidelines seems to be associated with a number of complications of pregnancy. www.obgynsurvey.com 573 Volume 72, Number 10 OBSTETRICAL AND GYNECOLOGICAL SURVEY Copyright (c) 2017 Wolters Kluwer Health, Inc.
