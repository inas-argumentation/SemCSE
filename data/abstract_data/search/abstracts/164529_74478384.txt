Predictors of family planning approval and adoption.
This study compares background attitudinal and perceptual variables as predictors of family planning approval and adoption. Data collected during a KAP/fertility survey by the Philippine Bureau of Census and Statistics in 1972 were utilized for 9085 ever married women 15 to 49 years of age. A total of 21 independent variables were grouped into 3 categories: 1) attitudes pertaining to respondents background: 2) preferences or expectations of family size and spacing; and 3) perceptions of attitudes held by significant others. Respondents approval of and use of family limitation practices were the 2 dependent variables used in the study. Multiple regression was used to analyze the relationship between the dependent and predictor variables. The majority of women over 64% expressed general approval of family planning. Findings indicate that the most accurate predictor of family planning approval and adoption is the perceived approval of significant others especially the husband. One implication of this is that family planning messages should focus on a wider audience. Respondents education and knowledge of birth limitation techniques were the strongest indicators of approval and use of birth control among the background variables. Correlation coefficients for the attitudinal variables were low and usually negatively related to approval and use of birth control.
