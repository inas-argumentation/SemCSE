Book Review
Reviewed by Kristen Huff The College Board New York Although published 3 years ago, Automated Essay Scoring: A Cross Disciplinary Perspective remains a relevant collection of chapters that underscores a persistent question in educational measurement: How can we use technology to enhance assessment? Shermis and Burstein stay true to the title by including chapters that address a variety of issues related to automated essay scoring (AES) however, it is this variety that is simultaneously the book's strength and weakness. The strength lies in the breadth of material addressed, such as coverage of the five foremost essay scoring engines as well as discussion of several critical measurement issues related to automated scoring of essays. However, the weakness lies in what are sometimes disjointed perspectives. Overall, the variety of perspectives offered, coupled with an exciting topic, make this a book worthwhile to read for those interested in any aspect of computerized scoring of constructed responses.
