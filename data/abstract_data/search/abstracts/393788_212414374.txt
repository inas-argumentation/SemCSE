Deep learning for lung nodule malignancy prediction: comparison with human observers and a clinical model on an independent dataset from a large lung screening trial
The availability of the LIDC IDRI dataset, a large public dataset of chest CT scans with lung nodule annotations, has led to an increase in research papers that describe deep learning models predicting the malignancy risk of lung nodules. However, the LIDC IDRI dataset does not provide histopathological ground truth for the annotated nodules, but only contains subjective labels provided by radiologists. As a result, almost all studies do not have performance metrics on a dataset with a solid reference standard. In this study, we investigate the performance of a deep learning system, trained on LIDC IDRI dataset, by testing it independently on two datasets of nodules from the Danish Lung Cancer Screening Trial (DLCST) with histopathological proof or follow up over a period of at least 2 years. We compare the performance of the system with a set of 11 human readers and an established clinical model. The system performs better than the clinical model and comparably with humans, with AUC of 0.941 in one dataset but shows weaknesses in the other dataset with AUC of 0.737.
