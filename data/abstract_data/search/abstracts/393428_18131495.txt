Workshop Training in Participatory Research What Is Participatory Research
The text used for the workshop is my book Participatory research: a primer published in 1999 by Prentice Hall in South Africa. The workshop is divided into objectives, theory and illustrations which are presented by myself, and exercises which are carried out by the participants. Overhead transparencies are used to highlight the main points for the exercises. Flipcharts are used to transcribe conclusions from the exercises. There is feedback for each exercise in the form of discussion. Themes of meanings emerge from the discussion, confirmed by the participants. Objectives for the following section are to: define participatory research; identify differences in collectivist and individualist cultures; explain the value base of participatory research. Theory What do you think of when you read the term "participatory research?" Like many students, you may think, `These things sound too complicated to understand, let alone do. This is not for me! Many of us are frightened by the idea of research, so to put an even bigger word in front of it might prevent us from continuing. Exercise In pairs, participants work out their definition of " research. " Feedback Traditional definitions are given, knowledge of the term is good. Resistance towards research is discussed. This emerges as a symbol rather than an actual experience. There is some difficulty with supervision, as supervisors' ideas can dominate, inhibiting the creativity of the student and demotivation occurs in the student. Theory Let us start with descriptions and definitions. Research entails finding out or discovering what we did not know before about a chosen subject or field in order to help other people find out more about it. They might not agree with your approach, but should understand why you reached your conclusions. Research must satisfy two conditions: 1. The subject of research must not yet be known; it must be new information. 2. The way you find out must obey certain rules Participatory means cooperating with other people. There are thus two important aspects to participatory research. Firstly, it entails a group of people planning together what they are going to research and how they are going to find out something new about the issue that is important to them.
