Keynote 1 IFHE Region of Europe 2018 Conference Abstract Booklet Page 5/41 Home Economics Impact on Health an International Perspective Prof. Dr. Sidiga Washi IFHE President, Professor of Community Nutrition, School of Health Sciences at Ahfad University for Women in Omdurman, Sudan. Health of individuals and families has been a concern to the field of Home Economics since its establishment few centuries ago. Early life is a critical developmental period that can shape health development trajectories throughout life. The seeds of illnesses that strike older adults were often planted early, when adverse social and environmental exposures and the establishment of unhealthy behaviors and risk factors can lead to life long consequences. The striking health and social disadvantages documented among infants, children, and adolescents in many parts of the world emphasize the importance of child and family services, support for education, especially in early childhood, and social services that safeguard young people. At the same time, public health and social policy solutions that target middle aged and older adults can produce important improvements in life expectancy and health, particularly because of the high prevalence of chronic diseases that afflict individuals at older ages. Globally, if we look at each area of Home Economics, one can say that it deals in a way or another with health. Patterns of food consumption are also shaped by environmental factors, such as actions by the agricultural and food industries, grocery store and restaurant offerings, and marketing. A stressful environment may promote substance abuse, physical illness, criminal behavior, and family violence. Asthma rates may be higher because of unhealthy housing and polluted air complicated by unemployment and a lack of health insurance. Other explanations may share antecedents, especially those rooted in social inequality. Still others may have no obvious relationship, as in the very distinct causes of high rates of obesity The health benefits of strengthening systems for health and social services, education, and employment; promoting healthy life styles; and designing healthier environments. As well, encourage individuals and industries to adopt practices that protect and promote health and safety. Prof. Dr. Elisabeth Leicht Eckardt Professor for Housing and Household Ecology at the University of Applied Sciences in Osnabruck This keynote looks at examples of three decades not only related to Home Economics but also related to the implementing political, social and technical development. Of course, this can only be a spotlight and focusses on German background and my personal experience and awareness. The political development, especially in Eastern Europe, followed by migration to the West, the fall of the German Wall, establishing and enlarging the European Union, actual the refugees to Europe from far away are some of the examples and challenges for Home Economics. The integration of computers and the Internet in our daily life changed our communication habits also in all fields of our profession. Many questions arise related to that: What did happen in our profession and its appearance in the last 30 years? Which topics are still the same, which vanished, which actually appear in practical and academic level of Home Economics? Are Home Economics consistent in its appearance, do they follow or reflect trends or are they even ahead of trends? What topics, and/or names pf topics, did change and thus now are established in Home Economics education and curricula? How did IFHE take over actual problems and who are the personalities, especially in European Home Economics, since 1988? The keynote aims not only to reflect on changes within the field of Home Economics but also to initiate conference delegates' own reflection on mentioned questions based on their experiences within the broad field of Home Economics. Abstract Keynote 3 Keynote 3 IFHE Region of Europe 2018 Conference Abstract Booklet Page 7/41 Taste education in home economics a new paradigm for food education A Danish example of thinking Home Economics Education differently Prof. Dr. Karen Wistoft Professor with Special Responsibilities at the Danish School of Education Aim: Traditionally and worldwide, home economics education has been concerned with taste and flavor based training associated with students cooking in the school kitchen. In contrast, new educational research on food knowledge in the Danish public school shows that taste is used as a didactic element 'overtaking' more traditional didactic elements like motivation and active participation. The purpose of this proposal is to present a systematic model for qualifying reflections on taste in home economics and food education. The objective is to identify dimensions of taste represented in model that can be used innovatively in home economics education. In order to teach taste, one has to provide a definition of taste and identify the main dimensions of taste according to the definition. Consequently, this presentation addresses two basic questions: What is taste? In addition, how do we teach taste in food education? Method: The presentation features theoretical work. It aims to push the boundaries of theory and research in order to seek out a new paradigm, including a model of seven basic taste dimensions. Based on this model it suggests new ways of framing home economics education. The starting point has been a literature review on children, learning, food and taste, followed by analyses in a value reflective pedagogy perspective as well as quantitative and qualitative research in home economics education. This has been combined with systems theory developed by the German sociologist Niklas Luhmann, providing concepts for the different dimensions of taste education and their interrelations conceptualized as 'structural couplings' We characterize each of the dimensions based on a range of philosophical, theoretical and practical contributions, including concepts from the philosophy of aesthetics (Kant 1793) health education and practice, science, and moral philosophy. The basic point is that 'taste' is more than 'tastiness' According to the analysis, taste has as least seven empirical dimensions that can be observed through seven different general symbolic media: 1) Aesthetics, i.e. a matter of tastiness, 2) Health, i.e. a matter of wellbeing or nutrition, 3) Sensorics, e.g. perceived taste, 4) Morality, with political or ethical reflections, 5) Religion, i.e. a matter of trust or belief, 6) Love, i.e. a matter of passion or care, and 7) Style, i.e. a matter of trend. Conclusion: It is my assumption that this systematics of taste within the seven dimensions of taste forms a new paradigm for food education and a framework for home economics education with a view to qualifying innovative taste didactics. Moreover, they should be oriented towards pupils' trainings for their independent and practical solving of real life situations. Home Economics teachers take care of continuous professional development, topical contents and quality Home Economics education. The aim of the research was to establish elementary school teachers' attitudes towards the significance of Home Economics literacy, and to learn their opinions about the needs of society for updating the fields and goals of Home Economics education. The research, based on quantitative and qualitative research approach, used a questionnaire and the method of focus group. Thirty elementary school teachers teaching Home Economics contents participated. Based on the survey results, basic teachers' attitudes were identified, and an in depth analysis of attitudes was carried out with focus interviews, involving seven elementary school teachers.
