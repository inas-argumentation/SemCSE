Hepatic artery vasoconstriction associated with reversible cerebral vasoconstriction syndrome
A 53 year old Caucasian femalewith history ofmajor depressive disorder took a high dose of fluoxetine, a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI) 80 mg daily for almost 2 years. She initially presented to the emergency room with sudden onset of severe headache with emesis. CT of head with contrast showed a cortical subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH) in the left frontal lobe (Fig. 1A) and a cerebral angiogram showed a diffused cerebral vasospasmwithout aneurysms (Fig. 1C) Her headache got progressively worse. Additionally, she began to suffer a new deficit with right legweakness, repeated CT of head revealed an intracerebral hematoma (ICH) in the right parasagittal region within the anterior cerebral artery territory (Fig. 1B) Four days later, patient experienced gastrointestinal bleeding as well as other GI symptoms. An abdominal angiogram was done and demonstrated hepatic artery constriction (Fig. 1D) Patient did not have hypertension; nor hematological issues. Additionally, she did not take antiplatelet or anticoagulant. Lumbar puncture was normal and rheumatological workup was negative. Clinically shewas diagnosedwith reversible cerebral vasoconstriction syndrome (RCVS) related to fluoxetine use as other differential diagnoses including hypertensive ICH, vasculitis, fibromuscular dysplasia and aneurysmal SAH are less likely based on workup results. Her motor deficits and headache improved during the hospitalization after offending agent was discontinued and supportive cares were provided. She was discharged home with home health service. At discharge, she only exhibited minimal motor deficits. On the follow up visit four months later, she did well without any neurological deficits. A cerebral angiogram was recommended but was not done due to several nonmedical reasons.
