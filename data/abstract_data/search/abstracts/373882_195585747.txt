Consumer protection and the EU Single Market rules The search for the 'paradigm consumer'
The success of the EU Single Market depends on consumers and their willingness to purchase goods and services cross border. In a Europe with different regulatory traditions and solutions, protecting different groups of consumers coming from different cultural backgrounds, there is no real Euro consumer. The European Court of Justice has in its search for a 'paradigm consumer' found one: a consumer who plays a crucial role in building Europe's Single Market. The question is whether this concept of consumer is sufficient to make sure that not only consumers should be aware and alert 'caveat emptor' but also sellers 'caveat venditor' This article explores this intriguing question by holding the concept of a paradigm consumer against the light of the Lisbon Treaty, which may have strengthened the consumer dimension of EU law. Furthermore, some of the current challenges are discussed that have an impact on consumer policy, such as the economic, food and climate change crises, or the digitalisation. Lastly the article presents some final observations on the role of EU legislation in developing EU consumer policy and providing for a more harmonious relationship between EU Single Market law and consumer protection.
