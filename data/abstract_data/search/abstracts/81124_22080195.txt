Research on spectrum sensing technology of channelization filtering based on optical frequency comb
Future applications demand military radio systems that can handle higher frequencies and greater bandwidth, whereas the real time and high precision radio frequency (RF) spectrum detection can be hardly realized due to the volume and power consumption of traditional RF devices. Through the development of photonic technology, it is perceived that photonic technology will become the new analog signal processing platform with the advantages of the high bandwidth and low loss, so that the function of perception and frequency conversion could be separated from the DSP, thus avoiding digital processing of mass and high energy consumption. In this paper, the received microwave signal is loaded onto the optical frequency comb (OFC) wave, and then the RF signal is input to the Fabry Perot filter, which has a comb transmission spectrum and filters for a particular frequency periodically. Moreover, the filter has a different free spectral range (FSR) from the OFC. It is for this reason that the frequency of the N th carrier of OFC differs from that at the N th transmission peak of the Fabry Perot filter, and the OFC after comb filtering is spatially divided into different channels by a wavelength division multiplexer. The power of each channel can be obtained by photoelectric detectors and used to estimate the frequency of the RF microwave signals. As the simulation demonstrated, the solution can measure RF microwave signals in a real time, high speed, parallel and broadband way.
