Bran& Expression of the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGF R) and the closely related erbB 2 receptor is correlated with early metastasis in breast cancer patients. To elucidate the role of EGF R and erbB 2 in invasion and motility of breast cancer cells, we analyzed disaggregated cells and cell clusters from freshly dissected breast cancer tissues for their capability of invasion in correlation to their expression of erbB oneogenes. We used three mammary carcinoma cell lines (SK BR 3, MCF 7 and MDA MB 468) expressing different levels of EGF R and erbB 2 as a control. A model for the venular vessel wall was constructed by growing human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVEC) on porous membranes coated with basement membrane matrigel. Invasive cells were characterized by an immunoeytochemical method for simultaneous staining of cytokeratin and one of the erbB receptors. In the breast cancer cell lines, the expression level of erbB 2 determined by western blot techniques correlates positively with the invasivity of the cells. 1 l of 14 breast carcinoma tissues contained tumor cells capable of invasion and all invasive cells were erbB 2 positive. These correlations were not observed for the expression of EGF R. In some tumors we observed clusters of carcinoma cells that have migrated through the endothelial cell monolayer and the basement membrane. From these experiments on individual cancer tissues we conclude that erbB 2 plays an important role in breast cancer invasion and metastasis.
