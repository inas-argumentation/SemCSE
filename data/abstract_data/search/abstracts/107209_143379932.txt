One of the shortcomings of traditional substance abuse treatment is its reliance on a noncollaborative, all or nothing approach to change. Words like "dirty" are commonly used to describe clients whose urines have positive toxicity. This approach is also patriarchal and dominance based versus strengths based. Some clients who received such treatment were likely to discontinue treatment or engaged in many more treatment attempts before meeting their goals. Women with sexual assault histories, which make up a significant percentage of women with addictions, are likely to experience these interventions as replicating the shame which contributed to their addictions (Najavits, Weiss, Shaw, 1997; Switzer et al. 1999) A lack of gender responsive programs points to the need for a new model. Enter motivational interviewing (MI) born in the 1980s and built on a foundation of Rogerian empathy and the behavioral interventions of William R. Miller. Years later, although MI has caught on in many corners of the substance abuse treatment field, little has been written about how to integrate it effectively into group treatment with this population. Linda Carter Sobell and Mark B. Sobell have written a cutting edge workbook that fills this gap and adds much to the field of substance abuse and evidence based group work. Group Therapy for Substance Use Disorders: A Motivational Cognitive Behavioral Approach is a vibrant and useful text that effectively blends the philosophy of MI with practical examples on how to build a working group based on its principals. Richly peppered with tables and handouts (almost 30 handouts for therapists and clients alike) the book is divided into three sections. Part I presents the evidence behind the guided self change model pioneered by Mark and Linda Sobell, which serves as the foundation of the approach used in this book. It deals with the rationale for using a motivational interviewing approach and provides a blueprint for a well planned assessment in working with individuals who will be attending the substance abuse group. The interventions are based on evidence and linked to solid outcomes. The book's primer on MI may be useful in many areas of mental health treatment.
