The Relationship of Fetal and Infant Mortality to Residential Segregation: An Inquiry Into Social Epidemiology
SIR ARTHUR NEWSHOLME in i909 wrote: "Infant Mortality is the most sensitive index we possess of social welfare and of sanitary administration, especially under urban conditions." This concise statement, quoted many times, has remained a keystone in public health thinking. In the City of New York during the three years I945 1947, 2,060 infants of resident non white parentage died before completing the first year of their lives. If the same infant mortality rate had prevailed among them as among infants of white parentage, only 1,130 would have died. For these three years the annual average non white infant mortality rate was 87% higher than the white rate. These striking contrasts are not limited to the City of New York, and in a general way they are well known to public health workers.
