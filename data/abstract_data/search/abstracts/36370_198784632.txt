Co creating narratives with youth: Producing 'authentic' stories in the community arts
Writing from a practitioner perspective, this paper discusses two socially engaged arts projects that used life narrative and co creative processes to capture and portray young people's personal experiences. The discussion highlights the usefulness of co creation, while drawing attention to the complexities and often troubling processes of working with participants' personal stories. The digital storytelling project 'Indigenous Stories, Stories of Place' and the short play 'Elephant in the Room' were developed and delivered in 2016 17 by Bundaberg based arts production company Creative Regions. In both projects collaborative, co creative approaches to storytelling were vital tools for bringing personal narratives into the public realm and amplifying seldom heard perspectives within communities; yet achieving outcomes that were accessible and engaging for audiences, while remaining true to the participants' meanings and intentions for their stories, was a complex, negotiated process. In the two case studies discussed local teenagers, aged 13 17, engaged as participants and storytellers, sharing personal stories then working with Creative Regions to shape and refine these into the digital and theatrical narratives that were screened and performed to audiences. Each project aimed to give voice to the issues, concerns and experiences of young people who live in the Bundaberg region, and to enable these to be acknowledged and better understood within the community. Creative Regions aimed to provide participants with opportunities for self representation and self expression, and to ensure the project outcomes were authentic portrayals of young people's lives and perspectives. In both cases, co creation represented a shared struggle towards the production of authentic stories: practitioners endeavoured to ensure each project's processes and outcomes were meaningful and authentic for participants, balancing the youth storytellers' personal objectives with their own agendas around artistic quality and audience expectations. By unravelling some of these complexities, this paper demonstrates the difficulties but also the value of co created narrative processes in the socially engaged arts.
