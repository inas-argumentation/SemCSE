The Semantics of the Positional Predicates Liegen/Legen, Sitzen/Setzen, and Stehen/Stellen.
In a discussion of contrasts involving lexical morphemes in German and English, Hawkins notes that "German regularly forces a semantic distinction within a lexical field where English uses an undifferentiated and broader term" (28) For example, German kennen, wissen, and k6nnen can all be rendered by English know, but the German verbs are more restricted in their semantic coverage and cannot easily be substituted for one another. Hawkins does not discuss the rules that govern the use of these verbs or the others that he cites in his discussion of this particular German/English contrast. The differences between German kennen, wissen, and k6nnen are well known and treated in virtually every textbook on German.2 However, the semantic peculiarities of other important lexical items have received little attention in the linguistic literature and often receive little or no attention in textbooks. In particular, the positional predicates liegen 'lie' sitzen 'sit' and stehen 'stand' are used with great frequency in German, yet the semantic restrictions on their use have received little attention.
