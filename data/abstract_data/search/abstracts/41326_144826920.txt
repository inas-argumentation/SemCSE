TRANSFERENCE AND COUNTERTRANSFERENCE: A HISTORICAL SURVEY
In the papers presented as part of the Panel on "The Traditional Psychoanalytic Technique and Its Variations," two fundamental themes seem to stand out: the concepts and technical handling of transference and countertransference and the theory of neurosis itself. As a prelude to these papers, I propose here to deal historically with the concepts of transference and countertransference and their implications for technique. I am excluding direct consideration of the theory of neurosis. The term "survey" in the title is used advisedly inasmuch as I seek broad perspectives rather than an exhaustive review of the literature. If I confine myself to the mainstream of psychoanalysis, with scant attention to tributaries or adjacent streams, it is because of the magnitude of the task and the limitations of space. Failure to mention significant contributions prior to 1952 is due to inadvertence.
