Hybrid Entrepreneurship as Experimentation with Innovative Business Ideas
Innovative business ideas are an important precondition for start ups with high growth potential. A high degree of innovativeness, however, also increases the uncertainty surrounding the business and thus the likelihood of business failure. In this study, I show how entrepreneurs mitigate this risk associated with innovative entrepreneurship by initially testing their business idea at a small scale next to their paid employment. These small scale start ups (also called hybrid entrepreneurs) are often regarded as low value businesses and thus usually neglected by research and policy making. In this paper, I argue and empirically show that, in fact, these hybrid entrepreneurs are more innovative than entrepreneurs who leave their paid employment to start a business. These results imply that the current neglect of hybrid entrepreneurship by policy makers runs counter to their general goal of fostering innovative start ups.
