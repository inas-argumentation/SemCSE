CCAAT/Enhancer binding Protein b Deletion Reduces Adiposity, Hepatic Steatosis, and Diabetes in Leprdb/db Mice*
CCAAT/enhancer binding protein b (C/EBPb) plays a key role in initiation of adipogenesis in adipose tissue and gluconeogenesis in liver; however, the role of C/EBPb in hepatic lipogenesis remains undefined. Here we show that C/EBPb inactivation in Leprdb/db mice attenuates obesity, fatty liver, and diabetes. In addition to impaired adipogenesis, livers from C/EBPb x Leprdb/db mice had dramatically decreased triglyceride content and reduced lipogenic enzyme activity. C/EBPb deletion in Leprdb/db mice down regulated peroxisome proliferator activated receptor g2 (PPARg2) and stearoyl CoA desaturase 1 and up regulated PPARa independent of SREBP1c. Conversely, C/EBPb overexpression in wild type mice increased PPARg2 and stearoyl CoA desaturase 1 mRNA and hepatic triglyceride content. In FAO cells, overexpression of the liver inhibiting form of C/EBPb or C/EBPb RNA interference attenuated palmitate induced triglyceride accumulation and reduced PPARg2 and triglyceride levels in the liver in vivo. Leptin and the anti diabetic drug metformin acutely down regulated C/EBPb expression in hepatocytes, whereas fatty acids up regulate C/EBPb expression. These data provide novel evidence linking C/EBPb expression to lipogenesis and energy balance with important implications for the treatment of obesity and fatty liver disease.
