Cultivating the Professional Virtues in Medical Training and Practice
Medical schools and residency programs endeavor to provide an excellent standard of training and to promote a culture of respect and responsibility to every patient, learner, and colleague. This is demanding work, especially when individual or collective effort is obscured by pressing emotional and exhaustion related personal health issues (1 6) or subverted by unprofessional behaviors, such as dishonesty (6 8) bullying (9) or violation of professional boundaries (10, 11) or self interested behaviors, at the expense of protecting and promoting the needs of patients. Teaching ethics and professionalism is critically important in this effort to attain high professional standards. This issue of Academic Psychiatry presents a remarkable compendium of articles on ethics and professionalism. Three of the articles are surveys of learners (12 14) The others focus on topics such as readers' recommendations on informative texts (15) boundaries (16, 17) consent issues (18) and the value of self reflection (19) These articles develop the content and strategies for teaching ethics and professionalism. We would like to identify common elements of these articles related to the teaching of ethics and professionalism and to the development of a culture of excellence in training medical students and residents. We will begin by describing the fundamental virtues of professionalism and their contributions to the practice of medicine. We will then comment on how the articles published here relate to these virtues, because, mostly, these relationships were not explicitly identified. This will enable us to underscore the contributions of our authors to the promotion of a virtuesbased culture of excellence in educational and clinical practice settings.
