Book Review Relapse Prevention: Maintenance Strategies in the Treatment of Addictive Behaviors 2nd Edition
Relapse Prevention: Maintenance Strategies in the Treatment of Addictive Behaviors 2nd Edition G. Alan Marlatt and Dennis M. Donovan, Eds. New York, London: The Guilford Press, 2005. ISBN: 1 59385 176 6, 416 pages; price: $45.00. This 2nd edition of Relapse Prevention provides a comprehensive overview of the various applications of relapse prevention (RP) It begins by stating the purpose of RP, and then describing the cognitive behavioral model of relapse, including precipitants and determinants of addiction. A brief general literature review is provided in the first chapter, wherein both the successes and limitations of RP are described. Subsequent to the findings to date, the authors then describe a reconceptualization of their model for RP, which includes more nonlinear dynamic components. The purpose of these changes is to address the fact that small changes in one variable may affect significant change in another owing to interactive feedback loops. This more complex model is considered more likely to allow for more tailored treatment for individual needs. The authors recommend further study in this area. In chapter 2, the authors point out that treatments developed in majority populations may not work for nonmajority clients. They suggest that researchers start by testing the relevance of constructs within diverse populations. They also suggest that, although we are waiting for more research on minority populations, clinicians and researchers pay close attention to cultural differences and use available knowledge to tailor RP programs to meet the clinical and cultural needs of ethnic minority clients. The additional 10 chapters are devoted to the use of RP techniques with various addictions and issues specific to the addiction are addressed. These chapters include the original addictions upon which the RP approach was tested, including alcohol and stimulant dependence, but they also include other addictions such as eating disorders, and sexually risky behaviors. Most of the chapters summarize the relevant literature and describe how specific components of the RP model are applied to their unique populations. Chapter 3 focuses on alcohol addiction and discusses the utility of motivational interviewing in assessing the client's desire to change. The chapter also describes the applications of various components of RP therapy and summarizes the results of treatment outcome studies. The question of the desire for moderation or abstinence is addressed, as are issues associated with treating dually diagnosed clients. Chapter 4 focuses on smoking cessation or reduction and discusses the harm reduction approach to treatment. The authors address various assessment issues and then describe the components of treatment in terms of proximal versus distal interventions. Strategies for coping with lapses also are addressed. Chapter 5 focuses on stimulus dependence, wherein the authors describe the major components of treatment (assessment, treatment goals, the structure of treatment, and major interventions) A summary of findings from treatment outcome studies is included and the authors conclude that there is a need to build on approaches to foster the strength, applicability, and generalizability of RP. Chapter 6 focuses on opioid dependence. This chapter addresses abstinence versus harm reduction. As with other chapters, the authors describe motivational, cognitive behavioral therapies, contingency management, and group therapies, and also residential programs. They also describe pharmacologic therapies and they describe available alternative therapies. Chapter 8 describes the 'Club Drugs, including hallucinogens, inhalants, and steroids. Information about the various drugs is provided. Also, the authors delineate National Institute on Drug Abuse 13 treatments and describe how RP principles can and cannot be applied to meet those principles.
