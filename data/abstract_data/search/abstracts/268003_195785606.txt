ICBS1801 HLA B, HLA C Genotypes and Red Blood Cell Content in Umbilical Cord Blood Are Related to CD34 Cell Viability after Thawing Diana Maria Vanegas* Cristian Camilo Galindo, Lorena Xiomara Gonzalez Acero, Ivan Aurelio Paez, Bernardo Armando Camacho, and Ana Maria Perdomo Arciniegas. Instituto Distrital de Ciencia, Biotecnologia e Innovacion en Salud Background/Case Studies: Hematopoietic stem and progenitor cell (HSPC) transplantation is an important therapeutic option for patients with hematological pathologies. Umbilical cord blood (UCB) is an important source of HSPCs due to its risk free collection, immediate availability, and less severe graft vs host disease outcomes. Cord blood banks are specialized institutions for collecting, processing, cryopreserving, and distributing UCB units suitable for transplantation with minimum requirements of HLA match, total nucleated cell (TNC) content, CD34 cell count and viability and clonogenic efficiency. HSPC CD34 recovery and viability after thawing are important requirements in selecting a UCB unit for clinical purposes, and it has been demonstrated that a higher CD34 cell dose improves engraftment and overall survival and decreases transplant associated mortality in the patients undergoing either allogeneic or autologous transplantation. However, the factors influencing cell viability and recovery after UCB thawing have not been completely understood. Study Design/Methods: Seventy two UCB attached segments were thawed for confirmatory tests, from which 53 were HLA typed in high resolution. TNC and other variables were evaluated by a hematology analyzer while CD34 cell viability and recovery were evaluated by flow cytometry with the ISHAGE protocol. A linear multivariate analysis was performed to determine the pre cryopreservation variables influencing post thawing CD34 cell recovery and viability. Variables covariation was also evaluated. All statistical analyses were performed in R software. Results/Findings: CD34 cell recovery after thawing fit a linear model including pre cryopreservation CD34 and CD45cell counts, RBC density, white cell recovery percentage in volume reduction and neutrophil percentage (R 0,12, p 0,02) while CD34 cell viability did not (R 0,06; p 0,197) However, CD34 cell viability data were adjusted to a linear model including pre cryopreservation red cell density and HLA B (R 0.89; p 0.004) or HLA C (R 0.59; p 0.007) genotypes, including alleles such as HLA B*40:02g and HLA C*07:02g, among others. CD34 cell viability data were not adjusted to the linear model with HLA A, HLA DR, or HLA DQ genotypes (p 0.3; p 0.4; and p 0.1, respectively) Conclusions: CD34 cell recovery after thawing is partially explained by cellular data available before cryopreservation. CD34 cell viability is inversely proportional to red cell density before cryopreservation and is associated with certain HLA B and HLA C genotypes, including some common alleles. To our knowledge, this is the first report in which there is an association of particular HLA genotypes to CD34 cell survival in UCB units, which is a crucial factor in HSPC transplantation clinical outcomes. Department of Pediatrics, West China Second University Hospital, Sichuan University, Stem Cells and Regenerative Medicine Research Center, Sichuan Stem Cell Bank/Sichuan Neo life Stem Cell Biotech Inc. Background/Case Studies: Neonatal hypoxic ischemic encephalopathy (HIE) is a severe disease with no effective treatment. In recent years, a large number of animal experiments have confirmed that stem cell transplantation has shown a great potential in regenerative medicine, such as in the treatment of hypoxic ischemic brain damage. The most widely used cells are human umbilical cord derived mesenchymal stem cells (UCB MSCs) and mononuclear cells from cord blood (UCB MNCs) because of their ample availability. However, there are still many problems on their applications; for instance, it is unclear which type of cells is more effective for HIE. Study Design/Methods: HIE brain damage was produced using the Rice Vannucci method on postnatal day 7 (P7) in rats. Briefly, after a 30 minute rest, the rats were exposed to a hypoxic environment of 8% at 37degC for 2 hours. Twenty four hours later, UCB MSCs and UCB MNCs labeled with PKH26 and Hu Nu, respectively, were transplanted into the lateral ventricle of rats. A control group underwent ligation of the left carotid artery and hypoxia in the same manner, but received an equivalent volume of phosphate buffered saline alone. The sham group underwent neither left carotid artery ligation nor hypoxia. At 24 hours after transplantation, the number of apoptotic cells was detected by TUNEL, and Western Blot was performed to detected the expression level of caspase 3. The migration of transplanted cells was monitored, as were the expression of myelin basic protein (MBP) and glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP) at 1 and 2 weeks after transplantation, respectively. A water maze experiment was conducted to test their learning ability at 3 weeks after transplantation. Results/Findings: On the first day after transplantation, UCB MSCs and UCB MNCs were mainly located near the cortex and corpus callosum around the ventricle. However, hardly any labeled cells were found 2 weeks after transplantation. The apoptotic cells in the UCB MSC group and UCB MNC group were significantly fewer in the control group. At 1 and 2 weeks after transplantation, the GFAP positive cells of cortex in UCB MSCs and UCB MNCs were decreased, while MBP positive cells in corpus callosum were significantly increased compared with the control group. Place navigation test of the Morris water maze demonstrated that escape latency of the rats in UCB MSC group and UCB MNC group was shorter than in the control group. For the spatial probe test, the experimental group showed better memory compared with the control group, but there was no significant difference between the UCB MSC group and UCB MNC group. Conclusions: Both UCB MSCs and UCB MNCs could have a beneficial effect on recovery of neurological function in HIE rats, although the possible mechanisms may be different between the two groups. The data suggest that UCB MSCs and UCB MNCs could serve as a potential approach for the treatment of neonatal hypoxic ischemic encephalopathy and provide guidance in clinical cellular therapeutics. Bloodworks Northwest, SSM Cardinal Glennon Children's Hospital Background/Case Studies: Banked unrelated donor umbilical cord blood is a readily available source of stem cells that can be utilized without full HLA matching, providing access to hematopoietic cell transplant for patients lacking a fully matched donor. Cord blood units (CBUs) provide multiple benefits, but many CBUs do not contain enough cells to treat larger patients. Even when adequately dosed, there is little margin for loss of potency of the cells during cryopreservation and thawing. CBUs are stored for extended periods; assurance of retained potency is important in unit selection. A number of tests can assess potency using an integrally attached segment from the CBUs. However, the colony forming unit (CFU) assay reflects biologic functionality and is the one most correlated with speed of engraftment. Yet the assay is subjective, not well standardized, and least understood. Study Design/Methods: A survey of public cord blood banks (CBB) was conducted to determine the practices used to determine post freeze potency. One half of CBBs reported using growth in the CFU assay; acceptable results ranged from growth to a defined percent recovery. CBBs that perform the assay are required to participate in proficiency testing. Variations in laboratory methods were tracked: media and dishes, concentrations of cells per well, and methods of counting and scoring colonies. Results/Findings: High coefficients of variation were observed. There was considerable disparity in the variables listed above. In addition, residual red cells in the sample inhibit colony growth and conservatively evaluate potency. Plating density affects colony growth; light concentrations may not provide a sufficient number of cells to establish colonies, while heavy inoculums may exhaust nutrients in the medium and prevent growth. Expected results of potency testing are 50 75% conservation of CFUs from the pre cryopreservation values, factoring in the natural demise of mature granulocytes. Participating laboratories find that segment samples that produce fewer than expected colonies may indicate poor potency. However, in many cases when the CBU is made unavailable, the sacrificed unit demonstrates satisfactory growth. The reverse is not observed.
