Hearts and Minds: The Theory Oriented Anti 'Daily Life Corruption' Model
In this paper, we analyze daily life corruption on the micro level that affects average citizens in their daily lives. One way to illustrate this is by using what we call The Tipping Point model (Schelling, T. Micromotives and Macrobehavior, 1978) Similar to the spirit of Schelling's tipping model, we study how the preferences of potentially corrupt individuals influence others to exhibit similarly corrupt behavior. Here, we define the tipping point and show the requirements necessary to create a tipping point in the context of corruption. In this model, we introduce variables representing punishment and reward. We also show that there is an inverse relationship between the probability that cheaters will be caught and the tipping point. By analogy, the higher the reward level awarded to the non cheaters if the cheaters are caught, the lower the tipping point. In addition, the lower the level of intimidation imposed on the non cheaters if the cheaters are not caught, the lower the tipping point. The policy making implications of this should focus on introducing a punishment for cheaters, strengthening legal enforcement to increase the probability of catching corrupt individuals, and increasing the rewards given to whistle blowers who report corrupt practices while providing them with more protection. We further discuss other issues such as legal failures, increasing transparency, and how one should conduct an anti corruption campaign.
