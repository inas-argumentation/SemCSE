Amyloidosis: a strong need for clinical diagnosis in India
According to the World Health Organization (WHO) cardiovascular and kidney diseases are the major cause of mortality throughout the world, including India. As per the Global Burden of Disease study report, nearly 25% of deaths in India occur due to cardiovascular diseases (CVD) and have been predicted to cause 4.6 million deaths by 2020. In addition to CVD, chronic kidney disease (CKD) and End Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) have shown prevalence in India and are distributed throughout the country. The estimated incidence rate of ESRD is about 229 per million people. Surprisingly, in several cases of CVD and CKD, the underlying etiology remains unknown. Previous, clinical investigations have shown that such cases of unknown etiologies may be associated with amyloidosis, where protein deposits as long fibrils rich in cross beta sheet structures. Amyloids of transthyretin (ATTR) immunoglobulin light and heavy chain (AL, AH) serum amyloid A (AA) and apolipoprotein A I (AApoAI) proteins are generally involved in cardiac and renal amyloidosis [1] Moreover, AA amyloidosis is associated with infections and various chronic inflammatory conditions such as tuberculosis and rheumatoid arthritis [2] Therefore, CVD, CKD, ESRD with unknown etiologies and presence of chronic diseases in India's large population make a very strong case for the presence of amyloid diseases. Considering global scenario, about thirty million patients of these conditions may have amyloid presence. In such scenario, diagnoses of patients become imperative to know the exact prevalence and management of these diseases. To support this, some scattered reports have been published from India, mainly for kidney amyloids and a few other amyloid types [3 5] However, these studies only report preliminary presence of amyloid and therefore lack a systematic approach for protein sequence identification and genetic analysis. This level of advance diagnosis becomes important to treat patients in an amyloid protein specific manner. Advance diagnosis will not only confirm already known amyloid forming proteins and mutations in them, but new proteins and new mutations in diverse Indian population could be also discovered. In addition, increasing average life span in India increases likelihood of amyloid formation by wild type proteins such as transthyretin. A systematic approach is required to diagnose the patients suspected with amyloidosis. Without this, prediction models of amyloidosis may not be developed. Without such data, clinicians and pathologists would not be specialized immediately. Thus, a collective effort in a research mode is required from amyloid researchers, clinicians and pathologists. Amyloid researchers along with clinical investigators can design inclusion and exclusion criteria to screen patients of CVD, CKD, ESRD and patients with chronic inflammatory conditions. Pathological studies for Congo red binding to amyloid fibrils and immunohistochemistry can be done on the biopsies obtained. Research investigators can extract, characterize and identify the deposited protein from the biopsies with protein sequencing techniques. They can also sequence respective gene for the identified protein involved in amyloid formation. Systematic diagnosis of patients up to the protein/gene level will identify the type of amyloid disease and enable treatment strategies in a protein specific mode. This can be executed by clinical investigators in collaborations with amyloid centres throughout the world. Young clinicians and researchers must be involved in this effort from the very beginning of their career so as to gain the required training in amyloid diagnosis and treatment along with basic research. In future, amyloid specialized centres can be developed in number of All India Institutes of Medical Sciences, while state level hospitals can act as referral hospitals for the suspected amyloidosis cases. These systems could be eventually approved and regulated in consultation with Ministry of Health and Indian Council of Medical Research for making robust guidelines for amyloid diagnosis system. Through continuing medical education, conferences, workshops, seminars and public engagements; medical and research students, government officials, funding agencies and public at large must be sensitized for yet untreatable, perhaps manageable amyloid diseases. Similar approaches could be applied in other countries where amyloid diagnosis is yet to be embarked.
