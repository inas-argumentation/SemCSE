TRANSPORT, THE ENVIRONMENT AND SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT. CHAPTER 19. EFFICIENCY AND THE ENVIRONMENT: POSSIBILITIES OF A GREEN GOLD COALITION
The author considers the conflicts between economic (gold) and environmental (green) interests in the development of an environmentally friendly transport policy. In denouncing the idea that environmentally friendly systems are inherently economically inefficient three propositions are explored a) environmental costs are real economic costs, b) environmental benefits have a real economic value and c) an environmentally friendly transport policy is being constructed that is economically more efficient than those which have been predominant. Questions of defining and assigning costs are considered and examples discussed. Changes occurring in urban transport policies are noted. The author concludes that environmentallly friendly transport benefits both economic and environmental interests and that being the case it should be possible to find a way to implement environmentally friendly transport policies.
