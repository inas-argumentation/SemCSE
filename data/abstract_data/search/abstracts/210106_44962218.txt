Asian perspectives on patient education and health care communication.
In this issue of Patient Education and Counseling there are five papers from Asian authors, two of them from Mainland China, two from Hong Kong and one from Taiwan. In the e pages, papers published electronically only, there are other papers from Asian authors. This reflects a growing interest in Asian countries on patient education and communication in health care. Take China as an example. In the Scopus database there are less than hundred papers altogether with 'Patient Education' as the title, abstract or keywords published in China before 2000. Over the last few years more than 200 papers are published yearly in the same categories. Similar developments are taking place in other Asian countries. Three of the five papers in the printed December issue of PEC concern patients' knowledge of and perceptions on diseases. Qingqing and collaborators review 34 papers on patient education in Mainland China [1] They review the different approaches to diabetes education seen in China and concluded that the programs have had positive impact on glycemic control, at least in the short run. Chan et al. [2] have investigated the knowledge on hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection of pregnant women in an area where HBV infections have been endemic. They concluded that misconceptions about HBV transmission is till common and call for an increased emphasis on patient education programs. Yan et al. [3] have explored illness perception among Chinese patients with myocardial infarction (MI) They found that many Chinese patients lack knowledge on MI. Among 12 common symptoms of MI, patients recognized on average only between 3 and 4 symptoms. These authors also emphasize the need for effective interventions to promote better knowledge and more efficient coping strategies. Taken together, these three papers indicate a growing interest in the patient perspective in terms of patient knowledge of disease factors and perception of symptoms and disease transmission in China. A number of interesting patient education programs have been implemented and proposed. In a paper from Taiwan, Lee and Lin [4] have examined the impact of trust in the physician on patient outcomes among patients with diabetes. They found that trust in the physician was positively related to glycemic control, physical health related quality of life and patient satisfaction over a 12 months period. The study is one of relatively few Asian research studies on clinical communication.
