What User Experience (UX) Means for Academic Libraries
The concept of the User Experience (UX) is increasing in profile and priority for academic libraries. In 2014, the Weave Journal of User Experience was launched with a purpose "to provide a forum where practitioners of UX in libraries can have discussions that increase and extend our understanding of UX principles and research." The following year in 2015, a UK conference (Cambridge University 2015) took place entitled "User Experience in Libraries" that explored how users respond to libraries' digital services and physical spaces. UX as a concept can be traced back to Norman's book (1988) The Design of Everyday where the phrase 'user centred design' was introduced. A central pillar of the book was the importance of designing products/services from the users' perspectives. Norman further developed his argument that it is not just about simplicity but also about designing products and services that eliminate confusion. Academic libraries across the globe are investing time and effort into exploring and defining the UX they offer.
