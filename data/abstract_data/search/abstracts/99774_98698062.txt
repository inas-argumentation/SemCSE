Optimizing the preparation of multi layered polyamide membrane via the addition of a co solvent
Polyamide membranes with controlled surface morphology were prepared by the interfacial polymerization of 1,3 phenylenediamine (MPD) with 1,3,5 benzenetricarbonyl trichloride (TMC) on polysulfone ultrafiltration supports. The addition of co solvents (acetone, ethyl acetate, and diethyl ether) into the organic phase was used to control both the surface morphology and the polyamide network structures. The as prepared membranes had multi layered polyamide structures, which consisted of large (1 mm) and ordinal (100 300 nm) ridge and valley formations. Permeate flux and rejection reactions were successfully controlled by the types and amounts of co solvents that were added. The optimal membrane conditions included the addition of 3 wt% ethyl acetate, with a NaCl rejection of more than 99% and a permeate flux of more than 1.8 m3/(m2 d) at 1.5 MPa, which was more than three times higher than the membranes prepared without a co solvent.
