Response to "actual body weight or perceived body, comments on 'childhood obesity and school absenteeism: a systematic review and meta analysis' "
Dear Dr Zhang and All, We would like to express our sincere gratitude to your thoughtful comments on our systematic review. We appreciate your valuable input, which helps us gain new insights in the research field of childhood obesity and school absenteeism, and improve the quality of our future work. Our specific response follows. We agree with Dr Zhang and his colleagues' comments on the overlapping samples from their two studies Li et al. (2012) included 3,572 participants aged 6 18 years from the NHANES 2005 2008(1) and Duncan et al. (2017) included 3,113 participants aged 12 19 years from the NHANES 2003 2008(2) Even though the two samples shared the same participants aged 12 18 years from the NHANES 2005 2008, they also differed in their age distribution so that they were not completely interchangeable. In the main meta analysis reported in the publication, we treated them as separate estimates. However, we also conducted a sensitivity analysis (not reported in the publication) in which we only included Li et al. (2012) in the meta analysis because its age range from 6 to 18 years were more similar to that of other studies. The results are reported in the Table 1 in the succeeding paragraph. Because the pooled estimates from this sensitivity analysis were fairly comparable to those obtained in the main meta analysis, we decided to report the results from the main meta analysis in the publication. Considering Li et al. (2012) and other included studies with multiple age groups, we decided to combine different age groups in a study (e.g. children aged 6 11 years and adolescents aged 12 18 years) to form a single sample. By doing this, we are able to meet the requirement of meta analysis on one sample per study. In Li et al. (2012) the adjusted odds ratios (OR) of school absenteeism among overweight children (OR 2.27, 95%CI 0.64, 8.03) and overweight adolescents (OR 1.33, 95% CI 0.68, 2.59) were combined using meta analysis. The pooled effect size (OR 1.50, 95% CI 0.83, 2.70) was subsequently used in the main meta analysis with other studies. Similarly, we combined obese children (OR 3.93, 95% CI 1.55, 9.95) and obese adolescents (OR 1.26, 95% CI 0.48, 3.23) in Li et al. (2012) to estimate a pooled effect size (OR 2.24, 95% CI 0.73, 6.82) which was subsequently used in the main meta analysis with other studies. We agree with Dr Zhang and his colleagues' comments on the importance of perceived body weight/image in relation to school absenteeism. Currently, relevant studies are still lacking. A separate review on this topic may be warranted as evidence starts to accumulate in the future.
