Guest Editorial Special Issue on Selected Papers from PRIME 2017 and SMACD 2017
This Special Issue of Integration, the VLSI Journal is devoted to the 2017 edition of the Conference on Ph.D. Research in Microelectronics and Electronics (PRIME 2017) and the International Conference on Synthesis, Modeling, Analysis and Simulation Methods and Applications to Circuit Design (SMACD 2017) which were co located and held in Giardini Naxos Taormina, Italy, from 12th to 15th June 2017. PRIME has been established over the recent years as an important conference where PhD students and post docs with less than one year post PhD experience can present their research results and network with experts from industry, academia and research. SMACD has become the reference forum devoted to modeling, simulation, design and synthesis tools and methods for analog, mixed signal, radio frequency and multi domain integrated circuits and systems. As for the past edition, PRIME and SMACD were co located in 2017, broadly covering many aspects of modern electronic systems, circuits, devices and modeling. PRIME 2017 and SMACD 2017 together received more than 230 submissions from all over the world and more than 220 attendees. The conference program included the participation of 3 keynote speakers and about 160 selected papers distributed among 32 thematic sessions, including a Ph.D. Competition on Electronic Design Automation (EDA) with live demonstrations. This special issue of Integration, the VLSI Journal aims at publishing extended versions of top ranked papers in the two conferences, referring to state of the art in terms of IC design techniques, trends and challenges, or EDA tools applied to standard and/or emerging technologies. The papers invited to contribute in this special issue were subjected to a standard peer review process by outstanding researchers in the field. Articles in the special issue The PRIME 2017 papers selected for this issue are summarized as follows. The first paper is titled "Architectural exploration of perpendicular Nano Magnetic Logic based circuits" and deals with the interesting emerging technology of perpendicular Nano Magnetic Logic (pNML) which has unique features compared to standard transistor technologies. Namely, it is inherently suited for 3D circuits and each device behaves both as a memory and a logic element. The paper presents several complex architectures implementing both combinational and sequential circuits, including also the first Finite State Machine based on pNML. The second paper is titled "A TVWS receiver with balanced output selfcalibrated IIP2 LNTA employing a low noise current multiplier" and deals with a broadband, inductorless receiver suitable for TV White Space applications. It includes a noise canceling low noise transconductance amplifier (LNTA) made up of a common gate stage with a feed forward noise canceling common source path. A low noise current multiplier is also introduced to balance the output currents and a self calibrating loop boosts the IIP2 above 52 dBm across process corners. The LNTA bandwidth ranges from 100 MHz to 1GHz with a power consumption of only 3 mW. The next paper titled "3 Path SiGe BiCMOS power amplifier on thinned substrate for IoT applications" describes a fully integrated, transformerbased, class A Differential Power Amplifier (DPA) for the Internet of Things (IoT) The DPA is fabricated in a cost effective 95 GHz fmax, 0.25 mm SiGe BiCMOS technology and utilizes a thin Si chip with a thickness of 45 mm in order to be embedded into flexible electronic foil systems. The DPA achieves 10 dB small signal gain at 5.5 GHz and delivers an output power of +9.4 dBm after thinning process. In "A K Band low noise bipolar class C VCO for 5G backhaul systems in 55nm BiCMOS technology" the challenges of achieving low phase noise VCOs, required by 64 QAM for 5G communication systems, are discussed in detail. Comparison between CMOS and BJT devices is also investigated and the impact of the base resistance in BJT based VCOs is addressed demonstrating ~2dB better phase noise, whereas CMOSbased VCOs are found unsuitable for the targeted tuning range due to thick oxide devices parasitics. Excellent measurements on prototypes realized in a 55 nm BiCMOS technology and operated under 2.5V supply are provided. In "1 GigaRad TID impact on 28 nm HEP analog circuits" the radiation hardness of advanced bulk CMOS technologies (specifically, TSMC 28nm) is evaluated on both single device parameters variation and Total Ionizing Dose effects induced on a read out channel for High Energy Physics experiments (upgrade of CERN Large Hadron Collider) Preliminary experimental results indicate that nMOS structures are more resistant than pMOS counterparts, but a considerable leakage current increment is observed. In the particular case, the high radiation level induces a gain reduction and a slowdown of the time response. In "CMOS event driven tactile sensor circuit" a device for low latency compressive pressure sensing, using of a piezoelectric material coupled to mixed mode neuromorphic CMOS circuits, is presented. The sensing element is based on the Piezoelectric Oxide Semiconductor FieldEffect Transistor (POSFET) device, whereby the polarization induced by pressure in the piezoelectric material is exploited to bias a CMOS transistor. The resulting output current feeds a subthreshold Leaky Integrate and Fire neuron that encodes the sensed force into digital pulses. The device is prototyped in a 180 nm CMOS technology coupled with the piezoelectric material and highlights as major improvements smaller silicon area, higher sensitivity and lower power consumption. The last paper is titled "Scalable lumped models of integrated transformers for galvanically isolated power transfer systems" and presents a review of integrated transformers for galvanic isolation with particular focus on their modelling in power transfer systems.
