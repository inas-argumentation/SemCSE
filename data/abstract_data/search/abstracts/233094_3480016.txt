Introduction to the special issue on Recent advance in signal processing, biomedical engineering and informatics
The field of biomedical engineering and informatics has been advancing rapidly in this information age. Signal processing and information technology progress continually to provide new methodologies to enable researchers and scientists to address challenges and problems in biology, medicine and healthcare. This special issue, following two others on the related topic published in January 2012 and January 2013, includes eleven papers focusing on the new development of signal processing and informatics approaches to address the challenges and problems in biomedical engineering and bioinformatics. It is hoped that this special issue will benefit researchers from multiple disciplines including engineering, computing, and informatics. The papers are organized as follows. Automatic speech recognition (ASR) technology has been providing important functionality in computer assisted language learning applications. In the paper 'Improving Mispronunciation Detection using Adaptive Frequency Scale' Ge et al. present a Hidden Markov Model (HMM) based method for the detection of mispronunciations. Exploiting the specific dialog scripting employed in language learning software, HMMs are trained for different pronunciations. New adaptive features have been developed and obtained through an adaptive warping of the frequency scale prior to computing the cepstral coefficients. Experimental results show that the adaptive frequency scale yields a better coefficient representation leading to higher classification rates in comparison with conventional HMMs using Mel frequency cepstral coefficients. The alignment of rat brain tissues in magnetic resonance images (MRI) is critical for quantitative analysis of brain extracellular space (ECS) Liu et al. present a mutual information (MI) based method for automatic registration in the paper 'Mutual Information Based Three Dimensional Registration of Rat Brain Magnetic Resonance Imaging Time Series' The method is specially designed to address the specific properties of contrast enhanced MRI time series. The method adopts a segmentation method to extract the brain tissue mask, and uses a two stage registration method that firstly aligns images using deformable registration and then matches rigid registration. Experimental results show that the proposed method has a high degree of accuracy and reliability, and is adequate to the task of three dimensional registration of rat brain MRI time series. Minimally invasive abdominal aortic aneurysm stenting can be greatly facilitated by overlaying the pre operative 3 D model of the abdominal aorta onto the intra operative 2 D X ray images. Accurate 2 D/3 D registration in 3 D space makes the 2 D/3 D overlay robust to the change of C Arm angulations. Typically at least two X ray images showing the abdominal aorta with contrast medium from different angles are needed for an accurate registration in 3 D space. Miao et al. propose a novel 2 D/3 D registration technique for using only one image with contrast medium in the abdominal aorta and one native image showing the spine to achieve accurate registration of the abdominal aorta in 3 D in the paper 'Toward Smart Utilization of Two X ray Images for 2 D/3 D Registration Applied to Abdominal' The proposed method utilizes the two X ray images in an integrated but differentiated way in order to best utilize the complementary information provided by the two types of images. A hierarchical registration scheme is deployed by a sensible partition of the registration parameter space based on the image acquisition protocol. The proposed method was validated using both synthetic and clinical datasets, and achieved significantly improved accuracy and robustness compared to the conventional methods. Asymptomatic carotid stenosis, as one of the etiological factors for stroke, has several risk factors such as hypertension, cardiac morbidity, smoking, diabetes, and physical inactivity. Understanding and determining the factors that predispose to asymptomatic carotid stenosis will help in the design of acute stroke trials and in prevention programs.
