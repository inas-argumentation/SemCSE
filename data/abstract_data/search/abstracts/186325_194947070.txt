Designing a Path to an Economically, Socially and Environmentally Sustainable Plan for Accessible Storage at Winterthur Museum, Garden and Library
Created by Henry Francis du Pont and opened to the public in 1951, Winterthur is a multifaceted institution with extensive research, educational and exhibit programming. Physically, it is a 1000 acre estate with 118 buildings. The property was acquired by the du Pont family in 1810 and the original 12 room house, in which Henry Francis grew up, was built by his great uncle and aunt in 1837. Henry Francis du Pont inherited the house from his father in 1927, and eventually enlarged it to include the current 175 rooms for display. Today this property includes a 60 acre naturalistic garden, a 100,000 volume research library, a comprehensive collection of nearly 90,000 fine and decorative arts objects, a historic home, a museum galleries building, and conservation facilities. These collections and spaces are used: for object based research by curators, conservators, fellows and visiting scholars; for study by beginners to experts; in local, national and international exhibitions; to support regular conferences and workshops; in master's level academic classes and Ph.D. research. The museum, garden and library collections are supplemented by a large number of objects associated with Winterthur history, as well as others used for handling in school programs, and display in interpreted house tours. Although not part of Winterthur's accessioned collection, these objects demand equivalent collection care. A significant portion of this broad collection is on long term open display. The balance is stored in more than 65, mostly inadequate, spaces that constrict access, limit educational use, and put objects at risk.
