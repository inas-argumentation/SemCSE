Entrepreneurial Intention of Students and Unemployed Persons in Romania: A Case Study on the South East Region and the South West Oltenia Region
ABSTRACT.The results presented in this paper are part of a study conducted in the project: 'Be a manager for your own business! that was financed by the European Social Fund, Sectoral Operational Programme Human Resources Development (SOP HRD) 2007 2013, and the Romanian Government, ID POSDRU/92/3.1/S/49297; during April 2011 March 2014. This paper investigates the entrepreneurial intentions of 653 final year students and 212 unemployed persons from the South East Region and the South West Oltenia Region of Romania. Only 244 persons have shown interest in enrolling on entrepreneurial courses developed through the Project with the declared intention of starting a new business, and less than one third, 80 persons, have initiated small businesses.JEL codes: 125; M13; RIOKeywords: entrepreneurship; youth employment; the theory of planned behavior; model of entrepreneurial event; entrepreneurial potential1. IntroductionThis paper presents a part of the results obtained in the Project 'Be a manager for your own business! that was implemented during April 2011 March 2014 by Spiru Haret University through the Faculty of Accounting and Financial Management Constanta and financed by the European Social Fund, Sectoral Operational Programme Human Resources Development (SOP HRD) 2007 2013, and the Romanian Government. The project activities focused on two regions of implementation: the South East Region and the South West Oltenia Region.The general Project objective was to create new experiences by developing entrepreneurial skills and competencies needed to ensure adaptability of target groups (entrepreneurs and people who want to start an independent activity) at the labor market's demands and to create a permanent balance between work and family life.The specific Project objective was to develop personal and professional skills through the initiation, guidance, support and continuous improvement in business management and organizational culture.Moreover, the project idea was to create that event which converts entrepreneurial potential into entrepreneurial intention.2. Literature ReviewThere is a large literature on entrepreneurship and entrepreneurial intention models.The most mentioned work in entrepreneurial literature is the Schumpeter article: The Creative Response in Economic History; but the author analyzed the concept of entrepreneurship even earlier, in his book The Theory of Economic Development (1934) J. B. Say was the one who developed the theoretical distinction between 'scientific ability and 'entrepreneurial skill in 1855 (Jones and Wadhwani, 2006) but the various facets of entrepreneurship have much older roots in economics, starting with Adam Smith and Ricardo's studies.According to Schumpeter (1947: 151) the entrepreneur has a defining characteristic: "the doing of new things or the doing of things that are already being done in a new way (innovation)" "An Entrepreneur is someone who perceives an opportunity, and creates an organization to pursue it" based on the definition of Bygrave and Hofer (1991: 14) In most empirical studies, however, an entrepreneur is simply defined as someone who is self employed. (Berglann et al, 2011)The literature regarding entrepreneurship was summarized by Stevenson and Jarillo in 1990; they grouped the studies in three main categories: "what happens when entrepreneurs act; why they act; and how they act." We could mention the fact that corporate entrepreneurship is often named now intrapreneurship (see Parker, 2011, and others) Later, in 2003, Nijkamp made an entrepreneurial literature review "from the perspective of geographical seedbed conditions and network constellations" and reminded Hebert and Link's (1982) book, The Entrepreneur, where the authors identified 12 "species" of entrepreneurs: person who takes risk associated with uncertainty, who supplies financial capital, an innovator, a decision maker, an industrial leader, a manager or superintendent, an organizer and coordinator of economic resources, the owner of en enterprise, an employer of factors of production, a contractor, an 'arbitrageur' or an allocator of resources among alternative uses.
