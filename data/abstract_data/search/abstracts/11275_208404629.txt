G353(P) Using the socks teaching programme to increase awareness of online safety/cyberbullying in primary school children. socks: stamp out cyberbullying and keep safe!
Introduction Adolescents are highly vulnerable to online abuse and multiple campaigns are in place to try and reduce this. We sought to target primary school children through a novel teaching programme, 'SOCKS' to foster online safety awareness prior to abuse commencing. We report on the development of this programme and pilot results. Aims To improve knowledge/awareness of online safety in primary school children. To reduce future online abuse for the children in this programme. Method The programme was developed after conducting focus groups and interviews with children and young adolescents. A 1 hour long, interactive, teaching session was developed. Results 60 Year 5 children took part in the pilot. At the beginning 18/60 (30% 9 10 year olds questioned signalled that they have a social media profile. One 10 year old boy stated: 'Snapchat is my life! 6/60 (10% children admitted that they have friends online who they do not know in real life. Qualitative feedback following the SOCKS teaching: 'I probably would have written 'that's really funny..lol! (on seeing a classmate's bad karaoke video online) but after this session I don't think I would because that would probably upset her' Girl, Age 10 There was also positive feedback from the teachers: 'The children found it very useful to learn about how to keep themselves safe online. They got a chance to ask a variety of questions, to engage in structured discussion and to share the results of their discussion. Conclusion Our pilot programme highlighted how common use of social media was even in this young age group. The SOCKS programme is an engaging teaching programme to help develop online safety awareness skills. It was universally well received by staff and students. Plans for further development include training volunteers to roll the programme out more widely. Once a number of schools have participated follow up studies can be conducted to see if reports of online abuse are lower than where this teaching is not offered. Further research will show whether this has a protective effect from online abuse.
