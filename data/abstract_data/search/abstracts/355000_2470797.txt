Detection and typing of epidermodysplasia verruciformis associated human papillomavirus types in cutaneous cancers by a nested PCR approach
Papillomavirases (PV) are small DNA turnout viruses with a life cycle inseparable connected with differentiating epithelium. Infection of the skin may result in benign tumours which reveal limited growth and tend to regress spontaneously. Some PVs, however, cause lesions with a high risk of progression into carcinomas and do contribute to the origin of certain cancers in man. The development of a papilloma demands the infection of basal cells in the epithelium, however, frequently this event does not result in overt lesions but rather in latent infections. This is demonstrated by observations that, (i) PV genomes can commonly be detected in normal tissue samples, (ii) latent papillomavirus genomes in animals can be reactivated by skin irritation and (iii) tumours often reoccur in the vicinity of surgically removed lesions To identify the hitherto unkown primary target cells of papillomaviruses we experimentally infected domestic rabbits with the cottontail rabbit papillomavirus (CRPV) and detected infected cells with the help of in situ hybridization. This search identified keratinocytes located in hair follicles as the first ceils exhibiting viral gene expression as early as 11 days after infection. To further investigate the nature of these cells we microdissected hair follicles from rabbits, cut them in five fragments, isolated keratinocytes from each of the fragments and performed a clonaI analysis. Our results provide evidence that the primary target cells of CRPV are highly permissive for the expression of the viral oncogenes E6 mid E7 and do co localize with cells having growth properties expected of epidermal stem cells.
