The Epistemology of the Minor Native in Transcolonial Border Zones
In her now classic essay, "Where Have All the Natives Gone?" Rey Chow likens modernity's preoccupation with nativizing cultures to a colonial visual technology in which the native becomes useful and falsely knowable to her onlooker. Using photography as her key example, Chow argues that the reproduction of the native as the visual other continuously challenges our mode of analysis: do we critique the image itself, expose the machinery of its truth effects, or call for a substitute? The native, as concept or body, image or historical experience thus becomes a shifting ground for "more general questions of exploitation, resistance and survival."1 In this chapter, I propose shifting the terms of recognition around the native through the minor native as a guiding episteme, situated within transcolonial border zones rather than colonial topography. In what follows, I use performance as a site and method of analysis. At the center of my analysis is a one woman show "Details Cannot Body Wants" (Singapore 1992, New York 1997, Australia 1996) by Peranakan (Straits Chinese) American performance artist scholar Chin Woon Ping. This play provides a preliminary model of re spatialization that opens a portal to one transcolonial border zone where the minor native is encountered. A Chinese Malaysian emigre, Chin resides in the United States and teaches at Dartmouth College, but has a tri national cultural background that informs her Peranakan Singaporean Malaysian, and Asian American identification.
