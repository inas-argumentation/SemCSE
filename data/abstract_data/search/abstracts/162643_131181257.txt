Paper ID 20100389 Effective Medium Modeling Of Gas Hydrate Filled Fractures Using Sonic Velocity In The Krishna Godavari Basin, Eastern Indian Offshore
The Indian National Gas Hydrate Program Expedition 01 has established one of the richest gashydrates deposits in the clay rich marine sediment of Krishna Godavari (KG) basin in the eastern Indian margin. The morphology of gas hydrates varies from complex vein structures (grain displacing) to invisible pore filling. The available rock physics models assume isotropic pore filling gas hydrates and thus mislead the estimation of gas hydrates for fractured fine grained sediments. The anisotropic behavior of sediment in KG basin provides additional complication. First time, we apply the differential effective medium theory to incorporate grain displacing morphology by which gas hydrates are included as vertical ellipsoids with aspect ratios corresponding to thin veins to nodules in an elastic anisotropic background. The results from sonic velocities at Hole 10D in KG basin show the saturations of gas hydrates in the depth range of 60 140 meters below sea floor (mbsf) as 35 42, 2730 and 33 41% of total porosity for three different morphologies of (i) pore filling, (ii) grain displacing and (iii) combination of grain displacing and pore filling, respectively. The saturation is the highest at ~67 mbsf for any morphology but the estimations differ among different morphologies. For pore filling morphology, the maximum (56% gas hydrates saturation is 18 22% higher than that of the graindisplacing morphology and 2 9% higher than that of the combined morphology.
