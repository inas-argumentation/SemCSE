Sleep and Brain Development
Abstract While the sleep related mechanisms that participate in the maturation of the brain are largely unknown, there is little question that sleep plays a critical role in normal brain development and maturation. Seminal work suggested that rapid eye movement sleep (REMS) affects brain maturation processes, including synaptic plasticity mechanisms that support the ongoing strengthening and weakening of the connectivity between neurons that characterizes brain maturation Roffwarg, Muzio, Dement, 1966 It is now known, however, that both REMS and non REM sleep stages contribute to brain maturation. Experimental studies of the role of sleep in brain maturation across number mammalian species are presented herein.
