SHADES OF ECONOMIC INDIGENISATION IN NIGERIA'S SECOND AND THIRD DEVELOPMENT PLANS, 1970 1980: REVISITING A NATIONAL DEVELOPMENT IMPERATIVE FOR A POSITIVE CHANGE
Nigeria's economic development journey from the 1940s up to the first half of the 1960s independence decade is replete with several kinds of development plans. But, a close study shows that none of these plans was able to factor 'economic indigenisation' as a critical precondition for Nigeria's development until the launch of the Second and Third National Development Plan between 1970 and 1980. Therefore, the paper examines how 'economic indigenisation' was pursued, and the extent of its outcome, in the Nigeria's Second and Third National Development Plans. The discourse is underpinned with 'dependency theory' as the most relevant framework of analysis and, adopts a qualitative historical method of research. The study, in its findings, argues that economic indigenisation as 'a national development strategy' was very visible in the two aforementioned development plans, and was impactful but, nevertheless, suffered external and internal 'contradictions' in its practical pursuit which the Nigerian government could not foresee or, perhaps, shyed away from. However, there is the optimism that with the right implementation of the 'Local Content Policy' of the present Nigerian Government indigenization of the Nigerian economy is assured.
