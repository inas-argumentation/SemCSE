Urbanization and Urban Government in China's Development: Toward a Political Economy of Urban Community?
The growth and transformation of cities have been at the heart of industrialization and economic development in the West. The relationships, both geographic and social, between industry and agriculture and the general patterns of land usage, occupational structure, and the social division of labor which have resulted have not only shaped theories of "modernization" in all the social science disciplines but also lie at the heart of contemporary views about the limits and nature of political community in modern society. Yet in recent years, the view that the contemporary Western city, or some variation of it, must be an integral part of not only our own future but of people living in non Western societies has increasingly been challenged by urban planners and at least some "modernization" theorists.1
