Intramedullary fixation with a third generation nail versus the sliding hip screw for trochanteric hip fractures: A randomised trial of 512 patients
400 patients with a trochanteric hip fracture were randomised to fixation with either a 220mm long Targon PF (proximal femoral) nail or a Sliding Hip Screw. All surgery was undertaken or supervised by one surgeon. All patients were followed up for a minimum of one year by a blinded observer. The mean age was 82 years (range 27 to 104 years) 20% were male. In addition there were two cases of cut out, three of plate detachment from the femur and one non union in the SHS group, requiring secondary surgery. There were only once compilations in the nailed a case of cut out which required secondary surgery. At follow up there was no difference in pain scores between groups but there was a tendency to improved mobility in the nailed group (p=0.004) These results suggest that with improved designs and surgical technique, the newer versions of short nails for proximal femoral fractures may not suffer from the complications of the earlier short intramedullary nails. Intramedullary fixation can result in a lower re operation rate (3.5% versus 0.5% and improved mobility in comparison to the sliding hip screw.
