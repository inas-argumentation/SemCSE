Correlation Between Exercise and Motor Skills with Elderly Patients Cognition (P2.178)
Objective: The objective of this cross sectional study was to investigate the possibility of establishing a positive correlation involving motor skills and physical activity level in elderly patients cognition. Background: According to the World Health Organization, by 2020, the number of people aged 60 years and older will outnumber children younger than five years. Thus, the association between physical activity and cognition, as well as between the level of physical fitness and motor skills, is a subject that begs for scientific investigation. Design/Methods: Data was gathered from 36 men aged 64.50 years of age on average (SD 3.49) and 58 women aged 67.89 years old on average (SD 3.40) All participants were randomly selected out patients of authors' Neurology clinic, inhabitants of Brazil. All participants answered a questionnaire in which they informed how many hours they spent on daily household activities, such as washing the dishes or cleaning the house; on transportation to and from places; on leisure and free time activities; and on proper physical exercise. Points were awarded for each action. In order to evaluate motor skills, data was gathered from clinical evaluation of muscle strength; joint flexibility; and cardio resistance. And lastly, to assess cognitive impairment, three types of memories were clinically tested, episodic; semantic and procedural as well as attention focus. Results: Results showed that among men, physical activity had a statistically significant correlation with semantic memory (r .456; p .023) and among women, cardio endurance had a statistically significant correlation with episodic memory. (r .312; p .012) All other data did not show statistically significant correlations with cognitive impairment or motor skills. Conclusions: These results suggest that the higher level of physical activity is a powerful protector of semantic memory among men and cardiorespiratory endurance similarly for episodic memory among women.
