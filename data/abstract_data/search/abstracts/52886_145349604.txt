Dreaming on the Pyramid: Responses to Globalism in Mexican Visual Culture
The term "globalization" was coined at the beginning ofthe 1980s in the management schools ofHarvard, Columbia and Stanf ord. It soon spread quickly, initially popularized in a series ofarticles on marketing strategies designed to rescue the world's advanced economies from the stagnation still lingering from the oil crisis of the mid seventies a crisis that had interrupted the pattern ofpostwar growth in the North American economy, the longest in history up to that time. Initially an economic concept, the word "globalism" would soon come into a dazzling critical fortune outside the marketing sphere upon being appropriated by neoliberal political discourse in the definition ofa "new" capitalism. The term very soon penetrated the languages ofsociology and anthropology as well, particularly in their application to the cultural field, where the concept ofglobalism threatened structures and mechanisms of social insertion and the very ideas ofthe world itself.
