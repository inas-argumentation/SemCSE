Clarification of the Role of the Supraspinatus Muscle in Shoulder Function*
The purpose of this study was to deter mine the torque produced by the supraspinatus muscle within the functional motion planes of forward flexion and elevation in the plane of the scapula. An isokinetic dynamometer was used to measure the reduction in torque produced by isolated paralysis of the suprascap ular and axillary nerves in ten normal volunteers A calculation of the potential torque of the supraspinatus and deltoid muscles was obtained from the product of the moment arm, cross sectionalarea, and integrated electromyographic signal. The resultsof boththe dynamometermeasurement and the calculation of torque supported our conclusion that the supraspinatus and deltoid muscles are equally responsible for producing torque about the shoulder joint in the functional planes of motion. CLINICAL RELEVANCE: This important role of the supraspinatus muscle in producing motion and torque in the glenohumeral joint should be considered when evaluating or operating on a patient with a rotator cuff tear. The contribution of the supraspinatus muscle to the power and motion of the shoulder has been unclear. Many everyday activities require that the arm be used between the two planes of forward flexion and elevation in the plane of the scapula7. This use repetitively traumatizes the supra spinatus tendon portion of the rotator cuff from impingement by the coracoacromial arch and the anterolateral edge of the acromion7. The supraspinatus muscle tendon unit is there fore the most vulnerable component of the rotator cuff mech anism. A clear understanding of the biomechanical role of this muscle tendon unit in the function of the shoulder appears to have significant clinical implications in the evaluation and management of patients with a glenohumeral disorder. Shoulder motion is extremely complex; however, at tempts to study it can be facilitated by examining isolated planes of motion2356'Adeg'2. Forward flexion is comprised of various inputs from the anterior part and a portion of the middle part of the deltoid, the supraspinatus, the coraco brachialis, the long head of the biceps brachii, and the clavicular portion of the pectoralis major muscles4. Eleva tion in the plane of the scapula is attributed to various inputs from the middle and a portion of the anterior part of the deltoid, the supraspinatus, and the force couple comprising the infraspinatus and subscapularis muscles45. The input from these other muscles must be accounted for in order to isolate the contribution of the supraspinatus muscle in the functional planes of forward flexion and elevation in the plane of the scapula. Calculation of Torque Determination of the moment arm. The determination of the moment arm of the supraspinatus and deltoid muscles requires that the direction of the muscle pull and the center of rotation of the humeral head be known The moment arm is defined as the length of a line from the center of rotation perpendicular to the force vector of the muscle.
