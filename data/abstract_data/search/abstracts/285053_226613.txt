Amyloid b and hyperphosphorylated tau synergy drives metabolic decline in preclinical Alzheimer's disease
This study was designed to test the interaction between amyloid b and tau proteins as a determinant of metabolic decline in preclinical Alzheimer's disease (AD) We assessed 120 cognitively normal individuals with [18F]florbetapir positron emission tomography (PET) and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) measurements at baseline, as well as [18F]fluorodeoxyglucose [18F]FDG) PET at baseline and at 24 months. A voxel based interaction model was built to test the associations between continuous measurements of CSF biomarkers, [18F]florbetapir and [18F]FDG standardized uptake value ratios (SUVR) We found that the synergistic interaction between [18F]florbetapir SUVR and CSF phosphorylated tau (p tau) measurements, rather than the sum of their independent effects, was associated with a 24 month metabolic decline in basal and mesial temporal, orbitofrontal, and anterior and posterior cingulate cortices (P<0.001) In contrast, interactions using CSF amyloid b1 42 and total tau biomarkers did not associate with metabolic decline over a time frame of 24 months. The interaction found in this study further support the framework that amyloid b and hyperphosphorylated tau aggregates synergistically interact to cause downstream AD neurodegeneration. In fact, the regions displaying the metabolic decline reported here were confined to brain networks affected early by amyloid b plaques and neurofibrillary tangles. Preventive clinical trials may benefit from using a combination of amyloid b PET and p tau biomarkers to enrich study populations of cognitively normal subjects with a high probability of disease progression in studies, using [18F]FDG as a biomarker of efficacy.
