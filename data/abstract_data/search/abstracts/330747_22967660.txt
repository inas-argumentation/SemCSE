Review of "A Multi Modal Parcellation of Human Cerebral Cortex" by Glasser M, Coalson TS, Robinson EC, Hacker CD, Harwell J, Yacoub E, Ugurbil K, Andersson J, Beckmann CF, Jenkinson M, Smith SM, Van Essen DC in Nature 536: 171 181, 2016.
he elucidation of the structure and function of the human T cerebral cortex has been a long sought after goal in the neuroscience community. Glasser et al have made tremendous progress by creating a map of the cerebral cortex, which describes 180 areas per hemisphere, 97 of which were newly elucidated and 83 of which have been previously described. These findings will help shed light into how individual variations in cortical areas lead to cognitive traits, learning disabilities, aging, neurodevelopment, and psychiatric pathologies. Areas of the cerebral cortex differ from one another in 4 ways: cytological architecture, functional specialization, connection with surrounding areas, and topographic organization. Previous attempts at mapping the cortex focused on a single property (eg, Brodman's 52 distinct histologic structures) while this study combined all 4 properties to create a more dynamic understanding of the cerebral cortex.
