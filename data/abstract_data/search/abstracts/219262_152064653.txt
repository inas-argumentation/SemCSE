Media Realities: Visual Competence in Social Context
AbstractRelationships between classes of visual messages and "reality" differ, with consequent differences in the social rules for interpretation. Advertising presents an idealized world, often a fantasy world; news presents itself as a window on the "real world." Problems of comprehension and awareness of artifice and intentionality thus vary according to message class. Visual competence is the ability to classify and interpret messages according to visual conventions and social rules for each class. Research with elementary school children indicates that the formalism of advertising images and real life experience as consumers facilitate visual analysis and recognition of messageness, but do not teach awareness of the embedded ideological functions of advertising. Conversely, the realism of news images and lack of real life experiences against which to compare them disarm visual analysis; the viewing contex of news images tends to be the real world, albeit a "real world" often composed primarily of news.
