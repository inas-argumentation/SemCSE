Elucidating structure performance correlations in gas phase selective ethanol oxidation and CO oxidation over metal doped g MnO2
Despite of considerable efforts on the MnO2 based catalytic combustion, the different structural and component requirements of MnO2 for gas phase selective oxidation and complete oxidation largely remain unknown. By comparing four types of MnO2 with different crystal structures (a, b, g and d) g MnO2 was found to be the most efficient catalyst for both aerobic selective oxidation of ethanol and CO oxidation. The structural effect of g MnO2 was further investigated by doping metal ions into the framework and by comparing the catalytic performance in the gas phase aerobic oxidation of CO and ethanol. Among ten M g MnO2 catalysts, Zn g MnO2 showed the lowest temperature (160 degC) for achieving 90% CO conversion. The CO oxidation activity of the M g MnO2 catalysts was found to be more relevant to the surface acidity basicity than the reducibility. In contrast, surface reducibility has been demonstrated to be more crucial in the gas phase ethanol oxidation. Cu g MnO2 with higher reducibility and more oxygen vacancies of Mn2+/Mn3+ species exhibited higher catalytic activity in the selective ethanol oxidation. Cu g MnO2 achieved the highest acetaldehyde yield (75% and space time yield (5.4 g gcat 1 h 1) at 200 degC, which are even comparable to the results obtained by the state of the art silver and gold containing catalysts. Characterization results and kinetic studies further suggest that the CO oxidation follows the lattice oxygen based Mars van Krevelen mechanism, whereas both surface lattice oxygen and adsorbed oxygen species involve in the ethanol activation.
