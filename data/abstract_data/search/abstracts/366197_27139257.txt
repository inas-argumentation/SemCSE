Condom failure is on the increase
Condom failure has become the leading cause of unwanted pregnancy says a paper published in this months British Journal of Family Planning (1996;22:6 9) The paper says that users should have more information and education about the way in which condoms work and what to do if they fail. The study presents data from 4666 women who had a termination of pregnancy at the Marie Stopes Centre in Leeds between 1989 and 1993. All the women were counseled with the aim of identifying reasons for contraceptive failure. The data from these consultations show that although the number of women presenting who used no contraceptive method at all has fallen the number who said that condom failure led to their pregnancy has risen. In the most recent year of the study more than 46% of the women blamed condom failure for their unwanted pregnancy. A potential problem with the study that users might blame condom failure rather than admit that they used no contraception at all is common to all surveys in this area.
