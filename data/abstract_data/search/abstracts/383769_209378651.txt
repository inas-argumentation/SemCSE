Joint Control in European Data Protection Law How to make Sense of the CJEU's Holy Trinity A case study on the recent CJEU rulings (Facebook Fanpages; Jehovah's Witnesses; Fashion ID)
Within twelve months, the CJEU has delivered three eagerly awaited judgments on the question of (joint) control in European data protection law. The following case study examines the factual and legal backgrounds as well as points out the respective key findings of the Facebook Fanpage ruling (II.1) the Jehovah's Witnesses ruling (II.2) and Fashion ID ruling (II.3) Even though the CJEU has provided valuable insights on how it interprets the concept of (joint) control, a plethora of logical gaps and questions, in particular with regard to the applicability on other scenarios remains widely unclear. Against this background, this article intents to critically evaluate these three judgments in the light of the general criteria establishing controllership (III.1 to III.3) and based on these findings establish a cohesive system respectively testing scheme, to facilitate a manageable and verifiable assessment of other (potential) joint control cases (III.4)
