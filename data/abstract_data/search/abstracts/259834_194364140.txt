The Qatsi Trilogy. Godfrey Reggio, dir. Criterion Collection CC2211BD, 2012, Blue ray Disc.
Even though art is typically viewed as a solitary profession, like so many things in today's world, it is actually extremely collaborative. Film, more so than most other media, brings a number of creative people together because the artistic object itself requires a team to assemble it: from camera operators and sound engineers, to editors, composers, and music recorders. And although the prevailing idea of auteur theory in film studies still suggests that some directors have such a powerful creative vision that their films can be considered as objects from a singular authorial voice, Godfrey Reggio's thought provoking Qatsi trilogy (Koyaanisqatsi, 1983; Powaqqatsi, 1988; and Naqoyqatsi, 2002) must be viewed as a collaboration. Reggio will be the first person to acknowledge his colleagues: "any success that Koyaanisqatsi enjoys is the result of a fruitful collaboration between [cinematographer] Ron [Fricke] [composer] Philip [Glass] myself and the provocative and intelligent crew that was assembled for the film."1 And although Ron Fricke did not work with Reggio after Koyaanisqatsi (he left to work on his own series of documentary films) Glass became an important collaborator, working with Reggio on all of his films and beginning work on the music at the start of each film's development: "Philip is involved with the concept; he goes on location, looks at all the rushes, [and] is collaborating on a daily basis with me."2 Those who have never seen the Qatsi films will find them atypical at best. The films themselves have no narrative in the traditional sense and even lack dialogue as a means to guide a viewer. Instead, they are a succession of images and clips set to Glass's music. Each film has a theme that relates to its title in Navajo words that mean "life out of balance," "life in transformation," and "life as war," respectively. They can be challenging to watch, but will offer many viewers a means to reflect on modern life and the way we relate to each other and our environment. The collaboration that birthed the films is the theme of a vast number of special features packed into the Criterion Collection's recent release of the Qatsi trilogy on Blu ray. Among them are previously released interviews from the early 2000s included on previous DVDs, alongside interviews conducted in 2012 with Reggio and Fricke and some truly rare films and demos.
