Aerodynamics Simulations for the D 8 " Double Bubble " Aircraft Using the LAVA Unstructured Solver
The D8 "double bubble" is a proposed design to reduce emissions and noise in domestic passenger aircraft of the Boeing 737 class. It features boundary layer ingesting engines located under a non load bearing tail and a lightweight low sweep wing, with a cruise speed of Mach 0.7. The D8's wide body supplies more lift than that of the Boeing 737, and its tapering rear thickens the boundary layer for ingestion by engines with distortiontolerant fans. To investigate these claims, unstructured viscous grids generated over a fine surface triangulation using Star CCM+ are used to model the unpowered 1:20 scale D8 with flow conditions mimicking those in the MIT Wright brothers wind tunnel at angles of attack from 0 to 12 degrees. LAVA, the Launch Ascent and Vehicle Aerodynamics CFD solver, is used to carry out simulations on unstructured grids. Unstructured results are compared to wind tunnel data, and to data from structured overset grid solutions obtained with LAVA and OVERFLOW.
