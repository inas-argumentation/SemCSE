The Influence of "Slip" on Rheological Measurements on a Mycelial Broth of Aspergillus niger
It has been shown' that mycelial fermentation broths have highly viscous and nowNewtonian rheological properties. Despite the significance of these properties for bioprocess design, only a few studies have attempted to quantify the relationship between transport processes (such as heat, mass, and momentum transfer) and rheological properties of fermentation media. Apparently, one of the major reasons for the lack of progress in this area is the difficulty involved in measuring the rheological properties of heterogeneous fluids such as mycelial broths. Some investigators3* argue that the disparities among rheological parameter estimates for a given broth result from the use of standard "absolute" rheological measurement devices such as the concentric cylinder and pipeline viscometers which are prone to particle settling and a "slip velocity" near the wall. Despite the confusion in the literature, limited work has been done to compare results from various viscometers and to observe and assess these "slip" effects. This study involves a comparison of pipeline, coaxial cylinder, and impeller viscometers with emphasis on wall slip and its relevance to bioreactor design.
