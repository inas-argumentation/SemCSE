A Review of "How Youth Ministry Can Change Theological Education If We Let It: Reflections from the Lilly Endowment's High School Theology Program Seminar."
How Youth Ministry Can Change Theological Education If We Let It represents the culmination of years of youth ministry done by Lilly Endowment funded theology programs for high school youth since 1993, as well as years of research, conversation, and reflection on those programs. This book offers a wealth of information, imagination, and inspiration for those who are concerned with Christian religious education. Across the different High School Theology Programs (HSTPs) represented in this book, there are commonalities about what they offer and the outcomes they see from participants. Each HSTP includes imaginative pedagogy, opportunity for intentional vocational discernment, community, opportunities for and expectation of deep theological reflection, mentors for young people, and engagement with Scripture and theological texts. The emphases and methods of the HSTPs are, however, diverse. This is especially true because of the different traditions represented by these programs from Mennonite to Catholic. A theme that runs strongly throughout the book is that the youth in these programs are taken seriously, as Christians and as theologians. The youth and the theological school faculty teaching them seem to find this both refreshing and surprising. As the two introductory chapters of this bookmake clear, these HSTPs offer intentional Christian formation for the most committed, interested, and religious young people. These are the youth who stand outside of the expectation of what young people in the United States are like in regard to their faith, especially as we have learned from the National Study of Youth and Religion. These are the youth who, although they may participate in "normal" youth group programming (often seen as moralistic and entertaining) are largely unsatisfied by it. These youg people are not dissimilar from the adults who will find their way to seminary, pursuing more theological education than their local churches can provide. The chapters of this book are written by those who are most invested in the programs, often directors themselves. Many of the chapters focus on one HSTP in particular, but do so in conversation with other programs. The reflections on these programs seem fair and balanced, though clearly more focused on strengths of their programs than on weaknesses that may exist. An interesting and helpful addition to the conversation would be reflections on how these programs have developed over time (some have been operating for decades) What have they learned along the way? How have they changed over time and why? What missteps have they made? HSTPs offer the best of youth ministry and the best of theological education. The reflections on these programs will certainly inspire imagination for theological education broadly speaking, from that which takes place in local churches and youth groups
