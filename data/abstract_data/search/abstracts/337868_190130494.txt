Lines of Resistance: Dziga Vertov and the Twenties
Yuri Tsivian, ed. Lines of Resistance: Dziga Vertov and the Twenties. Intro. Yuri Tsivian. Trans. Julian Graffy. Gemona: Le Giornate del Cinema Muto, 2004. 422 pp. Frame enlargements.Some films age more rapidly than others. Those teaching Soviet cinema of the 1920s can attest to the fact that the films once regarded as the masterpieces of the "revolutionary cinema" have been losing their appeal with every new generation of students. There is, however, a surprising exception to this tendency: Dziga Vertov's Man with a Movie Camera. Whether it is the new general interest in documentary cinema or the Vertov inspired musical score by Alloy Orchestra (released in 1996) the film is enjoying its second renaissance, at least among film students. Yuri Tsivian was behind the digital video release of Man with the Movie Camera and supplemented it with a first rate audio commentary track. In 2004, he also helped organize a retrospective of Vertov's films in Italy at Le Giornate del Cinema Muto, the most important festival of silent cinema. Lines of Resistance, edited by Tsivian and published by the festival, is meant to accompany the films and provide their historical context, thus making the study of Vertov's legacy much easier.Lines of Resistance is an impressive volume that brings to the English speaking world original documents about Vertov's silent films made between 1919 and 1929. The collection expands and supplements the translation of Vertov's writings published over twenty years ago (Kino Eye: The Writings of Dziga Vertov. Ed. Annette Michelson, Trans. Kevin O'Brien, Berkeley: University of California Press, 1984) and shows that there is more to Vertov than his "loud" manifestos which inspired filmmakers in the West, such as Jean Luc Godard's Dziga Vertov Group. According to Tsivian the goal of Lines of Resistance is "to explain Vertov historically" and to "study the ecology of his time" (pp. 1 2) The volume lives up to this task. We often forget that Soviet Union of the 1920s was a social experiment, a market of ideas as well as a rapidly changing political climate. Vertov's films bear witness to these social changes and his ideas on cinema are part of a lively discussion on the nature of the medium. Therefore, it is crucial to see Vertov's films in their proper context rather than in the contexts filtered by the Communist party revisions or textual analysts in the West.Vertov was a controversial figure within the Soviet film establishment of the 1920s who thrived on provocations, clashes and battles with his numerous opponents. The anthology presents the debates and polemics surrounding his films and his ideas about cinema. The collection groups articles and reviews of Vertov's films both chronologically and thematically.
