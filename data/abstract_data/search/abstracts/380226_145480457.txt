The data reveal a great disparity between the teacher's involvement in testing, which is considerable, and exposure to training in measurement, which is minimal. He recommends modification of teacher training curricula, the publication of a short booklet with the basic information teachers need, and inservice training. While I look dimly on the booklet suggestion, fearing that the booklets would not be read, I hope very much that Goslin's total report may help to move the school and college administrators who could give life to the other two recommendations. With respect to the second general theme, attitude consistency, the data provide evidence of internal consistency in the belief systems of teachers concerning tests and their use. Especially evident was the great amount of confidence which teachers place in tests; this finding makes the previous recommendations appear even more important. There seems to be real attitudinal ambivalence only about whether teachers should give pupils or their parents access to test scores.
