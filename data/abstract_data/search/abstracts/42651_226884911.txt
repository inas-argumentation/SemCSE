An Evaluation of the Right to Privacy in the Digital Age
Privacy does not always connote the act of being left alone or protection from home evasion. Privacy is in fact infringed every day. Over the years, there have been vast improvements in the availability, storage, and removal of personal information online, supported by developments in big data analytics. Google, Yahoo, Bing, Ask.com have been deemed to be search engines that users cannot 'lie' to. Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, LinkedIn monitor companions and places of users, Google Music, Apple Music tell the type of songs the user likes, Google Books, Amazon, Wattpad, tells the type of books enjoyed by the user, CCTV tells who was here and why, Taxify, Uber, OkadaGo, tells the location of the user, amongst others, which shows the dire need of maintaining a balance on the benefits of receiving information with the threat to the right to privacy. The idea of privacy revolves around keeping to the exclusion of the public eye what an individual considers as private. Data is a pollution problem in the digital age, bringing forth demands such as how it is accessed, how it is used, what it is used for, how it is disposed and forgotten, and the law does not really show a visible line between these rights. This essay therefore dissected the various arguments to the long lingering question, 'Is privacy truly dead? drawing a conclusive remark thereafter.
