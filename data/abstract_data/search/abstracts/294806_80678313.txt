Dysbetalipoproteinemia and Type III Hyperlipidemia
Dysbetalipoproteinemia is an uncommon variant of normal human lipoprotein metabolism, most frequently conferred by the homozygous inheritance of the E2 isoform of apolipoprotein E. In the absence of coexisting hyperlipidemia, the apo E2,2 genotype is actually associated with hypolipidemia, with diminished levels of low density lipoproteins and reduced risk of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) However, in the presence of a coexisting cause of hyperlipidemia (genetic or acquired) the hypolipidemia is converted to type III hyperlipoproteinemia (HLP) with attendant increased risk of premature ASCVD as well as distinctive cutaneous xanthomas. Because of the insights it provides into both the physiological and pathophysiological regulation of lipoprotein transport and atherogenesis in humans and the paradox of its association with both hypo and hyperlipidemia, dysbetalipoproteinemia has long been of interest to endocrinologists and students of lipoprotein metabolism out of proportion to the prevalence of type III HLP and its associated ASCVD risk burden.
