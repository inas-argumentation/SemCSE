The industrial and agricultural significance of yeasts
Publisher Summary Yeasts are used in various industrial processes, such as production of alcoholic beverages and various metabolic products such as enzymes, vitamins, capsular polysaccharides, carotenoids, polyhydric alcohols, lipids, glycolipids, citric acid, ethanol, carbon dioxide, and compounds synthesized by the introduction of recombinant DNA into yeasts. Some of these products are produced commercially while others are potentially valuable in biotechnology. Yeasts are producers of the four leading fermentation products which include beer, wine, single cell protein and fodder, and baker's yeast. The fifth product is citric acid, which is made by yeasts and molds. A large number of yeast species are currently recognized for their ability to utilize hydrocarbons as sole sources of carbon and energy. Hydrocarbon utilizing yeasts can play a significant role in the microbial oxidation of oil spills on land and in aquatic environments. The metabolism of methanol by yeasts is different from that in bacteria, and its pathway has been elucidated by Quayle and coworkers. Various yeast products are also useful in the flavor industry. Ethyl acetate and acetaldehyde are produced by C utilis. Acetaldehyde is also formed by film yeast in the production of Spanish flor sherry. Esters and other volatiles are made by K. lactis and monoterpenes are formed by Ambrosiozyma monospora. Yeasts can be used for a number of bioconversions, which include ketoreduction, oxidation of hydroxyl groups, ester hydrolysis, and hydrogenation of double bonds in steroids; conversion of alkaloids; and xenobiotics.
