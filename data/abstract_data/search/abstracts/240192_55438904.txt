Property rights and land disputes: theory and evidences from the Highland of Ethiopia
This paper investigates the link between insecure property rights and land disputes using household panel data from Ethiopia. We offer two main contributions. First, we develop a theoretical framework of land disputes. Our model predicts that in difficult times (i.e. when water is scarce) bargaining is more likely to breakdown and dispute to arise if property rights are ill defined. Second, guided by our theoretical framework, we empirically assess the causal relationship between land tenure security and clashes. Our identification strategy relies on the rollout of a land certification program in Ethiopia and on the exogenous variation in water availability, a trigger of land disputes.
