Nanocellulose mediated fabrication of sustainable future materials
Cellulose, a green and abundant material worldwide has been used as a substrate for many green smart materials without any environmental hazard. Amalgamation of this multitasked material with nanotechnology has attracted the attention of the scientific community nowadays for designing commercially viable materials. The extraordinary features, tailored surface chemistry, renewability, and biocompatibility of nanocellulose have suggested a plethora of their applications in diversified fields. The hydrophilic nature and presence of hydroxyl groups on its templates promote their usage in many industrial processes. However, the surface modification in nanocellulose provides more advanced applications. Both types of cellulose, that is, cellulose nanocrystals and nanofibers, have been documented for their potential application in paper, food sector, biomedical, hygiene, energy harvesting, environmental remediation, flexible electronics, water purification, skin care, paints, sensors, and automotive sectors. Despite these, some intrinsic propensities of cellulose are the major deterrent for its commercialization at pilot scale. Therefore, for the challenges regarding their production, cost benefit analysis, socioeconomic impacts, life cycle assessment, and toxicological profiling are needed to be addressed properly. The present chapter discussed the production methods of nanocellulose, properties, the current state of smart materials being developed, and also the future direction for the evolution of many sustainable functional materials.
