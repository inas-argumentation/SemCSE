Cryogenic Aerosols and Supercritical Fluid Cleaning and Surface Conditioning
The continuing need for nanometer sized particle removal without altering the underlying substrate properties (i.e. material loss, film damage, dielectric constant shift) has kept cryogenic cleaning as a viable cleaning technology where wet cleaning techniques are not possible or are difficult to implement. The current generation of cryogenic aerosols used at integrated circuit production facilities (mainly argon and/or nitrogen based) has worked well for many technology generations but now require modifications to achieve nanometer sized particle removal without damage. This chapter will include recent advancements of cryogenic aerosols to achieve these continually tightening defect removal specifications. Although supercritical processes are yet to be introduced into current semiconductor manufacturing, they offer a number of potential cleaning and surface preparation solutions that will become more acute as technologies scale. The removal of hardened resist, postetch residues, and particles from smaller and smaller features will require selective and chemically "green" cleaning methods. Supercritical fluids (SCF) meet these needs as well as repair etch damage and remove porogens from low k films. SCF also provide low surface tension for stiction free drying.
