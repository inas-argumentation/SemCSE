Spinal Hypotension in Women Undergoing Elective Caesarean Section after Spinal Anaesthesia with and without Prophylactic Ondansetron
Background: Spinal hypotension is a frequent complication of spinal anesthesia in females during caesarean section. It can result in adverse perinatal outcomes such as maternal nausea and vomiting, fetal acidosis and is an important contributory factor in maternal mortality related to regional anesthesia. Objective: To compare the frequency of spinal hypotension following spinal anaesthesia in women undergoing elective caesarean section with and without prophylactic ondansetron with reference to the baseline characteristics. Material and Methods: This comparative randomized trail included 100 pregnant women aged between 18 35 years (means 26.50 5 years) undergoing elective cesarean section under spinal anesthesia. These patients were randomly divided into two groups. In Group A conventional pre operative treatment whereas to Group B received additional dose of 4mg prophylactic ondansetron. This research was jointly conducted from February, 2017 to August, 2017 at department of Anesthesia, Arif Memorial Teaching Hospital, affiliated with Rashid Latif Medical College and Lahore General Hospital, Lahore. 2 Results: The BMI of the patients was 27.93+ 4.45 Kg/m Mean baseline systolic blood pressure of the patients was 119.13+ 10.57 mmHg. The frequency of hypotension was significantly lower in patients who received prophylactic ondansetron (34.0% vs. 64.0% p=0.003) as compared to controls. Similar differences were observed regardless of age, BMI and baseline systolic blood pressure groups. Conclusion: Prophylactic ondansetron significantly reduced the frequency of spinal hypotension in women undergoing elective cesarean section under spinal anesthesia which favors its routine use in future practice.
