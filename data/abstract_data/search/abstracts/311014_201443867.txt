Blockchain Meets Genomics: Governance Considerations for Promoting Food Safety and Public Health
Significant morbidity and mortality from foodborne illness continues to burden the developing and industrialized worlds. The United States alone faces millions of foodborne illness cases per year, resulting in substantial public health and economic costs. Advances in two emerging technologies promise to bolster regulatory and private responses to foodborne pathogens. First, food regulators have begun implementing whole genome sequencing (WGS) to identify pathogens and their geographic locations of origin. Second, food distributors including Walmart have begun to report blockchain pilot projects to trace contaminated food products through their supply chains. If combined effectively, the nexus of blockchain and WGS could enhance the capacity of public health officials and industry actors to respond to and prevent foodborne illnesses. However, the convergence of these technologies raises novel technical, regulatory, and governance issues. For example, the structure and ownership of blockchain platforms will have implications on regulatory compliance, the ability to interface with government WGS programs, and competition amongst food distributors. Responding to regulatory challenges while promoting the public health benefits of blockchain and WGS will require flexible and agile governance approaches while these technologies continue to mature. Rather than rigid command and control regulation, "soft law" instruments including voluntary regulatory programs and technical standards should offer an appropriate, initial balance for these competing considerations.
