Use of anti thyroid drugs in patients with hyperthyroidism: a case for shared decision making
Dear Sir, Struja and Schuetz [1] took a recently paper by Kitahara et al. [2] as a "sic et simpliciter" demonstration of the "association of radioiodine exposure and later risk of cancer in Graves' Disease (GD) patients" and asked for a revision of current guidelines such as from the American Thyroid Association [3] furthermore, they called for a wider use of anti thyroid drugs (ATDs) In addition, a multidisciplinary shared decision making approach is strongly advocated, in which the risks and benefits of each treatment for Graves' disease should be outlined. It is hard to see how any physician would not support the process of shared decision making between specialists and patients wherever the clinical situation offers the slightest possibility of doing so. Of course, this process does require proper evaluation of reported data, well beyond titles, and abstracts, in order to prevent ideologies from gaining the upper hand over scientific reason. Certainly, the paper by Kitahara et al. may on the surface insinuate a causal relationship between a moderate cancer mortality risk excess and radioiodine therapy of benign thyroid disease. However, an objective assessment of this paper inevitably leads to the conclusion that a multitude of limitations precludes the assumption offered when assessed with the necessary scientific rigor.
