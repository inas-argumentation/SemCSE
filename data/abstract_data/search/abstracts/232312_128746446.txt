Simulation of North Atlantic low frequency SST variability
The role of atmospheric circulation anomalies in generating midlatitude sea surface temperature (SST) variability is investigated by means of ocean general circulation model (OGCM) experiments, in which observed winds are prescribed during the period 1950 1979. The heat flux parameterization involves atmospheric advection equivalent to specifying an interactive atmosphere, albeit a simple one. Forced by the observed wind field, the model is successful in reproducing the large scale patterns of the observed wintertime SST in the North Atlantic. The local correlations between observed and simulated winter time SSTs are high enough to be significant around 40degN but are not as high in summer (when the scales of the anomalies are smaller) and north of 50degN. The model results are used to understand more about the mechanisms of creating interannual SST variability in winter.
