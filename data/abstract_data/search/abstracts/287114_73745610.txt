Attention to Food Insecurity in Clinical Practice: Continuing Medical Education Development
Objective: Food insecurity is a predominantly economic condition that poses numerous risks to children's health and development. For many children, the doctor's office is a place of regular contact outside the home. Knowledge of indicators of food insecurity allows health providers to identify patients at risk and to provide an appropriate intervention. However, food insecurity is not routinely included in health providers' training. Target Audience: Pediatric and family practice physicians and nurse practitioners, and other allied health providers. Theory, Prior Research, Rationale: Professional practice gaps identified in surveys of Oregon health providers (n1/4186) and literature were: understanding the health impacts of food insecurity; failure to screen; and, failure to offer safety net resources. Course content and format were reviewed by local and national experts, and is certified through the American Medical Association for continuing medical education credit (CME) Description: The online course provides a review research on health consequences of food insecurity with a goal of increasing health provider rates of awareness and monitoring of this condition in the pediatric population. Evaluation: A post course exam score of 75% is required for receipt of CME. Course ratings include content, quality, and relevance to practice. Course registrants are asked to indicate whether they would make a change in their practice, and to describe that change. Conclusions and Implications: Course content, quality, and relevance to practice were evaluated favorably. Of 415 registrants in 2014, 273 (66% said they would make one or more of the following changes: improve screening practices, display/distribute resources, increased sensitivity to problems, and train students and staff. Funding:Oregon State University Extension, Professional and Non Credit Education.
