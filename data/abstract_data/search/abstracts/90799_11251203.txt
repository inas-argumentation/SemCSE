Impact of estrogen receptor b expression on breast cancer prognosis: a meta analysis
Abstract Estrogen receptor (ER) b has been discovered for decades; however, its prognostic value in breast cancer patients remains controversial. We aimed to evaluate the impact of ER b expression on breast cancer survival. A systematic search of Medline, Embase, and Cochrane Library was performed to identify the association between ER b expression and outcomes in early breast cancer patients. Random effects meta analysis was conducted to generate combined hazard ratios (HRs) with 95 confidence intervals (CIs) for overall survival (OS) and disease free survival (DFS) A total of 6769 patients for ER b1, 2295 patients for ER b2, and 2271 patients for ER b5 from 21 studies were included. ER b1 protein expression was correlated with both favorable 5 year DFS and OS (HR 0.690, 95 CI 0.610 0.779; P 0.001; HR 0.632, 95 CI 0.533 0.749; P 0.001) while ER b1 mRNA had no significant association with DFS (HR 0.915, 95 CI 0.581 1.440, P 0.700) ER b2 protein was associated with improved DFS (HR 0.799, 95 CI 0.644 0.992; P 0.042) but not OS (HR 0.958, 95 CI 0.762 1.205; P 0.712) ER b5 protein was not significantly associated with DFS (HR 1.070, 95 CI 0.810 1.410; P 0.642) Subgroup analysis showed that higher ER b1 expression was associated with better 5 year DFS in both ER a positive and negative patients, but the positive association between ER b1 expression and 5 year OS was only seen in ER a positive patients. Wild type ER b (ER b1) and its variant ER b2 protein expressions are associated with better survival in early breast cancer patients. The prognostic significance of ER b1 for DFS is independent of ER a coexpression, whereas the impact on OS was only in ER a positive breast cancer.
