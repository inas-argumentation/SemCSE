Heavy Metal Stocks and Concentrations in Forest Soils
In this project we statistically analysed data from the National Forest Soil Inventory II (NFSI II) and compared the results with data from the NFSI I. The data comprised heavy metal contents and stocks in the organic layer and two increments of the mineral soil (0 5, 5 10 cm) Distinct influences of atmospheric deposition were found for the elements Pb, Cd and Hg, while this was less pronounced for As and Cu. The elements Ni, Cr and Zn were primarily determined by their geogenic base level. The contents of Pb, Cu, Cr, Ni and Hg show a clear differentiation with humus horizons, with increasing heavy metal contents in the order L Of Oh. In contrast, Cd and Zn contents show no clear differentiation with humus horizons. The atmospheric deposition of heavy metals decreased in the time between NFSI I and NFSI II. An exemplary analysis of NFSI I and II data from North Rhine Westphalia showed that heavy metals were translocated from the organic layer into the mineral soil, while the total stocks over the entire profile remained more or less constant. For the evaluation of heavy metal contents in the mineral soil, we used precautionary values from the BBodSchV. The contents are for most of the investigated heavy metals below the precautionary values in the majority of forest soils. This indicates that the forest soils in Germany are with respect to their heavy metal contents in a good quality state. Only the elements As and Pb appreciably exceed the precautionary values on 11.2% (As) and 22.1% (Pb) of the forest area.
