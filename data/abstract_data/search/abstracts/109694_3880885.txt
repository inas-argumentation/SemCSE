A unified birth defects research
We want to share with you the exciting news about the formal merger of Birth Defects Research into a single unified journal that will fully cover the broad range of interests of the Teratology Society beginning January, 2017. Under this format, we will continue to publish content in clinical and molecular teratology, developmental and reproductive toxicology, and cutting edge, topical reviews in developmental biology (formerly Parts A, B and C, respectively) but having all of this content under a single cover will better emphasize the synthetic, systems biology nature of the field. The journal Birth Defects Research is devoted to the publication of original scientific research that contributes to the understanding of the prenatal causative factors and mechanisms leading to adverse pregnancy outcomes, namely structural and functional birth defects, pregnancy loss, postnatal functional defects in the human population, and to the identification of prenatal factors and biological mechanisms that reduce these risks. This includes safety studies that are intended to shed light on the effects and the mechanism of action of chemical, biological, or physical agents on any aspect of reproduction and development in humans. Understanding the full range of activities in the developing embryo is, therefore, central to deciphering the pathogenesis of birth defects. Important advances have been made into mapping the sequence of events leading to normal and abnormal development, empowered by the technological advances of biochemistry, cell biology, molecular genetics, and more recently informatics, digital modeling, stem cell biology, and regenerative biomedicine. Birth Defects Research represents Teratology as an interdisciplinary science and welcomes studies of humans, animal models, or tissues in vitro, and ranging from genomic, proteomic, and epigenetic approaches through molecular and morphological studies to novel epidemiological analyses. With its present launch, Birth Defects Research will continue to develop and evolve, both in format and content, to reflect and report the dynamic development of our field. We will publish 20 issues per year rather than the previous schedule of 12 issues of BDR Part A, six issues of Part B, and four issues of Part C. While this is only a small change in the number of issues, we do not plan any reduction in the number of papers published. The new schedule provides us with increased flexibility, especially in being able to offer content from a variety of original research more quickly. The merged journal will be the leading forum for exchange of scientific ideas on Birth Defects Research. We encourage you to submit your work and promise you a rapid and fair review of your manuscripts, thanks in part to our efficient online submission and peer review process, available at https:/mc.manuscriptcentral.com/bdr. We are confident that under the Teratology Society umbrella and with the wide international reach of the publisher, the number of Birth Defects Research readers will be large and the citation potential for your work will be high.
