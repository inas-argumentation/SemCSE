Fatty acid composition and oxidation stability of hemp (Cannabis sativa L. seed oil extracted by supercritical carbon dioxide
Supercritical carbon dioxide (SC CO 2 was employed to extract oil from hemp Cannabis sativa L. seeds. For ground seeds, the supercritical extraction was carried out at temperatures of 40, 60 and 80 degC and pressures of 300 and 400 bar. Different solvent ratios were applied. Supercritical CO 2 extractions were compared with a conventional technique, n hexane in Soxhlet. The extraction yields, fatty acid composition of the oil and oxidation stability were determined. The seed samples used in this work contained 81% PUFAs, of which 59.6% was linoleic acid (o 6) 3.4% g linolenic (o 3) and 18% a linolenic (o 6) The highest oil yield from seeds was 22% corresponding to 72% recovery, at 300 bar and 40 degC and at 400 bar and 80 degC. The highest oxidation stability corresponding to 2.16 mM Eq Vit E was obtained at 300 bar and 80 degC.
