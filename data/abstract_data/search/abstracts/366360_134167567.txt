Rapid prototyping of functional ceramic composites
Laminated object manufacturing (LOM{reg_sign} is one of several manufacturing systems developed recently to produce a solid part directly from a 3D CAD drawing, without the use of hard tooling or dies. These systems begin with a conventional 3 D CAD file solid model or wire form model that is converted to a de facto standard format for flexible manufacturing .STL file) and sliced into thin cross sections by a separate computer program. These slices are then used to control the equipment that produces the component slice by slice. This paper describes an application of the CerLOM approach to produce multilayered composites of ZrO{sub 2} and Al{sub 2}O{sub 3} The choice of materials is based on previous studies that showed that a beneficial increase in toughness can be achieved with multilayered microstructures in this system. The toughness increase is due to martensitic transformation in the ZrO{sub 2}phase. Because the CerLOM process produces the component directly from a CAD file without tooling, dies or molds, the production is rapid and cost effective. Components of complex shape can be fabricated readily, and design changes can be made easily.
