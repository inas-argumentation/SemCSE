VISUAL SEARCH CENTRALITY AND MINIMUM MAP SIZE
Cartographers are frequently faced with the task of designing maps for publications in which space allocated for graphics is small. This study examined the relationship between map size (visual angle) and visual search centrality in light of the findings of Enoch (1959) who reported that a visual angle of 9deg was critical to several parameters of visual search. Eye movements for 16 subjects were recorded during three map analysis tasks involving a total of 96 maps in which four different sized graphics were used. Experimental results indicated that the larger the image, the greater the number of fixations. The duration of each fixation, however, was shown to decrease with increasing visual angle. It appears, as suggested by Enoch, that maps at least 9deg in size and larger promote visual search centrality and may result in more efficient visual processing.
