Prophylactic Antibiotics in Implant Based Breast Reconstruction
The Surgical Care Improvement Project (SCIP) evidence based guidelines for the safe and effective use of prophylactic antibiotics were initially published in 2006 and state that antibiotics should be given within one hour before incision and discontinued within 24 hours.1 By contrast to these recommendations, prolonged postoperative antibiotics have been routinely prescribed in implantbased breast reconstruction (IBBR) because of concern for bacterial contamination of the prosthesis during insertion, presence of drains, and the use of acellular dermal matrix (ADM) which provides support for the implant.2 There have been several studies comparing infection rates with and without prolonged prophylactic antibiotics in IBBR; despite these studies, there is still no definitive conclusion of the optimal time course for antibiotics in the literature.3 This study evaluates the infection outcomes of changing one surgeon's policy regarding prophylactic antibiotics after discharge after IBBR. This study is a retrospective review of a consecutive series of patients who underwent IBBR surgery by the same surgeon from April 2013 until September 2016. Patients were all given a preoperative dose of antibiotics. Patients in this study underwent mastectomy by several different surgeons with one reconstructive surgeon for all patients. Patients are prepped routinely with chlorhexidine gluconate, or in the case of allergy, betadine is used. After mastectomy is completed, the tissues are irrigated with cefazolin or clindamycin solution, the operating team gowns and gloves again, and new instruments including electrocautery are used. A subpectoral pocket is created, and ADM is irrigated with saline cefazolin or saline clindamycin and sutured at the inferior mastectomy flap medially and inferiorly. Two Jackson Pratt drains are placed, one under the superior mastectomy flap and the other inferiorly between the ADM and implant. The tissue expander (TE) is opened from the box at the time of placement to minimize risk of contamination. Once on the field, the TE is irrigated without being touched, using antibiotic solution. The mastectomy pocket is then again irrigated with antibiotic solution. At this time, the reconstructive surgeon changes his gloves and is the only person to handle the TE. The TE is placed without touching the skin under the pectoralis major muscle superiorly and ADM inferiorly. The superior aspect of ADM is sutured to the released inferior pectoralis major muscle and laterally to serratus fascia. The skin is closed and dressings including biopatch on drain sites are applied. Patients are then admitted overnight and routinely continued on 24 hours of intravenous antibiotics in keeping with SCIP guidelines. In April 2015, it became standard practice for patients to be discharged without antibiotics after receiving SCIP protocol prophylactic antibiotics. The control group was made up of patients who were discharged home to complete a course of oral antibiotics for five to seven days with cephalexin unless precluded by allergies; then, clindamycin was used. The study group was composed of patients who were discharged home without oral antibiotics. There was no other change in care in this time period. The rates of infection requiring TE removal and requiring antibiotics with or without TE removal were compared with Fisher's exact test. Comparisons were made both for patients and for individual TEs. The group of patients discharged with antibiotics (control) contained 26 patients with 49 breasts undergoing reconstruction, whereas the group of patients discharged without antibiotics (study) contained 22 patients with 37 breasts undergoing reconstruction.
