Freedom or Control in the First World War: (The Great Crisis of May 1915)
On 19th May 1915 the Prime Minister, H. H. Asquith, rose in his place in the House of Commons in order to state that 4 4 steps are in contemplation which will involve the reconstruction of the Government on a broader personal and political basis." Asquith thus announced the downfall and ruin of one of the great Ministries of recent British historv. In May 1915 the Liberals had been in power for nearly a decade; and were justly proud of the record of achievement secured by their party and Government since they gained office in December 1905. Their first victory had been won almost at once when in the famous General Election of January 1906 they dealt their Conservative enemies a series of battering blows and scored an electoral triumph almost without parallel in the annals of British politics. Thereafter the Liberals proceeded to attain, in the face of many difficulties, a series of political, constitutional, social and legislative achievements that in combination transformed the life of the British people and effectively carried the nation into the modern era. Their record in Imperial affairs, in social legislation, in the reform of the Army and Navy, in diplomacy and in politics was one they could look back upon with satisfaction. In the years between 1908 and 1914 their Government, and its allies, had regularly overcome the fierce and protracted opposition of the Tories during a time that has been called, aptly enough, one of the bitterest eras in British political life. Asquith himself, in the great crisis of August 1914, had gained the respect and approbation of nearly all when he managed to lead a united nation into the war against Imperial Germany. Nine months of war had eroded some of this feeling and there had been much criticism of the wartime administration of individual Ministers.
