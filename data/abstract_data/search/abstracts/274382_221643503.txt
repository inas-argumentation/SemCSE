Open Surgical Repair of Juxtarenal Abdominal Aortic Aneurysms in the Elderly is Not Associated with Increased Thirty Day Mortality Compared to Fenestrated Endovascular Grafting.
OBJECTIVE Endovascular repair of juxtarenal abdominal aortic aneurysm (JAAA) with fenestrated grafts (FEVAR) has been reported to decrease operative mortality and morbidity as compared to open surgical repair (OSR) However, prior comparisons of OSR and FEVAR have not necessarily included patients with comparable clinical profiles and aneurysm extent. Although FEVAR is often chosen as the first line therapy for high risk patients such as the elderly, many patients do not have anatomy favorable for FEVAR. At present, there is a paucity of literature examining the operative outcomes of OSR in elderly patients for JAAA relative to FEVAR. For this reason, we chose to perform a propensity matched comparison of OSR and FEVAR for JAAA repair in patients aged =70. METHODS Patients =70 years old undergoing elective non ruptured JAAA repairs between 2012 2018 were identified in the American College of Surgeons National Surgical Quality Improvement Program (ACS NSQIP) Targeted EVAR and AAA databases. FEVAR patients were identified in the Targeted EVAR database as those receiving the "Cook Zenith Fenestrated" endograft. Because our study specifically examines JAAA, OSR patients who had supra celiac proximal clamping or concomitant renal/visceral revascularizations were excluded. A 1:1 propensity match algorithm matched OSR and FEVAR patients by preoperative clinical and demographic characteristics, operative indications, and aneurysm extent. Thirty day outcomes including mortality, major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) pulmonary and renal complications were compared between propensity matched groups of OSR and FEVAR patients. RESULTS A 1:1 propensity match was achieved, and the final analysis included 136 OSR patients and 136 FEVAR patients. There was no significant difference in 30 day mortality (4.4% vs. 3.7% OR=1.21; 95% CI=0.36 4.06; P=0.759) between OSR and FEVAR. OSR was associated with a higher incidence of MACE compared to FEVAR; however, the trend was not statistically significant (8.1% vs. 3.7% OR=2.31; 95% CI=0.78 6.82; P=0.131) Compared to FEVAR, patients undergoing OSR did have significantly higher rates of pulmonary (19.1% vs. 3.7% OR=6.19; 95% CI=2.30 16.67; P<0.001) and renal complications (8.1% vs. 2.2% OR=3.90; 95% CI=1.06 14.31; P=0.040) CONCLUSIONS In the samples assessed in this study, open repair of JAAA in the elderly did not differ from FEVAR with respect to thirty day mortality despite higher pulmonary and renal complications. Although FEVAR should remain as the first line therapy for JAAA in elderly patients, OSR may be an acceptable alternative for select patients with anatomy unfavorable for FEVAR.
