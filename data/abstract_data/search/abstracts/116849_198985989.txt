End to end Recurrent Multi Object Tracking and Trajectory Prediction with Relational Reasoning
The majority of contemporary object tracking approaches do not model interactions between objects. This contrasts with the fact that objects' paths are not independent: a cyclist might abruptly deviate from a previously planned trajectory in order to avoid colliding with a car. Building upon HART, a neural class agnostic single object tracker, we introduce a multi object tracking method MOHART capable of relational reasoning. Importantly, the entire system, including the understanding of interactions and relations between objects, is class agnostic and learned simultaneously in an end to end fashion. We explore a number of relational reasoning architectures and show that multi headed self attention outperforms the provided baselines and better accounts for complex physical interactions in a challenging toy experiment. We find that it leads to consistent performance gains in tracking as well as future trajectory prediction on three real world datasets (MOTChallenge, UA DETRAC, and Stanford Drone dataset) particularly in the presence of ego motion, occlusions, crowded scenes, and faulty sensor inputs.
