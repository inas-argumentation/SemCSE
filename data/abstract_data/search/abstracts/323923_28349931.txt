DYNAMIC 3 D PARTICLE TRACKING DEEP INSIDE LIVING TISSUE
Sensitive detections methods allow tracking of individual fluorescently labelled particles with nanometer localization accuracy. Such single particle tracking (SPT) can reveal mechanistic behaviour in inhomogeneous environments that cannot be resolved by bulk averaging. However, common SPT techniques are limited to the focal plane of the instrument. Several approaches allow encoding 3D information in 2D microscopic images by engineering of the point spread function (PSF) or by using detectors with slightly displaced focal planes. All of them are restricted to an accessible z range of 1 2 mm off the focal plane [1] This limitation can be overcome for individual particles if the relative distance between sample and detection objective is continuously adjusted by a feedback loop such that the particle is kept in focus. Light sheet fluorescence microscopy provides an optical sectioning effect comparable to confocal microscopy, but features parallel detection of the object field [2] Thus, images can be acquired well beyond video rate using high sensitivity EMCCD cameras, enabling tracking of rapidly moving single particles in living tissue [3] Our light sheet microscopy setup was modified by insertion of a weak cylindrical lens to create an astigmatic PSF. While the centroid of the PSF corresponds to the lateral position, its shape yields unambiguous information on the axial localization of the emitter [4] This information was obtained in real time 50 ms) by a fast image analysis algorithm and fed back to the sample stage to keep the emitter in the focal plane of the instrument. Thus, 3D trajectories with millisecond temporal resolution could be obtained in inhomogeneous biological specimen while the illumination light was always confined to the focal plane. A scanning mirror was used to rotate the light sheet within the sample, reducing shadowing artefacts in imaging as well as tracking applications. The instrument was used to follow trajectories of individual nanoparticles more than 100 mm deep inside living tissue. For this purpose, fluorescent beads were micro injected into nuclei of C. tentans salivary gland cells and their 3D trajectories reconstructed throughout the entire nuclear volume of (70 mm)3. Finally we aim at tracking single fluorescently labelled mRNA particles inside the nucleus.
