Latent Variable Regression Discontinuity Design with Cluster Level Treatment Assignment
The regression discontinuity design (RD; Thistlewaite Campbell, 1960) is a common choice for researchers who seek to estimate the causal effects of an intervention when randomized controlled trials are infeasible. When assignment to treatment is determined by whether an individual's score on a running variable (RV; e.g. pretest scores) is above/below a specified cutoff value, an RD design allows the researcher to estimate local average treatment effect (LATE) at the cutoff. Many published educational and clinical RD studies utilize running variables that are noisy measures of latent constructs (e.g. total scores of proficiency tests) In addition, the collected data often have a hierarchical structure due to the existence of clusters, such as classrooms or clinics. In the current article, we propose a multilevel latent RD analysis to account for measurement error (ME) and a hierarchical sampling design in estimating the LATE. Under the conventional RD design, the assignment to treatment is often made based on observed summed or averaged item scores; to estimate the LATE, this MEcontaminated RV is entered in the regression model to control for its effect on the outcome. Measurement error in RVs is known to support a causal interpretation of the LATE (Lee Lemiux, 2010) However, we argue that using available item response data to estimate a measurement model is preferable to ignoring ME. Furthermore, ME in the RV may result in invalid statistical inferences of the LATE. The multilevel RD design called cluster RD (CRD) is especially prone to ME and sampling error because participants are assigned to treatment groups based on RVs measured at the cluster levels, whereas outcomes are measured at the individual level. Accordingly, the simultaneous estimation of measurement and structural models is considered the gold standard if feasible. The proposed multilevel latent RD model adapts the conventional CRD model by allowing the RV, covariates, and outcome variable to be treated as latent variables (LVs) This approach aids not only in the generalization of the LATE with respect to latent space but also in the examination of heterogeneity of the LATE at the observed cutoff value. Because multilevel LV modeling with categorical response data presents computational difficulties that can make fullinformation maximum likelihood estimation of the model infeasible, we develop R code to implement the Metropolis Hastings Robbins Monro (MH RM; Cai, 2010) algorithm. The proposed model outperforms the conventional CRD model in the recovery of treatment effect across various conditions.
