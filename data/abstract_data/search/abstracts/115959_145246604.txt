The answer now seems obvious. "History," with apologies to the venerable Carr, "is the conceptual space, the time of human experience, in which social scientific knowledge and, most of all, prediction is proven wrong." Or, if you prefer, "any succession of rupturing events which together bring to light our misunderstandings and misrecognitions of the present."2 In the past few years, pace Francis Fukuyama's3 prognosis of the "end of history," there has been an awful lot of it about. Indeed, if History is Dead, its rigor mortis appears unusually vigorous.
