Poverty as a Social Phenomenon in Russian Society
Poverty is a feature of any modern society. It has existed in Russia throughout Soviet history although for ideological reasons the term "disadvantaged" [maloobespechennye] was used instead of "poor" [bednye] On what scale has it existed in Russia in the last two decades? A simple instrument for assessing this is the subsistence minimum that establishes the poverty line and distinguishes that segment of the population whose per capita family income is lower than the subsistence minimum.1 We can evaluate (albeit unevenly) the extent of poverty beginning in 1975, when benefits were introduced for disadvantaged families. At that time the government set a poverty line, declaring a per capita family income of fifty rubles per month (independent of family structure) as the criterion for assessing need. A guaranteed minimum wage and minimum pension was set at this level. The subsistence minimum was calculated by official organizations attached to the Ministry of Labor.
