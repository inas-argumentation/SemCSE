Vetta explains how the support for SRS is based on the class experiences of the transitional societal transformations and the lack of an organized left. Anticapitalist and antiglobalist stances have been, in her explanation, more alluring than pure nationalism within the SRS ideology, as contextualized through a short but brilliant overview of the history of impoverishment of the Serbian population throughout the last decades. The final and third part of the book on 'Good Governance' provides an in depth analysis of a specific program: Community Revitalization through Democratic Action (CRDA) funded by USAID. Grounded in her data collected in 2007 and 2008 in Kikinda and Subotica and opposing the discourses diminishing or negating any civic heritage in Serbia, Vetta raises questions around CRDA failures, notably the (intentional) dismantling of existing structures of local communities organization in the Mesne Zajednice (MZ) Finally, in the last chapter, the author discusses the juxtaposed state NGO relations by focusing on welfare reforms and power tensions between NGOs and state social welfare institutions. Under the brand of a Social Innovation Fund, decentralization and privatization were conducted through further NGOization while deepening class divisions as NGOs became 'safe havens' for the elites, elites seamlessly shifting between NGOs, political parties and governmental structures. Despite the lack of structure around a more explicit 'fil rouge' to help readers follow the overall flow of the book, this book presents a rare and sharp exploration of cultural essentializations between the centre and the periphery and refined insights into the dynamics between the aid industry and NGOs in the transitional context. Vetta avoids simplified conclusions throughout her book and presents us with a very proper image of contemporary Serbia. Continuously connecting her fieldwork to larger theoretical frameworks, Vetta succeeds in presenting a book that is of interest to an audience much larger than area studies of the Balkans or post socialist studies: the book is of high relevance to graduate students and scholars working on aid, reform, and Europeanization policies, and importantly, could provide valuable insights for policymakers and development aid circles.
