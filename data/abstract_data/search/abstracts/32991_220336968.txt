Responses to the letter on "Transfer learning for neural network model in Chlorophyll a dynamics prediction"
The discussers said that "The training data statistically do not seem to be enough to avoid overfitting." As we know, the definition of overfitting is: Given a hypothesis space H, a hypothesis h H is said to overfit the training data if there exists some alternative hypothesis h H, such that h has smaller error than h over the training examples, but h has a smaller error than h over the entire distribution of instances (Mitchell 1997) LeCun et al. (2015) also pointed out that when discussing model overfitting, both training data set and test data set must be identically distributed. However, this prerequisite fails in the case of the original paper because the data distribution is not identical over time (Tian et al. 2019) Therefore, for the entire training and testing process in the original paper, the circumstance of discussing the issue of overfitting does not exist. Actually, the main point of this paper is to improve the generalization ability of the neural network model under different data distributions, rather than identical data distribution. Although the overfitting on the entire training and testing process in this case is unable to be discussed, reducing the probability of the overfit that may occur in the training process is necessary. In our paper, parameter norm penalties (PNP) and dropout are employed to decrease the probability of model overfitting. In addition, for the model optimized by the transfer learning (TL) method, new data will be continuously used for supplementary training after initial training. Thus, whether the TL model is overfitting during the initial training or not, more training data can be gradually supplemented by the TL method to improve the generalization ability of the model on different data distributions.
