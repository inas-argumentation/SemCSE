Expanding the Rainbow poses great teaching potential for courses on gender, sexualities, medical sociology, sociology of the family, and sociology of aging and life course. Closer to the end of this volume, Andrea Miller discusses teaching strategies to implement when using this resource in the classroom. With keen attention to the discomfort these topics bring to students and instructors alike, Miller emphasizes the importance of critical pedagogies in sociological teaching. When teaching this volume, it is crucial to redirect the narrative from the dominant gender and sexual scripts that students usually come into the classroom with to a broader scope wherein students develop an understanding and receptivity to alternative social scripts. In this vein, "refusing to sensationalize or 'other' a topic, as well as tending to each topic equally" becomes an important part to introduce these themes into a teaching environment (p. 349) In order to reassess dominant social scripts, Kitchen and Nelson (2019) provide a class activity wherein students can identify what assumptions about sex are embedded in their discourse and sexual behaviors normalized in their use language. This is a textbook that can also complement interdisciplinary and cultural studies departments with its vast findings and writing styles. TRAILS provides useful classroom activities that would go along nicely with a reading of sections of this volume. For example, Ferguson (2012) shares in her syllabus an outlined activity for an upper level college course that encourages students to write an analytical paper that applies "the theories and scholarship we read throughout the semester to a memoir that centers on the lived experiences of race ethnicity, social class, gender, sexuality, and/or disability" (p. 2) With several personal reflections from faculty members shared in Expanding the Rainbow, such assignments encourage students to think beyond the established sociological paradigms to further their grasp of theoretical frameworks and critical analyses. When using said activities, I would recommend setting class time aside for peer reviewing and collaboration. Oftentimes the topics discussed in the volume are ones that are rejecting social taboos. When encouraging student conversation, we contribute to the ongoing process of undoing the stigmatization that prevents these topics to be academically spread and developed. Brandy L. Simula, J. E. Sumerau, and Andrea Miller have curated a volume of essential sociological developments regarding contemporary genderand sexuality minoritized subjectivities. The format of this book is as complex as it is approachable, including all authors' backgrounds and pronouns for an enhanced connection between authors and readers. One critique scholars might have when reading this text is that many of the small samples in different studies reflect the need for more development in these research areas. However, this fact also reminds research communities how hard it is to come up with the needed sample for these types of studies. Last, the diversity of writing styles exposes young social science scholars to the endless possibilities of research agendas they could pursue should they continue their education and research careers. Expanding the Rainbow is uniquely tailored to illustrate the ongoing processes of centering the experiences of marginalized subjectivities pertaining to issues of gender and sexualities.
