Operative death and pediatric neurosurgery missions: international perspective and transcultural considerations
Abstract International surgical missions that perform and teach surgery to improve the care of children in less industrialised nations embody core altruistic values that resonate with physicians and nurses across the world. During such enterprises, challenges arise not only from the logistics of providing clinical care in unfamiliar surroundings, but also from interpersonal barriers that exist due to cultural and language differences. The scenario in which these barriers must be transcended is the surgeon parent discussion of an intraoperative death of a child. It is imperative to develop a thoughtful preoperative relationship with the family of sick children and a process by which families can be made aware of a tragic outcome. The topic of intraoperative death, in the context of international surgical missions, is discussed in order to provide insights that may lessen the occurrence of long term, complicated grief for parents whose child dies.
