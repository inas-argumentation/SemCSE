Does strategic planning influence the performance of ICT SMEs in Kenya
th May, 2013 Most countries acknowledge the critical role that ICT SMEs contribute to their economies, both as an engine of growth and sustainable development. However, many questions remain unanswered as to the determination of the critical challenges facing SMEs in the Information, Communication and Technology (ICT) and how these challenges could be addressed to improve their performance and sustainability. Theoretical literature suggests that strategic planning is an essential activity that generates positive outcomes for firms of all sizes. However, the results of the previous studies examining the relationship between strategic planning and firm performance have been inconclusive and has had mixed results. These challenges have increased the need for empirical information which is essential for decision making in addressing issues that are likely to enhance ICT SMEs survival and growth in Kenya. The objective of this study was to establish the effect of strategic planning on the performance of ICT SMEs in Kenya. Descriptive survey research design was used. Two hundred and thirty nine (239) responses were received from 123 ICT firms hence achieving a response rate of 55 and 84.2 respectively. Multiple linear regression analysis was used to test the statistical significance of the independent variable (strategic planning combined processes) on the dependent variable of performance. Performance was measured by multidimensional performance factors of learning and growth, internal business processes, competitive advantage and firm profitability. The study results revealed that strategic planning has significant and positive influences on performance of ICT SME's in Kenya. The test of hypotheses of the specific objectives revealed that strategic planning has positive and significant influence on learning and growth and explain 24.1 of learning and growth in ICT SMEs business performance. This study has developed a general framework that integrates key strategic planning practices and characteristics and its link with the multidimensional measures of performance and the moderating effects of the environmental factors and organisational characteristics. The study justifies that strategic planning as a key driver of business performance of ICT SMEs and effective strategic planning processes result in improved learning, business processes, and enhanced business competitiveness. It is apparent that entrepreneurs of ICT SMEs cannot ignore strategic planning as significant changes in learning and growth, internal business processes, and competitive advantage are the result of change or effective application of strategic planning. Policy makers and academicians may need to address the capacity needs of SMEs and develop strategic planning model that address the challenges that SMEs encounter in a dynamic environment.
