Nanoencapsulation of food ingredients by niosomes
Abstract Niosomes (nonionic surfactant vesicles) are small vesicles consisting of an aqueous core enclosed by a membrane of nonionic surfactants that form closed bilayer structures based on their amphiphilic nature. The lipophilic groups are located in the interior of membrane, while the hydrophilic groups are exposed to the aqueous medium. It is shown that in general, niosome and liposome structures do not form spontaneously and they need some energy input (e.g. physical agitation or heat) which leads to different preparation techniques. These vesicles entrap the bioactive compound/drug which can be released at a controlled rate. Niosomes are a potential vehicle for bioactive delivery systems such as in food applications, since they can be prepared by natural products with low costs compared with other types of vesicles. Different methods can be used for niosomal preparation, being the more commonly used such as ethanol injection, hydration thin film, or sonication. The selection of the appropriate method is crucial for the final product obtained in terms of size, size distribution, stability, and encapsulation efficiency. In the present chapter, a review of the techniques used for niosomal preparation and characterization for food applications will be summarized.
