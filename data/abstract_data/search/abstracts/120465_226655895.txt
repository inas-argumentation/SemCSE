Imagining and Performing Marginalization: Hip Hop and the Arab Spring of 2011
During and after the so called Arab Spring, artists and observers of local hip hop communities stressed the potential of hip hop to articulate social and political resistance to state actors in the Middle East. Their argument was that hip hop functioned as a tool for marginalized people to articulate resistance, to speak truth to power and give a voice to the voiceless. This argument fits a historical narrative of hip hop being a culture 'from below' practiced by marginalized people to intervene in the socio cultural sphere. At the same time, critics decry hip hop as a materialist, misogynistic and nihilistic enterprise. Instead of defending one specific interpretation, this article deconstructs and contextualizes both narratives as myths and proposes an approach to hip hop myth making as a performative cultural practice that is closely connected to issues of positionality and identity.
