Top MCS Downloads for 2010: Sexual Content, Video Game Violence, and Gender Studies Grab Attention
We are anticipating the first journal impact factor for Mass Communication and Society (MCS) this summer. That impact factor evaluates how often articles published in our pages over the previous two years are cited in other journals being evaluated. But before any such impact can be made at the publishing level, articles must first be found and read by students and scholars. Publications have always attempted to measure print readership. But in the electronic age we can at least get a sense of an article's attractiveness to readers through counting how many times an article is downloaded in an online database. Last year, we looked at MCS downloads from 2009 and found that those generating the most interest investigated video game violence. Sex was also a hot topic with politics viewed third most (Perry, 2010) Will the articles that are accessed the most for those topics be the same ones that are generating the MCS impact factor? It is hard to know, but there should be some correlation. On the other hand, students in classes who produce papers that may never be published could easily be generating more of the downloads than the scholars who are publishing further on a topic. So the download numbers could be representative of instructional use. That would be a question for further research. This year, we looked only at the 15 most downloaded topics from 2010 on the Informaworld service for individual and institutional subscribers to the journal as well as the top 15 downloaded articles from the EBSCO database. EBSCO does not receive the most recent volume of the journal, so downloads exclude a year's worth of articles. The article with the highest download frequency on Informaworld (2178 times) shows continued interest in sexual content.
