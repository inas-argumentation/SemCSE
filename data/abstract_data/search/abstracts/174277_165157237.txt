SCALING OF TIME HISTORIES FOR RESPONSE HISTORY ANALYSIS FOR PERFORMANCE BASED DESIGN OF TALL BUILDINGS
Tall building design in high seismic regions are becoming exclusively designed and analyzed using Performance Based Earthquake Engineering principles. As such, design and analysis are being performed using alternative means of compliance allowed by the building codes following analysis procedures developed by the Tall Buildings Initiative and Los Angeles Tall Buildings Structural Design Council. In response to the newly released ASCE 7 16 guidelines, these procedures now recommend that nonlinear response history analyses be performed following the criteria defined in ASCE 7 16 Chapter 16. ASCE 7 16 defines the procedures for developing the Maximum Considered Earthquake ground motion (MCER) response spectrum and provides some commentary on selection of time histories needed for the response history analysis, including specifying the minimum number of time histories used in the analysis. For RHA collapse prevention evaluations, ASCE 7 16 defines the seismic demand based on the 5% damped, maximum direction MCER response spectrum. A single response spectrum (uniform risk spectrum) or two or more site specific target spectra (referred to as either scenario or condition mean spectra) are allowed. A suite of not less than 11 ground motions are required for each target spectrum. These new procedures, on top of the change in definition of the MCER from geomean intensity to maximum direction that was enacted in the ASCE 7 10 guidelines, introduce additional conservatism to the ground motion development process and response history analysis producing a probability of collapse that is smaller than the stated ASCE 7 16 goal of 1% in 50 years. 1Principal Engineer, Wood Environment Infrastructure Solutions, Inc.
