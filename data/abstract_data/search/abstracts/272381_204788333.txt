Very rare right hepatic artery variant unnoticed before surgery
Extrahepatic arterial variations are clinically important and challenging as the incidence of hepatic arterial injuries and associated organ damage increases in the presence of aberrant anatomy. Knowledge of variations of the coeliac trunk and hepatic artery is important in hepatopancreatobiliary surgery, liver transplants and vascular interventional radiology. In the preoperative period, only 60 80% of the arterial variations can be identified by imaging methods not focused on vascular disease. Although multidetector computed tomography (CT) angiography and processed digital images by software sources are possible in non invasive imaging, angiography is still the gold standard for delineation of variations of the coeliac trunk and hepatic arteries. Although there are many international classifications related to hepatic vascular anomalies in the literature, there are rare variants that are not included in these classifications. In this case presentation, we aimed to report a very rare hepatic artery anomaly of an accessory right hepatic artery (aRHA) originating aberrantly from the gastroduodenal artery (GDA) A 49 year old male patient presented to the urology outpatient clinic with complaints of right upper quadrant pain. Stone protocol CT revealed a dilation of the common bile duct, 'a choledochal cyst' which was measured 23 mm at the widest point. The patient was referred to the general surgery outpatient clinic. Magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP) evaluation was also reported to be consistent with a type 1 choledochal cyst (Figs S1, S2) The patient underwent laparotomy with a right subcostal incision. After complete Kocherization of the duodenum, the porta hepatis was palpated for right and left hepatic arterial pulsations. The common hepatic artery originated from the coeliac trunk and branched into the GDA and proper hepatic artery, then right and left hepatic arteries as is usual. There was no palpable pulse in the left lateral edge of the pancreas or behind the portal vein and the common bile duct which would have indicated the presence of a replaced right hepatic arter. However, pulsation of the proper hepatic artery in the left lateral edge of the hepatoduodenal ligament raised suspicion of aberrant arterial anatomy. During the common bile duct and the choleduct exploration, an artery entering the liver from the anterior segment of the right lobe was detected. It was running along the inferolateral side of the common bile duct and the choleduct. The artery extending towards the right anterior segment was an aRHA originating from the GDA (Fig. S3) This aberrant aRHA was identified, dissected and preserved. Type 1 choledochal cyst excision and Roux en Y hepaticojejunostomy were performed. Post operative pathology evaluation was reported as a benign choledochal cyst. Hepatic artery anomalies are quite common. In an autopsy study published in 1966 by Michels about variations of the hepatic artery, an aRHA originating from the GDA was reported to be very rare. In the majority of cases, an aRHA originates from the superior mesenteric artery, propria hepatic artery or aorta. An aRHA originating from the GDA was detected in only 1 2% of cases where it tends to be located behind the cystic canal and the gallbladder. In our case, it was at the inferolateral part of the common bile duct and the choleduct. Polguj et al. reported an autopsy case of an aRHA that branched from the GDA and Yamashita et al. reported a case of an aRHA branching from the GDA detected in the preoperative period. In our case, we focused on the choledochal cyst in the preoperative period. Preoperative diagnostic evaluation was performed with MRCP. However, MRCP cannot show blood vessels precisely because it involves a very long echo time. Hence, the aRHA originating from the GDA was unnoticed before surgery and it was detected only during the intraoperative dissection. In this case report, a very rare aberrant aRHA was identified intraoperatively and successfully preserved. Hepatic artery variations are frequent and can be revealed prior to complex hepatopancreaticobiliary surgical procedures by contrast enhanced CT angiography to minimize the risks of surgery and injury. Any physician interested in this field should know the normal and aberrant anatomy of the coeliac trunk and hepatic artery. Even if vascular variations are not detected before hepatopancreaticobiliary surgery, the surgeon should still perform the operation with suspicion of variation.
