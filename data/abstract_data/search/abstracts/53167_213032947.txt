If all veracity mechanisms can be found in "Tolkien's literary mythology" and if "this provides much of the explanation for why these texts have given rise to fiction based religions, while no religions have emerged from science fiction and fantasy texts that do not have these mechanisms" (41) the argument disregards classical logic. 'Because of the presence of a in b, c follows' does not explain that 'because of the absence of a in e and f, there is no c' Apart from that, I doubt that any works of fantasy fiction can be found in which these mechanisms are fully absent. In addition, 'religious affordance' of supernatural fiction seems not always unintended by the respective authors. Fictional protagonists who achieve their aims by way of religious practices, or survive by religious beliefs, usually mirror the authors' religious convictions. To include the latter as a factor in the model would probably be felicitous.
