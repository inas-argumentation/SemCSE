Army Air Forces Research and Development: Second of a series on the War Department Research and Development Program
THE planes of today, which are establishing so many records for speed and distance, may one day be antiquated by high speed supersonic planes, nuclear powered airships, and guided missiles traveling at meteoric speeds. This is already indicated by the advancements in the fields of aeronautics, physics, chemistry, nucleonics, and mechanics. The Army Air Forces, cognizant of the part science will play in their future development, now have reorganized and are planning to expand their facilities to emphasize research and development. At the Air Forces Headquarters in Washington research activities have been elevated from a subsection of A 4, procurement and supply division, to a special group under a deputy chief of air staff. This group, which is a policy organization, is headed by Maj. Gen. Curtis E. Le May. Gen. Le May, an engineer from Ohio State University, is a veteran of both the European and Pacific wars, and is best known for his work
