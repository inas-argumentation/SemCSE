Introduction to the TOPC Special Issue on Innovations in Systems for Irregular Applications, Part 2
Irregular applications, characterized by irregular memory accesses and communication patterns, arise in numerous computing domains. They pertain both to well established and emerging fields, such as Computer Aided Design (CAD) bioinformatics, social network analysis, transportation systems, communication networks, the power grid, and computer security. Irregular applications are inherently parallel, but present unpredictable memory access patterns, control structures, and/or network transfers. They typically operate on large sets of data organized in pointer or linked lists based structures (such as graphs, sparse matrices, unbalanced trees, and irregular grids) which are difficult to partition on distributed memory systems in a work balanced manner. Moreover, they often require fine grained synchronization and communication among partipating processes (and threads) Current high performance architectures are designed to exploit spatial and temporal data locality through caches, sufficient computation to amortize data access costs, and structured data that can be efficiently distributed among participating processes. Consequently, current highperformance system frameworks and infrastructures provide limited performance and scalability for irregular applications. Due to emerging analytics workflows, there is an increased need for solutions to simultaneously execute both regular and irregular workloads with similar efficiency. These include, for example, data analysis requiring both graph and table views of data, workflows where combinatorial or graph methods pre process data, scientific simulations that model complex physical systems and infer new knowledge, and workflows that integrate information aggregation and machine learning. Addressing the issues of irregular applications on current and future system architectures, and enabling efficient integration of regular and irregular workloads, have become critical scientific challenges that need to be addressed urgently. This is the second part of our thematic special issue focused on irregular computing of the ACM Transactions on Parallel Computing (TOPC) The entirety of the special issue collects significantly extended papers from a series of venues dedicated to irregular applications, such as the The International Conference for High Performance Computing, Networking, Storage, and Analysis (SC) Workshop on Irregular Applications: Architectures and Algorithms (IA) and the IEEE International Parallel and Distributed Processing (IPDPS) Workshop on the Intersection of Graph Algorithms and Machine Learning (GraML) as well as novel submissions. Of the 13 submissions we have received, the first six selected papers explored the breadth of the research on irregular applications, addressing algorithms, graph frameworks, compiler optimization, and novel computing paradigms.
