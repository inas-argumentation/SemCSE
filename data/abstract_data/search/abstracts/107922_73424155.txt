Vancomycin Area under the Curve and Acute Kidney Injury: A Meta analysis.
Objective To analyze the relationship between vancomycin AUC and acute kidney injury (AKI) reported across recent studies. Methods A systematic review of Pubmed, Medline, Scopus and compiled references was conducted. We included randomized, cohorts and case control studies that reported vancomycin AUCs and risk of AKI from (January 1st, 1990 to January 31st, 2018) The primary outcome was AKI, defined as an increase in serum creatinine of =0.5 mg/L or a 50% increase from baseline on 2 or more consecutive measurements. Odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (95%CIs) were calculated. Primary analyses compared the impact of AUC cut point ~650 mg*h/L) and AKI. Additional analysis compared AUC vs. trough guided monitoring on AKI incidence. Results We identified 8 eligible observational studies with a total of 2,491 patients. Five studies reported on AUC0 24 and AKI, 2 studies reported AUC24 48 and AKI, and 2 studies reported AKI associated with AUC vs. trough guided monitoring. AUC less than ~650 mg*hr/L was associated with significantly reduced risk of AKI for AUCs compiled in the first 24 hours, i.e. AUC0 24 (OR 0.36, 95% CI 0.23 0.56) as well as for the second 24 hour period, i.e. AUC24 48 (OR 0.45, 95% CI 0.27 0.75) AKI associated with AUC monitoring strategy was significantly lower than trough guided monitoring (OR 0.68, 95% CI 0.46 0.99) Conclusion AUCs measured in the first or second 24 hours and lower than ~650 mg*h/L may result in a decreased risk of AKI. Vancomycin AUC monitoring strategy may result in less vancomycin associated AKI. Additional investigations are warranted.
