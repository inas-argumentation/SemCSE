Two Immigrant Humor Styles of Stand up Comedy A Linguistic Perspective
Two immigrant humor styles of stand up comedy: word play and performing are discussed in this study with reference to related linguistic theories. By a comparative analysis of two immigrant comedians' stand ups, we conclude that figurative language is a distinctive feature in Joe's jokes to make people laugh and the paralinguistic features are preferred by Russell. According to the global popularity of the two comedians, we tentatively conclude that a stand up with active vocal and physical strategies is more easily identified with by audience of diversified social backgrounds, and can often get immediate feedback. Whereas, although a word play style often leaves with deeper impression and makes one laugh longer, it's hard to be taken in by all people with different cultural backgrounds. Keywords Immigrant humor style; Stand up Comedy; Rhetorical Device; paralinguistic features
