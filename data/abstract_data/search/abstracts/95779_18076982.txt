The goal of this workshop series is to provide a forum for presenting recent research on constraints in discourse. The target areas include the recognition of discourse structure as well as the interpretation and generation of discourse in a broad variety of domains. The workshop is open to talks that further our theoretical understanding of the role of constraints in discourse, as well as empirical studies that shed light on their empirical validity. The workshop is explicitly intended for discussion and comparison of theoretical accounts that lay the ground for applications. It is well accepted that dialogues are structured by various moves that the participants make. Participants answer questions posed by other dialogue participants. They ask follow up questions. They explain why things that they said happened happened; they provide narrative sequences in which events are temporally and spatially ordered together. They elaborate and defend claims that they have made. In this talk, I will investigate how agents can reason about their own dialogue moves.
