Neurally plausible sparse coding via local competition and thresholding
While evidence indicates that neural systems may be employing sparse approximations to represent sensed stimuli, the mechanisms underlying this ability are not known. We present a class of neurally plausible locally competitive algorithms that correspond to a collection of interesting sparse coding principles that attempt to minimize a weighted combination of MSE and a coefficient cost function. These systems operate via local inhibitory competitions and thresholding and are amenable to analog hardware implementation for time varying inputs. We examine one of these systems and illustrate several important properties: it encourages sparsity as much as possible (exhibiting performance comparable to greedy algorithms) but allows sparsity to be slightly sacrificed to get increased regularity when encoding time varying inputs.
