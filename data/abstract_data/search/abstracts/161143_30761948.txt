Protein Content of the Diabetic Diet
The protein content of the diabetic diet remains a controversial issue because of the potential for both restricted and liberal protein intakes to exert beneficial and adverse effects in diabetes (1 5) In addition to adequate provision of energy, a certain level of protein intake is required to ensure normal growth, development, and maintenance of tissue protein stores. Variation in dietary protein may influence metabolic control of diabetes by altering gluconeogenic substrate availability as well as insulin and counterregulatory hormone secretion. Protein has also been implicated in the pathogenesis of diabetic renal disease. Diabetic nephropathy is now the single most common disorder leading to end stage renal failure. A high protein intake is well documented to increase renal workload and glomerular filtration rate and may contribute to the progression of diabetic nephropathy (6,7) Conversely, numerous studies now indicate that lowprotein diets may impede or delay the rate of development of diabetic renal disease (8) Consideration of any potential advantages or disadvantages to changes in protein intake cannot be viewed in isolation, however, because the intake of other macronutrients is also altered and may result in independent metabolic effects and consequences in diabetes. To date, there is limited scientific data on which to establish firm nutritional recommendations for protein intake in diabetes mellitus (9) In many cases, no information is available. Often, results are derived from short term studies that do not necessarily reflect the longterm effects. Other data is inferential in nature, arising from studies in nondiabetic subjects, and so must be cautiously interpreted before extrapolation to those with diabetes. Another issue that warrants consideration is the accumulating evidence that the protein source, whether animal or vegetable in origin, may be as important as the quantity of proteins consumed in affecting the progression of renal disease. Finally, the diabetic state can develop at any age and spans the range from infancy to the elderly. Its presence is often associated with a variety of special circumstances that may influence protein requirements and needs to be considered before making recommendations about protein intake. This review will examine the relevant scientific information that addresses these issues to provide rationale for the dietary protein recommendations for individuals with diabetes mellitus. Information presented will be restricted to that obtained in humans with an emphasis on studies performed in diabetic subjects.
