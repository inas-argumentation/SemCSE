Oxidation of 14C Glycerol Administered by Ventriculocisternal Perfusion in the Dog 1
Summary 1 314C Glycerol in artificial CSF was perfused from the third ventricle to the cisterna magna in dogs. The appearance of 14CO2 in the perfusate and in blood from the confluens sinuum was studied after 95, 135, and 175 min of perfusion. Specific activity of CO2 in the perfusate at all times exceeded that of the venous blood which in turn exceeded that of arterial blood. The calculated rate of oxidation of glycerol was between 2 and 10 nmoles/min in the majority of animals. The disappearance of counts from the perfusate indicated a consistent uptake of the labeled glycerol. A significant fraction of the labeled glycerol was recovered in the perfused tissue.
