One could note the difficulty of gauging the emotions in a modern email or text message and thus question the possibility to do the same in a 2000 year old text written in a different language and within a different culture. Subjective or not, this line of inquiry does lead to creative theological engagement with the text. I can recommend this commentary to scholars, pastors, and any student of Luke's Gospel. It is enjoyable to read due to Spencer's engaging writing style and lively prose. An example, regarding Jesus's calling Peter and John to fish for people, Spencer argues, "We must resist taming this rough and tumble mission scenario into some whistling, bucolic tale of fishing the local creek on a lazy afternoon" (p. 136) The commentary is thus a scholarly, creatively theological reading of Luke's Gospel, written in a manner that holds the reader's attention throughout its 732 pages, which is quite an achievement.
