Relationships between Office Characteristics and Employee Reactions: A Study of the Physical Environment.
Greg R. Oldham and Nancy L. Rotchford This research examined the relationships between objective office characteristics (openness, office density, workspace density, accessibility, and office darkness) and several measures of employee reactions (satisfaction, behavior during discretionary periods, and spatial markers) In addition, the research examined the extent to which three sets of intervening variables explained these relationships. The intervening variables were interpersonal experiences (conflict, friendship opportunities, agent feedback) job experiences (task significance, autonomy, task identity) and environmental experiences (crowding, concentration, privacy) Data were collected from 114 clerical employees of 19 offices. Each of the office characteristics related significantly to one or more of the employee reaction measures. Moreover, office characteristics affected several employee reactions through their impact on the intervening variables.
