ONE YEAR OLD BRITISH infants of Pakistani heritage consume more chips, sugar sweetened drinks and commercial sweet baby meals than their white counterparts, research shows. The Born in Bradford 1000 Study also reveals that this group of babies consumes more fruit and less processed meat and, beyond age of 18 months, these differences continue. The study by Leeds Beckett University monitored the diets of more than 1,250 young children with a food frequency questionnaire completed by their parents when each child was aged between 12 and 18 months. Professor of nutrition and childhood obesity Pinki Sahota, who led the study, which was published in Public Health Nutrition, said that the findings highlight the importance of early and culturally adapted interventions aimed at establishing healthy eating behaviours. 'Evidence shows that consumption of sugar sweetened beverages contributes significantly to obesity, she said. 'Our study has highlighted the early consumption of such drinks, with quantity consumed increasing with age, and a particularly higher intake amongst Pakistani infants. Professor Sahota added: 'The restriction of sugar sweetened drinks should therefore constitute a key message for early interventions aimed at promoting and establishing healthy infant dietary behaviours. Expectant mums should avoid overuse of paracetamol, suggests a study by Edinburgh University. Research into use of the drug in pregnancy found that it may lower testosterone in unborn boys. Martin Ward Platt of the Royal College of Paediatrics and Child Health said the findings related to paracetamol use over 'at least' several days, adding that sometimes small doses of the drug are necessary to treat fever.
