Social Networks And Health Care: The Case Of Elderly Latinos
Major medical needs are still unmet for many non institutionalized ethnic minorities in the inner city even though important policy changes have certainly improved the medical care of the elderly. An ethnographic and survey study (conducted be tween 1985 and 1989) of 48 elderly Latinos in East Harlem in New York City examined the impact of several aspects of social networks on two health related measures: meeting medical needs and mode of utilization of the medical system. Our main finding was that strong social networks predict addressing medical needs and uti lizing the health care system on a more regular basis. We discuss the implications of these findings for health care and public policy.
