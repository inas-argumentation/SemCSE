Author's Reply: Optimal Resources for Children's Surgical Care: Executive Summary
Dear Editor, Thank you very much to Ford and colleagues for their letter to the Editor regarding the Optimal Resources for Children's Surgical Care (OReCS) Executive Summary [1] We were excited to learn that the medical student and junior doctor led international research network OxPLORE is planning to use the OReCS document for a worldwide cross sectional analysis of childhood surgical services and resources. We were also pleased to hear that the OReCS document was useful in assessing children's surgical care in a high income setting. The study you have done, and the one you are proposing, is exactly what the Global Initiative for Children's Surgery (GICS) had in mind when the OReCS document was created. Given your planned research and the likelihood that you will find major gaps in the resources for children's surgical care in lowand middle income countries (LMICs) we would like to encourage you to think about how these deficits might eventually be addressed. There is no better time to begin thinking about the solutions to these complex problems than while you are collecting data. Indeed, one of the most pressing questions in global surgery is to determine how emergency and essential surgical services can best be implemented in countries where the needs are greatest and where health systems are least developed [2] At present, far too few examples of well functioning surgical services for children in LMICs can be found, and little research on what factors make them successful has been conducted. Scaling up surgical care for children in LMICs requires much more than theater personnel, equipment, and infrastructure; it also requires education and training, functioning methods and processes, data systems, and an enabling environment [3] Your research group is well positioned to answer these important questions. To scale up surgical care in LMICs, several countries have created national surgical, obstetrics and anesthesia plans (NSOAPs) and many more are in the process of developing such plans, but children's surgical care is not being included in most. An important role for the OReCS document is to help countries developing NSOAPS to identify what resources are needed to care for children with surgical problems. We hope your group will also help drive the process of inclusion of children's surgery in NSOAPs, as you embark on the worldwide cross sectional analysis. In summary, congratulation on your exciting project! We look forward to seeing the results of your study and assisting you with your research in any way we can. Finally, we hope to see as many of you as possible at the 4th GICS meeting, which will be held in Johannesburg, South Africa, January 2020 [4] The 2 day conference will focus on strategies for implementing changes and supporting the inclusion of children in National Surgical, Obstetrics, and Anesthesia Plans. Stephen W. Bickler sbickler@ucsd.edu
