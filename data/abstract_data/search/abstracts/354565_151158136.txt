Love's labor found: Contribution from feminist theory to the "labor of care" in the work of public administration
Work is taking place in public and non profit agencies across the United States. Employees are creating, distributing, regulating, manag ing, building, organizing, and making goods and services in quantities measured relatively easily. These employees are also providing benefits to citizens that extend beyond material consumption of goods and ser vices. Meaning, compassion, and care are also being offered, but in not so easy to measure quantities. This is work anchored in the concept of care and "caring labor." The value of caring labor in government and non profit manage ments, and ultimately in public administration, is currently understated. As government and non profit organizations increasingly lean toward the adoption of policies that are based on for profit business models and because the value of care, rooted in feminist economic theory, is largely ignored in public economics literature and research, the true value of the work of public and non profit agencies is grossly underesti mated. Recognizing the contribution of feminist theory to public ad ministration through the concept of the "labor of care" is linked to understanding the true value of the publicly provided work. The under valuing of public and non profit agency service is tied to lack of empha sis on non market based explanations of work offered by feminist theories. Incorporating these theories in public administration may al low us to discover true worth and the true contribution of public and non profit agencies to society and to human welfare. We know caring labor when we see it. It is the nurse who takes the time to listen to the personal story of a patient. It is the teacher who spends time outside of work hours to tutor a struggling student. It is a social worker or a police officer that chooses to work in public service although his or her work may be compensated at a rate lower than in the private sector. It is more than good bedside manner or friendly cus tomer service and is an ethical and normative stance of public adminis trators.
