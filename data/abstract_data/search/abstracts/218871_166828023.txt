High yielding cows and their calves' behaviour in the perinatal period
The study was carried out on 29 cow calf pairs. Observations of behaviour of individual cows and calves were conducted from the beginning of the delivery and a further period of 3 hours after. Cow's behaviour before and during calving: position change frequency, urination and defecation frequency, cow's body position during parturition, postnatal behaviour such as licking the calf, vocalization frequency, forage intake, self licking, lying, and stand ing were observed. Following calf behaviour traits were examined: stand up attempts' number, standing and lying time, time from the birth to stand up. Ali the features of behaviour were confronted with the course of parturition and the age of female. Cows have well developed maternal instinct. Those after the 3 and 4 calving have spent the most time licking the offsprings. The parturition course influenced the frequency of sounds uttered by the cows, their lying, and standing time after calving. Cows after difficult deliveries were lying longer or standing shorter and less vocalized to their offspring than the cows of normai delivery with alittle assistance, which seems to be a result of a larger fatigue of cows during calving. In case of the calves, the parturition course affected dramatically the time from birth to stand up, number of attempts to standing up, lying and standing time. After difficult deliveries, calves were standing up later, spent more time lying.
