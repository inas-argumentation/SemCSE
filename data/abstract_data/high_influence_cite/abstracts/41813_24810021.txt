A novel strategy to generate virus vaccines with expanded genetic codes
The invention and application of vaccines for combating infectious diseases represent one of the greatest human accomplishments within the medical and health sectors in the history of mankind. The smallpox virus, once devastating and widespread across the continents, is the first and sole human pathogen that has been eradicated globally, and this great success is attributed to the use of vaccinia vaccines. Efficacious vaccines have been developed and applied successfully for the prevention of a number of infectious viral diseases, such as smallpox, poliomyelitis, measles, mumps and rubella (Hajj Hussein et al., 2015). However, substantial challenges remain in obtaining safe and efficacious vaccines for a variety of newly emerging and re-emerging viruses, such as the severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus (SARS-CoV), Middle East respiratory syndrome (MERS)-CoV, Ebola virus, rotavirus, H5N1 and H7N9 influenza, and Dengue and Zika viruses, as well as chronic infections, such as human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) and hepatitis C virus (HCV). In the field of vaccine development, there is also a concern that live attenuated vaccines could revert to their pathogenic forms.
