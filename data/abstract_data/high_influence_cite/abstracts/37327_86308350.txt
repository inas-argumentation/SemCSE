Angiotensin II–Induced Ca Influx in Renal Afferent and Efferent Arterioles
Angiotensin II (Ang II)–induced Ca 21 signaling was studied in isolated rat renal arterioles using fura-2. Ang II (10 nmol/L) caused a sustained elevation in [Ca ]i, which was dependent on [Ca ]o in both vessel types. This response was blocked by nifedipine in only the afferent arteriole. Using the Mn 21 quench technique, we found that Ang II stimulates Ca 21 influx in both vessels. Nifedipine blocked the Ang II–induced Ca 21 influx in afferent arterioles but not in efferent arterioles. In contrast to Ang II, KCl-induced depolarization stimulated Ca 21 influx in only the afferent arteriole. Cyclopiazonic acid (CPA, 30 mmol/L) was used to examine the presence of store-operated Ca 21 entry in myocytes isolated from each arteriole. In efferent myocytes, CPA induced a sustained Ca 21 increase that was dependent on [Ca]o and insensitive to nifedipine. This mechanism was absent in afferent myocytes. SKF 96365 inhibited Ang II–induced Ca entry in efferent arterioles and CPA-induced Ca 21 entry in efferent myocytes over identical concentrations. Our findings thus indicate that Ang II activates differing Ca 21 influx mechanisms in preand postglomerular arterioles. In the afferent arteriole, Ang II activates dihydropyridine-sensitive L-type Ca 21 channels, presumably by membrane depolarization. In the efferent arteriole, Ang II appears to stimulate Ca 21 entry via store-operated Ca 21 influx. (Circ Res. 2000;87:551-557.)
