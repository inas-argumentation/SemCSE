Human Capital, Industrial Growth and Resource Curse
One of the arguments for the detrimental effect of natural resources on the manufacturing is that it could be a byproduct of the capital accumulation process in the resource-abundant open economy. Substitution of capital for labor in resource sectors might slow down the accumulation of a highly skilled labor force. We provide empirical support for this argument. We show that high-skilled labor intensive sectors develop disproportionately slow in resource-rich countries. While low-skilled and average-skilled labor intensities do not have differential effects on industrial growth. Our findings emphasize the importance of educational policies aimed at skilled labor force to ensure the sustainability of economic development in resource economies.
