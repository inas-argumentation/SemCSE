Fundamental Limits of Quantum-Secure Covert Communication Over Bosonic Channels
We investigate the fundamental limit of quantum-secure covert communication over the lossy thermal noise bosonic channel, the quantum-mechanical model underlying many practical channels. We assume that the adversary has unlimited quantum information processing capabilities as well as access to all transmitted photons that do not reach the legitimate receiver. Given existence of noise that is uncontrolled by the adversary, the <italic>square root law</italic> (SRL) governs covert communication: up to <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$c\sqrt {n}$ </tex-math></inline-formula> covert bits can be transmitted reliably in <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$n$ </tex-math></inline-formula> channel uses. Attempting to surpass this limit results in detection with unity probability as <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$n\rightarrow \infty $ </tex-math></inline-formula>. Here we present the expression for <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$c$ </tex-math></inline-formula>, characterizing the SRL for the bosonic channel. We also prove that discrete-valued coherent state quadrature phase shift keying (QPSK) constellation achieves the optimal <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$c$ </tex-math></inline-formula>, which is the same as that achieved by a circularly-symmetric complex-valued Gaussian prior on coherent state amplitude. Finally, while binary phase shift keying (BPSK) achieves the Holevo capacity for non-covert bosonic channels in the low received signal-to-noise ratio regime, we show that it is strictly sub-optimal for covert communication.
