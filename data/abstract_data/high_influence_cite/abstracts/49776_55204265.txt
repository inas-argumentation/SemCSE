Catalytic Naphtha Reforming; Challenges for Selective Gasoline; an Overview and Optimization Case Study
A new trend in catalytic naphtha reforming requires the decrease of aromatics hydrocarbons particularly benzene in reformate, while maintaining high octane rating. At present, production of reformulated gasoline with low content of benzene is one of the main challenges in the transportation fuel industry. In the catalytic reforming of realistic naphtha over bi-functional Pt-Re-S/Al2O3-Cl catalysts the: (i) liquid yield (C5), (ii) yield of aromatics, (iii) isoparaffin/aromatics ratio, (iv) side reactions (hydrocracking, hydrogenolysis, coking) as responses can be altered by controlling the independent reaction parameters (Temperature, Pressure, Liquid Hourly space velocity (LHSV), Hydrogen to hydrocarbon ratio (H2/HC ratio), chlorine and the addition of different promoters to the catalyst (Re, Sn, Ir, etc).
