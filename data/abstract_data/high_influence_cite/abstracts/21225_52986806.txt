Understanding the Related-Key Security of Feistel Ciphers From a Provable Perspective
We initiate the provable related-key security treatment for models of <italic>practical</italic> Feistel ciphers. In detail, we consider Feistel networks with four whitening keys <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$w_{i}(k)$ </tex-math></inline-formula>, <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$i=0,1,2,3$ </tex-math></inline-formula>, and round functions of the form <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$f(\gamma _{j}(k)\oplus X)$ </tex-math></inline-formula>, where <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$k$ </tex-math></inline-formula> is the master key, <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$w_{i}$ </tex-math></inline-formula> and <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$\gamma _{j}$ </tex-math></inline-formula> are efficient transformations, and <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$f$ </tex-math></inline-formula> is a <italic>public</italic> ideal function or permutation accessible by the adversary. We investigate the key-schedule conditions that are sufficient for security against XOR-induced related-key attacks up to <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$2^{n/2}$ </tex-math></inline-formula> adversarial queries. When the key schedules are <italic>non-linear</italic>, we prove security for four rounds. When only <italic>affine</italic> key schedules are used, we prove security for six rounds. These also imply secure tweakable Feistel ciphers in the Random Oracle model. By shuffling the key schedules, our model unifies both the DES-like structure (known as <italic>Feistel-2</italic> scheme in the cryptanalytic community, also known as <italic>key-alternating Feistel</italic> due to Lampe and Seurin) and the Lucifer-like model (previously analyzed by Guo and Lin). This allows us to derive concrete implications on these two (more common) models and helps understanding their related-key security difference.
