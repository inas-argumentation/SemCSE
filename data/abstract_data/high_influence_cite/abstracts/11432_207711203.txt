Regulation of brain PPARgamma2 contributes to ketogenic diet anti-seizure efficacy
&NA; The ketogenic diet (KD) is an effective therapy primarily used in pediatric patients whom are refractory to current anti‐seizure medications. The mechanism of the KD is not completely understood, but is thought to involve anti‐inflammatory and anti‐oxidant processes. The nutritionally‐regulated transcription factor peroxisome proliferator activated receptor gamma, PPAR&ggr;, regulates genes involved in anti‐inflammatory and anti‐oxidant pathways. Moreover, endogenous ligands of PPAR&ggr; include fatty acids suggesting a potential role in the effects of the KD. Here, we tested the hypothesis that PPAR&ggr; contributes to the anti‐seizure efficacy of the KD. We found that the KD increased nuclear protein content of the PPAR&ggr;2 splice variant by 2–4 fold (P < 0.05) in brain homogenates from wild‐type (WT) and epileptic Kv1.1 knockout (KO) mice, while not affecting PPAR&ggr;1. The KD reduced the frequency of seizures in Kv1.1KO mice by ˜ 70% (P < 0.01). GW9662, a PPAR&ggr; antagonist, prevented KD‐mediated changes in PPAR&ggr;2 expression and prevented the anti‐seizure efficacy of the KD in Kv1.1KO mice. Further supporting the association of PPAR&ggr;2 in mediating KD actions, the KD significantly prolonged the latency to flurothyl‐induced seizure in WT mice by ˜ 20–35% (P < 0.01), but was ineffective in PPAR&ggr;2KO mice and neuron‐specific PPAR&ggr;KO mice. Finally, administering the PPAR&ggr; agonist pioglitazone increased PPAR&ggr;2 expression by 2‐fold (P < 0.01) and reduced seizures in Kv1.1KO mice by ˜ 80% (P < 0.01). Our findings implicate brain PPAR&ggr;2 among the mechanisms by which the KD reduces seizures and strongly support the development of PPAR&ggr;2 as a therapeutic target for severe, refractory epilepsy.
