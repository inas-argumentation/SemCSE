Variation in Chilling Sensitivity among Eight Dieffenbachia Cultivars
This study evaluated chilling sensitivity of eight popular Dieffenbachia cultivars. Tissue culture liners were potted in 15-cm diameter pots using Vergro Con- tainer Mix A and grown in a shaded greenhouse under maximum photosynthetically active radiation of 285 mmolm -2 s -1 for 5 months. After determining growth indices, the plants were chilled in walk-in coolers at 2, 7, or 12 8C for 6, 12, or 24 h. Chilled plants were placed back in the shaded greenhouse for chilling injury and growth evaluation. Visible symptoms of injury included chlorosis, necrosis, water-soaked patches on leaves, or complete wilting. In addition to leaf injury, stems of some cultivars chilled at 2 8C for 24 h became water-soaked at the base, which resulted in the death of either entire shoots or entire plants depending on cultivars. Leaf injury occurred in all cultivars chilled at 2 8C, except for 'Panther'; and the longer the exposure at this temperature, the greater the injury. No visual injury was observed among plants chilled at 7 and 12 8C except 'Tropic Honey' that had 26% of leaves injured at 7 8C. Based on the percentage of injured leaves 12 days after chilling at 2 8C for 24 h, the sensitivity of the eight cultivars ranked as follows: Tropic Honey > Sterling > Carina $ Octopus > Camille > Camouflage > Star Bright > Panther. In addition to visual injury, plant growth was also affected by chilling during the subsequent 3 months of growth. All 'Tropic Honey' chilled at 2 8C died re- gardless of the tested chilling duration. Growth indices of all other cultivars except for 'Panther' chilled at 2 8C for 24 h significantly decreased compared with those of controls. 'Camille', 'Camouflage', 'Carina', and 'Sterling' also exhibited significant growth re- duction after chilling at 2 8C for 12 h. This study showed that genetic variation in chilling sensitivity exists among cultivated Dieffenbachia. The identified chilling-tolerant culti- vars could be used for breeding of new chilling-tolerant cultivars. The use of chilling- tolerant cultivars in production may reduce the chance of injury during heating outages and shipment.
