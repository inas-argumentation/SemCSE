Correlation between neuroanatomical and functional respiratory changes observed in an experimental model of Parkinson's disease
What is the central question of this study? What is the relationship between neuroanatomical and functional respiratory changes in an experimental model of Parkinson's disease? What is the main finding and its importance? Sixty days after induction of Parkinson's disease in a rat model, there are decreases in baseline breathing and in the number of neurons, density of the neurokinin‐1 receptor and density of astrocytes in the ventrolateral respiratory region. These results provide the first evidence that neuroanatomical changes occur before functional respiratory deficits in a Parkinson's disease model and that there is a positive correlation between those sets of changes. The neuroanatomical changes impair respiratory activity and are presumably a major cause of the respiratory problems observed in Parkinson's disease.
