Weighted Bernstein-type inequalities, and embedding theorems for the model subspaces
Weighted estimates are obtained for the derivatives in the model (shiftcoinvariant) subspaces K Θ, generated by meromorphic inner functions Θ of the Hardy class Hp(C+). It is shown that the differentiation operator acts from K Θ to a space Lp(w), where the weight w depends on the function |Θ′|, the rate of growth of the argument of Θ along the real line. As an application of the weighted Bernstein-type inequalities, new Carleson-type theorems on embeddings of the subspaces K Θ in L p(μ) are proved. Also, results on the compactness of such embeddings are obtained, and properties of measures μ for which the norms ‖ · ‖Lp(μ) and ‖ · ‖p are equivalent on a given model subspace K Θ, are established. Introduction Let Θ be an inner function in the upper half-plane C, that is, a bounded function analytic in C and such that limy→0+ |Θ(x+ iy)| = 1 for almost all x ∈ R with respect to Lebesgue measure. With each inner function Θ, we associate the model subspace K Θ = H p ∩ ΘHp of the Hardy class H in the upper half-plane. It is well known that for p ∈ (1,∞) the subspaces K Θ are the only subspaces of H that are coinvariant with respect to the semigroup of shifts (Ut)t≥0, where (Utf)(x) = ef(x). The shiftcoinvariant subspaces are of great importance in function theory and in operator theory (see [1, 2]); in particular, they play a key role in the construction of the Nagy–Foiaş functional model for contractions in a Hilbert space. If Θ = B is a Blaschke product with simple zeros {zn}, then for 1 < p ≤ ∞ the model subspace K B coincides with the closed linear span in L (R) of the proper rational fractions with poles in the set {zn} in the lower half-plane. The Paley–Wiener space PW p a , which consists of the entire functions of exponential type at most a the restrictions of which to the real line R are in L(R), is also closely related to the shift-coinvariant subspaces. Namely, if Θ(z) = exp(iaz), a > 0, then K Θ = PW p a ∩H. Consider the differentiation operator D : f 7→ f ′, f ∈ K Θ. It is well known that every f ∈ PW p a , 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞, satisfies the Bernstein inequality ‖f ‖p ≤ a‖f‖p (see [3]).
