Estimating Renyi Entropy of Discrete Distributions
It was shown recently that estimating the Shannon entropy <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$H(p)$ </tex-math></inline-formula> of a discrete <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$k$ </tex-math></inline-formula>-symbol distribution <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$p$ </tex-math></inline-formula> requires <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$\Theta (k/\log k)$ </tex-math></inline-formula> samples, a number that grows near-linearly in the support size. In many applications, <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$H(p)$ </tex-math></inline-formula> can be replaced by the more general Rényi entropy of order <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$ \alpha $ </tex-math></inline-formula> and <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$ H_{\alpha }(p)$ </tex-math></inline-formula>. We determine the number of samples needed to estimate <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$ H_{\alpha }(p)$ </tex-math></inline-formula> for all <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$ \alpha $ </tex-math></inline-formula>, showing that <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$ \alpha < 1$ </tex-math></inline-formula> requires a super-linear, roughly <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$k^{1/ \alpha }$ </tex-math></inline-formula> samples, noninteger <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$ \alpha >1$ </tex-math></inline-formula> requires a near-linear <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$k$ </tex-math></inline-formula> samples, but, perhaps surprisingly, integer <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$\alpha >1$ </tex-math></inline-formula> requires only <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$\Theta (k^{1-1/\alpha })$ </tex-math></inline-formula> samples. Furthermore, developing on a recently established connection between polynomial approximation and estimation of additive functions of the form <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$\sum _{x}f (p_{x})$ </tex-math></inline-formula>, we reduce the sample complexity for noninteger values of <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$\alpha $ </tex-math></inline-formula> by a factor of <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$\log k$ </tex-math></inline-formula> compared with the empirical estimator. The estimators achieving these bounds are simple and run in time linear in the number of samples. Our lower bounds provide explicit constructions of distributions with different Rényi entropies that are hard to distinguish.
