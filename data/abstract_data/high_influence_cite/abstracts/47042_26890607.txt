Steady States and Universal Conductance in a Quenched Luttinger Model
We obtain exact analytical results for the evolution of a 1+1-dimensional Luttinger model prepared in a domain wall initial state, i.e., a state with different densities on its left and right sides. Such an initial state is modeled as the ground state of a translation invariant Luttinger Hamiltonian $${H_{\lambda}}$$Hλ with short range non-local interaction and different chemical potentials to the left and right of the origin. The system evolves for time t >  0 via a Hamiltonian $${H_{\lambda'}}$$Hλ′ which differs from $${H_{\lambda}}$$Hλ by the strength of the interaction. Asymptotically in time, as $${t \to \infty}$$t→∞, after taking the thermodynamic limit, the system approaches a translation invariant steady state. This final steady state carries a current I and has an effective chemical potential difference $${\mu_{+} - \mu_{-}}$$μ+-μ- between right- (+) and left- (−) moving fermions obtained from the two-point correlation function. Both I and $${\mu_{+} - \mu_{-}}$$μ+-μ- depend on $${\lambda}$$λ and $${\lambda'}$$λ′. Only for the case $${\lambda = \lambda' = 0}$$λ=λ′=0 does $${\mu_{+} - \mu_{-}}$$μ+-μ- equal the difference in the initial left and right chemical potentials. Nevertheless, the Landauer conductance for the final state, $${G = I/(\mu_{+} - \mu_{-})}$$G=I/(μ+-μ-), has a universal value equal to the conductance quantum $${e^2/h}$$e2/h for the spinless case.
