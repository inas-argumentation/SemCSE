EFFECTS OF AORTA BENDING AND RENAL LIGATION ON THE BLOOD PRESSURE: A SIMULATION ON ARTERIAL STIFFENING
Motivation: Arterial stiffening is thought to be the principal cause of hypertension [1-5]. In modern hemodynamic theories, it amplifies the pulse pressure by affecting wave reflection and reducing the cushion function. Methods: In this study, we performed aorta bending experiment on rats to simulate the arterial stiffening in vivo. Results: The blood pressure (both the steady and the pulsatile component) dropped rather than rose, which is quite different from deduction from models of "flow theories". Conclusion: It is a strong support that the artery is a transmission system for the pressure wave and the coupled resonance mechanism [6-14] accounts for the improving transmission efficiency. It may help us to understand the mechanism of hypertension.
