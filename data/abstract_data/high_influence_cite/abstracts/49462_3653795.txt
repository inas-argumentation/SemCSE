Low-Power CMOS Vision Sensor for Gaussian Pyramid Extraction
This paper introduces a CMOS vision sensor chip in a standard <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$0.18~\mu \text{m}$ </tex-math></inline-formula> CMOS technology for Gaussian pyramid extraction. The Gaussian pyramid provides computer vision algorithms with scale invariance, which permits having the same response regardless of the distance of the scene to the camera. The chip comprises <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$176 \times 120$ </tex-math></inline-formula> photosensors arranged into <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$88 \times 60$ </tex-math></inline-formula> processing elements (PEs). The Gaussian pyramid is generated with a double-Euler switched capacitor (SC) network. Every PE comprises four photodiodes, one 8 b single-slope analog-to-digital converter, one correlated double sampling circuit, and four state capacitors with their corresponding switches to implement the double-Euler SC network. Every PE occupies <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$44 \times 44~\mu \text{m}^{2}$ </tex-math></inline-formula>. Measurements from the chip are presented to assess the accuracy of the generated Gaussian pyramid for visual tracking applications. Error levels are below 2% full-scale output, thus making the chip feasible for these applications. Also, energy cost is 26.5 nJ/px at 2.64 Mpx/s, thus outperforming conventional solutions of imager plus microprocessor unit.
