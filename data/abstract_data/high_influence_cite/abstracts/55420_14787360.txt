Response to letter regarding article, “histone deacetylation inhibition in pulmonary hypertension: therapeutic potential of valproic acid and suberoylanilide hydroxamic acid”.
In their letter, Bogaard et al appreciate that HDAC inhibitors “may mitigate pulmonary vascular remodeling through effects on lung endothelial cells or vascular smooth muscle cells”1,2 but are concerned about the possibility that “HDAC inhibitors threaten the heart’s adaptive response to pressure overload.” These concerns, which were addressed in our articles,1,2 arise from their own study using trichostatin A, a broad-spectrum HDAC inhibitor, and valproic acid, which is not a broad-spectrum HDAC inhibitor, in a rat pulmonary artery banding model.3 In their study, trichostatin A (but not valproic acid) worsened right ventricular function and was associated with exaggerated right ventricular fibrosis and capillary rarefaction.
