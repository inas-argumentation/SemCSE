Influence of food composition on the biokinetics and tissue distribution of zinc and americium in mussels
A series of radiotracer experiments was conducted to investigate the influence of food type on the biokinetics of zinc and americium in the common mussel Myfilus edulis. Four types of food a diatom, a green alga, glass beads, and egg albumin particles were labelled with gamma-emitting isotopes (either 65Zn or 241Am) and fed to the mussels. The beads and albumin particles, of comparable size to the algal species, were used as representative pure mineral and pure protein particles, respectively, enabling a direct comparison of the assimilation of the 2 metals from a common source. Wet weight concentration factors of 65Zn and 241Am in whole mussels ranged from -12 to 35 after 5 d of feeding. 241Am was retained longer than 65Zn by shells, while 65Zn was retained much longer than 241Am in the soft parts. In the soft parts, 241Am was predominantly found in the digestive gland, while 65Zn crossed the gut lining far more effectively than 241Am and was distributed to a greater extent in the gills, muscle, mantle and foot. This was probably a result of the rapid desorption of these radionuclides from the food particles into the acidic gut of the animals, followed by binding of 65Zn to specific ligands or molecules that introduce it into specific metabolic pathways.
