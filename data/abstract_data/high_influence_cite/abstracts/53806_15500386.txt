A Framework for Solving Turing Kernel (Compression) Lower Bound Problem and Finding Natural Candidate Problems in NP-intermediate
Kernelization is a significant topic in parameterized complexity. Turing kernelization is a general form of kernelization. For kernelization, It has been established a nice hardness theory [Bodlaender etc. (ICALP 2008, JCSS2009), Fortnow and Santhanam (STOC 2008, JCSS 2011), Dell and van Melkebeek (STOC 2010, J.ACM 2014), Dell and Marx (SODA 2012), Drucker (FOCS 2012, SIAM J. Comput. 2015)], which can obtain lower bounds for kernel size. Unfortunately, there is yet no tool can prove Turing kernel lower bound for any FPT problem. Thus, it was proposed as an open problem in different occasions [Fernau etc.(STACS 2009), Misra etc. (Discrete Optimization 2011), Kratsch (Bulletin of the EATCS 2014), Cygan etc. (Dagstuhl Seminars on kernel 2014)]. Problems that are in NP but are neither in the class P nor NP-complete are called NP-intermediate. Ladner [J.ACM 1975] proved that if P \not = NP, then there exist infinitely many NP-intermediate problems. However, the constructed problems by Ladner is artificial. Thus, finding natural candidate NP-intermediate problems is an important open question in computation complexity. This paper develop a new framework based on some reasonable complexity theoretic assumptions. The framework has three main applications. First and foremost, under the assumption of EXP hierarchy do not collapse, the framework can rule out many Turing kernel lower bound of FPT problems. These problems include edge clique cover, \Sigma_k^{QBF} parameterized by treewidth for constant k \ge 3. etc.. Besides, the framework can also prove better kernel lower bound of these problems under the assumption PH do not collapse. Last but not least, the framework figure out many natural candidate problems in NP-intermediate. Or more precisely, it is proved that if these problems in NP-complete then PH collapse to S_2^{NP}, or if these problems in P then NP \subseteq DTIME(2^{polylogn}).
