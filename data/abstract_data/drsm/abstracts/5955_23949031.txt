Gorham's disease of the spine
Gorham's disease of the spine is very rare and usually associated with poor prognosis. Till date very few cases have been reported in the literature. It is characterized by osseous invasion by angiomatous vascular mass without skip areas, eventually causing lysis of affected bone. Morbidity and mortality are high in those with spinal and/or visceral involvement. Neurological complications increase the mortality to 33% whereas with chylothorax, mortality increases to more than 50%. To describe the clinical course and the challenges in planning and implementing effective rehabilitation services for management of patients with Gorham's disease of the spine. We report two patients with Gorham's disease who had neurological deficits secondary to spinal cord involvement, and their rehabilitation perspectives. Both patients achieved satisfactory ambulation and functional outcomes. Multiple revision surgeries may be required to ensure spinal stability. When working with these patients, one must remain vigilant about spinal stability as well as about possible serious pulmonary complications, and be prepared to make appropriate management decisions when necessary.
