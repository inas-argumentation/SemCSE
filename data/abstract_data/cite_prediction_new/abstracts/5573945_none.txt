Effect of atenolol on red blood cell deformability
Beta-adrenergic blockers are being used prophylactically in the secondary prevention of myocardial infarction, although the precise mechanism by which they exert this effect is not known. Boogaerts et al. (1983) have shown that both propranolol and metoprolol increase red blood cell (RBC) deformability, and suggested that this might be a contributing factor to their cardioprotective effect. Both possess membrane stabilizing activity and this is a possible mechanism for this effect on RBC deformability. In order to determine whether the increase in RBC deformability is due to a beta-adrenoceptor antagonist rather than membrane stabilizing effect, we studied atenolol, a beta-blocker devoid membrane stabilizing actions. In a pharmacokinetic study of oral administration of 100 mg atenolol, blood levels of atenolot after reaching steady state were between 200 and 400 ng/ mt (Ishizaki et al. 1983). We, therefore, chose these two concentrations to incubate with the RBC's. Fifteen millilitres blood samples were obtained from seven healthy volunteers by venepuncture and anticoagulated with sodium heparin. The RBC's were separated after centrifugation and then washed three times in phosphate buffered saline (PBS-A) containing 0.25% human albumin. The washed RBC's were then suspended in PBS-A and PBS-A containing either 200 or 400 ng/ml of atenolol (final concentration) to make a 5% (vol/vol) suspension. The RBC suspensions were then incubated at 37 °C for 30 rain. At the end of the incubation the RBC's were filtered using a gravity driven filtration technique already described by Sowemimo-Coker et al. (1984). The data were analysed by the Mann-Whitney test. No difference was found between the atenolol and placebo-treated RBC's, median relative filterabilities for placebo, atenolol 200 ng/mt and 400 ng/ ml being 0.700, 0.700 and 0.710 respectively. Our results show that atenolol does not influence the deformability of red cells obtained from healthy volunteers. This suggests that the cardioprotective action of beta-adrenoceptor antagonists involves mechanisms other than, or in addition to beta-receptor blockade, although it is possible that the influence of beta-blockade on red cell deformability may be different between healthy volunteers and patients with myocardial infarction.
