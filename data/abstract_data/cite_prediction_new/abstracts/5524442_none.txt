Remissions induced by chlorambucil in chronic granulocytic leukemia.
Elson1demonstrated that single doses of busulfan (1,4-dimethansulfonyloxybutane) (Myleran) produced a selective depression of granulocytes in the rat, whereas chlorambucil [p-(di-2-chloroethyl) aminophenylbutyric acid] was more specifically injurious to lymphocytes. He suggested that these two polyfunctional alkylating agents showed some specificity of action for these different cell types. For this reason, Galton and co-workers2employed busulfan in the treatment of chronic granulocytic leukemia and chlorambucil in treatment of chronic lymphatic leukemia and Hodgkin's disease with beneficial therapeutic results. Subsequent workers3have continued clinical trials of busulfan in chronic granulocytic leukemia and chlorambucil in chronic lymphatic leukemia and lymphomas, and their reports have served to support the view that these alkylating agents have separate and specific effects in these conditions. If correct, this is of extraordinary significance, since it would indicate that by chemical modifications polyfunctional alkylating agents could be designed to inhibit proliferation of specific tissues.
