Chronic bronchitis versus asthma--or what's in a name?
I have never liked the adversary system in medicine. The word versus suggests that there is a contest with one clear winner, one clear loser, and that issues can be easily compartmentalized and thus resolved in a definitive fashion. Those who care for patients with cough, wheeze, spit, and varying degrees of dyspnea realize that it “just ain’t so.” The original definitions of the American Thoracic Society published in 1962 sought to offer a workable definition for chronic bronchitis, asthma, and emphysema.’ Fortunately, I had the unique opportunity to sit in on the deliberations, resulting in these definitions, hear the arguments and almost endless disagreements, and realize early on in my training in pulmonary medicine that definitions to which everyone will agree are difficult if not impossible to set down. Remembering this after much more involvement with numerous patients with diseases characterized by expiratory airflow limitation, I decided to entitle the fifteenth Aspen Lung Conference on bronchial asthma, “Reversible Obstructive Airways Disease (Formerly Called Asthma).“’ The look on the faces of my friends with formal training in clinical immunology, including the editor of this JOURNAL, as they first picked up the program was worth more words than I could ever write in this editorial!
