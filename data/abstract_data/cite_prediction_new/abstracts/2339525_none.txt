The Teacher as Teacher Educator: New Roles in Professional Development Schools
In the movement to restructure schools, teachers are taking on many new roles and responsibilities. In these “boundary blurring roles” as Lampert (1991) calls them, teachers are both challenging traditional conceptions of teachers' work and changing the contexts for teaching and learning. We are particularly interested in the roles teachers adopt as teacher educators in newly created professional development schools. Professional development centers are attempts to reform the career-long education of teachers through the collaborative efforts of schools and universities (Holmes, 1990). New roles for teachers in professional development schools, such as supervisor of student teachers or co-instructor of university classes, have much in common with other teacher leadership roles described in this issue. They require an expanded vision of teaching as a profession, reallocation of time and resources to accommodate new responsibilities, and the development of new skills and perspectives.
