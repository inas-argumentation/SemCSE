Vitamin E deficiency. A sequential light and electron microscopic study of skeletal muscle degeneration in weanling rabbits.
VIrAMIN E deficiency in young rabbits is characterized by extensive acute degeneration of skeletal muscle.l4 Similar pathologic changes are seen in calves and lambs with enzootic nutritional muscular degeneration.57 This nutritional disease, termed "white muscle disease" in lambs and calves because of the gross appearance of the muscular lesions, is of considerable economic importance. Although many reports are available on the light microscopic and biochemical alterations seen in affected tissue,8'8 few ultrastructural studies on degenerated skeletal muscles of Vitamin E-deficient animals have been recorded. Rumery and Hampton9 described the ultrastructural alterations of skeletal muscles of young Vitamin E-deficient rats at late stages of the deficiency syndrome. They reported that mitochondria and sarcolemma appeared relatively resistant to destruction and that the sarcoplasmic reticulum and contractile material of degenerated fibers were extensively altered. Howes, Price, and Blumberg 10 described the ultrastructural changes in skeletal muscles of adult rats fed a Vitamin E-deficient diet for up to 12 months. Degradative mitochondrial changes, numerous cytoplasmic inclusion bodies, formation of myelin figures, and disruption of Z lines and granular degeneration of myofibrils were reported. Cheville11 described mitochondrial alterations, dilation of sarcoplasmic reticulum, and focal myofibrillar destruction in skeletal muscle of young Vitamin E-deficient chicks. The present study, utilizing light and electron microscopic examination of skeletal muscle at various intervals during the development of Vitamin E deficiency, was undertaken to elucidate the sequence of morphologic changes occurring in the muscle fiber during degeneration.
