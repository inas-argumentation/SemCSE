Total duodenal necrosis with retroperitoneal perforation in an adolescent with jejunal intussusceptions
The duodenum is a section of the digestive tract, the necrosis of which is extremely rare. However, duodenal necrosis (DN) as well as its related complications is quite fatal because its diagnosis and surgical management remain difficult, with a mortality rate of approximately 40%. The most frequent conditions leading to DN include necrotizing pancreatitis (NP), traumatic and caustic injury, sepsis and polyarteritis nodosa. Single DN cases have been reported to be caused by high jejunal loop obstruction secondary to malrotation, local volvulus, incarcerated internal hernia, post-surgical adhesions, malrotation with midgut volvulus and congenital bands. Here we reported a case of total DN caused by jejunal invagination.
