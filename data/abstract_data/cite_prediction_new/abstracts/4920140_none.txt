Is Deliberation for Everyone? Variations in the Capacities of Participants in an Irish Deliberative Experiment
Many claims have been made for the impact of deliberative democracy in generating change in people’s opinions, and often in predictable ways. Some claim that people involved in deliberation become more responsible and active citizens. We also know that political participation and attitudes towards certain issues depend on political knowledge and civic education. So to what extent are these linked? Do certain types of people react differently to their involvement in deliberation and is opinion change contingent on the varying capacities of participants. Using data from Ireland’s first national exercise in deliberative democracy – the We the Citizens pilot Citizens’ Assembly that took place in Dublin in the summer of 2011 – we test to see if certain groups are more likely to shift in opinion, and if certain groups benefit (subjectively) from deliberation. The results will inform us about the suitability of using this approach to make policy decisions, or if some groups are in some ways systematically excluded by the deliberative process.
