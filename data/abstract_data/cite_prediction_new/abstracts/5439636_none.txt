Orientation Dependence of Photochemical Reduction Reactions on SrTiO 3 Surfaces
Polished and annealed surfaces of randomly oriented crystallites were used to study the orientation dependence of the photochemical activity of SrTiO 3 surfaces. Silver cations reduced from an aqueous solution produce solid silver metal at the reaction site. The amounts of silver produced by a fixed exposure were used as a relative measure of each grain's activity. The surface structure of the grains was observed using atomic force microscopy and the surface orientation of each grain was determined by electron backscattered diffraction. Surfaces annealed in air for 6h at 1200° C were bound by some combination of the following three planes: {110}, {111}, and a complex facet inclined approximately 24° from {100}. By correlating the orientations of individual grains to the amount of deposited silver, we conclude that surfaces with the complex {100} facet are the most active.
