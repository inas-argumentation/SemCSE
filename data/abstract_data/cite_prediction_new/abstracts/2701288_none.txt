The effects of some ovulation inhibitors on the different plasma lipid fractions.
Plasma lipids were analyzed in 24 women taking Anovlar (.05 mg ethinyl estradiol and 4 mg norethisterone acetate combined Ovulen (.1 mg mestranol and 1 mg ethynodiol diacetate combined) and Volidan (.05 mg ethinyl estradiol and 4 mg megestrol acetate combined) and in 3 ovariectomized women given various estrogens and progestins. Blood samples were taken before and after 1 3 6 and 12 months of oral contraception and before and at 1-3 week intervals after hormones. Cholesterol was lowered for 6 months by Anovlar. Triglycerides and total phospholipids were raised by Ovulen and Volidan and total phospholipids were lowered by Anovlar. Lecithin and cephalin were increased by Ovulen and Volidan and lysolecithin and sphingomyelin decreased. Anovlar increased sphingomyelin. In the ovariectomized women .01 mg ethinyl estradiol increased triglycerides total phospholipids and decreased lysolecithin. 5 mg norethisterone and ethinyl estradiol decreased triglycerides and total phospholipids. The lipid pattern with 4 mg megestrol acetate and ethinyl estradiol resembled that with ethinyl estradiol alone. The results show that the steroid combination the estrogenic or progestogenic effect and the use of 19-nortestosterone or 17-alpha-hydroxyprogesterone derivatives all have independent effects on the various plasma lipids.
