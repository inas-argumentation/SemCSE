Development and study of narrow pressure probes-stethoscope applications
Pressure probes have been widely used in many research and industry fields to achieve real time efficient pressure fluctuations measurements. This work describes the development of a maintenance tool: a pressure probe to be used to acquire an acoustical signature of flow behavior inside a combustion chamber. Attempting to provide a technological improvement, with a pre-test bench tool, on the assessment of primary swirl nozzle/fuel nozzle misalignment of CFM56-5 gas turbine engine, a new design is suggested and a robust mathematical model is derived using the low reduced frequency model that contemplates viscous-thermal damping due to the development of an inner acoustic boundary layer. New configuration has a stethoscope-type design where in one end a microphone is attached to measure pressure fluctuations and in the other end a pressure sensor is included to acquire mean total/static pressure data. The developed mathematical model proved to be versatile and indispensable once a non-viscous approach was implemented and compared. Pressure probe validation was computed between 10-1000Hz, and a sensitivity analysis performed with ambient noise at 77dB and 88dB stated probe’s applicability. An analogous primary swirl nozzle/fuel nozzle assembly case study was carried out to assess probe’s sensitivity on small changes on flow characteristics, and proved to be a promising tool to be included in maintenance facilities.
