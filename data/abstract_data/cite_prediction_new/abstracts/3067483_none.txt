Alzheimer's disease: Molecular consequences of presenilin-1 mutation
Alzheimer's disease is characterized by accumulation in the brain of a family of insoluble amyloid peptides (Aβ peptides), which are produced as a result of the normal processing of β-amyloid precursor protein (β-APP). Russo et al. claim that a truncated Aβ peptide that lacks the first ten amino acids accumulates in the brains of patients carrying a mutant form of pre-senilin 1 (PS1), a protein that is involved in cleavage of β-APP. However, we have found that this same species is also overrepresented in Alzheimer's patients with mutations in β-APP itself. Our findings do not support the conclusion of Russo et al. that pathogenic PS1 mutations may control cleavage of β-APP by β-secretase.
