Addressing a gap in current IMRT quality assurance.
The New York Times articles of 2010 (1) refocused attention on radiation therapy safety, of which patient-specific quality assurance (PSQA) is an important ingredient. PSQA compares treatment planning system (TPS) predictions of delivery with measurements of actual delivery, using a measurement device (eg, film, diode array, portal imaging). Similarity of predicted and measured deliveries is quantified, typically by use of a mathematical algorithm with preset threshold, which classifies measured outputs (2-dimensional or 3-dimensional “images”) as safe or containing sufficiently large deviations to be of concern, here termed “error-free” and “errored” deliveries. Errored deliveries must be investigated and corrected before reaching the patient (eg, by replanning or recalibrating equipment that has drifted out of tolerance). Deviations between calculated and measured images arise from data transfer errors, dose computation algorithm inaccuracies, miscalibrated or malfunctioning multileaf collimator (MLC) leaves, and deviations in physical positions of field-defining components (gantry, jaws, MLC leaves) that vary within normal mechanical tolerances. The challenge for the PSQA algorithm is to separate errored from error-free images in the presence of inherent within-tolerance deviations. An overly sensitive algorithm will erroneously interpret noise and within-tolerance mechanical deviations as delivery errors. In statistical terms, it generates false positives, investigation of which wastes time and resources. Alternatively, the algorithm can produce false negatives, indicating error-free delivery even though an errored delivery occurred. Analogously, in mammography, the challenge is to detect bona fide cancers while rejecting confounding features such as calcifications. Classification performance of a new mammography algorithm is quantified by use of standard statistical metrics: sensitivity, specificity, and overall accuracy (ie, percentage of correct classifications).
