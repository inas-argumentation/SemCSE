The management of existing water distribution system (WDS) is challenged due to ageing 13 of infrastructure, population growth, increasing of urbanization, climate change impacts and 14 environmental pollution. Therefore, there is a need for integrated solutions that support decision 15 makers to plan today the potential interventions, considering the possible consequences and 16 variations in the mid and long term perspective. This research study is part of a more comprehensive 17 project, where advanced hydraulic analysis of WDS is coupled with a dynamic resources input18 output analysis model. The proposed modelling solution could be used for the optimization of the 19 performance of a water supply system while also considering energy consumption and 20 environmental impacts, therefore, providing a robust support tool for the management of water 21 supply systems also in the stage of intervention planning. This work presents a possible application 22 of the proposed method in pipe rehabilitation/replacement planning, based on which the network 23 mechanical reliability is maximized while minimizing, at the same time, the risk of unsupplied 24 water demand and pressure deficit evaluated at nodal level, under given economic constraints. 25
