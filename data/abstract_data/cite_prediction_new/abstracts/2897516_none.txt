Presence of Minority Resistant Variants After Failure of a Tenofovir, Emtricitabine, and Rilpivirine Regimen
INTRODUCTION The routine sequencing techniques only detect viral populations if they account for more than 15%–20% of viral population. However, presence of HIV-1 minority resistant variants (MRV), whose rates are under the detection threshold of Sanger sequencing, seems to have an impact more or less important according to the molecules used. This impact has been mainly shown for nonnucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NNRTIs) in first-line treatment. MRV can be detected and quantified by ultra-deep sequencing (UDS) at a far lower frequency. However, the detection of such MRV that could be selected at virological failure has not been deeply studied. This work complements the prospective study already published in the same journal, which followed 155 patients with plasma HIV-RNA ,20 copies per milliliter at baseline and switched to tenofovir, emtricitabine, and rilpivirine from September 2012 to May 2013 at Saint-Antoine Hospital (Paris, France). Indeed, rilpivirine is a recently approved NNRTI widely used but few data are available for this drug in real life. Here, we studied virological failure to tenofovir, emtricitabine, and rilpivirine of the previous study by UDS (454; Roche Life Sciences, Branford, CT) to evaluate the selection of MRV and the impact on following treatments. METHODS From September 2012 to May 2013, all patients with viral load (VL) HIV-RNA ,20 copies per milliliter and switching to tenofovir, emtricitabine, and rilpivirine were included in a prospective study at Saint-Antoine Hospital (Paris, France). Sanger sequencing was performed before baseline in plasma and Sanger and UDS (454; Roche Life Sciences) were performed in patients with 2 consecutive VL .20 copies per milliliter.
