The effect of arabinose-specific enzyme synthesis on recombination in the arabinose genes of Escherichia coli.
IVE genes are known to be specifically involved in the metabolism of the pentose sugar L-arabinose by Escherichia coli B/r. The araA and araB genes are the structural genes for the L-arabinose isomerase and L-ribulokinase (LEE and ENGLESBERG 1962). The araD and araE genes are probably structural genes for ~-ribulose-5-phosphate 4-epimerase and L-arabinose permease respectively (ENGLESBERG et al. 1962; ISAACSON and ENGLESBERG 1964). a r d is a regulatory gene whose product is required for synthesis of the enzymes and permease (HELLING and WEINBERG 1963; ENGLESBERG, IRR, POWER and LEE 1965). It is now well established (at least in microorganisms) that all genes do not function to produce a gene product (enzyme) all of the time. Appropriate regulatory mechanisms ensure that many enzymes are produced only in response to a specific need (see JACOB and MONOD 1961). In E. coli synthesis of the arabinose enzymes and permease is induced by arabinose; when the arabinose is removed from the medium synthesis ceases. If regulation of enzyme synthesis occurred at the level of the gene (transcription level rather than or in addition to regulation at the level of translation of mRNA into protein), one would expect a gene in its “active” state to behave differently in recombination or any other process involving the gene, than if it were not actively engaged in mRNA and protein synthesis. This prediction is confirmed by the results reported here. In the experiments described in this paper, I looked to see what effect arabinose-specific enzyme synthesis had on recombination in the structural genes for these enzymes. The frequency of recombination was found to be higher when the enzymes were being synthesized than when they were not being synthesized. The frequency of recombination in the adjacent leucine genes was unaffected.
