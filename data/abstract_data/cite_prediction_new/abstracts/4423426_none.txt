Extensive deep venous thrombosis resulting in compartment syndrome of the thigh and leg. A case report.
Increased intramuscular tissue pressure and compartment syndrome resulting from extensive venous thrombosis of the lower extremity are well accepted factors in the pathophysiology of phlegmasia cerulea dolens, the most severe form of deep venous thrombosis of the extremity. Without intervention, this condition progresses to venous hypertension, ischemia in the limb, venous gangrene, and, often, systemic circulatory failur&”. Compartment syndrome resulting from less severe forms of deep venous thrombosis often is missed”'. We report the case of a patient in whom a compartment syndrome of the thigh developed secondary to deep venous thrombosis. The localized involvement of the thigh and ongoing anticoagulation therapy led to initial confusion concerning the underlying etiology and to a delay in recognition of the problem.
