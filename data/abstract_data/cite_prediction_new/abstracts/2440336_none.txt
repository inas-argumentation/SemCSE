Perioperative management of rheumatoid medications.
t j e RHEUMATOID ARTHRITIS (RA) is a chronic, progressive disease characterized by symmetric polyarthropathy with destruction of joints and upporting soft tissues. Medical management of RA has mproved with newer medications, including diseaseodifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs) and bioogic agents. These have prevented the level of destrucion previously seen and have thus decreased the need or surgical intervention. There is, however, still a role for surgical intervention in RA patients who are unresponsive to medical treatment. In addition, these patients might require surgical treatment for unrelated conditions, such as traumatic injuries. If surgery is necessary, a decision must be made regarding routine RA medications in the perioperative period, striking a balance between the risks of postoperative infection and wound healing problems with the risks of postoperative flares that can prolong recovery and delay rehabilitation.
