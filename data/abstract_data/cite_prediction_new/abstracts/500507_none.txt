Clinical-radiological relationship in fasciolosis: A case report
Fasciolosis is a zoonosis caused by the entire Fasciola hepatica trema, which affects herbivorous vertebrate animals (cows, sheep, and goats, among others) and humans. The infection is acquired due to the intake of various raw aquatic plants, some terrestrial, or water contaminated with metacercariae, the infecting form. It is estimated that there are at least 2.4 million people infected in 70 countries. No continent is free of species of the genus Fasciola (Fasciola hepatica and F. gigantica), and it is considered that where there are cases of these parasites in animals, there are also human cases.1,2
