The report by Myint and colleagues states that their instructions for care on the postnatal wards involved counting and recording of respiratory rate for ‘every 30 min for 2 h and hourly for a further 22 h’. The incident occurred 40 min after the injection and disaster was narrowly averted, principally because medical assistance was close at hand. Perhaps an earlier observation e.g. at I5 min would have detected a change and the incident totally avoided. We check on the mothers’ respiratory status every 15 min for 2 h, but only record the rate if they are asleep, since the presence of spouse and newborn usually provide enough stimulation to keep the mother awake (and breathing). Supplemental oxygen is given should the mother wish to sleep, and an oximeter with a loud alarm is applied. We aim to keep mothers on the delivery ward for at least an hour postinjection of epidural opioid, although in our experience the oximeter readings have always stabilised after 15 min. Intra-operative administration of any dose of intravenous diazepam precludes the subsequent use of epidural opioid in our unit.
