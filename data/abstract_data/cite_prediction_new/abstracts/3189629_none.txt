Extremal Characterizations of the Schur Complement and Resulting Inequalities
Let \[ H=\pmatrix{H_{11}&H_{12}\cr H_{12}^*&H_{22}} \] be an $n\times n$ positive semidefinite matrix, where $H_{11}$ is $k\times k$ with $1 \le k < n$. The {\it generalized Schur complement} of $H_{11}$ in $H$ is defined as $$ S(H) = H_{22} - H_{12}^*H_{11}^{\dag}H_{12}, $$ where $H_{11}^{\dag}$ is the Moore\,--Penrose generalized inverse of $H_{11}$. It has the extremal characterizations \[ S(H) = \max\left\{W: H - \pmatrix{0_k & 0 \cr 0 & W\cr} \ge 0, W \hbox{ is } (n-k) \times (n-k) \hbox{ Hermitian} \right\} \] and\rule[-4.75pt]{0pt}{0pt} $$ S(H) = \min \left\{[Z|I_{n-k}] H [Z|I_{n-k}]^*: Z \ \hbox{ is } \ (n-k) \times k \right\}. $$ \noindent These characterizations are used to deduce many old and new inequalities for Schur complements of positive semidefinite matrices. In many cases, stronger statements and shorter proofs can be obtained using the extremal characterizations.
