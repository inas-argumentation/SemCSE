High-Dose Methotrexate in Fibroblastic Osteosarcoma
TO THE EDITOR: We report a case of therapeutic drug monitoring of high-dose methotrexate (HDMTX) in a patient with fibroblastic osteosarcoma. Case Report. A 41-year-old Bruneian man was diagnosed with high-grade osteogenic sarcoma of the left tibia in September 1997. He underwent limb salvage surgery followed by a course of radiotherapy. In July 1998, he developed local recurrence with soft tissue extension into the thigh, and lung metastasis. Chemotherapy comprised of doxorubicin and cisplatin was given for six cycles; a good partial response was achieved. In July 1999, progression was documented in all the sites of disease. He was administered intravenous HDMTX 8 g/m2 over six hours. The dose of MTX for the second cycle was adjusted to 10 g/m2 to achieve a maximum concentration (Cmax) of ≥450 mg/L. Blood sampling during both cycles was performed at four and six hours after the start of infusion and 10, 14, 24, 36, 48, 72, and 96 hours after the end of infusion. Plasma MTX concentrations were assayed using fluorescence polarization immunoassay. The disease stabilized after two cycles of treatment. He subsequently underwent disarticulation of the left hip in September 1999 with hindquarter resection as he continued to have pain from the local disease. Histology revealed extensive cystic necrosis, and hemorrhage was noted in the tumor. Baseline serum creatinine values during the first and second cycles were 1.5 and 1.1 mg/dL, respectively. Folinic acid was started and continued until MTX concentrations were <0.02 mg/L. Mouthwashes with chlorhexidine were initiated to prevent mucositis. Other medications during both cycles included naproxen 275 mg three times daily, codeine phosphate 30 mg three times daily, lansoprazole 30 mg once daily, and metoclopropamide 10 mg three times daily. Discussion. In this patient, the Cmax and AUC were increased by 27% and 15%, respectively, during the second cycle (Table 1). The slightly prolonged elimination half-life during cycle 1 could have been due to inhibition of active tubular secretion of MTX in the distal tubules as a result of concomitant lansoprazole administration.1 The patient did not experience any episode of mucositis or neutropenia during both cycles of HDMTX therapy. Renal function was normal during both cycles. Delepine et al.2 showed that higher doses of MTX (≤20 g/m2) resulted in substantial increase in mean Cmax, with substantially higher rates of histologic response in surgically resected specimens (66% vs. 45%, respectively) and greater disease-free survival at five years (92% vs. 74%, respectively). Bacci et al.3 showed MTX Cmax ≥318 mg/L and histologic subtypes to be significant predictive factors of histologic response. The percentage of patients achieving complete response was higher in those who achieved Cmax ≥318 mg/L compared with those who achieved lower concentrations (28.8% vs. 9.9%, respectively). Patients with chondroblastic subtype were less responsive (complete response 6.1%) compared with patients with osteoblastic subtype (16.3%), fibroblastic subtype (33.3%), and telangiectatic subtype (42.3%). It is important to note that response rates in osteosarcoma patients vary according to histologic subtypes. Dosage adjustment of HDMTX according to histologic subtypes of tumor, in combination with individual patients’ pharmacokinetic parameters, can lead to better response rates. This case study showed that a targeted Cmax can be used to optimize HDMTX therapy in fibroblastic osteosarcoma patients as well as minimize toxicity and improve oncolytic response.
