Palaeoclimate science: Causes and effects of Antarctic ice
Some 34 million years ago, there was a rapid growth of ice on Antarctica. A modelling study indicates that the ultimate cause of this glaciation was a decrease in the concentration of atmospheric carbon dioxide. See Letter p.574
 Antarctica developed an ice sheet about 34 million years ago, following a period of ice-free conditions known as the 'super-greenhouse' climate. Previous research has suggested that ice sheet growth was driven by either the thermal isolation of Antarctica arising from the opening of ocean gateways or by progressively lower atmospheric CO2 content. Matt Huber and colleagues now use a climate model to show that the growth of the ice sheet — forced by changes in CO2 — drove the changes in ocean circulation. In contrast, the opening of the gateways had relatively little impact on ocean circulation.
