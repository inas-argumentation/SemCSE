Comment on Ramage et al.: Consensus Guidelines for the Management of Patients with Digestive Neuroendocrine Tumours: Well-Differentiated Colon and Rectum Tumour/Carcinoma
Rectal Neuroendocrine Tumors 1 2 cm Nevertheless, even nowadays some patients present with rectal carcinoids 1 2 cm in size. According to the SEER database, 60– 80% of rectal carcinoids 1 2 cm metastasize [7, 8] . Therefore most clinicians in the USA, Japan and Europe agree that (neuro-)endocrine tumors of the rectum 1 2 cm should be managed surgically including lymph node dissection [8–13] . Exulcerated colorectal neuroendocrine tumors should be treated along the same lines [6] . Williams [14] recommends that endocrine tumours of any part of the large intestine that are 1 20 mm in size, that invade the muscularis propria or blood vessels, and that show necrosis, a mitotic frequency of 1 2/10 HPF or ‘atypical’ cytology should be treated in the same way as colorectal adenocarcinomas.
