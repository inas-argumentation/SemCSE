Neuron survival depends on the efficacy of the entry into axons and retrograde transport into the perikaryon of neurotrophic factors sourced in the non-neural cells, especially Schwann cells, of the damaged nerve [2]. Of the many factors stimulating regeneration which are currently being studied, pyrimidine derivatives have received particular attention. After nerve trauma, the pyrimidine derivatives MS-818 and MS-430 stimulate nerve fiber regeneration [10, 11]. The possibility that pyrimidines affect surviving neurons and Schwann cell phenotypes remains unclear. Preliminary experiments have demonstrated the potential of one pyrimidine derivative, xymedone (1,2-dihydro-4,6dimethyl-N(ß-hydroxyethyl)pyrimidone-2), which stimulated regeneration of myelinated fibers in the rat sciatic nerve [1]. The aim of the present work was to assess the population size and phenotype of surviving sensory neurons and Schwann cells in conditions of pharmacological stimulation of nerve regeneration.
