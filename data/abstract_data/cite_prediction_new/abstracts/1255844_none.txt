Trial of a bacterial screening system for rapid detection of mutagens and carcinogens.
A bacterial test system for detection of mutagens and carcinogens based on a DNA polymerase-deficient mutant strain of Escherichia coli was applied to the study of several newly synthesized nitrosamines and other compounds. Our results with compounds previously assayed in the system agreed qualitatively with those reported; however, we found the system to be less sensitive, i.e. , zones of inhibition were much smaller than those previously reported with compounds such as methylnitrosourea and diethylnitrosamine. Azaserine was found to inhibit growth of the mutant E. coli preferentially without need for metabolic activation. In the presence of a microsomal system, the following compounds were found to inhibit growth of the mutant strain preferentially: acetylaminofluorene, cycloheximide, diethylnitrosamine, melphalan, O -( N -nitroso- N -methyl-β-alanyl)-l-serine, O -( N -nitrososarcosyl)-l-serine, and puromycin. However, the size of the zone of differential inhibition did not exceed 2 to 4 mm, in contrast to the large zones of inhibition previously reported for carcinogens. These compounds were inactive in the absence of the microsomal activation system. Three of these compounds, acetylaminofluorene, diethylnitrosamine, and melphalan, have been reported to be carcinogenic in animals, and the others have not been tested. A nitroso derivative of neutral red did not inhibit growth. Among known chemical carcinogens included in this study, only benzo( a )pyrene failed to preferentially inhibit growth of the test E. coli strain.
