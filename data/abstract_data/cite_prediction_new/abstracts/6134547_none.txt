Tubulovesicular structures in Gerstmann-Sträussler-Scheinker disease
Tubulovesicular structures (TVS) are unique structures which have been observed by thin-section electron microscopy in brains affected with transmissible spongiform encephalopathies [1, 4]. Recently, we reported for the first time the occurrence of TVS in human brains with Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (CJD) [5]. A few investigators still claim, however, that "TVS are inconsistent features of transmissible spongiform encephalopathies" but admit that they "are not described for other degenerative encephalopathies" [3]. Here, we report the presence of TVS in brain tissue of a patient with Gerstmann-Strfiussler-Scheinker disease (GSS) who belonged to the original Austrian family first described in detail in 1936 [2]. Numerous brain (cerebral cortex and cerebellum) specimens, obtained at autopsy at 3 h postmortem, were fixed in 2.5% buffered glutaraldehyde and embedded in Epon 812. Several serial grids were examined ultrastructurally at an initial magnification of • 7000. Fifty Epon-embedded blocks from six cases of Alzheimer's disease (two biopsies and four early autopsies) served as controls. Ultrastructurally, the GSS brain revealed typical features of numerous kuru type and multicentric amyloid plaques in both cerebral cortex and the cerebellum. A neuronal process containing numerous TVS was found in only one location of the cerebral cortex (Fig. 1). TVS were very difficult to find because of dense astrocytic gliosis which virtually replaced all other cellular elements in most of the examined specimens. TVS were smaller and denser than synaptic vesicles but larger than cross-sectioned microtubules. They were also distinct from glycogen granules and multivesicular bodies. Available data on TVS were discussed in a recent paper [5] and will not be recapitulated here. Given their rarity in the brain which we examined, it is not surprising that they have not been detected in previous GSS reports.
