Reply to "Prognostic stratification by extramural depth of tumor invasion of primary rectal cancer based on the Radiological Society of North America".
AJR 2014; 203:W750 0361–803X/14/2036–W750 © American Roentgen Ray Society Reply to “Prognostic Stratification by Extramural Depth of Tumor Invasion of Primary Rectal Cancer Based on the Radiological Society of North America” We thank Tong and Gollub [1] for their comments and opinions on our study [2]. With regard to the first comment, we addressed the point on the third limitation of our study. We thought that preoperative chemoradiotherapy (CRT) might be one intervening factor because it is well known that the interpretation of perirectal fat infiltration after CRT is much more difficult than that in the primary rectal cancer without CRT. Therefore, a mixed cohort with irradiated and nonirradiated cancer might present a risk of distorting the results. We thought that the potential of MRI in the case of irradiated cancer should be considered separately. Thus, the scope of our study was focused on the primary rectal cancer without CRT. As for the second comment, Table 3 in our article [2] showed the composition of recurrence sites as well as distant metastases. Therefore, the 3-year local recurrence rate in our study cohort was 8.2% (12 local recurrences in 146 patients: five patients in an anastomosis or the pelvis side wall and seven patients in an internal iliac lymph node). With respect to the third comment, preoperative CRT became the standard of care in locally advanced rectal cancer after the German Rectal Cancer Study Group trial [3] in 2004. However, it might take a few years to completely change the therapeutic planning in Asian countries or institutions. Additionally, indications and methods of preoperative CRT are still variable depending on the country [4]. During the period of our study, preoperative CRT was not routinely provided in our institution. Most patients with T3 disease underwent total mesorectal excision with adjuvant radiotherapy (50.4 Gy) and selective adjuvant chemotherapy (Mayo regimen) after multidisciplinary team discussion. From January 2000 to November 2008, the 3-year local recurrence rate for all rectal cancer stages in our institution was 3.8% in the laparoscopic surgery group and 5.9% in the open surgery group [5]. However, after late 2007, preoperative CRT was established in the routine clinical setting at our institution, and all patients with stage II or III disease underwent longcourse preoperative CRT followed by curative surgery after 6–8 weeks, with curative intent. Currently, the local recurrence rate at our institution is approximately 2%. I believe that the current local recurrence rate in our institution is similar to the rate in major worldwide cancer centers. We hope that further research overcoming the limitations of our study will be conducted and show valuable results. Again, we thank Tong and Gollub [1] for their interest in our study. Seung Hyun Cho Kyungpook National University Medical Center, Daegu, Korea
