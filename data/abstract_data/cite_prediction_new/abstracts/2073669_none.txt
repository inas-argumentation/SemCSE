Selective non-enzymic cleavage of the myosin rod. Electron-microscopic studies on crystals and paracrystals of light meromyosin-C.
Cyanogen bromide selectively cleaves the rod section of the myosin molecule at two specific methionyl residues lying approximately 950 A from the C-terminus. As a consequence, a large segment of the α-helical coiled-coil backbone of myosin (light meromyosin-C) is released fully intact without internal peptide bond interruptions. Since this molecule (unlike light meromyosin produced by tryptic cleavage) is a chemically homogeneous fragment of myosin, its aggregation properties have been examined with the electron microscope over a wide range of solvent conditions in an attempt to prepare true three-dimensional crystals. At least four ordered phases of light meromyosin-C aggregates have been identified. These phases are: (a) unbanded filaments; (b) paracrystalline tactoida which show a 143 A period when stained; (c) paracrystalline square nets with a 685 A repeat distance; (4) true crystals, based upon a stacking of layers of parallel strands running alternately in two mutually perpendicular directions. These reticulated crystals appear in projection as square nets with a repeat distance of 390 ± 10 A, and a plausible model has been built to account for their electron microscopic features.
