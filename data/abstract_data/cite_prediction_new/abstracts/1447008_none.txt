Effective and efficient platelet transfusion strategies that maintain hemostatic protection
latelet transfusion guidelines ensuring hemostatic protection in patients with severe thrombocytopenia vary considerably among institutions. Recommendations differ regarding the platelet count at which transfusions should be given (transfusion trigger), posttransfusion target platelet count, number of platelets required to attain the posttransfusion target count, posttransfusion platelet functional assessment, and recommended transfusion regimen. Unfortunately, transfusion management strategies aimed at providing platelet hemo-stasis effectively and efficiently are frequently eclipsed by the necessity to transfuse whatever platelet components are available. As technical and testing expertise improve, effectiveness and efficiency become increasingly relevant. In this issue of TRANSFUSION, Hersh et al.' present theoretical calculations and transfusion experience supporting their recommendation for smaller, more frequent platelet transfusions. In developing optimal platelet transfusion strategies that maintain platelet hemostatic protection, consideration must be given to recent findings supporting the effectiveness and efficiency of administering larger, less frequent platelet transfusions.
