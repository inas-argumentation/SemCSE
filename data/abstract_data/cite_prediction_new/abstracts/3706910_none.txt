STREAMING POTENTIALS AND OTHER WATER‐DEPENDENT EFFECTS IN MINERALIZED TISSUES
In general, when a liquid phase comes into contact with a solid phase, there is a separation of electrostatic charges; one phase becomes negative with respect to the other. This charge separation produces a potential difference between the solid and the liquid. This difference in potential gives rise to the electrokinetic phenomena of electrophoresis, electroosmosis, and streaming potentials. In an aqueous medium, amino acids and proteins acquire their charge mainly through ionization of carboxyl and amino groups to give COOand NH,+ ions. The ionization of these groups, and consequently the net molecular charge, depends upon the pH of the solution. At low pHs, an amino acid or protein molecule will be positively charged, and at high pHs, it will be negatively charged. The pH at which the net charge due to ionization is zero is called the isoionic point, and the pH at which the electrophoretic mobility is zero is termed the isoelectric point. These two points will only differ noticeably when ion absorption has a significant effect on the net charge. I n an aqueous medium, the surface charges on a protein-containing surface a re opposed by a layer of absorbed counterions of opposite polarity electrostatically bound to the solid surface (FIGURE I ) . To a first approximation, this electrostatic double layer may be regarded as a parallel plate capacitance. In addition to this aspect, a diffuse, weakly held layer of ions extends away from the interface into the bulk of the liquid. This diffuse layer will contain both positive and negative ions but not in equal numbers. As a consequence, this diffuse layer is electrostatically charged. When the liquid is forced to move against such a surface, the region of the diffuse layer outside the hydrodynamic slip plane is carried along with the rest of the liquid. Such a movement of charged liquid constitutes an electric current and therefore causes a difference in potential between two electrodes placed in the streaming liquid (FIGURE 2). These voltages are therefore referred to as “streaming potentials.” The potential difference between the bulk of the liquid and that which exists a t the hydrodynamic slip plane is termed the zeta potential (FIGURE 3). All electrokinetic phenomena may be related to the zeta potential for a particular solidliquid interface.
