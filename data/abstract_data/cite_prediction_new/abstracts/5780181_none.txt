Revenue and Land-Use Effects of Proposed Changes in Sin Taxes: A General Equilibrium Perspective
In recent years, large budget deficits have been of great concern to the U.S. government as well as to the general population, leading to pressures for alternative sources of revenue. One possibility is raising income taxes. However, this revenue source is extremely unpopular and fraught with liabilities for any government official or agency who might support it. A more popular proposal is to increase the federal excise taxes on alcohol and tobacco. It has been suggested in Congress that the revenue raised through these additional taxes be earmarked for funding a 1.3 billion dollar shortfall in drug rehabilitation programs. The most common proposals to achieve this funding are to alter Section 5701(b) of the tax code by increasing the sales tax on cigarettes from $0.16 to $0.32 per pack and to alter Sections 5041 and 5051 of the tax code by increasing the taxes on beer and wine to the level on distilled spirits, which is based on alcohol content.' Increases in alcohol and cigarette taxes have been justified both politically and economically.
