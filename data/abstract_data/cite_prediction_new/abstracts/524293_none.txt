Risky business: Negotiating new knowledge, new values, new behaviours
Despite Nilsson coining the term ‘Internationalisation at Home‘ in 1999, discussion of internationalising the curriculum is still dominated by ways of inducting international students into ‘How the West is Done’ (Doherty and Singh, 2005). Research has repeatedly shown that the focus of internationalisation needs to be on the majority home student (Dunne, 2009; Haigh, 2009a; Henderson, 2009), yet universities generally struggle to make any real commitment to curricula change, preferring to hope that the individual innovators among us will make a difference. At Oxford Brookes University policy changes are ensuring attention to internationalisation for all students is addressed across the curriculum. Three critical areas have been identified for development: interacting with non-Western modes of thought; interacting with the other; and responsible citizenship. In this session the facilitators will provide stimulus material (texts and role play) for discussion and activities in these areas to challenge participants to start to rethink their curricula in the light of the needs of students preparing for living and working successfully in a globalised 21 st century.
