Unilateral or asymmetric bilateral optic nerve involvement produces a relative afferent pupillary defect, but bilaterally symmetrical, severe optic nerve damage results in reduction or loss of the light reflex. While cortical blindness is commonly due to embolic infarction or severe hypotension, the mechanism for optic neuropathy (ON) is less clear. We have encountered two patients within nine months of each other, in whom ON with blindness was noted shortly after cardiac surgery complicated by superior vena cava (SVC) obstruction.
