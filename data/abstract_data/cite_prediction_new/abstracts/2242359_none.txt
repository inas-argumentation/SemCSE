International Cooperation – The Ben-Gurion University and the University of California, Los Angeles: A Case Study
This paper discusses economic development and the large differences existing among and within different countries. Social and human factors are playing an important role and focus on the need and value of education. Academic co-operation between universities is valuable generally, and can serve and encourage development in particular.The programme of academic cooperation between the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA) and the Ben-Gurion University of the Negev (BGU) in Beersheva, Israel has been a mutually beneficial relationship for ten years and has resulted in many exchanges, joint research, conferences and publications. This paper describes the background of the two institutions, the history of the programme, and its organization, initial expectations, activities and experience.Finally, it discusses in more general terms some issues and factors involved in academic co-operation aimed at development. These include questions of conflicts of priorities, faculty development, management and evaluation, mutual benefit, and funding.
