Transport of Specimens for Clinical Chemistry Analysis
Packaging Many laboratories issue their own labelled containers and forms specifying what information is required. If these are not used, it is essential to label the specimen with the full name, the date and time of day, and the type of specimen (for example, plasma, serum, or urine). The form (or accompanying letter) should, in addition, give date of birth and sex of the patient, the clinical details relating to the request, the conditions under which the specimen was taken (for example, fasting, fluid restriction and any drug therapy that might affect the result or interfere with the estimation), and the type of anticoagulant or preservative used. If urine is sent it should be clear whether it is an aliquot of a larger timed specimen (in which case the total volume and times of colIection should be stated). If more than one specimen is sent each should be clearly timed, the number in the series noted, and any accompanying procedure (such as administration of antidiuretic hormone) should be stated in detail: this should include dose and volume given, route and preparation used (for example, oily or aqueous suspension), and the time of administration.
