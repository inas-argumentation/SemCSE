Studies of rubella and its prevention with immune globulin.
During a rubella epidemic the effects of immune globulin (0.5 cc/kg of body weight) on rash, virus-shedding, and serologic conversion were prospectively tested. If virus excretion or a fourfold titer increase represent infection, then 34 of 43 persons were infected, of whom ten had apparent disease, giving a ratio of two inapparent infections for every apparent infection. The difference in rates of infection between test and control groups was insignificant, but fewer virus isolations were made in the test group than in the control group. Virus isolations and occurrence of rash were inversely related to baseline antibody levels. Isolates were made as early as 12 days before and as late as 45 days after the appearance of rash. A 16-year-old girl with the congenital rubella syndrome had a rubella reinfection. Generalized adenopathy, enanthem, mild fever, leukopenia, and an increased sedimentation rate were features of the acute illness.
