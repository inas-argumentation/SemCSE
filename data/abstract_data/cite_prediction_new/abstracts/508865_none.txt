Biases in aid allocations against poorer and larger countries
The oil price rise has, among other effects, reminded the donor countries that there are very wide differences in needs for external resources among what had often been seen implicitly as an undifferentiated ‘Third World’. The volume of economic aid remains quite large $1 1.3 billion in Official Development Assistance (or ODA) from OECD ‘Development Assistance Committee’ countries and $2.2 billion from OPEC countries in 1974 (DAC, 1975, p. 16) ~ and can provide a useful addition to the import requirements of recipients, particularly if it is concentrated on those which need it the most. This paper analyzes the statistical determinants of the past inter-country allocation of aid, and examines the feasibility of ameliorating, if not eliminating, the apparent biases which emerge.1
