Factors associated with need: Community health and social services among the aged
Some say the aged are the most neglected source of information in the evaluation of community services. This study examines the extent to which demographic characteristics, attitudes, and life changes explain the elderly's perceived needs for community services. Data were obtained from interviews with 169 randomly selected men and women 65 or over. Their responses indicated their greatest needs were transportation, legal aid, and programs involving in‐home services or care that would enable persons to remain in their homes. Projected use of services had the most substantial independent effect. Such variables as beliefs about political intervention by the aged, sex, health evaluation, and past use influenced perceived needs for community services to a more limited degree. Surprisingly such decremental changes as loss of health, income, and the ability to participate had no direct effect on the perceived need for services.
