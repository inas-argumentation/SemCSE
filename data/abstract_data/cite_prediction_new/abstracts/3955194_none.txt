Treatment evaluation issues in research on addictive behaviors.
The growing development of treatment programs for addictive behaviors has not always been coupled with complete evaluations of these programs. General issues in treatment outcome methodology are relevant to addictive behaviors investigations. Necessary factors of internal and external validity are examined for establishing causal relations. The logic and advantages of both the within-subject and between-subject experimental designs are discussed. Assessment issues are presented which deal with reliability, validity, multiple dependent measures, symptom substitution, patient and therapist variables, and data analysis. Finally, criteria such as generalization, follow-up, standardized improvement indices, cost-effectiveness, and clinical significance are suggested as guidelines for evaluating treatment outcome.
