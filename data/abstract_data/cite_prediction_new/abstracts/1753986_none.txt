Phenolic Compounds from Berries of Three Vaccinium Species
Much research has found that inclusion of berries in the diet can have a positive effect on human health [1–3]. Northern wild small berries are considered one of the richest sources of natural antioxidants, which are responsible for most of the positive therapeutic effects [4–7]. These compounds are mainly vitamin C and polyphenols such as anthocyans, phenolic acids, flavones, and tanning agents. The polyphenol composition and quantitative characteristics are highly variable factors that depend primarily on the plant species and habitat [8]. Therefore, the goal of the present work was to study the polyphenol composition of aqueous EtOH extracts obtained from Vaccinium oxycoccus L., V. myrtillus L., and V. vitis-idaea L. berries collected in Khanty-Mansiysk Autonomous Okrug of the RF. Fresh berries were collected in August-September 2014 in Surgut District, Khanty-Mansiysk Autonomous Okrug, in the vicinity of Saigatina town. They were extracted with aqueous EtOH (80%) at room temperature for 1 d. The extract was decanted, filtered, and evaporated in vacuo at 65°C. The extraction and evaporation were repeated five times. The resulting extracts were combined and diluted with H2O–EtOH (1:1). Phenolic compounds were analyzed by HPLC using an Agilent 1290 Infinity chromatograph and gradient mode over a ZORBAX RRHD SB-C18 column (2.1 100 mm, 1.8 m) with a mobile phase consisting of aqueous formic acid (0.1%) and MeCN containing formic acid (0.1%) and a diode-matrix detector at 280 and 325 nm. Absorption spectra were recorded in the range 210–600 nm in 2-nm steps. Anthocyans were separated using aqueous formic acid (5%) as the eluent and detection at 520 nm. Phenolic compounds were identified by retention times and absorption spectra of the corresponding analytical standards. The contents of pure compounds were quantified using a calibration for the corresponding standard samples. Anthocyan concentrations were given in units of glucosides of the corresponding anthocyanidins. Table 1 presents the analytical results for anthocyans in extracts from these berries. Table 1 shows that high contents of cyanidin and peonidin glycosides, especially galactosides and arabinosides, that were characteristic of V. oxycoccus were found [9]. Anthocyans of V. vitis-idaea were practically only cyanidins, primarily cyanidin galactoside [10]. V. myrtillus contained high concentrations of 15 anthocyans. In contrast with V. oxycoccus, peonidin galactosides and arabinosides had the relative minimum concentrations [9]. Table 2 presents analytical results for hydroxybenzoic acids, flavan-3-ols, hydroxycinnamic acids, and flavonols. Tables 1 and 2 show that the extract from V. myrtillus was rich in anthocyans; from V. vitis-idaea, procyanidins. Analogous results were obtained by other researchers [11, 12]. Our analysis showed that the main phenolic compound in V. oxycoccus extract was chlorogenic acid. The concentration of chlorogenic acid in extracts of V. vitis-idaea growing wild in Alaska was 1.5 times greater than that in V. macrocarpon according to previous reports [13]. Like other researchers, we found an increasing concentration of polyphenols in the order V. oxycoccus–V. vitis-idaea– V. myrtillus. The chemical composition of polyphenols in berries from the northern Ob region (Surgut district) was in principle the same as that of analogous berries from North America. The quantitative differences obtained for individual polyphenols were probably related to geographic and local natural conditions.
