Rate-of-return regulation and the behavior of the return on equity for electric utilities
This study tests whether the time-series behavior of the return on equity (ROE) for electric utilities follows a partial adjustment rather than the pure adjustment process implied by the cost-plus policy of rate regulation. The study performs a similar test on a control sample of manufacturing firms to compare the speed of adjustments between regulated and nonregulated industries. The study further tests the sensitivity of the speed of adjustment to incentive regulation and to the level of ROE. The results show that movements in ROEs follow a partial adjustment process for both utilities and manufacturing firms. Strikingly, the adjustment horizon appears to be longer for utilities than for manufacturing firms. Moreover, the speed of adjustment is sensitive to incentive regulation and level of ROE, and has implications for future profits and share prices.
