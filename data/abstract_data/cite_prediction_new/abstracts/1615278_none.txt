An Integrated view of Suffering in Palliative Care
Diseases in general, and life-threatening ones in particular, can cause different kinds of suffering by various means. When one dimension of a person is threatened, a simultaneous disruption may occur in other dimensions (4, 5, 18, 20), affecting the person’s integrated whole. Suffering manifests itself in a significant proportion of patients and leads to decreasing well-being and quality of life (21). Related factors may be classified as physical, psychological, socio-cultural, or spiritual / existential. While these factors may contribute separately to suffering (22), a synergy often occurs among them (18). Consequently, the occurrence or persistence of problems in one dimension can provoke or add to problems in another dimension, leading to an overall increase in suffering for patients and their families. Although not all patients at the end of life present with suffering (4, 5, 20), those who do perceive each symptom or problem in diverse ways depending on the appraisal process (23), their previous experiences, and the context (24). As a result, the same symptom or problem can constitute a greater or lesser threat to an individual patient at different moments, since suffering is dynamic.
