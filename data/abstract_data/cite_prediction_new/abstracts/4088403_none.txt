Congenital hip disease in Utah: the influence of genetic and nongenetic factors.
Even though discord appears in the medical literature concerning the causal relationship between dysplasia and dislocation of the hip (see Rabin et al., 1965), it is generally agreed that both anomalies have the same underlying etiology; therefore, they can be grouped together for epidemiological studies. Some investigators support the hypothesis that the basic abnormality is dislocation. Dysplasia represents a recovery stage which may proceed to normal. Others conclude that the basic abnormality is dysplasia; the stress of malposition, motion, or weight bearing may lead to dislocation. The purpose of this paper is to present the results of studies carried out in Utah designed to determine the influence of genetics, maternal age, birth order, and season of birth on the occurrence of this congenital malformation.
