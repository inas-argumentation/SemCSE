Vertebral artery stenosis is a very unlikely cause of Horner syndrome.
To the Editor: We have read with interest the article of Kim on Horner syndrome caused by vertebral artery stenosis. We think it provides an interesting discussion on the potential etiologies of this syndrome. However, we would like to share our experience on this topic to clarify some aspects. First of all, the author mentions that the patient did not have enophthalmos because of contralateral blowout fracture. Enophthalmos is part of Horner syndrome in some animals. However, this is not the case in humans (in our species, it is only an illusion of enophthalmos caused by narrowing of the palpebral fissure). Second, it is worth to remember that the patient had blunt trauma on his left eye and left orbital fracture. Lesion to pupil constrictor muscle or left eye Adie’s pupil could also justify the asymmetry. Indeed, tonic pupil has been described after orbital floor fracture repair. To rule out this possibility, pupils should be measured under photopic and scotopic conditions and some pharmacological testing should be carried out (cocaine or apraclonidine and pilocarpine 0.125% test). Third, after discarding other potential causes, the author concludes that Horner syndrome was caused by vertebral artery stenosis. We feel that this is highly unlikely because vertebral artery is not part of the oculosympathetic pathway. The patient presented multiple potential causes of Horner syndrome. As the author asserted in the discussion, Horner syndrome has been described after first rib fracture and chest tube insertion. The fact that the tube was in place when the x-ray was taken does not exclude potential damage to the second or third sympathetic neuron during its insertion. In addition, pneumothorax has also been reported as a cause of Horner syndrome. and cases of Horner syndrome secondary to blunt trauma without carotid artery dissection have also been reported. In summary, we think that vertebral artery stenosis is a very unlikely cause for the reported syndrome.
