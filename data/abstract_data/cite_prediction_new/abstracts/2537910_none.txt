Retinal wall-to-lumen ratio: a new marker of endothelial function?
A consistent body of evidence indicates that microvascular disease plays a pivotal role in the pathogenesis of macrovascular disease and cardiovascular events. Remodelling of arterioles and small arteries is an early step in the continuum from hypertension to cardiovascular disease as it precedes other phenotypes of subclinical organ damage. Alterations in microvascular coronary structure and function are independently associated with an increased risk of myocardial infarction, heart failure and cardiovascular mortality [1]. Furthermore, microvascular changes of small arteries [i.e. increased wall-tolumen ratio (WLR)] from subcutaneous fat tissue have been associated with chronic heart failure and incident cardiovascular events in human hypertension [2,3]. These observations, however, derive from small studies due to the invasive nature of the procedure; this limitation may seriously impair the clinical and prognostic significance of these studies on microvascular disease.
