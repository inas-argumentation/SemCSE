At least one metabolite of tacrolimus (31-demethyl-tacrolimus) has been shown to have an immunosuppressive effect. Then, one cannot exclude that part of the immunosuppressive effect could be due to other metabolites and particularly the less studied glucuronides. This could maybe explain at least partially the high degree of variability in the immunosuppressive effect. In conclusion, little is known on drug metabolism through glucuronidation pathway. This pathway is not commonly explored. However, it might have critical impact for drugs with narrow therapeutic range such as tacrolimus. We provided consistent evidences from two independent studies that direct glucuronide of tacrolimus formation might be a cause on concern. On the one hand it interferes with parent drug during LC-MS/MS analysis and on the other hand, one should not underestimate the potential role of glucuronide on the drug pharmacokinetic profile. Therefore, the role of direct glucuronide of tacrolimus should be further investigated.
