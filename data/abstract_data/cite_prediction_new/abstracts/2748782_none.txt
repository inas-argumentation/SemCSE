The Use of Metatarsal Bone Graft in the Treatment of Saddle Nose Deformities
We always find it interesting as readers of scientific journals in plastic surgery to read about surgical techniques used by plastic surgeons around the world. The procedure the author suggests for correction of depressed noses is interesting. In the 1950s and 1960s I ended up using bone grafts to reconstruct and improve the nasal dorsum. Initially, I harvested grafts from the iliac crest. Later, at the suggestion of the craniofacial plastic surgeons, I also used bone grafts taken from the external surface of the temporal and parietal bones. I always found it difficult to carve the bone grafts to achieve a good and perfect aesthetic result. Also at the end of the 1950s, I began to use bone grafts harvested from the posterior border of the ulnar bone near the olecranon because it has a form very similar to that of the nasal dorsum. Dr. Barragan, a plastic surgeon in our service also used the same procedure some years later. I stopped using bone grafts in nasal repairs because of reports in the plastic surgery literature and at plastic surgery congresses on significant absorption of the bone grafts. Also, in some of my patients I observed a high level of absorption in the nasal dorsum after 15 to 20 years. I have many years of experience using autologous cartilage grafts for reconstruction of the nasal dorsum. Initially, I used only cartilage grafts, but later, I had great success using cartilage grafts wrapped with deep temporal fascia [1,2]. By carving cartilage grafts, I obtained very good aesthetic results without the notorious sharp borders that unfortunately developed with bone grafts. I have many patients who have undergone these techniques using cartilage grafts with excellent evolution and minimal absorption. I comment on the author s assertion that the bone graft from the fourth metatarsal bone of the foot proves insufficient to correct the nasal dorsum appropriately. In his experience, depression was corrected only partially, and I certainly think that in future cases, he will need to take almost the whole fourth metatarsal bone, which would cause a fracture of the donor bone. With my concept, the functionality of the donor foot is annoying for several days. I agree totally with the author s idea of giving preference to autologous grafts instead of foreign implants that frequently can cause foreign reactions and develop into complications and secondary deformities. I congratulate the author on his efforts to avoid complacency in plastic surgery used for nasal dorsum rehabilitation.
