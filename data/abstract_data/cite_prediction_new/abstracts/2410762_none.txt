Problems for Online Bayesian Inference in Neural Networks
Recently, a number of approaches have been developed which allow for approximate Bayesian inference over the parameters of large neural networks. These algorithms can be more robust against overfitting, and can provide useful estimates of model uncertainty. In domains such as active learning Golovin et al. (2010) and Bayesian reinforcement learning (Vlassis et al., 2012), an ideal learner needs to not only estimate the posterior given the current training data, but also needs to reason about how new observations might affect that posterior, so that new data can be collected as efficiently as possible. We argue that existing batch techniques for Bayesian neural networks are not well suited to such online inference, and in this preliminary work we discuss ways in which these algorithms can be adapted to allow for efficient online inference.
