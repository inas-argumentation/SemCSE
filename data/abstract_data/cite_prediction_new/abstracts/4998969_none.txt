Pragmatic and effective regulation: A Swiss perspective on the regulatory process
The efficient operation of financial markets depends critically on their confidence that market participants operate according to rules and procedures that are fair and effective. As a general principle, regulation should only be maintained and implemented where it is needed in addressing a market failure and where the benefits of a regulation are greater than the cost that the regulation entails. The implementation of this general principle presents many complexities and differs considerably among jurisdictions. This paper describes the regulatory process in Switzerland on the basis of two examples: the adoption and implementation of the Anti-Money Laundering Ordinance of 2002 and the ‘regulatory streamlining exercise’ and how the principles guiding this process are being applied in practice. The objective is to show how the regulatory process can be designed in a way that maximises its efficiency and achieves effective regulation with a minimum of regulatory resources.
