Thin nerves with simple endings containing cholinesterase in striated human eye muscle
THE MORPHOLOGIST is able to demonstrate a multitude of nerve endings in extraocular muscles, but has difficulty in determining the nature and function of these nerve endings. In order to understand more definitely the physiology and possible pathophysiology of nerve endings, a combination of the Hortega techniquel for staining nerves and the KoelleFriedenwald methodz,3 for staining cholinesterase has been used. The presence or absence of the acetylcholine-cholinesterase system at nerve endings gives some morphologic indication of their function. Both superior rectus muscles of 4 patients without known eye muscle disease were obtained at autopsy within twelve hours after death and fixed in neutral formaldehyde at +4OC. for about six hours. Frozen sections of the muscles were incubated for about sixty minutes at 37°C. and pH 5.3 in a solution of copper glycine and acethylthiocholine as outlined by Coers and WoolfZ as well as by Kupfer.4 The cholinesterase deposits in the tissue cause a localized white precipitation of copperthiocholine. The precipitates of white copperthiocholine crystals are then changed into brown copper sulphide crystals by treatment with ammonium sulphide solution. These black spots (figure 1) represent reliable and permanent histologic proof for the presence of cholinesterase. The Hortega nerve fiber stain1 can be added without difficulty to those sections of muscle that already have been stained for cholinesterase.
