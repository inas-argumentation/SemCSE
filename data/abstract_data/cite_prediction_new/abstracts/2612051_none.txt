Phenylalanine hydroxylase activity in dilute and nondilute strains of mice.
The metabolism of phenylalanine has been studied in dilute and nondilute strains of mice. The enzyme, phenylalanine hydroxylase, which converts phenylalanine to tyrosine was found to have only 50% of normal activity in mice homozygous for the dilute allele ( d ) and 14% of normal activity in mice homozygous for the dilute lethal ( d l ) allele. This decrease in activity was caused by an inhibitor which was found in the particulate fraction of liver homogenates from dilute mice (DBA/1J). An increase in the production of an abnormal metabolite of phenylalanine, identified as phenylacetic acid, paralleled the decrease in tyrosine synthesis. The possible relationship of the constant production of this compound in vivo to the seizures observed in the DBA strain under audiogenic stimuli and the spontaneous seizures which occur in the dilute lethal strain have been discussed.
