On the genetic stability of bacteria to freezing and thawing.
Summary The effects of repeated cycles of freezing to and thawing from −196°C on the behavior of Pseudomonas species have been studied. Between each cycle of freezing and thawing, survivors were tested for their sensitivity to a wide range of antibiotics and sulfonamides. No change in the response of Pseudomonas to freezing and thawing damage or to drugs was observed over the course of 18 cycles of treatment. Freezing at various temperatures between 0°C and −196°C was not found to produce mutations in Escherichia coli try − when the reversion of this auxotroph to the prototroph was used as a test system to investigate the possibility that freezing and thawing procedures result either in mutations or selection. It is suggested that freezing and thawing is not, in itself, mutagenic to bacteria.
