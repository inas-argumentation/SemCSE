Prolonged hematological remission by mitoxantrone and interferon ‐ α‐2b in a child with resistant Philadelphia chromosome‐positive chronic myelogenous leukemia
To the Editor: The use of interferon alpha 2b (IFNa) has resulted in hematological and cytogenetic response in Philadelphia chromosome (Ph)-positive chronic myelogenous leukemia (CML) (1,2,3). As judged by results from in vitro studies, a combination of IFNa with other cytotoxic agents could be more effective (4). Hematological remission in patients with Ph-positive CML has been achieved with mitoxantrone (MIT) and cytarabine (ARA-C). Thereafter, IFNa was used for maintenance therapy. However, cytogenetic response was poor ( 5 ) . Here we report prolonged remission in a child with resistant Ph-positive CML by using MIT and IFNa. An 8-yr-old boy was admitted in June 1990 with a 6-month history of pallor and fatigue. His physical findings were normal except for splenomegaly which extended 17 cm below the costal margin. His hemoglobin was 8 g/dl, platelet count 600 x 109/1, and white blood cell (WBC) count 270 x 109/1 with 10 promyelocytes, 27 % myelocytes, 26% metamyelocytes, 5 % eosinophils, 6% basophils, 23 % polymorphonuclear leukocytes, 3 % lymphocytes. Bone marrow aspiration showed 3% blasts with predominating myeloid cells. A Ph chromosome was found in all metaphases in cytogenetic examination. The leukocyte alkaline phosphatase score was 1.8 (normal: 30150) and fetal hemoglobin was 2 % . The patient was treated with busulphan (BU) in a dose of 2 mg/kg/dl for 3 months. As seen in Fig. 1, the WBC count decreased initially, but increased above the pretreatment value within the next month. Subsequently the patient was treated with adriamycin (1 mg/kg/wk) and ARA-C ( 5 mg/kg twice a week) without a response. Then MIT (8 mg/m2/wk) combined with IFNa ( 5 x lo6 U/m’/d) was started. The WBC count decreased to l l x 109/1 with a concomitant decrease in the size of spleen within a month (Fig. 1). Immature myeloid cells disappeared from peripheral blood. Treatment was continued with IFNa (3 x lo6 U/m’/ daily) and ARA-C (0.3 mg/kg/daily) subcutaneously every other day, for 6 months. However, no cytogenetic response was obtained. During this period WBC count remained below 10 x 109/1 and no toxicity was noted that was related to treatment. Unfortunately his parents discontinued the therapy. However, he has been in a very good condition for more than 20 months after discontinuation of therapy. For the time being he is doing well. On his last visit in February 1993 all his physical findings were normal except for splenomegaly (3 cm below the left costal margin). His hemoglobin was 10 g/dl, platelet count 300 x 109/1, and WBC count 10 x 109/1 with 3 % metamyelocytes, 2% eosinophils, 1 % basophils, 69 % polymorphonuclear leukocytes and 25 % lymphocytes. Chronic phase of CML can be maintained in a benign course with a single agent, such as BU, in most cases, although mean duration of survival of 3-4 yr is not significantly prolonged (6, 7). Hematological remission is obtained in 60430% of patients with Ph-positive CML by using IFNx and 30 % of them also had cytogenetic response (1,2). In resistant patients, there is no alternative therapy except for bone marrow transplantation (6 , 7). Our patient was resistant to previously used agents, but hematological remission was obtained with MIT and IFNa, and maintained with low-dose ARA-C and IFNx combination, even though he is still in a good condition without having received any therapy for more than 20 months. Although cytogenetic response did not occur, at least prolonged maintenance of the chronic phase was achieved.
