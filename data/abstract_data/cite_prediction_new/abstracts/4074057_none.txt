Electromechanical intervals in the healthy newborn.
Summary Four electrocardiograms (16-lead) and four phonocardiograms (minimum of 6 recordings in each of 4 chest positions) were taken on 61 late-clamped infants during the first week of life. Four recordings of systolic blood pressure and 2 determinations of heart volume were obtained at the same time. This permitted serial evaluation of changes in electromechanical intervals (Q-S 1 , Q-S 2 , S 1 -S 2 , Q-S 2 /Q-T), Q-T, Q-T c , per cent duration of electrical systole and diastole, and assessment of the influence on these intervals of various factors. The Q-S 1 , interval showed no change with age. The Q-S 2 , S 1 -S 2 and Q-T intervals decreased during the week but showed no relationship to heart volume. Per cent duration of mechanical systole and diastole showed less variation with the same increments in heart rate on the first day of life than is seen in older individuals. The relationship of these changes in intervals to hemodynamic adaptation to extrauterine life in discussed. These normal values form the basis of a subsequent study on acute experimental changes in blood volume in newborns of comparable age.
