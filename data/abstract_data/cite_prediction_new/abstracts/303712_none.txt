Effect of ligand on chemical modification of proteins. Graphical determinations of dissociation constant and number of essential residues affected by ligand binding.
In the chemical modification of biopolymers, often enzymes, the effect of ligand is frequently studied to determine whether or not the inactivation is due to modification of residues at the ligand-binding (active) site. However, it is generally difficult to solve algebraically the simultaneous differential equations that relate the concentration of molecular species to time in the presence of ligand, since there is a product term for free ligand and free polymer. To overcome this difficulty, a method has been developed to determine quantitatively the dissociation constant for ligand binding to polymer from the initial rates of inactivation and the total (not free) concentration of ligand added. Also, a graphical method for identifying the number of essential residues affected by ligand binding has been developed by considering the probability (fraction) of residues remaining at any time in the absence and presence of ligand. The equations obtained are applicable to many cases, since these methods use quantities that are easily determined, viz. remaining activity and the total number of residues modified. Some analyses of experimental data taken from the literature are presented.
