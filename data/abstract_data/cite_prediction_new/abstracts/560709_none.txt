Ganciclovir therapy for neonates with congenital cytomegalovirus infection
Letter to the editor: We fully support the article and conclusions published in this month’s online European Journal of Pediatrics [3] regarding the use of ganciclovir/valganciclovir (GCV/ VGCV) in neonates. In particular, the proper monitoring of potentially mutagenic drugs is long overdue. Although consultation documents have been produced regarding the licensing of medication in children, there is still little published pharmacokinetic data in the literature for the use of many drugs in children, particularly in the neonatal age group. Indeed, the recently published and revised British National Formulary for Children only provides a recommended dose of GCV for those older than 1 month [2]. It should be noted, however, that the case reports referenced in the above article do not all address the issue of congenital infection and often report unusual presentations of congenital cytomegalovirus, thus making their relevance to the issue of treatment of congenital infection in general of questionable relevance. The randomised study carried out by Kimberlin et al. is the only study of its kind and reported a reduction in hearing deterioration, which was maintained at 1 year of age [1]. Although the higher proportion of premature babies in the control group is noted, the hearing loss in this group of infants would be expected to be permanent and not progressive as found in congenital CMV, thus making it an unlikely bias to the results. Other small case series all report trends in benefit with the use of GCV. Fortunately, the paucity of data for the use of antiviral medication in this age group is being addressed both by European and American initiatives. The US-based Collaborative Antiviral Study Group (CASG) has already completed phase II pharmacokinetic studies of VGCV in neonates, the results of which will hopefully be available in the near future [4]. In addition, a randomised controlled trial evaluating the use of oral VGCV for 6 months compared with 6 weeks is in the final stages of planning.
