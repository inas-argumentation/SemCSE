Humidification techniques in high‐frequency ventilation. A review
High-frequency oscillatory ventilation (HFOV) is brought about by the movement of a piston pump or a diaphragm, causing the gas in the airway to oscillate back and forth, with the fresh gas being supplied by a biasflow between oscillator and patient (3). This method allows conventional heater-humidifiers to be used. A typical clinical application is the set-up designed by Froese et al. (4) for the ventilation of premature infants with respiratory failure. Fresh gas passes a heated cascade humidifier (Bennet) on its way to the port of the infant adapter. The setting of a screw clamp on the exit port in combination with a fine adjustment of the fresh gas flow-rate keeps the mean airway pressure at the fixed value. C02-accumulation in the circuit does not occur if the fresh gas flow does not fall below 8 l/min. According to the study by Poulton & Downs (5) this type of humidifier can adequately humidify this flow of fresh gas.
