The fine structure of the extensor digitorum longus IV muscle of Rana pipiens following calcium removal via EDTA.
Extensor digitorum longus IV muscles from Rana pipiens were examined in the electron microscope following immersion in normal and calcium-free EDTA ringers solutions for 3 hr. The morphologies of these fibers were compared to toe muscle from the contra-lateral control sides fixed without prior immersion in ringers. Muscle from both the control side and the side bathed in normal ringers exhibited similar morphologies in myofibrillae, mitochondria, basement membrane, plasma membrane, T tubules, and sarcoplasmic reticulum. Muscle from calcium-free EDTA ringers showed a loss of a well-defined basement membrane, and an increase in T tubule widths of up to 1300 per cent. These results are discussed in terms of the possible efflux of calcium and concomitant water from the muscle and the role basement membranes may play in normal muscle function.
