Echocardiography-guided alcohol septal ablation for hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy.
A 58-year-old man with mild emphysema and progressive exertional dyspnea underwent transthoracic stress echocardiography, which demonstrated a hypertrophied interventricular septum (IVS) with dynamic left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) obstruction. After several months of medical therapy without improvement, he was referred for contrast echocardiographyguided alcohol septal ablation. Following induction of general anesthesia in the cardiac catheterization suite, a baseline transesophageal echocardiogram was performed. Midesophageal 4-chamber and long-axis views revealed a thickened IVS (2.4 cm at the LVOT) with systolic anterior motion (SAM) of the anterior mitral valve leaflet, generating turbulent flow in the LVOT with a peak pressure gradient of 21 mm Hg and severe mitral regurgitation (MR) (Fig. 1) (see Supplemental Digital Content 1, Video 1, http://links.lww.com/AA/A270; see Appendix for video captions). After baseline coronary angiography, a balloon catheter was guided into the first septal perforator branch of the left anterior descending artery. After balloon inflation, Definity echocardiographic contrast (Lantheus Medical Imaging, North Billerica, MA) was injected into the artery. Transesophageal echocardiography demonstrated enhancement of the right ventricular side of the basal IVS, adjacent to the area of LVOT obstruction (Fig. 2). No contrast was seen in other areas of the myocardium. Two injections of ethanol, totaling 2.5 mL, were then made into the septal perforator artery, approximately 8 and 33 minutes after contrast injection, producing enhancement of the previously identified region of IVS (Fig. 3) (see Supplemental Digital Content 2, Video 2, http://links.lww.com/AA/A272; see Appendix for video captions). Fourteen minutes after the second injection, with hemodynamics similar to the patient’s baseline, Doppler imaging demonstrated an improved LVOT gradient of 8 mm Hg and decreased MR. The patient recovered well and returned home for outpatient follow-up. Alcohol septal ablation, first described in 1995, is an alternative to surgical septal myectomy for hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy (HOCM). Injection of ethanol into a septal perforator artery creates a discrete infarction in the basal IVS. Through reduction in septal thickness and systolic excursion, septal ablation has been shown to decrease LVOT pressure gradients and left ventricular mass index, and to improve New York Heart Association functional class. Because the right bundle branch courses through the right ventricular side of the IVS, an area usually perfused by the first septal perforator artery, the most common procedural complication is right bundle-branch block, with approximately 50% incidence. Myocardial contrast echocardiographic guidance improves the success and safety of alcohol septal ablation, modifying the interventional approach in up to 20% of cases. Commercially available myocardial contrast agents consist of microbubbles of perflutren (octafluoropropane) gas or air encapsulated in a shell of lipid, albumin, or galactose. The difference in acoustic impedance between the gas bubbles (or ethanol) and myocardium generates echocardiographic contrast within the area of injection. For contrast echocardiography during alcohol septal ablation, the ultrasound should be set to a low mechanical index (low power, high frequency) of 0.1 to 0.3. This setting minimizes ultrasonic destruction of contrast bubbles and causes them to undergo nonlinear oscillations, which reflect both fundamental and harmonic ultrasound frequencies back to the transducer. This enhances the bubbles’ echocardiographic distinction from myocardium, which reflects primarily the fundamental frequency. Before the procedure is begun, a complete baseline echocardiogram should be performed. The IVS thickness should be measured during diastole in the midesophageal long-axis view, at the point of mitral leaflet contact or maximal LVOT flow obstruction. After injection of contrast, midesophageal 4-chamber, 2-chamber, and long-axis views, and transgastric short-axis views, should be obtained to ensure that the artery selectively perfuses the basal IVS, ideally adjacent to the area of maximal LVOT obstruction. Enhancement of other areas of myocardium necessitates selection of an alternative target vessel or termination of the procedure. In ambiguous cases, sideby-side comparison with the baseline images is essential. During ethanol administration, and immediately after, midesophageal and transgastric imaging is again performed to exclude injection into areas other than the septal hypertrophy. Ethanol is incrementally injected until the septal region of interest is adequately opacified on echocardiography. Typically, 1 to 1.3 mL ethanol is required per 10-mm thickness of IVS. Postintervention, spectral and color-flow Doppler imaging should be used to assess the impact of septal ablation on the LVOT pressure gradient, degree of SAM, and severity of MR.
