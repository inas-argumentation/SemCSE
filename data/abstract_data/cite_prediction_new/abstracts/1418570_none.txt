Doctor—Patient and Doctor—Patient—Society Relationships after Health Care Reform
Health care reform in the United States is imminent. Public anxieties over access to care as well as the cost and the quality of that care have resulted in a Presidential mandate for change that will be difficult if not impossible to disregard. Although the details of the reform plan will be debated for many months, it is all but certain that fundamental changes in major aspects of the health care system will occur. The relationship between the physician and the patient is one vital component of health care that will be affected by a new national health care plan. This alliance, a bedrock of the health care delivery system, is immensely complex. Because the patient is dependent upon the physician in a time of need and cannot fully assess the quality of the service that is delivered, that relationship is intrinsically asymmetric, with the physician assuming a fiduciary role. Thus, it not only assumes therapeutic and humanitarian values, but also carries legal and ethical implications. In this report, we will consider one cause for change in the doctor-patient relation: the expanded role of societal needs. However, before considering the impact of health care reform, it is important to recognize that significant problems with the physician-patient relation are not new. Koos' in 1955 reported that 64% of patients were dissatisfied with their doctor-patient relationship. Blendon et reported that, in 1990,26% of patients respected their physicians less than they did one decade earlier, and 50% reported that they felt that doctors cared less about their patients than previously. Some of the causes for this deterioration do reflect changes in the health care delivery system. However, others do not. One recent infringement in the doctor-patient relation-restriction of counselling patients about abortion as a method of family planning-was unrelated to health care reform.' Thus, whatever the impacts of health care reform are, they will occur within the context of a relationship already troubled by many other potent forces.
