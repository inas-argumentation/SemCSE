Risk models for mechanical circulatory support in children.
The authors are to be commended on developing a therapeutic intensity score (TIS) for pediatric MCS patients. Outcomes studies in pediatric mechanical circulatory support (MCS) have been challenged by small numbers and few devices. The multi-institutional analysis by Blume and colleagues from the Pediatric Heart Transplant Study in 2006 emphasized the inferior survival among children supported with non-pediatric devices. After the introduction of the Berlin Heart Excor in North America in 2000, this durable pediatric-specific extracorporeal pump became a standard component of the pre-transplant armamentarium. Although tremendous progress has accompanied its increasing application, certain anatomic sub-sets (in particular, single-ventricle physiology, the Fontan circulation and small infants) remain a formidable challenge. A few risk factor analyses were available, but the development of a national database to examine outcomes for all pediatric devices would await the creation of PediMACs, the pediatric component of the National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute–sponsored INTERMACS database. Risk factor modeling will be an integral component of this registry, but few risk-adjusted outcomes data are currently available. Thus, any innovative predictive models in the realm of pediatric MCS are potentially important if they generate risk/resource stratification that facilitates clinical decisionmaking. Given the rapid evolution of this field and limited national experience, institution-specific algorithms and protocols have provided the basis for most decision areas. May and colleagues have developed a novel scoring system based on the intensity of medical therapy during the hospitalization for ventricular assist device implant. Unfortunately, because the study purpose was the description and validation of a tool (the TIS), the reader is left wondering about its ultimate application.
