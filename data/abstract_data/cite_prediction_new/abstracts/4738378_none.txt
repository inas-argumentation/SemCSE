by Exogenous or Endogenous Nitric Oxide Regulation of CD8 Expression in Mast Cells
We recently reported a novel CD8 molecule on rat alveolar macrophages and peritoneal mast cells (PMC). However, little is known about the regulation of CD8 expression and function on these cells. We investigated the regulation of CD8 expression on PMC by NO, because NO can regulate inﬂammatory responses and also because anti-CD8 Ab stimulates inducible NO synthase and NO production by PMC and alveolar macrophages. Ligation of CD8 (cid:1) on PMC with Ab (OX8) induced CD8 (cid:1) mRNA expression after 3–6 h, and ﬂow cytometry demonstrated that OX8 treatment increased CD8 (cid:1) protein expression compared with PMC treated with isotype control IgG1. To test whether NO mediates the up-regulation of CD8 (cid:1) , we used the NO donor S -nitrosoglutathione (500 (cid:2) M) and NO synthase inhibitors ( N G -monomethyl- L -arginine and N G -nitro- L -arginine methyl ester; 100 (cid:2) M). S -nitrosoglu-tathione up-regulated both mRNA and protein expression of CD8 (cid:1) in PMC compared with that in sham-treated cells, while NO synthase inhibitors down-regulated OX8 Ab-induced CD8 (cid:1) expression. To investigate how NO regulates CD8 expression on PMC, we examined the cGMP-dependent pathway using 8-bromo-cGMP (2 mM) and the guanylate cyclase inhibitor, 1H-oxadiazolo-quinoxalin-1-one (20 (cid:2) M). 8-Bromo-cGMP up-regulated CD8 expression, whereas 1H-oxadiazoloquinoxalin-1-one down-regu-lated CD8 CD8 expression mediator secretion, natural ligation of form of CD8.
