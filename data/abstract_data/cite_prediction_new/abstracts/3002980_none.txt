McSOCIAL WORK — PROFESSIONAL DECADENCE IN THE UNITED STATES
Professional social work in the United States is too often envied and too little understood. Its many failures of organisation, scholarship and purpose should encourage other nations to look to their own experiences in developing professions of helping. The process of professionalising social services in the US has been impeded by widespread hostility to poorer and lower status citizens. Yet social work itself is complicit with these attitudes, making free institutional choices that benefit the practitioner at the expense of the service recipient; by failing to develop a credible scholarship of service outcomes; by ignoring objective credentials in deference to implicit "experience"; by protecting mediocrity at the expense of quality; and by promoting psychological and individual explanations over structural explanations of social failure. Mortality may be the most hopeful vehicle of reform.
