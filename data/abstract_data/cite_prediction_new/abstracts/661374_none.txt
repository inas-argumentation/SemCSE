A tutorial on how to optimize transradial success by avoiding obstacles
I n recent years, many interventionists have taken up arms to join the so-called radial revolution. Potentially offering decreased bleeding complications, increased patient preference, improved quality of life, and decreased hospital costs, transradial access is an attractive alternative to transfemoral access.1-4 However, transradial access does carry some unique challenges. By highlighting these obstacles to transradial access and potentials for failure, we hope to provide insight on how to succeed at transradial interventions. So, when should you not use transradial access?
