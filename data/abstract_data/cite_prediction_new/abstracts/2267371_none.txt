Severe rhabdomyolysis secondary to simvastatin and acenocoumarol.
A 69-year-old man was admitted to our hospital due to muscular pain in his legs. He had a history of hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and hypercholesterolemia. In 1991, he had received a heart allotransplant in our hospital due to advanced ischemic heart disease and he had been taking immunosuppressive drugs ever since. In 2004, the patient was diagnosed as having non-Hodgkin's lymphoma and was treated with chemotherapy, after which he remained free of disease. He also had peripheral vascular disease. Three weeks before admission, the patient was found to have a lung thromboembolism, and therapy with acenocoumarol was initiated. His complete list of medications now included diltiazem, repaglinide, simvastatin, cyclosporin, azathioprine, pentoxifylline, pantoprazole, and acenocoumarol. Two days before admission, the patient started feeling weakness and intense pain in his thighs, which interfered with his walking. The patient denied experiencing trauma, doing any strenuous exercise, having a fever, or having a history of substance abuse. He had never before suffered similar symptoms. His physical examination was normal and, on neurological examination, weakness and pain in the legs was noted. Strength was 4/5 in both quadriceps muscles.
