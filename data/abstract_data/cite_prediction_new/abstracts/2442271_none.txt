The effect of estradiol-17 beta-monopalmitate on yields and quality of chicken roasters.
THE primary object of estrogen treatment of poultry is improvement of carcass quality. Estrogen treatment has at least some influence on each component of carcass quality, and for the most part the influence is favorable. Increases in subcutaneous fat improve the appearance of the dressed carcass, and muscle fat, important for juiciness, is consistently increased. The improved appearance of the bird generally results in a better grade, thus yielding greater economic returns to the producer. Estradiol-17 beta-monopalmitate was approved by the Food and Drug Administration for use in fattening and improving the finish of chickens June 6, 1964. The objectives of these studies were to determine the effects of estradiol-17 beta-monopalmitate on chicken roasters with respect to: 1. Growth rate and feed efficiency. 2. Dressing percentage. 3.
