Upper limit to the thermal coefficient of the refractive index of fused silica at 184.9 nm.
An imaging XUV telescope, the Wide Field Camera (WFC), is being built by a consortium of U.K. x-ray astronomy groups and will be launched into orbit on the German Röntgen satellite (ROSAT). In-flight spatial calibration of the uniformity of the focal plane of the telescope is provided by an UV calibration device shown schematically in Fig. 1. This device uses the 184.9-nm emission from a mercury lamp with an interference filter to remove the other unwanted mercury lines. It projects, from a mounting locked to the axis of the mirrors, a rectangular array of illuminated spots, ~100 μm in diameter and 5 mm apart, through a system of six Spectrosil A lenses onto a detector which lies in the focal plane. Apparent changes in the absolute and relative posi­ tions of these spots will allow the axial alignment of the mirror system and linearity of response of the charge amplifi­ ers on the detector to be monitored. In the course of testing this system to assess thermally induced defocusing effects over the expected operational temperature range in orbit, an upper limit to the thermal coefficient of refractive index of fused silica (Spectrosil A) was obtained. Measurements were made at two temperatures, T = 19.5 ± l°C and T = 30 ± l°C, and data were obtained for various separations between the detector and lens system straddling but close to the nominal focal length. Individual spot widths on the detector were measured by projecting the counts per pixel in each spot onto a plane perpendicular to the detector coordinate system to obtain a 1-D plot of intensity against distance. A constant background was subtracted and the rms width of the projection calculated. The widths in four projections were calculated for each spot.
