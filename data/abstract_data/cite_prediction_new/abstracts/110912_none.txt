STREAM ACIDIFICATION IN SINGAPORE AND ITS EFFECT ON LOTIC
Acidification is one of the major problems concerning freshwaters in the world today. Acidification of freshwater systems can be caused by natural and anthropogenic activities such as dissociation of carbonic and humic acids and industrial emissions of acidifying gases. Acidified streams are characterised by reduced density and species richness, potentially resulting in major loss of biodiversity. A survey was made to establish a baseline study of freshwater macroinvertebrates families in forested streams in Singapore. The variation in water chemistry, habitat and macroinvertebrate communities in acidic streams were investigated and compared with those in circumneutral streams. Acidity of streams is thought to be affected by the geology of its catchment, with the granite-based Bukit Timah high more susceptible to acidification. Three streams in the Bukit Timah area were sampled. Replicate (n=3) physicochemical, habitat variables and biological sampling were done at three reaches per stream. Macroinvertebrate samples were processed and diversity, richness and abundance were then calculated and compared. Conductivity, pH and most habitat variables were found to differ among and within the streams. On the other hand, macroinvertebrate diversity, richness and abundance were comparable. However, there seems to be a change in the composition of macroinvertebrates that made up each stream.
