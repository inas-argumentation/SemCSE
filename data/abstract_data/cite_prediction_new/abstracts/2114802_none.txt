Response to Letter to the Editor by T. Matys Grygar, 2015 Assessment of the contamination of riparian soil and vegetation by trace metals - A Danube River case study.
On behalf of myself and co-authors I appreciate the interest shown by T.Matys Grygar in our article “Assessment of the contamination of riparian soil and vegetation by tracemetals— A Danube River case study” (Pavlović et al., 2015). We agree with his statement that, in spite of intensive work in the past, the study of fluvial pollution on a larger spatial scale still remains a challenge. Indeed, the DanubeRiver Basin (DRB) is a complex system which requires a multidisciplinary approach to research. Naturally, researchers have their own point of view and data on the pollution of riverine ecosystems, including the riparian zone, depending on the scientific discipline. Hence, his particular discussion is mainly focused on the lack of geochemical mapping for the entire Danube River catchment area in the article. The main purpose of our article was to discuss the total levels of eight trace elements (As, Cd, Cr, Cu, Hg, Ni, Pb and Zn) in riparian soils and vegetation based on a longitudinal transect of the Danube during the international Joint Danube Survey 3 expedition JDS3 (ICPDR, 2015). These elements were also selected for analysis during previous Danube Surveys (Liška et al., 2008) because they are on the List of Important Contaminants for theDRB, as agreed by the International Commission for the Protection of the Danube River (ICPDR https://www.icpdr.org/main/issues/hazardous-substances). These pollutants have been selected by ICPDR expert groups based on analyses of respective datasets on thewater quality available in the ‘DANUBIUS’ database (www.icpdr.org). In this regard, physical, chemical, and selected biological parameters are regularly collected for ‘Danubius’, based on the TransNational Monitoring Network (TNMN operational since 1996 and currently including 111 sites with row data), and uploaded after being checked according to certain procedures. All laboratories responsible for data delivery for the TNMN participate in joint Quality Control, organized by the ICPDR and ‘Danubian’ countries, which ensures the high quality of information,more details of which are available in Chapter 3 (Sommerwerk et al., 2009) of the book ‘Rivers of Europe’, published by Elsevier Academic Press, Amsterdam 2009 and a recently published Springer monograph edited by Liška (2016). We would also like to underline that Joint Danube Surveys have been carefully planned for N15 years and are the result of the work of several expert groups under the coordination of the ICPDR. This planning covers selection of sampling sites, sampling and sample processing methodology, screening parameters, and the form of reporting.
