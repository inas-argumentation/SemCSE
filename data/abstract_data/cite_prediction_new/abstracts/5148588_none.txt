Quinones are widely distributed in nature. They are best known as lipid-soluble components of membrane-bound electrontransport chains, but in animal cells ubiquinone is found not only in the inner mitochondrial membrane but also in the endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, peroxisomes, and in the plasma membrane. This distribution suggests that ubiquinones may be involved in a number of biological processes beyond the respiratory electron transport (ref. 3, 5, 6).
