Delayed Horner's syndrome as a complication of continuous thoracic epidural analgesia.
ORNER'S SYNDROME has been reported as a complication of intermittent thoracic epidural anesthesia in trauma patients 1 and in obstetric patients receiving intermittent and continuous lumbar epidural dosing. 2-3 However, the concentrations of local anesthetics reported to cause the Homer's syndrome (0.5% lidocaine or 0.25% to 0.5% bupivacaine) 1-3 are much higher than those commonly used for postoperative analgesia. 4 In addition, the Homer's syndrome was observed shortly after dosing the epidural catheter. A case is reported of delayed Homer's syndrome occurring postoperatively in a thoracic surgery patient with the use of continuous lowconcentration bupivacaine-fentanyl thoracic epidural analgesia. CASE REPORT The patient was a 19-year-old black woman who underwent a right-sided, muscle-sparing thoracotomy and right upper lobectomy for a 3-cm simple cystic lesion. The procedure was performed under a combined technique using general inhalation anesthesia, a Univent endotracheal tube (Phycon, Tokyo), and thoracic epidural bupivacaine and fentanyl. Before surgery, the epidural catheter was believed to be placed 4 cm into the epidural space through the T6-7 interspace. A test dose of 3 mL of 1.5% lidocaine with 1:200K epinephrine confirmed that the catheter had not been placed intravascularly or intrathecally. The chest wall incision was made between the right fourth and fifth ribs and the resection proceeded without complications. After the operative procedure, the patient was extubated without difficulty and placed on patient-controlled epidural analgesia (PCEA) using 0.05% bupivacaine with 0.0005% fentanyl, 5 lag/mL, for postoperative pain control.
