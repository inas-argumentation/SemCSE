Effect of dietary protein source on zinc absorption and excretion along the alimentary tracts of chicks.
ZINC in soy protein diets is less available than in those containing animal protein (O’Dell and Savage, 1960). Apparently, a three-way interaction among calcium, zinc, and phytate ions forms a complex less available than is formed by any two of the ions alone (O’Dell et al., 1964). The deleterious effects of soy protein can be largely alleviated by autoclaving or addition of certain chelating agents including ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) (Kratzer et al., 1959; O’Dell et al., 1964). In the present report, the effects of dietary protein on zinc absorption throughout the intestinal tracts of growing chicks was investigated. Cerium-144, which is not absorbed by chickens (Mraz et al., 1964) was used as a marker (Chandler and Cragle, 1962).
