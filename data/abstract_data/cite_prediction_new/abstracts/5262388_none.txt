New challenges are “brewing” for the regulation of opiates.
Opioids, including the naturally occurring opiates morphine and codeine, are commonly prescribed medications for the treatment of chronic pain. Opioids for chronic pain are variably effective, since tolerance may develop with extended use. Because of their highly addictive nature and the sensation of euphoria commonly produced, opioids are often abused recreationally. Potential sources of opioids for recreational abuse include the diversion of prescription medications and illegal street production.

The opium poppy plant, Papaver somniferum, is the natural source for the opiates morphine and codeine, both of which produce analgesic effects, as well as thebaine, which is a precursor to synthetic opioids.
