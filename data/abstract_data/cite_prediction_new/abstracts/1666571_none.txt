Polyspermic fertilization in pigs after tubal deposition of excessive numbers of spermatozoa.
The incidence of polyspermy has been examined in mature pigs in relation to the site of deposition of whole semen. Oestrous gilts were inseminated surgically some 12 hours before induced ovulation, semen being introduced into the Fallopian tube on one side of the tract and into the contralateral uterine horn adjacent to the utero-tubal junction as a control. Animals were slaughtered between five hours 30 minutes and ten hours five minutes after completion of ovulation. 
 
 
 
There was no detrimental effect of instilling whole semen directly into the tubal isthmus on the proportion of eggs recovered. Ninety-three per cent of the 145 eggs examined were fertilized. Twenty-nine eggs recovered from nine animals had undergone polyspermic fertilization: 33.8% of the fertilized eggs from the inseminated tubes were polyspermic compared with 5.2% of the eggs from the side of uterine insemination (p < 0.001). Penetration of the vitellus by two spermatozoa constituted the principal anomaly of fertilization, but trispermy, tetraspermy and higher forms were also found. In such eggs, the female and only a single male pronucleus were conjugating. It is concluded that the incidence of polyspermic fertilization in pigs is significantly increased when large numbers of capacitated spermatozoa confront the newly-ovulated eggs. The rate of capacitation of boar sperm has been discussed, as has the fate of polyspermic eggs.
