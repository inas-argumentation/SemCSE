Application of High Speed Cameras for 4D Data Collection in S/TEM
Digital imaging in transmission electron microscope (TEM) has come a long way since 1982, when the first experimental camera based on a charge coupled device (CCD) was reported [1]. Some of the major developments in the past fifteen years include use of complementary metal-oxide semiconductor (CMOS) devices, and subsequently improvements in camera sensitivity, detective quantum efficiency (DQE) and speed. Some of the latest applications of high speed CMOS cameras from in situ experiments, electron tomography (ET) and four dimensional scanning TEM (4D-STEM) diffraction experiments will be presented here.
