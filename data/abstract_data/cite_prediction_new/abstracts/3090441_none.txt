Cost Optimization of High Strength Steel Structures
As part of RFCS project RUOSTE which was completed in 2012-2015, the attainable savings by employing high strength steel (HSS) compared with S355 (considered as regular steel) was assessed. Steel grades up to S960 were included in the study. Comparison of different steel grades was carried out by optimizing selected structures for manufacturing cost and structural weight. Welded beams and columns and welded tubular roof trusses were considered. Hybrid solutions, where different parts of the structures are made different steel grades (e.g. flanges and webs and chords and braces), were included in the comparison. Optimum plate dimensions were determined for welded beams and columns, and shape optimization was carried out for roof trusses. In shape optimization the height of the truss, gaps of welded gap-joints and locations of the joints along the chords were the design variables together with the sizes of the members. Constraints were derived from present Eurocodes including resistance and displacement checks and many requirements for the geometrical entities of tubular joints including eccentricities at the joints. During optimization iterations the global analysis model of the truss based on the exact geometry was updated. The resulting mixed-integer non-linear optimization problems were solved using the particle swarm optimization (PSO) algorithm. Manufacturing costs were calculated based on a generic feature-based cost calculation method including costs of all actions in the workshop. Material and manufacturing costs for the different steel grades were defined by the industrial partners of the project. The results of optimization indicate considerable savings, up to 50 %, in weight when using HSS instead of the regular steel. The cost savings were in the range 0-24 % when using HSS. The least cost savings were obtained for welded Iand box-beams. Significant cost savings were achieved for welded box columns. In tubular trusses the cost optimal solution was the hybrid truss with chords made of S700 and braces made of S355 steel. Warren type trusses without verticals were better than with verticals due to lower manufacturing costs (fewer members). Although the S960 yielded typically more expensive solutions than other steel grades, it resulted in very clear weight savings in all structures. Using S960 steel the weight of the structure could be halved in many cases compared to the S355 grade. The cost benefits of HSS increased when the loading increased in all cases. The results indicate, as well, that the cost optimal structure is not the weight optimal structure. PSO seemed to work adequately in all cases, but the global optimum cannot be guaranteed using this method. In all cases considered, hybrid solutions proved to be very appealing.
