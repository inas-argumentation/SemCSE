The physical chemistry of intestinal absorption of fats.
The concept of the fat absorption mechanism thirty years ago when my association with J. H. Schulman began was based on the belief that dietary triglyceride was rapidly and completely hydrolyzed and only water-soluble end products were absorbed. Resynthesis to triglycetide via intermediate phospholipid was thought to occur in the intestinal cell and the only pathway of entry was the thoracic duct. With Schulman, we were able to show that pancreatic lipolysis was partial and that the monoglyceride formed with long-chain fatty acids and bile salts brought about fine dispersion of the fatty material. The importance of fine dispersion to the fat absorption mechanism was demonstrated by many studies, including demonstration of paraffin absorption. The significance of the interfacial film at the surface of the fat particle to its behavior in the presence of proteins or various enzymes was the subject of pioneer studies. The significance of phosphatidyl choline to the stability of chylomicrons was demonstrated for the first time. The passage of short-chain fatty acids via the portal vein was also indicated. The final solution of the problems of the mechanism of fat absorption will owe much to Schulman's contributions over many years.
