Comparison of the effect on blood gases, ventilation, and perfusion of isoproterenol-phenylephrine and salbutamol aerosols in chronic bronchitis with asthma.
The effects of isoproterenol-phenylephrine were compared with those of salbutamol in 11 patients with chronic bronchitis with asthma and at least 20 per cent reversibility of airways obstruction. Among the variables measured, isoproterenol-phenylephrine produced the following significant changes: rise in arterial oxygen tension (Pa o 2 ) accompanied by a fall in carbon dioxide tension (Pa CO 2 ), increase in CO 2 output, and decrease in dead space-tidal volume ratio. Salbutamol, on the other hand, produced a significant fall in oxygen tension (Pa O 2 ) without change in carbon dioxide tension (Pa CO 2 ). Within the limitations of the indirect Fick CO 2 method, there was no apparent difference in the increase of cardiac output produced by the two preparations. The evidence presented suggests that salbutamol caused deterioration in ventilation-perfusion relationships with resultant increase in hypoxemia due to the redistribution of pulmonary blood flow. In contrast, the absence of increased hypoxemia with isoproterenol-phenylephrine would appear to be due to phenylephrine opposing the vasodilator action of isoproterenol, thus preventing disturbance of the homeostatic mechanism. Falls in arterial oxygen tension may occur with salbutamol but are unlikely after inhalation of isoproterenol-phenylephrine. The present results suggest that, in contrast to salbutamol, for the same degree of bronchodilation the changes in blood gases with isoproterenol-phenylephrine represent an improvement in the physiologic state of the patients studied and may give an additional margin of safety when aerosol bronchodilator therapy is used by an already severely hypoxemic patient.
