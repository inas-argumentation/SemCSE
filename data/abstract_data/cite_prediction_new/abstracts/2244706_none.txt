1 Uniform Exponence : Exemplification and Extension
In this paper we motivate and exemplify instances of the general constraint of Uniform Exponence stated in (1). (1) Uniform Exponence: a lexical item (stem, affix, word) has the same realization for property P in its various contexts of occurrence. It finds plausible psychological motivation on the assumption that words are stored in memory in their surface phonetic form. To the extent that two instances of a given lexical item share the same phonological structure, the amount of space required to store the words in memory is minimized. But this faithfulness relation is in tension with markedness constraints on phonological form. By familiar OT reasoning, a rich array of grammars arises from different rankings of the two classes of faithfulness and markedness constraints. In the traditional generative model the only way in which one word can affect the phonological shape of another word is to embed the derivation of one inside the other-the principle of the cycle (Chomsky & Halle 1968).
