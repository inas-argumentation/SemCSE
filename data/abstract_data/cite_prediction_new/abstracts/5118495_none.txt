Adaptation of the ektacytometer to automated continuous pO2 changes: determination of erythrocyte deformability in sickling disorders.
The ektacytometer is a visco-diffractometric method to quantitate erythrocyte deformability. A continuous-flow system has been added to the basic ektacytometer to record automatically cell deformation as ektacytometer index, (EI) as a continuous function of oxygen tension. Samples from normal donors (AA), donors heterozygous for the sickle cell gene (AS), and homozygotes (SS) were studied with this modified ektacytometer. As expected, the normal red cells showed no change in EI upon deoxygenation. At low pO2, the homozygotes were quite rigid, and their EI rose upon reoxygenation. Erythrocytes from AS subjects, when studied under conditions of physiologic pH and osmolarity, showed essentially normal deformability characteristics. When the osmolarity was raised to 310 and the pH lowered to 7.1, however, the AS cells also became rigid at low pO2. The system was also employed to study the effect of potassium cyanate, a known anti-sickling agent. By increasing hemoglobin-oxygen affinity, this agent shifted the pO2 versus EI curve to the left; thus, for any given pO2, the sickle cell population was more deformable in the presence of potassium cyanate than in its absence.
