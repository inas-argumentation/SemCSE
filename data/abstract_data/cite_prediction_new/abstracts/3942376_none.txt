Transition metal electron paramagnetic resonance related to proteins.
Publisher Summary This chapter deals with electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) studies of metalloproteins. The chapter also offers presentations of EPR theory at levels ranging from complete and rigorous to that for the beginner. Every EPR spectrum can in principle be described by a number of interactions between the unpaired electrons and their environment. Those that contain the importance in the protein chemist are (1) electronic spin interaction with the applied magnetic field, (2) spin–orbit coupling, (3) electronic-spin–electronic-spin interactions, and (4) electronic-spin–nuclear-spin interactions. The EPR spectra of transition metal ions invariably depend on the temperature at which they are recorded. A “good” or proper EPR spectrum depends on what the investigator desires from the spectrum. There are two situations where extremely different instrumental settings are desirable: first to obtain the best possible resolution, and second to obtain the greatest signal intensity.
