Correlation in ultrastructural cryoinjury of mitochondria with aspects of their respiratory function.
Mitochondria in cells of the renal cortex were studied as to comparative cryoinjury induced by methods of freezing and thawing considered to cause slight and extensive damage. Functional integrity of mitochondria was measured in terms of rate of oxygen consumption of tissue slices (QO2), respiratory control ratio and cytochrome oxidase activity. Ultrastructural manifestation of cryoinjury included change in shape, swelling, precipitation and loss of matrix, loss of dense granules, and irregularity and loss of cristae. Results of structure-function evaluation indicated that: (1) Freezing and thawing under conditions described uncoupled mitochondria; (2) mtochondrial physical damage was greater in slices frozen rapidly and thawed slowly than in those slowly frozen and rapidly thawed, as predicted; (3) values for QO2, respiratory control ratio and cytochrome oxidase activity correlated fairly well with this difference in cryoinjury; (4) per cent survival values of QO2 for slow freeze, rapid thaw, and for rapid freeze, slow thaw, respectively, were 20% and 10%; 54% and 43% for respiratory control ratio; and 80% and 53% for cytochrome oxidase activity. This showed greatest damage to tissue QO2 and suggested injury to extramitochondrial factors in respiration as well as to mitochondria.
