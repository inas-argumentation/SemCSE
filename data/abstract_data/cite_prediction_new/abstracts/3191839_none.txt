With the growing number of middle schools being established and the consequent decrease in the number of junior high schools, the need for intentional teacher preparation in middle level education has surged (McEwin, 1992). Indeed as early as 1983, Boyer "found that teachers felt they had not been prepared for teaching effective ly at the middle level and that specialized preparation programs would have been helpful" (cited in McEwin, 1984, p. 115). In the past, teacher education programs have been designed to emphasize content areas (secondary certification) or the teach ing of young children (elementary certifica tion). "Far too many teachers and other mid dle level personnel are simply unprepared for the task of understanding, coping with, and educating early adolescents" (McEwin, 1984, p. 109). Perhaps the middle school phi losophy has been so difficult to "sell" to the public that researchers have given too much attention to the public relations aspect rather than teacher preparation for this age group (Sheppard, 1991). However, teacher preparation pro grams are increasing, and it is critical for the middle level education movement that pro grams already existing be evaluated. According to Sheppard (1991), "Unless we evaluate our own strategies for teaching these middle school classes, we may be developing teachers who 'know', but who do not 'act'" (p. 49). Maryville University, located in subur ban St. Louis, Missouri, created a middle level teacher certification program for both undergraduates and graduates, beginning in the fall of 1990. Faculty members were com mitted to preparing teachers who, in Schon's (1982) words, "reflect IN action" as well as "reflect ON action." Further, the faculty believed that teachers must be makers of their own meaning, not people who mind lessly teach children or young adults with out consciously reflecting intellectually and ethically upon their own beliefs and prac tices.
