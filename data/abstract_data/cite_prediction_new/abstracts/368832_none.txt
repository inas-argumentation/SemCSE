Applicability and benefit of arterial transfer functions
Introduction It is widely recognized that deviating aortic blood pressure parameters (e.g. diastolic, pulse and systolic blood pressure) are important risk factors for cardiovascular disease [1]. However, there are no non-invasive methods to supply these aortic blood pressure characteristics directly. Therefore, worldwide research is aiming to achieve an indirect assessment of the aortic pressure waveform, which subsequently can be evaluated to supply relevant characteristics. A possible option might be the use of arterial transfer functions, which transforms peripheral blood pressure waveforms back to their assumed original aortic shape by correcting for the linear filter properties of the intermediate arterial segment [2,3]. Ideally, the transfer function should be obtained from a large test population to obtain an estimate for the averaged ratio of the spectral characteristics (magnitude and phase) of the aortic and peripheral blood pressure waveforms. The aortic pressure waveform is obtained by a microtip pressure catheter while the peripheral waveform is assessed noninvasively with a tonometer (e.g. at the radial artery). The merit of the concept of arterial transfer functions should follow from an improved estimate of aortic blood pressure waveform characteristics with a small bias and narrow margins of agreement (mean 2 SD, i.e. 95% confidence interval) compared to characteristics derived from the peripheral waveform. The study by Hope et al. [4] performed on 78 subjects, clearly demonstrates that, for some of the characteristics, the bias between peripheral and central waveform has a systematic nature. The use of arterial transfer function reduces this bias but hardly reduces the limits of agreement [4]. The authors conclude that the transfer function might be useful for some characteristics, but that it will not provide additional clinical benefit over the evaluation of the peripheral waveform. These conclusions corroborate the earlier results of Segers et al. [5] concerning the benefit of the radial–carotid transfer function for estimating the carotid augmentation index as a measure of the relative magnitude and timing of peripheral pressure wave reflections.
