Response to roles of EBUS‐TBNA in clinical practice
We appreciate the comments from Dr. Cioffi et al. regarding our study on the roles of endobronchial ultrasound guidedtransbronchial needle aspiration (EBUS-TBNA) in nonsmall cell lung cancer (NSCLC). We wish to thank Dr. Cioffi et al. for their positive response.While we did not list positron emission tomography (PET) in the flowchart of the article entitled “Roles of EBUS-TBNA in NSCLC,” we agree that the positron emission tomography (PET) scan is a fundamental step in the work up of patients with lung cancer. However, the aim of our study was to emphasize the roles of EBUS-TBNA in NSCLC, and, therefore, we did not list PET in the flowchart. In our institute, patients with NSCLC must receive PET before EBUS-TBNA. We have also mentioned in our previous report that PET limits its clinical usefulness for nodal staging in tuberculosis (TB) endemic areas. In our 2011 published report, of 54 lymph nodes with positive PET reactions, 30 lymph nodes were shown as true negative of malignancy; while, among 11 lymph nodes with negative PET reactions, four lymph nodes were shown as true positive malignancy. Therefore, PET had a high false positive (55.6%, 30/54) rate in TB endemic areas. However, when all lymph nodes were checked by EBUS-TBNA, the final false positive rate decreased to 0% (0/22). We also reported that EBUS-TBNA had a role for those patients with negative PET reactions. The nodes with PET negative reactions also had a high false negative (36.4%, 4/11) in the study, however, the false negative rate decreased to 9.1% (1/11) after EBUSTBNA. When all lymph nodes were checked by EBUS-TBNA, the final false negative rate was only 14 % (6/43). We also compiled data on patients with isolated intrathoracic lymphadenopathy who had not been diagnosed by EBUS-TBNA, and listed the final diagnoses in Table 1. Although most lymph nodes were of benign etiology, 9.5% of these lymph nodes were found to be of malignant etiology. Indeed, EBUS-TBNA could facilitate direct diagnosis of mediastinal lymph nodes in TB endemic areas. However, in those patients with a negative finding after EBUS-TBNA, surgical diagnosis should be mandatory, in case of false negative. Finally, we wish to thank Dr. Cioffi et al. once again for their letter and for sharing their experience of EBUS-TBNA in clinical practice.
