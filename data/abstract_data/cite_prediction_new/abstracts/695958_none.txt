Relations between flash onset-, Debye-, and glass transition temperature in flash sintering of oxide nanoparticles
The liquid-film capillary model describes the ultrafast densification kinetics of oxide nanoparticles during flash sintering. The glass transition temperature of the supercooled liquid film dictates the lower bound of the flash sintering temperature in oxides with fragile liquids. This lower bound tends to Debye temperature of the oxide in its crystalline form. The tendency of the two bounds to coincide increases with the decrease in liquid fragility. Spontaneous crystallization of the fragile liquid leads to temperature difference between the lower bound flash onset and Debye temperatures. Fragility of the liquid affects the kinetics and thermodynamics of the flash sintering process.
