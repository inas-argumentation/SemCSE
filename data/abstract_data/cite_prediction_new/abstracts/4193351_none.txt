Cooperative Design: Requirements on Network Technology and Document Architecture
Cooperation is one of the central requirements for design today. Design paradigms like participatory design, concurrent engineering, and total quality management all focus on teamwork. Cooperation takes place at several hierarchical levels, namely the user level, dealing e.g. with CSCW applications, the method level, working on network of documents, and the technical level offering basic communication technolgy. To work optimally each level has to be adjusted with the other levels, i.e. the lower levels have to offer services for the upper ones. Within this paper we derive requirements from cooperative design on the method and on the technical level. Starting with a scenario where the possibilities of cooperation in design are outlined we develop requirements on an architecture of integrated documents and on the technical infrastructure. Central requirement on an integrated document architecture is a stronger emphasis on the structure between documents than on structures within documents. Different kinds of links together with a broader range of functionalities on these links will lead to global, interstructure-oriented documents. Special user interfaces are required to visualize not only a view on one document but to highlight the dependencies between documents. The main demand on network technology is the provision of communication services needed by cooperative applications. These applications typically generate several different data streams, each with specific communication requirements. The actual mixture of streams and the number of participants may change dynamically during a design session. To express specific needs each communication stream has to specify its Quality of Service (QoS) parameters. Current communication architectures are not able to accommodate these requirements appropriately. In the paper, a QoS driven architecture will be introduced.
