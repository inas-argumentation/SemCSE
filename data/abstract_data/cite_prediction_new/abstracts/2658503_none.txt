Acylation of Escherichia coli tRNAtrp with 5-methyltryptophan by E. coli tryptophanyl-tRNA ligase.
1. Purified tRNA Trp from Escherichia coli can be acylated with 5-methyltryptophan by the homologous tryptophanyl-tRNA ligase. The level of charging can reach 900 pmoles per A 260 nm unit, as compared with 1700 pmoles for acylation with tryptophan. The method of synthesis of the analogue, which incorporates 14 C solely in the methyl group, and the use of purified tRNA Trp and Trp-tRNA ligase render artifacts unlikely. 2. The ester linkage between 5-methyltryptophan and the terminal adenosine was confirmed by the formation of 5-methyltryptophanyladenosine or 5-methyltryptophanyladenylate after digestion with pancreatic ribonuclease or snake venom phosphodiesterase, respectively. The isolated 5-methyltryptophanyl-tRNA Trp had a half-life, in 0.2 M Tris-HCl buffer (pH 8.8) only slightly shorter (14 min) than that of tryptophanyl-tRNA Trp (17 min). 3. Charging with 5-methyltryptophan is reduced to 50 % by the presence of tryptophan at a concentration as low as 0.1 % that of the analogue. Such competition and other kinetic results presented suggest a very low affinity of the ligase for the analogue.
