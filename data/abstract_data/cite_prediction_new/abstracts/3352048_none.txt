A Quixotic journey with a fairy tale ending? A brief history of hyperthermia.
Hyperthermia (HT) as a therapeutic option is mentioned in early medical texts from India. However, it is in the early eighties of the last century that the first wave of technology backed HT, which was tested in clinics. There is a vast body of laboratory data as an evidence for the utility of heat in treating cancer. The ability to sterilize hypoxic cells, induce apoptosis, inhibit DNA repair, influence temperature dependent intracellular processes, immune stimulation induction for changes and many other biological rationale, formed a formidable rationale for combining HT with radiation and later with chemotherapy. The late seventy’s and the eighty’s saw a surfeit of papers with mixed results. Interest started waning as clinicians who had hoped for a quantum leap, but witness a modest change. Biology was on the side of scientists but adequate technology was not with clinicians. The modality of HT got restricted to silos in a few countries of Europe and Japan. It was nearly a decade later that HT got approved by insurance agencies in USA. However, remuneration was not attractive to enthuse radiation oncologists. Hence, HT never was popular.
