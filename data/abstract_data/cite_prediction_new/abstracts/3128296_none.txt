Are anticholinergics plus antacids truly as effective as cimetidine plus antacids in the treatment of duodenal ulcer?
Elsewhere in this issue Adami et al report the results of a study comparing the effect of propantheline bromide (Pro-Banthine), 15 mg tid and 30 mg hs, with cimetidine, 200 mg tid and 400 mg hs, in patients with duodenal, pyloric, or prepyloric ulcers; 53 patients had duodenal ulcer and 18 had pyioric or prepyloric ulcers. The anticholinergic dose was adjusted to suppress salivary secretion by at least two thirds. Each patien t was permitted ad libitum use of a potent liquid antacid: 1 ml buffered 5.2 mmol of hydrochloric acid. The average daily antacid intake in each group during the first three weeks was three 15-ml doses/day; equivalent to about 250 mmol of buffering capacity. Endoscopy Was performed before and at 3 weeks. Patients with unhealed ulcers at 3 weeks remained on treatment, and a second endoscopy was performed at 6 weeks. Those that failed to heal at 6 weeks were switched to the alternate medication for an additional 6 weeks. All patients underwent endoscopy at 12 weeks. Ulcer healing was defined as complete disappearance of the ulcer crater without residual inflammation. Pain was recorded throughout the study. A t 3 weeks, 17 of 36 propantheline-treated patients, (47%) had healed their ulcers compared to 22 of 35 (63%) of cimetidine-treated patients; at 6 weeks healing was 86 and 94%, respectively. The differences between treatments at 3 and 6 weeks are reported to not be significant. Therefore, the authors conclude that propantheline plus antacids-is equally effective as cimetidine plus antacids in the treatment of patients with duodenal ulcer or pyloric or prepyloric ulcer.
