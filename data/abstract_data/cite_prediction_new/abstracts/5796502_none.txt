Transformation to Conservation Agriculture Shows Reduced Soil CO2 Emissions and Improved Soil Aggregate Stability in the First Season in Rain-Fed Areas in India
Conservation Agriculture (CA) is capable of improving soil health and ecosystem functions. 17 Soil carbon sequestration is one of the ecosystem processes that is of importance in sustainable land 18 management involving reduction in greenhouse gas emissions and adaptation to climate change. In 19 this study, we wanted to determine, during the first year of the process of establishing a CA cropping 20 system in rain-fed areas in Madhya Pradesh state of India, which soil health indicators show measurable 21 signs of improvement. Four field trials were selected, each comprising two neighboring plots. One plot 22 (15×15 m) was managed conventionally under farmer practice and was tilled before sowing seeds, and 23 in the adjacent plot Conservation Agriculture practices were applied. No mineral fertilizers or pesticides 24 were applied in both treatments. Soil health indicators of soil aggregate stability, soil-atmosphere CO2 25 fluxes, water infiltration, soil moisture, potentially mineralizable nitrogen, soil organic content and bulk 26 density were measured. Results demonstrate that soil CO2 emissions in CA soils decreased and soil 27 aggregates stability improved in the first year. Generally, in CA soils, there were measurable 28 improvements in all soil health indicators but only some of them were statistically significant. 29
