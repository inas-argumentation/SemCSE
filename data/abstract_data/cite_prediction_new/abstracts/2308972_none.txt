Benefits of Regional Cooperation in Providing Balancing Power
Within the European Union’s electricity grid the large penetration of renewable energy increases the need for balancing power. While secondary reserve energy quantities are dispatched in extending international cooperation (International Grid Control Cooperation, IGCC), tertiary energy remains purely nationally organized in Germany. Switzerland and Austria dispose of large amounts of hydropower and pumped storage capacities and thereby cooperation could provide benefits in providing balancing power for Germany and its neighbors. In the area of the secondary reserve the IGCC created significant cost reductions through more efficient dispatches than on a national basis. It stands to reason that such reductions may also be achieved on a joint tertiary reserve market. The paper examines this option by investigating four scenarios of different tertiary reserve market cooperations in a multistep GAMS model: A purely national scenario, two bilateral scenarios in which the German tertiary reserve market is unified with the Swiss or Austrian market respectively to create a common reserve market and finally a one-market scenario in which tertiary reserve energy flows are possible between all three countries in the so-called “Germalpina zone”. Using a cost minimizing approach, a detailed electricity grid and empirical data on reserve energy demand from September 2011, a four step model simulates the process of the reserve market and spot market. Results are promising to lower overall system costs by about 10% in the case of one unified tertiary reserve market called „Germalpina“, which seems to be preferable over the bilateral coalitions.
