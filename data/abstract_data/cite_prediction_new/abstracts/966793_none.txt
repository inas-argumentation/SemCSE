Gastric wall emphysema after endoscopic submucosal tumor biopsy.
A woman presented with an incidental finding of a submucosal tumor in the stomach. Upper gastroinestinal endoscopy revealed a 3-cm, well-circumscribed, submuosal mass on the anterior wall of the antrum (Figure A). Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) confirmed a well-circumscribed heterogeneous hypoechoic lesion that originated in the submucosal layer and measured 2.0 3.0 cm. It had an internal heterogeneous echo pattern with a low echo, which was suggestive of a cystic space, and a high echoic spot, which was suggestive of calcification (Figure B). The EUS findings were not pathognomonic for any type of submucosal tumor. Thus, a mucosal incision, followed by conventional biopsy, was attempted to achieve a definitive pathologic diagnosis. Four milliliters of hypertonic saline was injected beneath the submucosal layer into the top of the elevated lesion, and an incision was made in the mucosa using a needle knife. The overlying mucosa was partially undermined, and the tumor surface thus was exposed (Figure C). The tumor was white and soft. By using conventional biopsy forceps, biopsy of the tumor was performed. We obtained only 1 specimen because there was a moderate amount of bleeding during biopsy. Bleeding was controlled with metallic clips. Initial endoscopy, EUS, mucosal incision, and biopsy were conducted successively without a pause, all of which took 70 minutes.
