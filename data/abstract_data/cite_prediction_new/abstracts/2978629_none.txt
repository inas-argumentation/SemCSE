The endometrial effects of isoflavones: a discussion paper.
In their recent publication, Chandrareddy et al. describe three cases in which the consumption of soyfoods may have led to an assortment of uterine/ endometrial abnormalities, including leiomyoma, severe dysmenorrhea, uterine bleeding, and endometriosis. The authors suggest that isoflavones— a group of diphenolic compounds in soybeans that have a chemical structure similar to estrogen— were responsible for these abnormalities. They go on to emphasize that the effects of long-term use of phytoestrogens on human reproductive health are largely unknown and that only long-term prospective randomized controlled trials (RCT) can establish safety and efficacy. In my view, Chandrareddy et al. presented an overly bleak picture of the current understanding of the health effects of soybean isoflavones. Chandrareddy et al. begin by citing a 2007 Cochrane Review which recently concluded ‘‘there is no evidence of effectiveness in the alleviation of menopausal symptoms with the use of phytoestrogen treatments’’. However, while this is an accurate quote, this review actually found that of the nine trials evaluated that specifically involved soy isoflavone extracts, hot flash frequency and/or severity was significantly reduced in five.
