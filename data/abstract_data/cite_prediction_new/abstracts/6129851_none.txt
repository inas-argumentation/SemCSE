Commentary for “oromandibular and lingual dystonia associated with spinocerebellar ataxia type 8”
In this clinical vignette, Ushe and Perlmutter describe a very unusual patient with oromandibular and lingual dystonia who was ultimately proven to have spinocerebellar ataxia type 8. This vignette is unusual for two reasons: (1) The patient’s presentation was dominated by cranial symptoms with relatively modest ataxia and (2) oromandibular dystonia is a rare phenomenon in the inherited ataxias. Although precedent exists in the literature for unusual presentations of ataxia with similar symptoms (e.g., in ataxia telangiectasia and spinocerebellar ataxia type 1), prominent oromandibular dystonia is usually associated with disorders of the basal ganglia, such as Wilson’s disease, rather than primary disorders of the cerebellum. The investigators make a convincing argument that their patient’s oromandibular movement disorder is linked to the inherited ataxia, because it would be very unusual for two such rare conditions to occur by chance in the same patient. This case adds to the growing list of evidence linking disorders of the cerebellum with dystonia.
