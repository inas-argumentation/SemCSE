Globalization and Risk: A Contingent Response for Democratic Governance
Globalization has made Americans more aware of the role that uncertainty and risk play in our lives, but responses to this awareness vary widely. The analysis examines five prominent perspectives of risk across the social science literature: Ulrich Beck in Sociology, Theodore Lowi in Political Science, Peter Bernstein in Economics, Mary Douglas in Anthropology, and the National Research Council, representing science policy. The conclusion reached is that these perspectives can provide insights into globalization and the myriad ways in which uncertainty and risk manifest themselves, but they are inadequate for developing an adequate response by the public administration community. Turning to a philosopher of science, Ian Hacking, and the philosopher Richard J. Bernstein, the article concludes that the more fallibilistic approach of pragmatists has much to offer the public administration community.
