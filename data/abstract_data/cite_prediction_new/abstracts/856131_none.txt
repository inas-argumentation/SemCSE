Changes in plasma calcium, magnesium, phosphorus and hydroxyproline concentrations in ewes from twelve weeks before until three weeks after lambing.
SUMMARY In 51 Herdwick ewes the mean plasma calcium concentrations reached a minimum three to four weeks before lambing and then increased up to and after lambing. The mean plasma phosphorus concentrations were lower during the last six weeks of pregnancy than earlier in pregnancy but increased after lambing. Plasma magnesium concentrations were higher during the last three weeks of pregnancy but decreased at lambing and for three weeks after lambing. Plasma hydroxyproline concentrations started to increase four weeks before lambing, increased sharply at lambing and remained at this high level for another week. These changes in the blood composition of ewes are contrasted with the changes which occur at similar times in the blood composition of dairy cows.
