mVps34 is Activated by an Acute Bout of Resistance Exercise
Resistance exercise (RE) training results in an increase in muscle mass and force production. Amino acids (AA) taken immediately before or after RE potentiate the effects of training on muscle mass. Therefore a protein that controls AA availability would be of interest in studying muscle growth. One candidate is the class 3 phosphatidylinositol 3OH‐kinase (PI3K) Vps34. Mammalian Vps34 (mVps34) is involved in both autophagy and AA signalling to mTOR (mammalian target of rapamycin) and its downstream target p70 S6 kinase (S6K1). To determine the role of mVps34 in mTOR activation following RE we measured mVps34 and S6K1 activity in the distal hindlimb muscles of rats 30 mins, 3, 6 and 18 hr after acute stimulation. In the tibialis anterior (TA) we found a 366.3±112.1 (p=0.04), 124.7±16 (p=0.01) and 129.2±0.1 (p=0.01) percent increase in S6K1 activity at the 30 min, 3 and 6 hr time points respectively. mVps34 activity increased only at the 3 and 6 hr time point in the TA muscle showing a 100.9±18.2 (p=0.01) and 36±8.8 (p=0.02) percent increase respectively. We have also shown an increase in S6K1 Thr389 phosphorylation and a 43±5.2 (p=0.02) percent increase in mVps34 activity in response to low Dexamethasone in C2C12 cells suggesting regulation through internal AA. We propose that a decrease in [AA] following RE activates mVps34 in order to replenish the AA pool maintaining mTOR activity.
