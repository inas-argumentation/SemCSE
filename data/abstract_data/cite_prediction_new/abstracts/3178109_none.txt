Context-based Re-ranking and Grounding Classification of N-Best Hypotheses
In this project we explore re-ranking of speech recognition N-Best list hypotheses and classification of these into a dialogue system’s grounding categories. We have carried out two experiments with human subjects to investigate their ability to rank and classify recognition hypotheses. In addition, we have carried out a machine learning experiment to classify and rank speech recognition hypotheses automatically by using recognition and contextual information available at runtime in our dialogue system. An evaluation of the ranking task on the same test set shows that both the human subjects and the automatic classifier outperform the baseline (i.e. always choosing the topmost of an N-Best list) and that they perform better and better the more dialogue context is made available. Actually, the automatic classifier performs slightly better than the human subjects on the task of re-ranking and reduces sentence error rate 48% in comparison to the baseline.
