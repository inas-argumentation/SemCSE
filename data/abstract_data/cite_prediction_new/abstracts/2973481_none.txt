Although there were 70 nonfallers (5.8%) who incorrectly reported falling on the 12-month recall, the general classification of fallers or nonfallers has a high sensitivity (77.1%) and specificity (94.2%). Because participants in the Vital D study have been completing the monthly falls calendar for several years, it was possible to ascertain that only 35.7% of the 70 women who incorrectly recalled a fall had sustained a fall in the 3-months before the specific 12-month recall interval. The head-to-head comparison of the falls data poses an unavoidable limitation of the study, because the participants recalling their falls over the previous 12 months had also been posting a monthly record of their falls over the same period. The 22.9% of participants who did not recall their fall(s) is likely to be an underestimate, because the monthly calendar ascertainment would improve their recall of falls. Even with the most rigorous reporting methodology, it is likely that falls are underreported. It has been noted that denial can be a factor in underreporting, because some older people take pride in being nonfallers and ‘‘don’t want to blot their copybook’’ by reporting a fall. Older people can blame external factors for their fall and not count it as a ‘‘true’’ fall. Few studies would have sufficient power to detect differences between several-time fallers and regular fallers. The proportion of nonfaller, one-time faller, or more than one fall was 57.2%, 25.7%, and 17.1%. Asking older women to select from fewer categories provides a reasonable alternative for studies in which intensive ascertainment of falls is not feasible. With the aging of most Western populations, the consequences of injurious falls and their effect on quality of life and the economic burden to the health system continues to grow. It is hoped that, by reporting this head-to-head comparison of prospective ascertainment and 12-month recall, researchers can make informed choices in designing studies that incorporate some falls risk data but in which a more-intensive ascertainment of fall events is not feasible.
