A technique for constructing an umbilicus and a concealed catheterizable stoma
The Mitrofanoff procedure has become well established for constructing a catheterization conduit that provides easy access and a reliable continence mechanism [1]. The cosmetic appearance of the abdomen can be improved by concealing the cutaneous stoma within the umbilicus [1±4], but certain medical conditions may preclude the presence of an umbilicus. For example, patients with bladder exstrophy may require a Mitrofanoff but have no umbilicus. The umbilicus may also be absent in patients with a history of omphalocele or gastroschisis, in ̄ammatory disorders (umbilical sepsis, peri-umbilical necrotizing fasciitis), certain surgical procedures (umbilical herniorhaphy, abdominoplasty, laparotomy), or previous cancer surgery (e.g. malignant cutaneous melanoma, sarcomas) [5]. Furthermore, in some patients the umbilical skin may not be adequate for safe anastomosis to the conduit. All these patients are candidates for umbilical reconstruction. The umbilicus is the only scar with a natural aesthetic appearance. Absence or deformity of the umbilicus may at times even be associated with poor self-image and abnormal emotional behaviour [6,7]. Several techniques for umbilical reconstruction have been described [8±14]. Only one study, in patients with bladder exstrophy, described a technique combining umbilical reconstruction and the Mitrofanoff procedure [15]. Here we describe an alternative technique for umbilical reconstruction, with placement of a concealed continent catheterizable stoma.
