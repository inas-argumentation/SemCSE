An interesting coexistence of Patau syndrome; Spigelian hernia and undescended testes.
Trisomy 13 (Patau syndrome or Trisomy D) is considered an uncommon chromosomal disease. Patau syndrome (PS) is characterized by multiple anomalies, including defects of the cardiac system, central nervous system, eyes, and abdominal wall (omphalocele, umbilical hernia, inguinal hernia), males with cryptorchidism/abnormal scrotum and females with a bicomuate uterus and other malformations (6). Spigelian hernia (SH) is rare among the pediatric population. Among male infants with SH, 60% have associated undescended testes (UTs) (2). This coexistence may be congenital Spigelian-cryptorchidism syndrome.The child was a two-month-old male bom to a 20-year-old G2P2 mother at 39 weeks' gestation from a vaginal delivery. The parents were healthy and not related. The first son was healthy, and the family history was unremarkable. The Apgar scores were 5 and 8 at 1 and 5 min, respectively. The mother had no exposure to teratogens and did not use any medications throughout the pregnancy. On examination, his weight was 2.56 kg (10-25th centile), his length was 46 cm (The PS phenotype typically includes severe gastrointestinal system malformations, such as a displaced and imperforate anus, intestinal lymphangiectasia, malrotation, omphalocele, ulcers, umbilical and inguinal hernia, UTs, Meckel's diverticulum and Prune belly syndrome (4,13). Tsukada et al. reported 7 umbilical hernias, 2 inguinal hernias, and 1 UT in 16 patients with trisomy 13 (13). By contrast, Papp et al. and Hsu and Hou did not observe inguinal hernia, umbilical hernia, or UTs in their patients (4, 8). Despite the great variability of the clinical presentation of PS, the concomitance of PS with a congenital UT and SH has not been previously described among these patients. In the English literature (1950 to 2011), Bilici et al. reported 24 cases less than 15 years of age presenting these two pathologies concomitantly. However, 16 spontaneous SH cases not accompanied by UTs have been reported from 1955 to the present (2). The pathogenesis of SH during childhood remains unclear, and SH can be congenital or acquired (14). Our case had no history of surgery or trauma near the Spigelian regi- on, suggesting that the pathogenesis of SH is congenital in children. A congenital etiology would be due to a defect in the development of the abdominal muscular wall (15). As SH may be confused with an inguinal hernia, particularly in low SH cases, swelling of the lower abdomen and the inguinal area should be carefully evaluated (3). Bilici et al, Rushfeldt et al, Moles Morenilla et al, and Raveenthiran have claimed that the coexistence of SH and an ipsilateral UT might be Spigelian-cryptorchidism syndrome, which is observed in boys (2,7,11, 12). Spigelian hernia is associated with multiple anomalies including micrognathia, cleft palate, malformed left lower limb, meningomyelocele, cryptorchidism, umbilical hernia, inguinal hernia, multiple skeletal anomalies, and congenital diaphragmatic hernia (1, 5), and the combination of SH and a UT may be a component of the syndrome or a new syndrome. …
