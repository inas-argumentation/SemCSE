Differential Access and Utilization of Health Services by Immigrant and Native-Born Children in Working Poor Families in California
Although the proportion of people without health insurance increased gradually nationwide during the 1990s, the rates in California remained higher than in most other states. In 1998, more than 7 million Californians were uninsured, of whom more than 2 million were children.1 Many uninsured children come from poor or near-poor working families who do not have access to employment-based coverage, cannot afford health insurance, choose not to obtain public insurance, or are simply not eligible for public programs.1,2 Among the latter, a large proportion are immigrants. Approximately half of the noncitizens in California were uninsured in 1998.1
