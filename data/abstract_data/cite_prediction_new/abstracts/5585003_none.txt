Bromfenac ophthalmic solution 0.09% as an adjunctive therapy to topical steroids after cataract surgery in pseudoexfoliation syndrome.
Bromfenac ophthalmic solution 0.09% as an adjunctive therapy to topical steroids after cataract surgery in pseudoexfoliation syndrome I read with interest the well-written article by Coassin et al. The authors provide good evidence for the role of bromfenac in reducing anterior segment inflammation in patients with pseudoexfoliation syndrome (PXF) after cataract surgery. I remain more cautious of the posterior segment results, however. As the authors state, the use of nonsteroidal antiinflammatory drops (NSAIDs) after cataract surgery to reduce the incidence of cystoid macular edema (CME) remains contentious. This represented the secondary endpoint of the current study. Power study calculations were based on literature-based anterior chamber flare findings. The relative incidence of postoperative CME is markedly different, however, and to obtain a sample large enough to achieve similar power would require significantly greater numbers. This is likely to affect the clinical relevance of the calculations. Furthermore, as described previously in the literature, steroids work upstream on the phospholipase enzyme and NSAIDs work downstream on the cyclooxygenase pathway. Because the pathway is the same, these drugs provide “additive” not “synergistic” contributions in nature. Group 1 received dexamethasone 0.1% 4 times a day for 2 weeks, representing a total of 56 drops. Group 2 eyes were further provided with bromfenac 0.09% 3 times a day for 2 weeks, suggesting 84 drops in total. It remains possible, therefore, that additional treatment might help explain some of the findings in the current study. Adding 2 extra drops daily of dexamethasone or extending the treatment time for the control group daily might have reached the same result. The attention to sample size and study design is essential to providing clear outcomes in research. The authors are to be commended on bringing an objective measure to postoperative inflammation. An alternative way of presenting their results might also have been this: Eyes with PXF should have an additional NSAID course, or a prolonged course of their steroid drops, to reduce the inflammation associated with PXF after surgery.
