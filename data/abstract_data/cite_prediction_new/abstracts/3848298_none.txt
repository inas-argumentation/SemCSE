Intraocular pressure in patients receiving psychotropic medications.
This study was undertaken to confirm and refine previous work suggesting little clinically significant relationship between psychotropics and increased intraocular pressure with potential subsequent glaucoma. Schiotztonometry in 126 psychiatric patients receiving psychotropics did not demonstrate adverse drug effects on intraocular pressure, even in persons with possible additional risk factors such as moderate or heavy smoking or who were more than 50 years old. The mean intraocular pressure was 13.7 mm Hg, and no patient displayed pressure greater than 24 mm Hg.
