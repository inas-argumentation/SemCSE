Salvage oesophagectomy after local failure of definitive chemoradiotherapy (Br J Surg 2007; 94: 1059–1066)
Sir The article by J. M. Thomas is very interesting considering that it revisits an uncommon malignancy which may pose a formidable surgical challenge. Based on our experience with retroperitoneal tumours at the Tata Memorial Hospital there are a couple of points that we feel merit consideration prior to attempting resection of such tumours. Firstly, regarding the biopsy of these tumours, the author states that a preoperative biopsy is not essential1. We would like to mention that in cases where fibromatosis is being considered as a differential diagnosis, a biopsy is important. Retroperitoneal fibromatosis may be difficult to excise completely due to its location and proximity to major mesenteric vessels. If surgical resection is not feasible, then there are options available such as chemotherapy (Sulindac, Tamoxifen) and preoperative radiotherapy2. Secondly, it is important to try to determine the site of origin of the tumour, though this may be difficult owing to its large size. The epicentre of these tumours may be located either in the supracolic or infracolic compartments, or within the lesser sac or the root of mesentery. There are reports highlighting features that may help in localizing the organ of origin3. Needless to say, complete resection remains the key factor influencing the long-term outcome. However, to achieve this for tumours which arise within the root of mesentery remains very difficult. P. J. Shukla, S. G. Barreto and S. V.
