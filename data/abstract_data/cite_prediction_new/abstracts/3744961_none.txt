New Goals in health technology: annual oration 21st annual scientific meeting of the American Society of Therapeutic Radiologists.
Although American medicine has made great advances in the past few decades, most of which are attributable to technological innovations and developments, there is a growing outcry today for the careful reassessment of biomedical technology. The general feeling—among the public, the Congress and the scientific community—is that a closer relationship must be forged between the output of clinical research and its utilization in the practice of medicine (i.e., technology transfer). This paper traces the genesis of the concept of consensus development—a process whereby recognized experts attempt to reach general agreement or accord as to the soundness and feasibility of new technological interventions. Particular emphasis is paid to the continuing series of consensus development conferences sponsored by the National Institutes of Health and to the NIH component responsible for catalyzing and coordinating such events, the Office for Medical Applications of Research (OMAR).
