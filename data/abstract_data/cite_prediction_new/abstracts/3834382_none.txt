Hepatitis B mother-to-child transmission and infants immunization: we have not come to the end of the story yet
In this issue of Infectious Diseases, Wang and colleagues offer an interesting insight into the mechanisms underlying the lack of immunological response to hepatitis B virus (HBV) vaccination in newborns of HBsAg(þ) mothers [1], who, without proper prophylaxis measures, have a remarkable likelihood to vertically acquire the infection [2]. Mother-to-child transmission (MTCT) of HBV is associated with the highest risk of chronicity (around 95%), when compared with the chronicity rate of horizontally acquired infection acquired in subsequent ages [3]. Therefore, the implementation of adequate preventive measures in this setting is mandatory [4]. As a matter of fact, World Health Organization recommends universal HBV vaccination for all infants, by administering the first dose (of the three scheduled) shortly after birth, possibly within 24 hours [5]. The vaccine is safe and effective, but at least 5% of immunocompetent subjects are classified as ‘non-responders’, being their titers of anti-HBs lower than 10 mIU/ml [6]. How to manage these individuals is matter of debate, because an evidence on the efficacy of booster dosing is lacking [7]. In addition, the majority of studies on the failure of HBV vaccination involve adult subjects [6], often focusing on special categories such as drug users [8], HIV-infected persons [9], patients on haemodialysis [10] or with celiac disease [11]. Studies on immunological response to HBV vaccine among infants are quite heterogeneous. For instance, Chen and colleagues, in a multicentre study conducted in China involving 1063 mother-and-infant pairs, found that infants’ immune response rate as well as their antibody titres were negatively correlated with maternal anti-HBs levels, being lower among newborns of mothers with a titre of neutralizing antibodies 500 IU/L [12]. Another unanswered question in the paediatric setting concerns preterm and low-birth-weight (LBW) infants: what is, for these peculiar subjects, the best schedule as to active immunization against HBV? Although robust studies are lacking, a recent review of literature suggests a 4-dose schedule in order to overcome the unsatisfactory immunological response to current vaccination schemes by preterm and LBW infants [13]. However, the most intriguing issue is the HBV vaccination immunological response among infants born to HBsAg(þ) mothers. In a large American series enrolling 8654 HBsAg( ) newborns of HBsAg(þ) mothers, in the framework of the Enhanced Perinatal Hepatitis B Prevention Program (EPHBPP) conducted from 2008 to 2013, about 8199 (94.7%) infants showed vaccine response after a 3or 4-dose schedule [14].
