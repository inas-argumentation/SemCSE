Dopamine-receptor 2 gene-methylation and gambling behavior in relation to impulsivity
Pathologic gambling (PG) like other non-substance dependencies is strongly related to impulsivity. However, biological research about PG and impulsivity is rare. Impulsivity has been revealed to be associated positively with striatal dopamine D2/D3 receptor availability (Kim et al., 2014). Recent studies showed similar but also conflicting results for impulsivity in patients with PG. Boileau and colleagues described a positive correlation between [11C]raclopride binding (to assess D2/D3 receptor availability) in the dorsal striatum with gambling severity and impulsive behavior, while Clark and colleagues describe a negative correlation between [11C]raclopride binding and mood-related impulsivity in PG (Clark et al., 2012; Boileau et al., 2013). In a previous analysis we found that DNA methylation of the dopamine-receptor 2 (DRD2) gene is associated with pathologic gambling (PG) behavior (Hillemacher et al., 2015). In this study we analyzed impulsivity as a covariate using a general mixed model approach without finding a significant effect on DRD2-gene methylation. However, as impulsivity is one of the core characteristics in PG, a more detailed analysis of a possible interaction between impulsivity, PG and gene methylation seems promising. The present analysis was conducted in the same patients sample to further study a possible influence of impulsivity on the described association between PG and alterations of DRD2 methylation by using a more adequate statistical approach. We hypothesized that in patients with higher impulsivity the described association between PG and DRD2-gene methylation should be more prominent compared to patients with lower impulsivity. The study design and technical details were described in detail recently (Hillemacher et al., 2015). In brief, the present investigation was part of the German Stability of Recovery in Pathological Gamblers (STARPAG) study, a follow-up of the project Pathological Gambling and Epidemiology (PAGE) (Meyer et al., 2015). It was approved by the ethics committees of the University Lübeck and Greifswald, Germany. To assess PG the Gambling Section of the Composite International Diagnostic Interview was used. In a subgroup of 594 participants a more detailed clinical assessment was administered, including the Barrat Impulsivity Scale (BIS-11). We sent buccal swabs to obtain oral mucosa samples to individuals willing to participate in an epigenetic add-on study (N1⁄4129) and received adequate saliva samples from 77 participants. From this subgroup (females: 13; males 64), 41 described no gambling behavior in the last 12 months while 36 does. DNA preparation and bisulfite sequencing followed the formerly described protocol.
