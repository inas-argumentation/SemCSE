Tubuloreticular structures in peripheral blood cells from patients with AIDS-associated Kaposi's sarcoma
The AIDS-associated form of Kaposi's sarcoma (KS) differs clinically from the classical form [7], in that it may pursue a more aggressive course and most commonly affects young adults. KS lesions are often not restricted to the skin and mucous membranes but may extend to lymph nodes, lungs and the gastrointestinal tract. Serologically, there are at least two criteria distinguishing the classical from the AIDS-associated KS. Patients with classical KS have normal ratios of T4/T8 lymphocytes, and generally lack antibody to HTLV-III [2, 11]. In the present paper, we report the presence of tubuloreticular structures (TRS) in the peripheral blood of 3 patients with AIDS-associated KS. In addition, peripheral blood cell samples from 20 healthy control persons were studied. In the 3 patients with AIDS, the diagnosis was confirmed by a positive HTLV-III serology, an inverted T4/T8 cell ratio, the occurrence of opportunistic infections and KS. Immediately after being drawn, all the blood samples were heparinized, then centrifuged, and the resulting pellet of white blood cells was finally processed for electron microscopy using standard methods. By electron microscopy, all three peripheral blood cell preparations from the AIDS patients were seen to contain TRS in the lymphocytes (Fig. 1 a, b) and in the neutrophilic leucocytes (Fig. 2 a, b).
