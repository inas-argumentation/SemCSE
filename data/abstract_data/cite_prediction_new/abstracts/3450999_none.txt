Chemoenzymatic Ubiquitination of Artificial Substrates
The ubiquitin conjugation pathway is one of the central mechanisms regulating the function of eukaryotic cells. During this process, a small conserved protein, ubiquitin (Ub), is covalently attached to cellular proteins through an (iso)peptide bond between its carboxy terminus and amino groups from the target protein. Conjugation involves a multienzyme cascade in which Ub is successively activated to a Ub thioester by ubiquitin-activating enzyme E1, next transferred to a thiol group by the ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme E2, and finally transferred to a protein amino group by ubiquitin ligase E3. Conjugation can be reversed by proteases named deubiquitinating enzymes (DUBs), which cleave the (iso)peptide bond between Ub and the target protein. The tagging of cellular proteins with protein labels is a common regulatory mechanism in the eukaryotic cell : indeed, numerous Ub-like proteins (Ubl) have been discovered that follow pathways similar to Ub conjugation–deconjugation and are implicated in various cellular processes. Understanding Ub (Ubl) conjugation is a formidable task that requires new analytical tools and appropriate Ub (Ubl) molecular probes to decipher multiple protein interactions. The early achievements in Ub chemistry stemmed from the development of DUB substrates and inhibitors as well as the chemoenzymatic synthesis of poly-Ub chains with defined length and linkage. Recently the intein-cloning system has been employed to produce recombinant C-terminal thioesters of Ub and Ubl. 8] Treatment of an active thioester group with a panel of synthetic nucleophiles allows an efficient synthesis of DUB substrates and inhibitors. In the cell, the ubiquityl thioester functionality is generated from ubiquityl carboxylate in an ATP-dependent reaction catalyzed by E1. Interestingly, very early observations from Rose’s laboratory provided evidence that the ubiquityl moiety of the Ub–S-E1 thioester can be transferred onto small thiol molecules such as DTT and glutathione (GSH). In this report, we demonstrate that ubiquityl thioesters produced by E1 catalysis react readily with a cysteine residue to result in the formation of a stable peptide bond (Figure 1A). The implications of this finding for protein chemistry of Ub (Ubl) are shown.
