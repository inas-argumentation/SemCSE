The binding and regulation of biologically active proteins on cellular interfaces: model studies of enzyme adsorption on hydrophobic binding site lattices and biomembranes.
In the interaction of three-dimensional concentrations (i.e, molar concentrations) of a solute with molar concentrations of a protein containing multiple binding sites generally the binding of one solute molecule to the protein is independent of the other. However the interaction of two-dimensional concentrations (i.e., surface concentrations, mol m−2) of an immobilized solute with molar concentrations of a protein gives rise to cooperative protein-surface interactions. Cooperative, multivalent interactions of this type were studied (under conditions of equilibrium and by kinetic methods) in the adsorption of the enzyme phopshorylase b on the surface of agarose gels containing variable surfce concentrations of immobilized butyl residues. The adsorption of phosphorylase b appears to be accompanied by metastable states which are expressed in closed adsorption-desorption loops (adsorption hysteresis). The interactions of phosphorylase b with immobilized butyl residues are compared to those of proteins (e.g., lectins, insulin) binding to cell surface structures. It appears that the complex interactions of these proteins with cell-surface-binding sites (e.g., receptors) of varying surface concentrations may be analyzed in a similar manner as the binding of proteins to alkyl agaroses.
