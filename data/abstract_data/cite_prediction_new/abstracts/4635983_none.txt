Quantitative histochemical studies of changes in the intensity of formaldehyde-induced fluorescence and autofluorescence during storage of tissue sections
SummaryChanges are reported in (a) the fluorescence intensity of specific formaldehydeinduced fluorescence derived from monoamines, and (b) autofluorescence, during storage of tissue sections of the caudate nucleus and cerebral cortex of the rat brain. Storage as ribbons over 13 weeks was accompanied by a marked increase in autofluorescence, while storage as ribbons for 3 days after sectioning was associated with a marked increase in the specific formaldehyde-induced fluorescence of the striatal neuropil, which was prevented by mounting the sections in Entellan.
