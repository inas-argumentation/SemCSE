The Roosting Effect : Insights from Simulated Vampire Bats
In this paper, we present the roosting effect in three simulation experiments. By means of simulation, vampire bats (Desmodus Rotundus) food-sharing, a well-known form of altruistic behavior necessary for the survival of this species, is investigated in the light of ethological evidence concerning roost-switching behavior. We simulate the formation and maintenance of social structures (roosts) from initial populations as a way to preserve altruism from cheaters' exploitation, both in episodic case and under constant mutation. Results show that the roosting effect can cope with sensible mutation levels, and that the most robust size of roosts indicated by our simulations is comparable with the size actually found in nature. We also present a preliminary analisys of connection strategies that can be used to find out hints on the origin of roosting behaviour.
