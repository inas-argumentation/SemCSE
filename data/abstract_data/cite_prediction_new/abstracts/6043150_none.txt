Hormone and amino acid levels in altered nutritional states.
Studies were performed to characterize further the alteration in plasma amino acid, growth hormone, and insulin concentrations by dietary means in normal subjects. In addition, the change in plasma amino acid composition in normal and obese subjects during starvation was compared. The most striking changes occurred during the initial days of dietary deprivations and refeedings. Starvation, even for one day, increased the concentrations of all 3 branched-chain amino acids, α-aminobutyrate (α-ANB), and growth hormone, and decreased that of alanine and insulin. The levels of branched-chain amino acids (66 to 105 per cent increase) and growth hormone (fifteenfold increase) reached a peak value by the second day of starvation. Refeeding promptly lowered the elevated plasma concentrations of all 3 branched-chain amino acids, α-ANB, and growth hormone, and increased that of alanine and insulin. Isocaloric protein-free dieting did not change leucine, isoleucine, and growth hormone levels, but promptly decreased the plasma concentration of, valine, α-ANB, and insulin, and increased that of alanine. Resumption of normal dieting increased valine, α-ANB, and insulin and decreased the alanine levels. Except for a more gradual rise in the concentration of branched-chain amino acids in obesity, the alteration in plasma concentration of amino acids of obese and nonobese subjects did not singificantly differ. These studies suggest that the metabolism of branchedchain amino acids and α-ANB is intimately connected with lipid metabolism. In addition, the metabolic fate of alanine is sensitive to the state of carbohydrate metabolism in the body. A fall in insulin level alone cannot adequately explain the changes in plasma amino acid composition during the dietary deprivations.
