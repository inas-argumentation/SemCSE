A once-a-year intravenous treatment of osteoporosis in postmenopausal women: implications for the clinical nurse specialist.
The Food and Drug Administration approved zoledronic acid (Reclast) for postmenopausal osteoporosis (PMO) in August 2007. This once-a-year intravenous treatment increases bone strength and reduces fractures in the hip, spine, wrists, and arms. After 12 months, a single infusion significantly reduces turnover of bone and improves bone density in PMO. Zoledronic acid was first approved in 2001 and has been used for the treatment of Paget disease and for selected bone conditions in cancer under the name Zometa. Osteoporosis is an increasingly serious health problem with significant economic impact. In the United States alone, low bone mass or osteoporosis results in more than 432,000 hospital admissions, 2.5 million medical office visits, and more than 180,000 nursing home admissions. Behind the numbers are disability, depression, increased mortality, financial burden, and human suffering. Oral bisphosphonatesValendronate (Fosamax), ibandronate (Boniva), and risedronate (Actonel)Vhave been used for years for established PMO. Bisphosphonates reduce hip and nonvertebral fractures by inhibiting osteoclastmediated bone reabsorbtion. However, compliance with weekly or monthly oral dosing remains poor. Nearly half the women fail to continue treatment after 1 year, probably related to gastrointestinal intolerance, poor and variable absorption, low drug bioavailability, and frequent dosing requirements to obtain a treatment benefit. A once-ayear dosing offers significant opportunity to improve treatment adherence and benefits for PMO.
