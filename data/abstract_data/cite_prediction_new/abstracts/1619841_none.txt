Transmission of Salmonella typhimurium in battery-reared chicks.
It was evident from earlier studies that rearing conditions influenced the rate of transmission of salmonellae from infected dayold chicks to penmates. Snoeyenbos et al. (3) reported that ten Salmonella serotypes spread rapidly from infected day-old chicks to penmates reared on new litter; substantially all contact chicks were infected within 1 to 3 weeks. Olesiuk et al. (2) reported that transmission of S. typhimurium from infected day-old chicks to contact penmates was rapid on new litter but very slow on used litter, with only 17 to 65% demonstrated to be infected by 9 or 11 weeks postexposure. Williams (4) stated that "paratyphoid infection, when established in the brooder is rapidly transmitted by inhalation, fecal contamination of feed and water, or direct consumption of fecal matter by young birds." These studies with S. typhimurium were conducted with day-old chicks to determine the effect of battery rearing on transmission of infection.
