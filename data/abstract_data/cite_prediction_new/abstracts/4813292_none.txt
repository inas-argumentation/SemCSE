Adverse association of oesophageal atresia and cleft lip and palate
There were seven male and six female infants whose birthweight ranged from 1340 to 3300 (mean 2416) g. Ten infants had classical oesophageal atresia with distal tracheo-oesophageal fistula, two had atresia with proximal and distal fistulas, and one had isolated atresia. A total of 34 additional associated anomalies were identified (Table 1). According to Spitz risk classification criteria3, there were five infants in group 1, seven in group 2 and one in group 3. One infant with tetralogy of Fallot and bilateral renal dysplasia died without undergoing any surgical procedure. Two infants died from a cardiac anomaly, having undergone cervical oesophagostomy for failed primary anastomosis before the planned replacement procedure.
