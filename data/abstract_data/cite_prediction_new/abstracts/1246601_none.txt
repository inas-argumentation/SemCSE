RE: Futyma et al. use of uroflow parameters in diagnosing an overactive bladder—Back to the drawing board and ICS News 613
Because the ICS publication committee highlighted this publication on the ICS website and also some discussion had been initiated, further comments are indicated. It is common but not helpful when a publication includes uroflowmetry parameters but nothing is said here about the measurement procedure and about the technical aspects such as type of uroflowmeter, the accuracy and any calibration procedures, and if it meets ICS Standard, particularly here with respect to average and maximum flow rate. I would expect a careful control of equipment in a scientific study, as flowmeters are not particularly precise instruments and there are significant differences between the different types. Further, ICS standards emphasize that parameters as printed by the flowmeter need to be verified, and the average and maximum flow rate are least reliable in most common uroflowmeters. There is nothing said about data quality, also nothing is said about the potential value and meaning of such an uroflow index. Finally, I have serious concerns regarding the terminology used in these publications, again referring to the most valuable ICS Standardization.
