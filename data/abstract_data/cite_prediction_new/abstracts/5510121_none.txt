Portal hypertension - Role of transjugular liver biopsy and TIPS.
Dear Editor, 
 
 
We have read, with great interest, the article “Portal Hypertension” published in MJAFI 2012; 68:276-79.1 We would like to offer the following comments. 
 
 
1. 
 
In cases with ascites and deranged bleeding parameters, where conventional liver biopsy is contraindicated, the transjugular route can be employed. We routinely perform transjugular liver biopsies for such patients at this centre. 
 
 
2. 
 
The shunt blockage rate of a TIPSS (Transjugular Intrahepatic Porto Systemic Shunt) procedure was initially high due to the use of bare stents due to leakage of bile into the shunt tract, pseudoendothelisation and ingrowth of liver tissue. However these challenges have been effectively tackled with the advent of PTFE covered stent grafts like Viatorr®. These grafts have improved the primary patency rates which are now around 86–100% and 80–87% at the end of 01 and 02 years respectively (compared to 47% and 19% for bare stents).2,3 In expert hands, TIPSS is a very rewarding procedure that can help in effective management of cases with portal hypertension that have failed on medical management. Cost analysis has shown TIPS to be slightly more cost effective than distal splenorenal shunt in the long term with the procedures being as effective in prevention of variceal re-bleed rates.4 DIPS (Direct Intrahepatic Portosystemic Shunt) is a new procedure developed for cases with absent/occluded hepatic veins. TIPSS is also used as a bridge procedure while the patient waits for a suitable liver donor. 
 
 
3. 
 
Interventional Radiologist can also perform embolisation of the varices during the TIPSS/DIPS procedure to help control intractable UGI bleed.
