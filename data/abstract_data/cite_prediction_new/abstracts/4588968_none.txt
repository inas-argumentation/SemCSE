Impact of optical filter amplitude response on the performance of band-transfer between DD MB-OFDM metro rings
The performance of a direct-detection (DD) single sideband (SSB) multi-band (MB) orthogonal frequency division multiplexing (OFDM) metropolitan network, when optical filters with different amplitude responses are used to perform inter-ring band-transfer, is assessed numerically. A 42.8 Gbit/s 3-band SSB-MB-OFDM signal is considered. Each OFDM band has a bandwidth of 3.57 GHz and a band spacing of 6.25 GHz. Optical band-selectors (BSs) with non-rectangular amplitude response are employed to extract the band to be transferred. In particular, the performances using theoretical 2nd order super-Gaussian (SG) and BSs obtained from experimental data, both with a -3 dB bandwidth of 4.5 GHz, are compared. Additionally, the system performance is assessed when power-leveling is used (in order to compensate for the BS-induced power loss after band selection), and in its absence. When power-leveling is used, the maximum number of inter-ring band-transfers leading to an error vector magnitude degradation of 1 dB (relative to the absence of band-transfers) is 8 and 30, when theoretical 2nd order SG BSs and experimental BSs are employed, respectively. In the absence of power-leveling, a maximum of 1 and 7 inter-ring band-transfers are achieved, employing theoretical 2nd order SG BSs and experimental BSs, respectively. The increase of the maximum number of band-transfers when using the BS obtained from experimental data is due to its wider flat-top amplitude response when compared to the 2nd order SG BS.
