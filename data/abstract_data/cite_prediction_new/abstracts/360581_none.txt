Birth Weight Is Associated With Age at Menarche in US Girls
Observational research suggests that early age at menarche and other menstrual cycle characteristics are considered important risk factors for breast cancer. Endogenous estrogen, especially estradiol concentrations, peak with ovulation, stimulating cell proliferation in the breasts and ovaries. Earlier menarche or menopause at a later age may cause more lifetime exposure to higher estrogen levels, thereby increasing the risk for breast cancer. Birth size is an indicator of fetal growth and may reflect intrauterine exposure to estrogen. The association between birth size and age at menarche has been studied, with inconsistent results. Moreover, prenatal growth stress may lead to metabolic reprogramming in utero. Insulin sensitivity shifts to resistance, so that the fetus can uptake the maximum amount of available energy and nutrients. This metabolic adaptation remains after birth and, in turn, can trigger rapid postnatal growth and subsequently earlier onset of puberty and menarche. To date, limited research has been conducted with nationally representative data. Therefore, we examined the relationship using the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) cycles 2003-2006.
