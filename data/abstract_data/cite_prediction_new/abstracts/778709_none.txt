“WE SAW IT FIRSTHAND, NOT IN A TEXTBOOK!” INTERDISCIPLINARY LEARNING IN HISTORY MUSEUMS: A CASE OF A HIGH SCHOOL WIKI
Little research documents teachers facilitating follow-up activities after museum trips. Moreover, few studies show the potential of linking history to other subjects. This study fills this gap by investigating a post-museum activity—an interdisciplinary online wiki that students co-created to document their trip. As they created the wiki, students used skills from social studies, English, and technology. Experiential learning and history museum education are areas through which two questions are analyzed: (a) What do engaged teenagers learn during an interdisciplinary museum trip? (b) Building on this case, how can teachers design assessments to facilitate robust learning of history from museums? Through qualitative analysis, the researcher found three factors motivating adolescents to learn: choice, wonder, and social interaction. Building on these factors, the wiki was evaluated in order to recommend improving this type of assessment, including aligning it with the College, Career, and Civil Life (C3) Framework (National Council for the Social Studies [NCSS], 2013), using more virtual resources, and promoting critical thinking.
