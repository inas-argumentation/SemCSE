Reply: Comparison of Outcomes for Normal Saline and an Antiseptic Solution for Negative-Pressure Wound Therapy with Instillation.
Reply: Comparison of outcomes for Normal Saline and an Antiseptic Solution for Negative-Pressure Wound Therapy with instillation Sir: We would like to thank Drs. Katechia, Hodgins, and Erdinger for their letter regarding our recent publication on negative-pressure therapy with instillation.1 We agree that maintenance of a seal is an important component to this type of device. This is especially true when combined with the use of fluids as is the case with negative-pressure therapy with instillation. The result of a leak could cause maceration to the surrounding tissue and decrease the effectiveness of this therapy. We appreciate the technique tips as described by Drs. Katechia, Hodgins, and Erdinger; we have used both tincture of benzoin and DuoDERM (Convatec, Flintshire, United Kingdom) independently but not in combination. Thus, we will integrate this technique when needed in difficult-to-seal areas. We routinely use the “window pane” or “door frame” technique, whereby we cover the entire wound surface and the surrounding tissue with a drape, then cut out the portion of the drape that is covering the wound. This technique protects the surrounding tissue and allows the outer layer drape to adhere to the contact layer drape. Regardless of the technique used, it is important to be diligent about its application to ensure good technique. DOI: 10.1097/PRS.0000000000002215
