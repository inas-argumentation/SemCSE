It is possible that bilirubin may compete with propofol in human serum albumin (HSA) and change the free concentration of each other as discussed in the paper. Both bilirubin and propofol can bind to (or be carried by) HSA; however, from the published crystal data, there is no overlap of the binding site for bilirubin and propofol, indicating that there was no binding site competition in this protein to change the free concentration. There are two propofol-binding sites in HSA (1E7A): one is within sub-domain IIIA(B) and another is within IIIB(C) (Fig. 1). There is one binding site for bilirubin (2VUE) (Fig. 2), which is located in a pocket in sub-domain IB. The PDB files were obtained from the protein databank.* The figures are generated using SWISS PDB viewer.w Binding of propofol to HSA yields a K(d) of 65 mM and a stoichiometry of approximately 2, which is consistent with the crystal data, and indicates that HSA has a relatively strong binding to propofol. Even if there are other binding sites in HSA for bilirubin overlapping with propofol sites that are not detected by crystallography (unlikely), they are not strong enough to compete with propofol to change the free concentration of propofol. Although the bilirubin level may not change the free concentration of propofol, the free concentration of propofol may increase significantly if the concentration of the HSA declines significantly in these patients with impaired liver function.
