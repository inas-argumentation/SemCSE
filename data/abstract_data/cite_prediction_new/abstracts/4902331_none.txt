THE BIOCHEMISTRY OF BILE AS A FACTOR IN DETERMINING HOST SPECIFICITY IN INTESTINAL PARASITES, WITH PARTICULAR REFERENCE TO ECHINOCOCCUS GRANULOSUS * , †
The probability that the properties of the alimentary canal could play an important part in determining host specificity of intestinal parasites has long been recognised. These properties have been the subject of a number of reviews (Read, 1950, 1955; von Brand, 1952). It has also been appreciated that those secretions, which play an important role in intestinal physiology, such as bile, pancreatic juice, and succus entericus, could play a part in ‘trigger mechanisms’ involved in hatching of helminth eggs, excystation of protozoan cysts, evagination of cestode scolices, etc.
