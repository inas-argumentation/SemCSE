A 6 × 30 Gb/s Tunable Transmitter PIC With Low RF Crosstalk From an Open-Access InP Foundry
We demonstrate a six-channel tunable optical transmitter photonic integrated circuit fabricated in an open-access indium phosphide foundry process. The device monolithically integrates 20-nm tunable lasers and 30-Gb/s Mach–Zehnder modulators, without the use of Bragg gratings or quantum wells for phase modulation, to form a compact <inline-formula><tex-math notation="LaTeX">${\text{3.9}} \times {\text{4.5 mm}}^{2}$</tex-math></inline-formula> high-density <inline-formula><tex-math notation="LaTeX">$\text{6} \times \text{30}$</tex-math></inline-formula> Gb/s parallel transmitter. It exhibits a capacity density metric of 10 Gb/s/<inline-formula><tex-math notation="LaTeX">$\text{mm}^2$</tex-math></inline-formula>, comparable to the state-of-the-art results from the literature and low <inline-formula><tex-math notation="LaTeX">$< -{\text{50}}$</tex-math></inline-formula> dB inter-channel radio-frequency crosstalk. With an attractive price point of 7 USD/Gb/s, the device can be a low-cost solution for data center interconnects or residential access networks.
