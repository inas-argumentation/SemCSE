Liver and Kidney Function Tests as a Tool for Periodic Health Evaluation of Laboratory Workers
A laboratory can be somewhat loosely defined as "a building or part of a building equipped to conduct scientific experiments, tests, investigations, etc., or to manufacture chemicals, medicines, or the like."' All those employed at such premises are considered for the purposes of this paper as laboratory workers. During the last decades laboratory workers have become a subject of concern for occupational health services due to their probable, but rather poorly documented, association with the excessive development of various malignanciesz4 and adverse reproductive outcome^.^-^ Media interest in alleged clusters of such events in specific institutions contributed significantly to the wide public interest in the subject. While studies on the mortality of laboratory workers and related occupational groups are scarce and not always consistent, investigations of the health status of these populations are almost nonexistent. As a matter of fact, up to now no answer exists as to whether laboratory work affects the health conditions of those engaged in it. This great gray area in occupational health reflects the real-life situations, whereas any epidemiological study of the probable relationships between laboratory work and its potential health effects has to deal with a number of common methodological obstacles, intrinsic to the virtuality of laboratory work.
