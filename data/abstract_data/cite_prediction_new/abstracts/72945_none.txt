Overall, New Urban Renewal greatly advances our understanding of how and why urban neighborhoods change. Both the methods and the results of this study are innovative contributions to the field of urban sociology. Through its multilayered, comparative approach, New Urban Renewal reveals the global, national, and local processes responsible for transforming low-income black neighborhoods into gentrified communities. Moreover, this book helps us to think through what this process of neighborhood transformation illustrates about the racial and class structure of our society. New Urban Renewal combines the human ecology, political economy, and globalization paradigms to enhance our understanding of neighborhood change. It is written with clear, straightforward language that makes it easily accessible to undergraduates and of sufficient theoretical rigor to engage graduate students. New Urban Renewal will be of particular value to courses, scholars, and individuals focused on community development, race and class stratification, and urban politics.
