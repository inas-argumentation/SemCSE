Growth cones and axon trajectories of the earliest descending serotonergic pathway of Xenopus
Development of the earliest descending serotonin-immunoreactive axons in the Xenopus embryo was examined in order to characterize their growth cone morphologies, growth cone and axon contact patterns, axonal trajectories, and other distinctive features. Axons of this pathway first enter the spinal cord at stage 28. Growth cones are small and show a tendency to bear filopodia, rather than lamellipodia. Axons run parallel to the cord axis and are widely dispersed in the lateral marginal zone, with new axons adding dorsally and laterally. Growth cones do not fasciculate along earlier immunoreactive axons. Axons are usually small in diameter (<1 μm) and unmyelinated. They are distributed among unlabeled axons and usually contact glial cell processes. Sectioned growth cones of this pathway are irregular in contour and, with few exceptions, are located in the most peripheral region of the lateral marginal zone, closely contacting glial endfeet or the basal lamina of the neural tube. 
 
Our observations show that growth cones of this pathway are distinctive in morphology and behavior, and that its axons are added according to an orderly pattern. Furthermore, the consistent appearance of growth cones apposed to glial endfeet implies that this association is important for their guidance.
