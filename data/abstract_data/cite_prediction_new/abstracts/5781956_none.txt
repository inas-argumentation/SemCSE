Prospects and Problems of the Transition From Agrarianism to Socialism: The Case of Angola, Guinea-Bissau and Mozambique
The Lusophone countries of Africa have entered upon the path to socialism in a very different fashion from most of the African countries. This paper raises a number of key issues concerning their prospects. It initially investigates the theory and practice of the ‘non- capitalist path’ to socialism, placing it in the context of African socialist thinkers. It then turns to an examination of the political economy of the colonial period of the countries and finally assesses the problems and prospects of the transition to socialism in the countries, drawing some comparisons with China.
