Competencies of the First Dental Graduates from Universiti Sains Islam Malaysia (USIM): Graduates' and Employers' Perceptions
Objective: The present study assessed the perceptions of graduates and employers after six months of practising with regard to three domains of the existing curriculum at Universiti Sains Islam Malaysia (USIM): knowledge, skills and attitudes. Methods: A cross-sectional study was conducted on the first cohort of dental graduates from USIM, as well as their employers, in August 2013. This study employed a questionnaire that was derived from a list of objectives formulated by the General Dental Council (GDC, UK). A set of questions was distributed to the graduates and their employers after 6 months in practice. The survey included 17 objectives, addressing the three domains. The responses were rated according to a five-point Likert scale (1-very poor, 2-poor, 3-average, 4-good, 5-very good). In addition, the USIM dental graduates were also compared to other dental graduates who were under the supervision of the employers. Data were analysed using SPSS 20. Results: Respond rate of the graduates and their employers was 65.5% (n=19). The graduates were rated as demonstrating good levels of performance by more than 80% of their employers for objectives relating to knowledge. However, only 21%26% of the graduates perceived themselves as very good in relation to the knowledge objectives. Over 90% of graduates and employers rated skill being either good or very good. For the attitudinal scores 87% of the graduates rated themselves as good or very good, whereas 90% of the employer rated them as good or very good. Conclusion: This study has shown that the present USIM curriculum, is achieving satisfactory outcomes in the three measured domains; knowledge, skills and attitudes.
