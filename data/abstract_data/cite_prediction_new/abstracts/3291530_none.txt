The effects of guard rows on variety test results of maize
A 2-year field experiment was conducted in south coastal British Columbia to study the effect of shading in single and triple row plots on the testing results for maize hybrids. Seven early maturing and eight late maturing varieties were planted at separate locations in single and three row plots. The four variables studied were whole crop dry matter % (above ground portion), dry matter yield (t/ha), grain content % and % lodging. The precision of dry matter yield estimates for late varieties was higher for means from center rows than from single plots. Since the statistical significance of this result was marginal and precision did not improve for early varieties or for other variables, the practical importance of this is unclear. Differences in dry matter yields among hybrids differed significantly between single and side rows for early varieties but not for late varieties or between single and center rows. It was concluded that proper randomization and orientation of rows would reduce shading effects in single row plots to levels which are unimportant relative to experimental precision.
