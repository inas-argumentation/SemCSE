The role of clinical pharmacology in the development and assessment of drugs for cerebrovascular disease and stroke.
Stroke and associated cerebrovascular disease remains the third commonest cause of death in the developed world and is one of the most important factors contributing to morbidity and health care costs. In recent years there have been far reaching developments in the investigation and management of patients with cerebrovascular diseases. Computed tomography scanning is now widely available and is crucial to diagnose haemorrhage. Other technological developments have improved the accuracy of diagnosis of the causes of stroke and newer insights into pathogenesis have allowed potential approaches to be evaluated for revascularisation or limitation of ischaemic damage. These developments are described in a series of reviews, based on papers presented at a Symposium in Glasgow in July 1991 sponsored by the Clinical Pharmacology Section of the British Pharmacological Society and recently published in the Journal (Alps, 1992; Bullock, 1992; Grosset, 1992; Lees, 1992; Macrae, 1992; McCulloch, 1992).
