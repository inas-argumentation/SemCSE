Spermatophore deposition throughout the day by the plum rust mite, Aculus fockeui
IntroductionEriophyid mites transfer sperm indirectly via spermatophores deposited onleaves and they exhibit sex dissociation (syn. non-pairing). Males depositspermatophores both in the presence and the absence of females (and of theirodours), while females search for spermatophores and, regardless of thepresence of males, pick up sperm from a spermatophore. As shown byobservations of mites and pseudoscorpions, the rate of spermatophore depo-sition by non-pairing males can be inﬂuenced by the presence of females,competitor males or previously deposited spermatophores (Witte 1991; Proctor1992, 1998; Michalska 2000). Moreover, it can also depend on abiotic factorssuch as temperature (Oppedisiano et al. 1995) or a combination of both tem-perature and the presence of females or males (Rutkis 1987 as cited in Witte1991).The aim of my study was to examine whether spermatophore deposition byeriophyid mites changes throughout the day. So far, the impact of time of dayon the reproductive activity of mites has not been investigated, while in insects,the data on this issue relate only to species transferring sperm directly viacopulation (e.g. Hardeland 1972; Zuk 1987; Ziv et al. 1991; Giebultowicz andZdarek 1996). The object of my study was the plum rust mite, Aculus fockeui(Nalepa and Trouessart). It is one of the best known free-living eriophyidmites, easy to rear in a laboratory, and an important pest of plants from thegenus Prunus (Jeppson et al. 1975; Lindquist et al. 1996). Under laboratoryconditions, A. fockeui, similarly to the other eriophyids, Vasates robiniae Na-lepa and Cecidophyopsis hendersoni (Keifer), deposits spermatophores bothduring the day and night (Michalska, unpublished).
