External and internal representations appropriate for ART neural networks
In the design of current neural network models, three degrees of freedom exist: the adaptive learning rule, the firing rate transfer function describing neuron complexity, and the neural network interconnect topology. Once these are arranged into a computing system, a fourth degree -that of representing and interpolating the input signal with the internal knowledge representation -becomes exceedingly important. We wish to emphasize the encoding schemes in this paper; i.e., if there exists an underlying statistical relationship among input signals, the network should find it. However, an inappropriate encoding scheme should not permit discovery of these relationships; rather, the classification would be a side effect of an implicit biasing (representation) function. In this paper we establish, empirically and in an information theoretic sense, the nature of input encoding schemes as applied to Adaptive Resonance Theory (ART) neural network clustering algorithms.
