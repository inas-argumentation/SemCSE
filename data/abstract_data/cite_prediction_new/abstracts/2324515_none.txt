Polypharmacy in palliative care: optimising medications is an ongoing challenge
Quinlivan and colleagues, in their letter, raise the important issue of comorbidity management in patients with complex chronic diseases such as rheumatoid arthritis (RA). Reviewing the records of a sample of patients at their institution, they found significant deficiency in cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk assessment and posit the question of who is responsible. While the number of records surveyed was small, the findings are concordant with other published results of secondary CVD risk management, both within the specific context of RA patients and patients more generally. The question of responsibility, while fundamental to this, is complex, however, and involves both awareness of the problem, and the capacity and resources to act. Following a survey of our patients in 2011 for CVD risk awareness and risk monitoring that showed significant gaps in knowledge (19% of patients being aware of increased CVD risk with RA) and a significant deficiency in CVD risk monitoring, we embarked on a nurse-led education and monitoring programme. The programme, which involved direct nurse to general practitioner communication, proved effective and lifted the comprehensive CVD risk assessment rate from 31% to 60% within 6 months. However, the programme required considerable resource reallocation with 26 min of nursing time per patient review, approximating a 0.3 full-time equivalent nurse salary to cope with the work. The ability to deploy similar resources is clearly nonuniform and has a bearing on the ability/willingness to engage the problem. To gauge the prevailing attitude of Australian rheumatologists in relation to this issue, we conducted a survey of members of the Australian Rheumatology Association. The full results of the survey are to be presented at the forthcoming annual scientific meeting of the Association and submitted for publication in the Internal Medicine Journal. However, it is clear that among Australian rheumatologists there is a high level of awareness of the problem and encouragingly a high level of expressed if varied interest to address the problem. However, the majority felt that the responsibility either rests entirely with the general practitioner (38%) or is a shared responsibility (52%), and only 10% indicated that the responsibility for assessment and management is with the rheumatologist.
