Chemotherapy of Human Nematodes: Learning from the Problems in Sheep
Regular use of chemicals to control infective organisms, including parasites, frequently results in the development of drug resistant populations. In the majority of parasitic species, and especially in gastrointestinal nematodes, it is a matter of when rather than whether resistance will develop. The way chemicals are used in a population of animals or humans will have an important effect on the speed at which resistance emerges. In an untreated population of nematodes, genes for resistance are likely to be present in very small numbers. However, any factor which allows these genes to become more prevalent will enhance the development of resistance. A key issue is what proportion of the next generation of worms derives from those surviving therapy. With occasional or random treatment, worms surviving therapy will make negligible or little overall contribution to environmental contamination, but with mass therapy there is a potential for the rapid development of resistance.
