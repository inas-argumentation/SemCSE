Cell and explant culture of kidney tubular epithelium.
Publisher Summary The chapter discusses the isolation and culture of proximal tubular epithelium from rat and human kidney. Either kidney tubules or kidney cells can be easily isolated from the kidney with the help of enzyme digestion or mechanical sieving. The chapter describes identification of proximal tubule cells and discusses metabolic studies on isolated proximal tubule cells. Energy metabolism of isolated proximal tubule cells are studied by measuring metabolities under anoxic conditions in the presence and absence of glucose for 1, 5, 15, and 30 minute time intervals. Cells suspended in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) containing glucose and prebubbled with oxygen are used for controls at corresponding time intervals. The chapter discusses current methods of preservation of human cadaver kidneys for subsequent transplantation employ simple hypothermic storage, hypothermic pulsatile perfusion, or a combination of the two. Combinations of both types of preservation are used most commonly in clinical practice.
