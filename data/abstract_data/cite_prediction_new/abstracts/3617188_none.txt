Discrimination indices have no predictive value in differential diagnosis of iron deficiency anemia and beta‐thalassemia trait
Sir, We have read with great interest the article by Sirdah et al. (OnlineEarly Articles) published online in your journal which evaluates diagnostic values of different indices in differentiating iron deficiency anemia (IDA) from beta thalassemia minor (B-TT). We intented to compare the results of this study which was performed on an impressive number of patients with recently published three studies including our study (Demir et al., 2002; Beyan, Kaptan & Ifran, 2007; Ntaios et al., 2007). Because, the results in Sirdah et al.’s study stemmed from calculation of the receiver operative characteristic (ROC) curves and the area under the curves, we have calculated Youden index values of every discriminant index according to sensitivity and specifity results reported in study in order to compare with the results of other studies. Although, the area under the ROC curve is the most commonly used global index of diagnostic accuracy, the Youden index is also frequently used in practice (Bewick, Cheek & Ball, 2004). Youden’s index is one way to attempt summarizing test accuracy into a single numeric value and is calculated with the formula (= sensitivity + specificity)1). Youden index values of each discrimination index included in four studies were shown in Table 1. The results of four studies were greatly similar according to these values and the results of these similar studies performed in different populations showed that none of these discrimination indices is satisfactory in discriminating cases with IDA and B-TT. Finally, none of these different formulations are superior to one another in cases with IDA and B-TT. Serum ferritin, serum iron, transferrin saturation and hemoglobin A2 level should be obtained for accurate differential diagnosis of IDA and B-TT until more efficient tools develop.
