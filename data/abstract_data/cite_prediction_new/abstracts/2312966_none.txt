Evaluating Design Tradeoffs in Dual Speed Pipelines
Power reduction in general purpose high performance processors designed for a wide market and wide range of applications is an increasingly daunting challenge. The proposed solutions must be robust and the significant power gains must come at little or no performance impact. This paper proposes a processor with dual speed pipelines. The slow components of the processor can be driven at lower supply voltages and thus present an opportunity to save power. However, slow components also imply lower IPCs. Research has been done to identify “critical” instructions and use the criticality of instructions to selectivel y apply optimizations to improve performance. In this paper we use two of these heuristics to identify critical instructions and schedule them on the fast components to retain processor performance while achieving power savings by dynamically scheduling all other instructions to the slow components. Our preliminary results show that intelligent scheduling can offset a significant part of the performance penalty due to slow components.
