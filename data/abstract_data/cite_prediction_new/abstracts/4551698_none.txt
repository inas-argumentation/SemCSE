First isolation of Acinetobacter johnsonii co-producing PER-2 and OXA-58 β-lactamases.
Multidrug resistance in Acinetobacter baumannii has been described in different countries, but its incidence varies among Acinetobacter nonbaumannii species. In particular, the great majority of Acinetobacter johnsonii isolates were found to be fully susceptible, and few reports have described mechanisms of resistance to antimicrobial agents. The detection of VEB-1 β-lactamase was previously communicated in Argentina, and ISAba-3-OXA-23 and IMP-4 were reported in Ireland and the Philippines, respectively (Boo et al., 2006; Mendes et al., 2009; Poirel et al., 2007). Recently, Zong (2014) has described an A. johnsonii strain harboring blaPER-1, recovered from hospital sewage in western China. To the best of our knowledge, we report the first PER-2 and OXA-58 co-producing Acinetobacter johnsonii clinical isolate. Isolate 2199was recovered fromperitoneal fluid from a 56-year-old female patient with history of renal disease who underwent chronic peritoneal dialysis. She was admitted to our hospital with diagnosis of peritonitis in October 2013. The patient was treated with vancomycin (9 mg/kg) plus ceftazidime (7mg/kg); both antimicrobialswere administered intraperitoneally. After 72 hours of installed treatment, the patient showed signs of recovery, but unfortunately, we lack of records concerning her subsequent evolution. The identification was performed by with matrixassisted laser desorption ionization–time of flight mass spectrometry (3.1 software; Bruker Daltonik, Bremen, Germany). Genomic species identificationwas confirmed bypartial sequencing of the RNApolymeraseβ-subunit (rpoB) gene (Gundi et al.,2009). Susceptibilitywas determined using Vitek2 system (bioMérieux, Marcy-l'Étoile, France) and interpreted according to the clinical breakpoints established by the CLSI (2012). A double-disk synergy test (DDST), which included ceftazidime (CAZ) and clavulanic acid, was performed. Additionally, imipenem andmeropenemMICswere determined using Etest strips (bioMérieux). Detection of the class A β-lactamase genes: blaPER-2, blaGES, and blaCTX-M as well as OXA-type carbapenemases: blaOXA-51, blaOXA-23, blaOXA-58, and blaOXA-24 was performed by standard PCR with previously described specific primers and multiplex PCR, respectively, followed by sequencing (Woodford et al., 2006). The presence of integrons was screened by using specific primers for class 1 and class 2 integrases (Turton et al., 2005). The insertion sequence preceding the blaOXA-58 gene was detected by PCR using the reverse primer of the blaOXA-58 gene and the forward primer of the insertion sequences (ISAba-1, ISAba-2, and ISAba-3) (Turton et al., 2006). The isolate 2199 was identified as A. johnsonii and showed resistance to ampicillin, ampicillin-sulbactam, cephalotin, cefotaxime, ceftazidime, cefepime, gentamicin, ciprofloxacin, and cotrimoxazole but remained susceptible to piperacillin-tazobactam, amikacin, and colistin.
