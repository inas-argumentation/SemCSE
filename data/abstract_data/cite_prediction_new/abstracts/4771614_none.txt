SMA-Positive Fibroma With an Intramural Endometriotic Cyst Mimicking Ovarian Leiomyoma.
To the Editor: Fibroma is the most common pure stromal tumor accounting for 4% of all ovarian malignancies.1 Fibroma may occur at any age, but is most commonly seen in perimenopausal women. In contrast to fibroma, ovarian endometriotic cysts are predominantly seen in reproductive age women. Herein, we report a rare coexistence of ovarian fibroma with an intramural endometriotic cyst in a young woman. A 29-year-old unmarried woman presented with awareness of a mass in the right lumbar region for 4 months. Ultrasound examination and magnetic resonance imaging revealed a large echogenic soft tissue mass in the right adnexa measuring 14.7 12.7 10.2 cm. The right ovary was not visualized separately. Serum CA125 levels were elevated (589.8 IU/mL). Intraoperatively, a mass was seen to be arising from the right ovary, and a normal appearing ovarian parenchyma was seen in the posterior part. Excision of the mass with ovarian reconstruction was performed. Grossly, the tumor was well circumscribed, with a firm, whitish cut surface (Fig. 1A). A cystic area containing chocolate-colored hemorrhagic fluid was also noted. Microscopically, the tumor showed compact fascicles of spindle-shaped cells with bland elongated nuclei with tapering ends (Fig. 1B). Many areas showed collagen bands between tumor cells. Mitosis was infrequent; no areas of necrosis were seen. Cystic areas showed an endometrial lining epithelium with focal mucinous metaplasia (Fig. 1C). The subepithelium showed an endometrial stroma and numerous hemosiderin-laden macrophages (Fig. 1D). On immunohistochemistry, the spindle cells showed positivity for vimentin and smooth muscle actin (SMA) (Fig. 1E); however, they were negative for h-caldesmon (Fig. 1F), desmin (Fig. 1G), estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and CD10.
