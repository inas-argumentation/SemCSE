Complementation between a defective monkey cell-adapting component and human adenoviruses in simian cells.
A defective monkey-adapting component, designated MAC, has been detected in a strain of human adenovirus type 7 capable of replicating in simian cells. MAC does not replicate in simian cells in the absence of helper adenovirus because MAC is dependent upon the adenovirus for the provision of capsid protein. During joint infection of simian cells, both MAC and adenovirus have a latent period of 16 hours and attain maximum titers 40 hours after infection. MAC appeals to be lost upon plaque purification of the adapted adenovirus populations in human cells. The plaque-purified progenies are then unable to replicate or replicate poorly in simian cells unless SV40 is added as helper virus. MAC can be transcapsidated to heterologous types of adenoviruses, a procedure which encases MAC in the protein coat of the heterologous helper adenovirus. The newly formed MAC-adenovirus populations then have the ability to replicate in simian cells; prior to the acquisition of MAC, the parent adenoviruses can replicate in monkey cells only when SV40 is present. MAC does not possess known SV40 determinants, including the tumor antigen marker. The origin of MAC is not known.
