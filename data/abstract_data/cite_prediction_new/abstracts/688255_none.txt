Strategy and tactics of bypass surgery for moyamoya disease
Dear Editor, I have read a Letter to the Editor from Kimura et al. with a great interest. At first, I would like to express my special thanks for their acknowledgement of the value in our recent article on a novel methodology to preserve the middle meningeal artery (MMA) during bypass surgery for moyamoya disease with indocyanine green (ICG) videoangiography [3]. Needless to repeat here, it is a quite important issue to preserve the MMA during bypass surgery for moyamoya disease. In moyamoya disease, the MMA often provides collateral blood flow to the territory of the anterior cerebral artery (ACA), and thus its preservation is crucial, especially when a bypass procedure is targeted at only the territory of the middle cerebral artery (MCA) as they performed for this case. However, the MMA is known to anatomically have a complicated relationship with cranial bone around the lesser wing of the sphenoid, especially in adults [1]. As the first step, therefore, I have developed a novel surgical technique to preserve the MMA during surgery for moyamoya disease, as reported before [1]. Then, as the next step, I introduced ICG videoangiography to visualize the MMA Bbefore^ craniotomy. Interestingly, the visualization of the MMA Bbefore^ craniotomy completely depended on the caliber of the MMA and bone thickness over the MMA [3]. I am really honored to first demonstrate the present results in Acta Neurochirurgica. In their Letter, Kimura et al. have further advanced this concept and proposed the technique to visualize the MMA Bduring^ craniotomy, even in patients with thick bone over the MMA, by thinning it mechanically. I congratulate them on their positive attitude to improve the results of bypass surgery for moyamoya disease. I am sure that such efforts over the world would improve long-term outcome in patients with moyamoya disease. However, I would like to point out other critical issues in this case. First, they operated on the Basymptomatic^ left hemisphere. However, I wonder if they are planning another surgery on the Bsymptomatic^ right one. It is well known that long-term outcome is rather poor in patients with ischemic stroke due to moyamoya disease when they are medically treated. I strongly recommend them to consider another bypass surgery on the Bsymptomatic^ right side, even if the disturbance of cerebral hemodynamics is limited, especially when the moyamoya vessels are well developed. This theory is based on the fact that marked development of moyamoya vessels may contribute to maintain cerebral hemodynamics within normal limits, but carry high potential risk for subsequent hemorrhagic stroke. Second, I am concerned that the surgically revascularized territory is very limited on postoperative external carotid angiography. According to the finding on preoperative angiography, I speculate that the patients can be diagnosed as having advanced stage of moyamoya disease. Unfortunately, I do not know the findings on preoperative blood flow study, but the surgically revascularized area should be more extensive, because moyamoya disease is generally characterized by persistent ischemia in the widespread area fed by the internal carotid artery.
