EFFECTS OF UREA AND SODIUM BICARBONATE NUTRIENT DIGESTION AND RUMINAL CHARACTERISTICS OF DEFAUNATED SHEEP SUPPLEMENTATION OF A HIGH-FIBER DIET ON
Five sheep (average BW 48 kg) with ruminal, duodenal, and ileal cannulas were fed 63% roughage:37% concentrate diets (CP = 14.5%) in a 5 x 5 Latin square design to study effects of urea and sodium bicarbonate supplementation on nutrient digestion and ruminal characteristics of defaunated sheep. Diets were fed twice daily @MI = 1,076 g/d). Defaunation was accomplished with 25-ml doses of alkanate 3SL3hheep daily for 3 d. Control sheep were faunated (Treatment 1) and fed soybean meal as the major N supplement. Remaining sheep were maintained defaunated and fed either the same diet as Treatment 1 (Treatment 2). Treatment 1 with urea replacing 30% of the soybean meal N (Treatment 3), or Treatment 1 with 2% sodium bicarbonate in the diet (Treatment 4). Treatment 5 was a Combination of Treatments 3 and 4. Compared with the faunated control, defaunation decreased (P < .05) total tract DM, OM, NDF, ADF, and CP digestibilities (71.5 vs 69.4, 73.8 vs 71.7, 64.6 vs 61.4, 58.7 vs 55.8, and 74.2 vs 70.6%, respectively) and average (2 to 12 h postfeeding) ruminal fluid ammonia (23.5 vs 13.7 mgl dl) and isobutyrate (.9 vs .7 mM) concentrations. However, defaunation increased (P < .05) linoleic and linolenic acid flows (.58 vs .45 g .17 vs .14 g c18:dd) to and disappearance (SO vs .39 g c18:2/d; .14 vs .ll g c18:3/d) from the small intestine. Urea supplementation increased (P < .05) total tract DM (70.2 vs 68.6%) and OM (72.3 vs 71.0%) digestibilities of defaunated sheep but lowered (P < .05) ruminal fluid isobutyrate concentration (.6 vs .8 mM). Sodium bicarbonate supplementation increased (P < .05) ruminal fluid pH (6.4 vs 6.2), isobutyrate concenaation (.75 vs .60 mM), total tract ADF digestibility (57.6 vs 54.2%), and ruminal NDF (41.6 vs 28.5%), ADF (36.6 vs 22.8%). and CP (-5.5 vs -26.8%) digestibilities in defaunated sheep. Dietary supplementation of urea or sodium bicarbonate increased nutrient digestion by defaunated sheep.
