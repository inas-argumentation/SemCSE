Remodeling after myocardial infarction: An opportunity for early intervention
Ventricular remodeling can be defined as the changes in volume, shape and wall thickness that occur following myocardial infarction. Some of the early changes are adaptive and serve to improve cardiac performance, but only the detrimental aspects of ventricular dilatation will be the focus of this short review. Increasing ventricular volume strongly correlates with a poor outcome (9), being a better predictor for mortality after myocardial infarction than ejection fraction, end-diastolic volume or severity of coronary artery disease. Therapeutic strategies such as achievement of sustained infarct artery patency and treatment with a range of pharmacologic agents have been shown to prevent or reduce dilatation, and use of these therapies has resulted in significant reductions of both morbidity and mortality.
