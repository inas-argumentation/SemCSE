Eight months of continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) decrease tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNFA) in men with obstructive sleep apnea syndrome
ObjectivesThe aim of this study was to assess serum tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNFA) concentrations 8 months of continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) therapy.DesignThis study used prospective, observational clinical trial.PatientsSixty-six patients with newly diagnosed sleep apnea syndrome (12 women, 54 men), age 52.3 ± 9.8 (mean ± SD) with a body mass index of 29.7 ± 4.4 and an apnea-hypopnea index of 39.7 ± 26.8, were studied.InterventionCPAP was administered for a mean of 7.8 ± 1.3 months.Measurements and resultsTNFA concentrations using an ultrasensitive ELISA assay at baseline and follow-up. TNFA decreased in men with high (5.2 ± 1.7 h/night, −0.46 ± 1.1 ng/l, p = 0.001) and with low (2.5 ± 1.0 h/night −0.63 ± 0.77 ng/l, p = 0.001) adherence but not in women. Average number of hours of CPAP use correlated positively with delta TNFA (R2 0.08, p = 0.04)ConclusionLong-term CPAP positively affects TNFA even in men with poor adherence to CPAP.
