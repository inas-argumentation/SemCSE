Good outcome after prolonged ventilation in an infant with cystic fibrosis.
Cystic fibrosis (CF) typically presents in infancy with recurrent respiratory tract infection, malabsorption and failure to thrive. Each winter and spring the high prevalence of respiratory viruses in the infant population brings to light a number of patients in whom there are other serious underlying problems such as immune deficiency, congenital abnormalities of the respiratory tract or CF. Early damage to the respiratory tract, even in otherwise healthy children, can be associated with long term problems and abnormalities of lung function'. The presence of an underlying problem such as CF significantly increases the likelihood of these difficulties and shortens life expectancy2. Despite this, as the respiratory tract is still in a stage ofrapid development, significant recovery can occur, and it should be possible to provide a high quality of life for CF patients for a number ofyears even though they may present with quite severe lung disease in the early years.
