Guillain‐Barré‐like syndrome heralding small‐cell lung cancer
A 69-year-old woman presented with a history of 2 days of rapidly ascending weakness without sensory complaints. Past medical history was only remarkable for a 40-pack-per-year history of smoking. Examination revealed paresis of the arms [Medical Research Council (MRC) grade 4] and legs (MRC grade 3) with total absence deep-tendon reflexes. Sensory examination to light touch and pinprick, vibration, and joint position were normal. Head and neck muscles strength was normal. Routine laboratory data showed no abnormalities. Urinalysis, blood proteins concentrations and serologic determinations (cytomegalovirus, syphilis, Epstein–Barr virus, Brucella, HIV and Borrelia) as well as alpha-feto protein, CA 125, and CEA were either normal or negative. Autoantibodies, such as antigangliosides, antithyroid, anticardiolipin, ANAs, ANCAs, C-reactive protein, and erythrocyte sedimentation rate were also normal. A lumbar puncture showed six white cells and elevated protein content (0.65 g/l), with normal glucose levels and without abnormalities in microbiological cultures or cytology. An electrophysiological examination demonstrated evidence for an acute demyelinating neuropathy. Indeed, multifocal segmental conduction slowing in motor nerves with proximal dispersion and proximal conduction block, impaired sensory responses and reduced recruitment of motor unit potentials on needle EMG study with no active denervation or reinnervation were observed. Spinal MRI showed enhancement of the cauda equina nerve roots. A diagnosis of Guillain-Barré syndrome was suspected, and she was treated with a standard course of human intravenous immunoglobulin 0.4 g/kg/day for five consecutive days, which resulted in minimal improvement. As chest X-ray showed a paratracheal mass, a thoracic CT scan was then performed confirming the presence of a tumour confined to the sixth segment of the right lung, along with adenopathies in right hilium, mediastinum and paratracheal areas. The biopsy confirmed the diagnosis of small-cell lung cancer. Anti-Hu antibodies were detected in serum by indirect immunofluorescence. The patient was then treated with four cycles of carboplatin and etoposide without neurological or general improvement. Specifically, examination revealed paresis of the arms (MRC grade 4) and legs (MRC grade 3), with total absence deeptendon reflexes, throughout the disease course. No sensorial deficit was found. An electrophysiological examination, performed 5 months after the onset of symptoms, remained unchanged. The tumour did not respond to chemotherapy. Finally, she died, 10 months after the onset of picture, from small-cell lung cancer progression. Autopsy was not pursued.
