SOLUBILIZATION OF MYOCARDIAL ADENYL CYCLASE: LOSS OF HORMONE RESPONSIVENESS AND ACTIVATION BY PHOSPHOLIPIDS *
Adenyl cyclase is thought to occupy a central role in mediating the effects of many hormones on their respective target tissues. Attempts to purify the enzyme and to define the nature of the hormone-receptor-enzyme interaction have been made exceedingly difficult because the enzyme is associated with the membranous components of the cell. We recently reported a one-step method for solubilizing almost all of the adenyl cyclase present in cat heart homogenates using a nonionic detergent, Lubrol-PX, an ethylene oxide condensate of dodecanol. I The solubilized enzyme was not precipitated at centrifugal speeds as great as 250,OOOxg for two hours and readily passed through a 0.22 p millipore filter. Sephadex chromatography suggested a molecular weight of 100,000 to 200,000. In addition, the solubilized adenyl cyclase was not activated by the hormones that activate the particulate adenyl cyclase, norepinephrine,' glucagon,". and thyroxine.5 However, interpretation of the latter findings was difficult because the detergent was present and conceivably could have impaired hormone-responsiveness. This report describes a method for removing the detergent from the enzyme enabling more definitive studies to be performed regarding Sephadex chromatography of the enzyme and hormone responsiveness. Furthermore, data are presented demonstrating an activation of the enzyme by phosphatidylserine. phosphatidylethanolamine, and sphingomyelin.
