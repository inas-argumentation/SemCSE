Public Evaluation and Acceptance of Public Enterprise Policies in Japan: Evaluative Clusters, Political Parties, and Residential Areas
In a survey with 814 Japanese people, we attempted explore public evaluation of public enterprise policies, and found 3 dimensions (procedural fairness, priority of local residents, and priority of the government) in the evaluation of decision making and 4 dimensions (benefits, local and global costs, and distributive fairness) in the evaluation of consequences. Cluster analysis divided the respondents into 4 groups: critical, global, positive, and integrative. Although they were generally negative against current public enterprise policies, our finding that the positive and integrative groups occupied two thirds of the present respondents suggests that Japanese people had a critical but constructive attitudes toward the policies. The positive group was dominant among supporters of the governmental party (LPD), while the integrative one among supporters of Democratic Party, a largest nongovernmental party. Most of the supporters of Communist Party belonged to the critical group. The positive group was dominant in the provincial city and rural areas, while the integrative group was dominant in the metropolitan areas.
