Acquired bilateral nevus of Ota-like macules mimicking dark circles under the eyes
In this report, we demonstrate two cases of acquired bilateral nevus of Ota-like macules (ABNOM) located on the lower eyelids mimicking periorbital hyperpigmentation, so-called dark circles under the eyes. The patients were treated with the 1064-nm Q-switched (QS) Nd:YAG laser with low fl uence and presented clinical improvements. A 27-year-old Korean female with Fitzpatrick skin type IV visited our clinic complaining of dark circles under the eyes, which had persisted for 8 years. The patient had no treatment history for the pigmented lesions and no pertinent family or past medical history. Photographs were taken using the Canfi eld Visia CR System (Canfi eld, NJ, USA) with standard light (Figure 1A) and ultraviolet light (Figure 1B). The periorbital hyperpigmented lesions were mainly localized to the lower eyelids and composed of several discrete brownish macules. The light brownish lesions, which were slightly accentuated with the ultraviolet light, were also noted on the nose and malar regions, but not on the temples and upper eyelids. The patient was clinically diagnosed with ABNOM. We also observed a 30-year-old Korean female with Fitzpatrick skin type IV presenting the pigmented lesions on the infraorbital regions, which had been developed for 7 years. The patient had neither treatment history of the lesions nor pertinent family or past medical history. The several discrete brownish lesions were concentrated on the lower eyelids as well as on the root of the nose (Figure 2A). The lesions on the temples were brownish blue. A clinical diagnosis of ABNOM was made. Patients were treated with the 1064-nm QS Nd:YAG laser using MedLite C6 TM (HoyaConBio Inc., Fremont, CA, USA) at 2-week intervals. For local anesthesia, the face was cleansed and topical EMLA cream (eutectic mixture of 2.5% lidocaine HCl and 2.5% prilocaine; AstraZeneca AB, S ö dert ä lje, Sweden) was applied to the entire face or both cheeks under occlusion an hour prior to the laser therapy. The entire face or both cheeks, including the ABNOM, were treated with the settings of 2.2 – 2.6 J/cm 2 , 6-mm spot size, and two to three passes with appropriate overlapping as described in the previous report (1). Additional treatment on the ABNOM was directly followed with the settings of 4 – 6 J/cm 2 , 4-mm spot size, and two to three passes or until the appearance of fi ne petechiae, with appropriate overlapping. The patients presented clinical improvements with QS Nd:YAG treatments (Figure 2B). Dark circles under the eyes are referred to as round and homogeneous pigmented lesions on both infraorbital areas (2). Post-infl ammatory hyperpigmentation, superfi cial vasculature, periorbital edema, shadowing by skin laxity, and dermal melanin deposition may cause the formation of dark rings (2). Histologic studies on periorbital lesions have suggested that the dermal melanosis could be defi ned as dermal melanocytosis (3). However, the relationship between the dermal melanocytosis in dark circles and ABNOM has not been evaluated. ABNOM or Hori ’ s nevus is dermal melanocytic hyperpigmentation, which usually present discrete brown and blue macules depending on the location of the melanocytes (4,5). Mostly, ABNOM appears on malar regions, temples, upper eyelids, and the root and alae of the nose.
