A Comment on Distributional Externalities and In-Kind Transfers: Rejoinder
BRUCE YANDLE Clemson University -In Robert Pirsig's Zen and the Art of Motorcycle • Maintenance (1974: 8), it is suggested that men should concern themselves with the question "What is best?" rather than "What's new?" By pursuing exclusively the latter, we are reminded that the result may be "an endless parade of trivia and fashion, the silt of tomorrow." Edgar Browning (1976) has called attention to a framework of analysis which may be "best," or at least better than the one I used in discussing welfare in-kind. However, while I like his presentation, conventional indifference curve analysis has a lengthy heritage, too. Perhaps I can address the four points Browning has named as missing in my analysis and illuminate some aspects of them. The points are discussed in order. First, Browning has stated correctly that the equilibrium attained at point L in his Figure 2 is not an optimal allocation.
