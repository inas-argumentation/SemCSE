Practical discussion on switching studies and how this relates to managing lipids
Evidence has accrued from numerous clinical trials about the dyslipidaemic effects of various individual and combinations of antiretrovirals (Table 1), and a number of national organizations have responded by producing management guidelines. In treatment-naive patients, the range of drugs available is wide enough to allow the selection of a cardioprotective combination and avoidance of those known to be associated with raised lipids. The same is not true for heavily treatment-experienced patients, where the need to ensure control of viral load often overrides concerns about high lipids, the significance of which is difficult to quantify.
