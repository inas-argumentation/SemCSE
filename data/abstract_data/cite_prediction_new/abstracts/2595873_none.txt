In Situ Hybridization for Erythropoietin Messenger RNA Using Digoxigenin‐labeled Oligonucleotides a
The cellular origin of erythropoietin (EPO) within the kidney has remained controversial. In 1987 it was shown that EPO messenger RNA was detectable in the tubular but not in the glomerular fraction of disrupted renal cortex.' The tubular fraction contained tubular epithelial cells as well as adherent interstitial cells of various types, and therefore in situ hybridization studies were needed to establish the identity of the EPO synthesizing cells. Two studies reported the use of 3sS-labeled probes derived from EPO genomic sequences for in situ hybridization.*q3 A hybridization signal for EPO messenger RNA was found in the peritubular cells of the mouse renal cortex when EPO synthesis was stimulated by anemia or by phenylhydrazine and irradiation. Subsequent work using a 32P-labeled oligonucleotide probe found EPO messenger RNA in tubular cells of the renal cortex in mice subjected to hypobaric h y p ~ x i a . ~ In the present study, we used digoxigenin-labeled oligonucleotide probes that were complementary only to exon sequences of the murine EPO gene. Groups of adult male C57/BL6 mice were subjected to hypobaric hypoxia at 0.5 atmospheres for 6 hours and compared with normoxic controls. Kidneys were promptly fixed in 4% buffered paraformaldehyde, and 4 pm paraffin sections were used for in situ hybridization. Initially a single 45-mer oligonucleotide probe complementary to part of exon IV was 3' end-labeled with digoxigenin. No hybridization signal could, however, be detected by this method, and in order to increase sensitivity, a cocktail of six antisense oligonucleotides was employed. The probes were 5' labeled with digoxigenin, and HPLC purified. Use of the oligonucleotide probe cocktail gave a hybridization signal in the renal cortex of hypoxic mice (FIG. 1) with good cellular resolution. Hybrids were detected using antidigoxigenin alkaline phosphatase. Positive cytoplasmic staining was noted in a subpopulation of tubular epithelial cells. Some tubules contained both positive and negative cells, whereas in others the epithelial cells were entirely negative (FIG. 2). Signal appeared more intense in the inner regions of the cortex. No hybridization signal was demonstrable in kidney sections from normoxic mice. Hybridization in the absence of probe or with RNase predigestion produced no signal.
