Prenatal Treatment and Mothers’ Rights: The Legal Framework, Policy Considerations, and Research Needs in Three Problem Areas
Efforts to control the behavior of women take various forms and have various justifications. This paper discusses efforts to control women’s behavior in three different areas in the name of protecting the fetus. The first involves whether or not to compel pregnant women to undergo prenatal medical treatment. The second involves treatment of pregnant substances abusers. The third involves the problem of reproductive hazards in the workplace. As the paper will show, the pertinent law as well as the policy considerations and research needs in each area differ to a great extent; but, as it will also show, there are overlapping concerns. For in each area a focus on women’s choices and behaviors has reflected the view that women alone determine our children’s health and has served to hide other-likely more important causes-f fetal damage.
