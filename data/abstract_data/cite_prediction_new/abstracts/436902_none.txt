He 2 2 + molecular ion can exist in a magnetic field
We present a detailed study of the ground state of the Coulomb system $(\ensuremath{\alpha}\ensuremath{\alpha}ee)$ which corresponds to the ${\mathrm{He}}_{2}^{2+}$ molecular ion in a magnetic field $B=0\ensuremath{-}4.414\ifmmode\times\else\texttimes\fi{}{10}^{13}\phantom{\rule{0.3em}{0ex}}\mathrm{G}$ in parallel configuration (it is assumed that infinitely massive $\ensuremath{\alpha}$ particles are situated along a magnetic-field line). One uses a variational method, introducing a trial function which includes electronic correlation in the form $\mathrm{exp}(\ensuremath{\gamma}{r}_{12})$, where $\ensuremath{\gamma}$ is a variational parameter. It is shown that the quantum numbers of the lowest total-energy state depend on the magnetic-field strength. This state evolves from the spin-singlet $^{1}{\ensuremath{\Sigma}}_{g}$ metastable state at $0\ensuremath{\le}B\ensuremath{\lesssim}0.85\phantom{\rule{0.3em}{0ex}}\mathrm{a}.\mathrm{u}.$ to a repulsive spin-triplet $^{3}{\ensuremath{\Sigma}}_{u}$ state for $0.85\ensuremath{\lesssim}B\ensuremath{\lesssim}1100\phantom{\rule{0.3em}{0ex}}\mathrm{a}.\mathrm{u}.$ and, finally, to a stable strongly bound spin-triplet $^{3}{\ensuremath{\Pi}}_{u}$ state at stronger fields $B\ensuremath{\gtrsim}1100\phantom{\rule{0.3em}{0ex}}\mathrm{a}.\mathrm{u}.$ The lowest vibrational energy for the latter case is calculated.
