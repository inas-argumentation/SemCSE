Phosphorylation targets the functional gating of a glutamate channel
SerineÏthreonine and tyrosine phosphoprotein kinases and phosphatases are known to directly phosphorylate and dephosphorylate members of the three major classes of ionotropic glutamate receptors (AMPA, NMDA and kainate channels) (Moss & Smart, 1996). These enzymes also play a central role in various forms of synaptic plasticity such as longterm potentiation and depression. Therefore, it is tempting to suggest that phosphorylation might cause a long-term change in glutamate receptor function. This might be achieved through the insertion of new receptors into the subsynaptic membrane or through an alteration in the behaviour of pre-existing channels. In this issue of The Journal of Physiology, the paper by Traynelis & Wahl (1997) provides evidence that phosphorylation can modulate the intrinsic coupling between the binding of glutamate and the gating of the channel.
