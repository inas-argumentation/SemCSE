Chronic rhinosinusitis and persistent epiphora following conjunctivodacryocystorhinostomy
Conjunctivodacryocystorhinostomy (C-DCR) is the final treatment option for patients with persistent epiphora refractory to dacryocystorhinostomy (DCR) surgery. Other indications for C-DCR include canalicular agenesis or obstruction, common canalicular obstruction, and lacrimal pump dysfunction. This external procedure involves the creation of a large fistula between the medial commissural conjunctiva and the nasal cavity with the permanent placement of a glass Jones tube to restore lacrimal drainage. Postoperative complications after tube insertion are not uncommon, and include obstruction of the tube, malposition, granuloma formation, and infection. We present the unique case of a patient who developed chronic rhinosinusitis (CRS) and persistent epiphora following C-DCR. A 46-year-old woman was referred for evaluation of chronic sinonasal symptoms and left-sided epiphora. The patient had a history of mild allergic rhinitis, but noted that her sinus symptoms had worsened significantly after CDCR with Jones tube placement performed many years ago. Her epiphora had recurred a few months after surgery. Nasal endoscopy revealed dense scar tissue in the left superior middle meatus with no visible Jones tube. A CT scan demonstrated significant sinus disease in the left ethmoid, maxillary sinus, and frontal recess. A Jones tube was seen traversing the anterior ethmoid, piercing the middle turbinate and abutting the septum. The patient underwent image-guided endoscopic sinus surgery with replacement of her Jones tube. At operation, dense scar tissue encasing the Jones tube was encountered (Fig 1). The tube was removed. The middle turbinate was partially resected and a partial ethmoidectomy was performed. A shorter Jones tube (15 mm) was then placed under endoscopic visualization. There were no complications and the patient was discharged home. Postoperatively, she reported complete resolution of her sinus symptoms and epiphora. Endoscopy confirmed a patent and well-positioned tube. The patient was doing well 18 months after surgery. St. Louis University Institutional Review Board approval was obtained for this study.
