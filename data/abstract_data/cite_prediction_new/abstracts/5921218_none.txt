The use of tolerance and modification of planting times to reduce damage caused by root-knot nematodes (Meloidogyne spp.) in vegetable cropping systems at Bundaberg, Queensland
The extent to which cultural control measures could be used to reduce the need for nematicides was examined in field experiments on vegetable crops in Bundaberg, Queensland. Evaluation of the twelve most common vegetable crops grown in the region showed that all were susceptible to root-knot nematodes (Meloidowe spp.), as nematode multiplication occurred on all crops. Tomato, pumpkins, rockmelons, watemelons, eggplant and zucchini infested with Meloidogynes pp. yielded 15–36% less than nematode-free plants, whereas yields of sweetcorn, capsicum, french bean, chinese cabbage, broccoli and button squash were not markedly affected by the nematode. Results of experiments in which tomatoes were planted at different times of the year showed that root-knot nematode reduced yields by 16–36% when crops matured in summer. Yield reductions from nematodes were either not observed in winter or the losses were less important because of the higher yields that are achievable at this time of the year. These results suggest crop rotation and adjustment of planting time could possibly be used to reduce the economic impact of root-knot nematodes on vegetable production. Susceptible but tolerant crops (e.g. sweetcorn and capsicum) would be grown during the summer whereas susceptible intolerant crops (e.g, tomato) are best confined to the autumn-winter period.
