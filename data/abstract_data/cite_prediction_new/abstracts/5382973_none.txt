Tempering Behavior of the Fusion Boundary Region of an F 22 / 625 Weld Overlay
Postweld heat treatment (PWHT) is often required to relieve the residual stress and reduce the heat­affected zone (HAZ) hardness of Filler Metal 625 overlays on F22 (2.25Cr­1Mo) steel for use in the oil and gas industry, but may lead to interface embrittle­ ment due to carbon diffusion. The as­welded and PWHT conditions of F22/625 dissimilar metal weld overlay were investigated, with a major focus on understanding the effect of PWHT on the microstructure and hardness of the fusion boundary region. This region in­ cluded the F22 coarse grain heat­affected zone (CGHAZ), the planar growth zone (PGZ), and the Alloy 625 weld metal. The microstructure, hardness, and chemical composition gradient were evaluated using optical metallography, Vickers hardness testing, nanoindentation, and scanning electron microscopy with energy dispersive x­ray (SEM/EDX) analysis. The CGHAZ hardness decreased with an increase of the Hollomon­ Jaffe parameter (HJP), but a secondary hardening effect occured at HJP  19,100~19,400. The PGZ hardness increased with the HJP at a higher rate than the weld metal, but the scatter of the PGZ hardness was much larger than the CGHAZ and weld metal. Nanoindentation was used to reveal hardness peaks in the PGZ of PWH treated samples. The results of this investigation revealed the optimum PWHT conditions under which the hardness of the fusion boundary region will provide the highest resistance to hydrogen­ assisted cracking (HAC) and sulfide stress corrosion cracking (SSC).
