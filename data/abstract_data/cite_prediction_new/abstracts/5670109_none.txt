COMPARATIVE RECOVERY OF MENTAL FUNCTIONS AFTER ELECTROCONVULSIVE THERAPY WITH METHOHEXITAL AND THIOPENTONE ANAESTHESIA
A~IONG recent published accounts of short-acting intravenous barbiturate amesthetic agents, a number of evaluative studies have commented favourably of the efficacy of methohexital sodium. Clinical comparisons of methohexital with the thiobarbiturates, principally thiopentone sodium, have tended to be in agreement as to the relative advantages of methohexital. Its usefulness is said to be particularly evident for short procedures such as minor surgery in out-patient settings, dentistry and certain physical treatments in psychiatry. Opinion relating to its superiority over thiopentone generally places emphasis on its more rapid action, greater potency and significantly faster detoxification rate, the earlier arousal of patients, and a much lower incidence of clouded sensorium or "hangover" effect.
