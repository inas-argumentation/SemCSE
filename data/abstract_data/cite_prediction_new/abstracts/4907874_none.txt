Validating candidate reference genes for qRT-PCR-based gene expression analysis in nonfunctioning pituitary adenomas
Non-functioning pituitary adenomas (NFPA) are among the most frequent pituitary tumours in humans and much effort is being devoted to elucidating their molecular background. Most of studies that focus on aberrant gene expression, employ the ‘reverse transcription—quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qRT-CPR) method for assessing levels of gene expression. This technique is robust, low-time consuming and nowadays it has become the basic method of choice, however a sample normalization procedure is required for assessing such expression levels in any given gene. That most frequently used is to also assess, in parallel, gene expression of a gene exhibiting stable expression in any given sample; this thereby constituting an internal control and is termed as a reference gene [1]. Making an appropriate selection is critical and forms a key point of the MIQE guidelines; recommendations developed by an international academic consortium to establish standards of good quality in qRT-PCR analysis [2]. Accordingly, a candidate reference gene must be validated independently for each particular tissue type before calculating any qRT-PCR results. In practice, the most stable gene or a combination thereof is selected as reference from examining the candidates in each given tissue of interest. This approach is strongly justified because different reference genes, including the frequently used GAPDH and ACTB, may unexpectedly show variable expression for any given tissue [1]. Upon studying gene expression in human NFPAs, we however discovered there is hardly any data for evaluating reference genes in this type of tumour tissue. Most previously reported qRT-PCR analyses are based on a single a priori selected reference gene. There is only one, recently published paper that presents a systematic assessment of six candidate reference genes in different human pituitary tissues [3]. This prompted us to evaluate expression stability of ten candidate reference genes, that we successfully used for expression analysis in different tissues: ACTB, B2M, GAPDH, HMBS, HPRT1, RPLP0, SDHA, TUBA1B, YWHAZ and UBC on a group of 30 NFPA patients. We isolated RNA from freshly frozen tumour tissue samples, subjected equal RNA amounts to the reverse transcriptase reaction and assessed the genes expression levels with qRT-PCR. We included only good quality samples that met the absorbance 260/280 ratio[1.8 and RIN[7 criteria. To ensure comparability of the qPCR assays, the PCR efficiency was determined by amplifying 5 9 dilutions of control cDNA ranging between 89.3 and 107 %. Patients, sample and technical details along with primers’ sequences are presented in the Supplementary Materials. We used RefFinder, a comprehensive web-based tool (http://www.leonxie.com/referencegene.php) for analysing and validating the genes stabilities. This software generates a comprehensive expression stability ranking based on evaluating four implemented algorithms: geNorm, Normfinder, BestKeeper and Comparative delta-Ct method.
