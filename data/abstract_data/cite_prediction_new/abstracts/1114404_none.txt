A comparison of serum and testicular hyaluronidase.
The enzyme in human serum which degrades hyaluronic acid at pH 3–4.9 has been compared with bovine testicular hyaluronidase at similar activity levels under identical reaction conditions. Serum enzyme activity was maximum at pH 3.7, fell off precipitousty above pH 4.0, and could not be detected above pH 4.9. Testicular enzyme showed an initial optimum for short incubation periods at pH 3.5, but this optimum shifted with longer time of incubation to a broad plateau between pH 4.5 and 6.0. Testicular enzyme activity could be detected up to pH 7.6. At pH values lower than 4.5, testicular enzyme activity fell off much more rapidly from the initial rate than serum enzyme activity. The pH activity data for human testicular hyaluronidase paralleled those for bovine testicular enzyme. No evidence was found that these differences between serum and testicular enzyme with respect to the effects of pH could have arisen from inhibitors or activators in the natural systems. It therefore seem likely that these differences reflect truly intrinsic differences between the two enzymes.
