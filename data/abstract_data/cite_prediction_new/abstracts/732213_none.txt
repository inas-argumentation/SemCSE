Scheduling in a grid environment using high level policies
Grid computing has been proposed as a solution to increasing computational demands for many organisations. Grids consist of a wide range of computational resources, and typically a wide range of diverse jobs. We consider the problem of controllability, the ability for the organisation to specify a policy controlling which users and organisational units receive the best service, whilst using a Grid. We do this by considering scheduling, the assignment of jobs onto the resources. Most existing work on Grid scheduling considers each application as it arrives, thus implicitly giving preference to the jobs that arrived first. This is not often the required behaviour. We adopt a model whereby we constantly consider schedules for all jobs we are aware of. An early but low priority job will be allocated to a resource, but may be pre-empted by later submissions. We use an economic model of the problem to suggest a new paradigm for the scheduling phase, which can be extended to enforce an organisational policy. This extension is based on social welfare theory, but modifications are required to account for the differences between a Grid system and a market of consumers. We consider three variants of the economic model, corresponding broadly to three different ideologies of social welfare. We compare these models with the leading existing technique for enforcing policy and demonstrate a significant improvement. We also consider more advanced policies specifying descriptive requirements rather than proportional priorities, demonstrating that the economic model can be extended in order to satisfy a richer set of policies. This requires consideration of issues such as predicted resource availability, predicted job arrivals and dependencies between jobs. We show how these are incorporated into our underlying model. The combination of performance and reliability, often termed peformability, is also considered. We demonstrate the effects on peformability and predictability of increased performance information and the provisions for advanced reservations.
