Phosphatidylcholine and Chondroitin Sulphate in Peritoneal Dialysis Fluids to Preserve Membrane Function
I t is generally recognized that chronic exposure of the peritoneal membrane to dialysis fluid induces various disorders of its structure. These have been documented in the morphological studies of peritoneal biopsies obtained from continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis (CAPD) patients (1). Functional deterioration of the peritoneum is also observed in chronic CAPD patients, as a result of which more hypertonic fluids are required to maintain the transperitoneal ultrafiltration at sufficient levels (2). The biocompatibility of the currently used peritoneal dialysis fluids is relatively low, and this may be one of the reasons for the morphological and functional disorders in the peritoneum of the patients on chronic peritoneal dialysis (3). There is also growing evidence that the procedure of peritoneal dialysis itself induces a chronic inflammatory reaction within the peritoneal cavity which may also contribute to peritoneal damage ( 4). Discontinuation of peritoneal dialysis helps to restore the function of the peritoneal membrane (5). Elaboration of simple, low-cost methods to preserve efficient function of the peritoneal membrane as a dialysis membrane is one of the factors that may determine the future of peritoneal dialysis as a method for treatment of end-stage renal failure. Gotloibet al. described peritoneal dialysis as a process of continuous mesothelial injury followed by its regeneration ( 6). Di Paoloet al. have shown that regeneration of the peritoneal mesothelium can be supported and accelerated by intraperitoneal implantation of autologous mesothelial cells (7).
