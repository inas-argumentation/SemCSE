Cardiac troponin T concentrations in healthy adolescents.
Developments of sophisticated immunoassay systems in recent years have led to cardiac troponin testings with improved analytical sensitivity and precision. The high-sensitivity cardiac troponin T (hscTnT) and several cardiac troponin I (hs-cTnI) assays show imprecision profile that fulfil the recommended guidelines of the international cardiology societies [1]. The high analytical sensitivity also allows for the detection of circulating cardiac troponins in most of healthy persons, thus enabling establishment of a true reference range. However, most if not all of the studies on analytical performances of the troponins thus far were performed on Caucasian population in advanced age, and there were contradictory results with regard to sex-related difference in cardiac troponin concentrations measured. We have conducted a preliminary study on healthy adolescents using the pre-commercial hs-cTnT and report herein it's plasma concentrations and measurable rate in male and female subjects who had undergone routine hematological examination procedures. The study consisted of 47 male and 28 female third-year medical students who were volunteers of spending blood samples for studying normal hematological indices and blood smear examinations. They represent a young healthy population that has passed the routine medical check-up at study entry. All except 4 of study subjects were 20 y old. The EDTA plasma, obtained after centrifugation of the left-over samples, was kept frozen at −20 °C until analyses. Cardiac troponin T concentrations were measured with the new high-sensitivity electrochemiluminescence immunoassay on an MODULAR Analytics E 170 (Roche Diagnostics, Switzerland). According to manufacturer, the limit of detection, 99th percentile cut-off concentration, and the level at 10% coefficients of variation is 0.003, 0.014 and 0.013 ng/ml, respectively. Values less than limit of detection are reported as 0.003 ng/ml. Differences in themeanhs-cTnT levels between the sexeswere assessed by Mann–Whitney test. A pb0.05 indicates statistical significant difference. With the exception of 4 study subjects (all females), all others showed normal values of red blood cell indices, white blood count and platelet concentrations. The abnormalities of blood cell indices observed were only mild and have no clinical significance. The mean (±SD) level of hs-cTnT in males of 0.0053±0.0024 ng/ml was significantly (pb0.01) higher than that in females (0.0036±0.0017 ng/ml). Men displayed amaximumvalue of 0.014 ng/ml compared to that inwomen of 0.011 ng/ml. The measurable rate, defined as the percentage of measurable normal values (0.003–0.014 ng/ml),was 68.1% and 28.6% in males and females, respectively.
