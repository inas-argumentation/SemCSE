Piroxicam‐β‐cyclodextrin and photosensitivity reactions
Dear Sir In their article, Varela et al. (1) report data on 15 patients with photosensitivity reactions to piroxicam and piroxicam-b-cyclodextrin (PBCD). According to the authors, the prevalence of photosensitivity reactions to PBCD in Portugal is higher than to piroxicam; they therefore suggest that the use of PBCD in their country should be avoided. In my opinion, there are errors of methodology in the article, as well as incorrect information on PBCD. PBCD is a complex of piroxicam and an inert cyclic macromolecule, b-cyclodextrin. The complexation of piroxicam, a very poorly soluble drug, with b-cyclodextrin increases its rate of dissolution and solubility. This improves the rate of absorption of piroxicam. The clinical advantage of PBCD over piroxicam is a faster onset of action, which is advantageous when rapid analgesia is required. Contrary to what is reported in the article, the bioavailability of PBCD is no different from that of piroxicam. The pharmacokinetics of PBCD differs from piroxicam only by a faster rate of absorption. Plasma concentrations of piroxicam at 0.25 and 0.5 h post-dose were 10 and 3¿ greater, respectively, after administration of the PBCD tablet formulation compared with standard piroxicam capsules in a cross-over study (2). The AUC extrapolated to infinity was similar for PBCD and standard piroxicam, demonstrating a comparable overall amount of absorption. This finding demonstrates that the higher plasma concentrations obtained in the first 30 min after ingestion of PBCD are not a result of increased overall bioavailability compared with standard piroxicam (3). Varela et al. (1) make a statistical calculation by linking the incidence of photosensitivity to PBCD and piroxicam, 15 patients being observed between January 1996 and September 1997, to the sales of piroxicam as a class in Portugal in 1996. They conclude that, because the sales of PBCD were 16.6% of the total sales of piroxicam, the incidence of photosensitivity to PBCD (6 patients out of 15) is much higher than to piroxicam (9/15). I do not think that it is correct to correlate the 15 patients referred to the authors’ hospital to the whole population of patients exposed to PBCD and piroxicam in Portugal. It could only be by chance that the patients with photosensitivity were 6/15 with PBCD and 9/15 with piroxicam. It is evident that, in order to conclude that PBCD induces a higher incidence of photosensitivity than piroxicam, one should consider all cases with photosensitivity that were reported in Portugal, and make estimations according to the sales of the 2 drugs. A number of NSAIDs, particularly the phenylpropionic acids, have been implicated in photosensitivity reactions. The drugs implicated, apart from piroxicam, are ketoprofen, naproxen and tiaprofenic acid (4).
