Iatrogenic calcinosis cutis after subcutaneous LMW-heparin administration in a hemodialysis patient
Calcinosis cutis is a term used to describe a group of disorders in which calcium deposits form in the skin. It is classified into 5 major types according to etiology: dystrophic, metastatic, iatrogenic, idiopathic, and calciphylaxis [1]. In dialysis patients, if present, it usually manifests as calciphylaxis (calcific uremic arteriolopathy) or metastatic calcinosis cutis. A 63-year-old female has been on chronic hemodialysis due to autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD) since November 2010. Her Kt/V was constantly above 1.2. Renal anemia was corrected by i.v. use of epoetin beta (4,000 IU/W). Despite the use of vitamin D metabolites (Calcitriol 0.5 mcg/day), she suffered from advanced secondary hyperparathyroidism (Ca: 2.8 mmol/L, P: 1.9 mmol/L, and iPTH: 1,590 pg/mL). After multiple thromboses of the vascular access, the vascular surgeon prescribed her LMW-heparin (nadroparin calcium, Fraxiparin ), 2,500 IU s.c. on dialysis days and 2 9 2,500 IU s.c. on days without dialysis. 3 days after the first injection, the patient developed a subjective feeling of burning, itching, and redness at the place of application of the drug. During the next few days nodular, erythematous livid painful subcutaneous nodules developed with local necrosis and secondary infection (Fig. 1a). Suspecting an allergic reaction to heparin, appropriate tests have been performed (prick, intradermal test, and patch test) which could not confirm either early or late hypersensitivity reaction to nadroparin calcium. X-ray of the right upper arm showed presence of several clustered amorphous calcifications at the nadroparin calcium application sites (Fig. 2a, arrows). Pathological analysis of biopsy material confirmed the presence of calcium deposits along the collagen and elastic fibers in the dermis and hypodermis resulting in the diagnosis of calcinosis cutis (Fig. 2b, arrows). There were no calcifications in arterial vascular walls and no signs of giant cells or inflammation in the histology. Nadroparin calcium was excluded from treatment; calcitriol and her Ca-based phosphate binder were halted, and she received aluminum hydroxide 3 g/day temporarily for 1 month. Dialysate calcium concentration was reduced to 1.25 mmol from 1.75 mmol, and sodium thiosulfate solution (100 mL/25 g) was infused i.v. during the last hour of hemodialysis.
