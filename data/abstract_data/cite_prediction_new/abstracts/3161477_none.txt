Youth vulnerability in Europe during the economic crisis
The current economic crisis has had a major adverse impact on economic performance in Europe causing many countries to enter into recession. Besides falling asset prices, there have been substantial increases in unemployment, and probably more so for the vulnerable groups of our societies, including young people. During a period of economic hardship, when young people are faced with worsened economic circumstances, it is expected that the achievement of economic and residential independence –two corner stones in the transition to adulthood– is being postponed, or interrupted while the risk of entering poverty increases. We rank European countries on the basis of the annual change observed in three main aspects of young people’s lives: poverty, unemployment rate and co-residence with parents for young people using data from the EU-SILC survey for 26 European countries over the period 2005-2010, i.e., the period covering the beginning and the spread of the economic crisis. We investigate how the key markers of transition to adulthood have changed across time and space, with a focus on how welfare state provision might have buffered the adverse effects arising from the economic crisis.
