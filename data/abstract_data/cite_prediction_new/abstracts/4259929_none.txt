An alternative approach toward obtaining improved immunoperoxidase labelling of tissues is to make the tissue antigens more accessible to the conjugate without affecting postmortem changes in composition, location, or ultrastructural resolution of the target antigens. While investigating the glomerular flux of endogenous serum proteins in nephrotic rats, we discovered a simple method for the isolation of large numbers of single, decapsulated gbmeruli from fixed kidney slices. Other techniques for isolating gbomeruli have been described (3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 11-14, 16, 18, 21, 24, 25), mainly in accompaniment with metabolic studies (3, 6, 25), biochemical analyses of the gbmerular basement membrane (9, 13, 14, 21), or immunohistochemical demonstration of antigbomerular basement membrane antibodies (8, 11). These methods have usually involved pressing pieces of fresh renal cortex through a metal sieve and then purifying the gbomeruli by repeated centrifugation, or by repeatedly passing the cortical suspension through a series of sieves of graded mesh sizes. However, the precise gbomerular localization of diffusable antigens, such as serum albumin, is critically dependent upon maintenance of normal blood flow during tissue fixation (19).
