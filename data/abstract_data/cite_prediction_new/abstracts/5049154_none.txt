Conductivity of nafion for optimal use in a PEM fuel cell
As concerns for the environment continue to rise with the American dependency on foreign oil, the use of alternative types of energy become more advantageous and extremely important. One such alternative is the Proton Exchange Membrane (PEM) fuel cell. This device utilizes hydrogen gas, and an electrochemical reaction, which creates electricity. The material within the fuel cell that causes this reaction is a polymer called Nafion. This material is responsible for the separation of the reactant gases before the reaction occurs and for conduction of electrons to the external circuit. For a fuel cell to run properly, the Nafion must have the least internal resistance possible, while being thick enough to prevent the reactant gases from mixing prior to the reaction. This experiment tested three commonly used models of Nafion, of varying thickness, for their internal resistance in order to determine their conductivity. The conductivity of the samples was measured in terms of its resistance. The samples of Nafion were tested using an ohmmeter that measured the internal resistance using the potential difference equation. The results indicated that the Nafion 112 had the least internal resistance and is therefore the most conductive. One day fuel cells will power a wide variety of appliances from cars and buildings, to cell phones and computers. In order for this to occur the price of fuel cells must decrease and this price is greatly influenced by the type of Nafion model, which is utilized.
