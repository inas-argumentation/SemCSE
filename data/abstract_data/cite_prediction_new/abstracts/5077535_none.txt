A design for information systems which can adapt to changing organizational requirements
Systems requirements change, which necessitates expensive and error-prone maintenance. Such change is unavoidable, because requirements are intellectually and politically fluid. A five-stage method is suggested for developing systems which adapt to their host organization. Business rules are established during a negotiation stage, which consists either of making contracts if agents have resources to exchange and compatible goals, or of argumentation when such conditions do not apply. Business rules, specification, preconditions and postconditions, and program control flow are discussed, and transition rules specified for moving from one stage to the next. The environment enables users and maintenance programmers to renegotiate business rules at any time and to regenerate program control flow automatically.
