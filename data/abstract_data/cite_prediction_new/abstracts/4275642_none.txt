Cyberlaw: The Major Areas, Development, and Provisions
When the storm of Internet technology first swept over the Net into the United States and the world, the World Wide Web practically and for all legal purposes was the “Wild Wild West.” This country and others have always been regulated by a fundamental system of laws that had evolved over the centuries, however, and with later statutory and court refinements, these basic legal concepts proved adaptable to the latest extensive technological advancement, just as they had during the then-startling “new” age of the telephone, telegraph, xerox, and television. Prime substantive legal areas such as torts, contracts (now e-commerce), and criminal law continued in importance in this new medium, and others gained in significance—intellectual property rights (i.e., copyrights and trademarks), domain name conflicts, linking, rights of privacy, conflicts of law, and alternative dispute resolution. This chapter analyzes these areas, their development, and their effect in the United States and elsewhere in the world. The legal system is meeting this challenge, but our world will never again be the same.
