Brainstem Auditory Evoked Potential in Antisocial Personality
The antisocial personality continues to baffle clinicians and theoreticians alike. The literature abounds with themes about its nature and etiology which swing to and from organic to psychodynamic considerations. Quay' suggested that the sociopath quite frequently finds himself in a condition of stimulus deprivation. Since this condition is affectively unpleasant, the sociopath is motivated to change this affective state by seeking stimulation. Goldman" concluded that the socalled "simple" sociopath was possibly sensorily hypoaroused. In other words, behaviorally and possibly organically, the simple sociopath mimics and may be an adult version of an untreated hyperactive child. Wender 3 felt that biochemical abnormalities existed in the reticular activating system of children with MBD based on observations that these children tend to be hyperaroused, with decreased attentiveness, increased distractibility and inability to focus on relevant stimuli. Satterfield, et al." studied physiologic arousal in hyperactive children and found that the more symptomatic child showed lower levels of CNS arousal. They hypothesized that this is due to low central inhibition of sensory functions resulting in an inability to focus on relevant stimuli, while decreased inhibition of motor functions results in hyperactivity. Loiselle et al." using auditory evoked potentials in hyperactive boys demonstrated dysfunctions in selective attentions. This study proposes that individuals diagnosed as having an antisocial personality disorder, like hyperactive children, have deficits in the arousal mechanism which can be demonstrated by neurophysiologic means such as the brain stem auditory evoked potential,
