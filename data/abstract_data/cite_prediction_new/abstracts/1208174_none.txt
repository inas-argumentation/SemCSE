An epidemiologic survey of smoking patterns and chronic obstructive bronchopulmonary disease in Pôrto Alegre, Brazil.
This article reports the results of a survey of smoking habits obstructive lung diseases and lung function conducted in Porto Alegre Brazil in 1980 to assess the progress of a local antismoking campaign. Respondents over 35 years of ange were randomly selected from 60 streets representing various socioeconomic strata. The sample included 316 current smokers 102 former smokers and 122 nonsmokers. Of the 418 subjects with a smoking history 27.6% of the ment compared with only 16.9% of the women had given up smoking suggesting that men are more responsive to smoking cessation campaigns find it easier to give up smoking than women or are more subject to smokings harmful effects. The rate at which subjects stopped smoking was more associated with proximity to the citys center than with socioeconomic status. This suggests that residence in a city center that is the site of an intense antismoking campaign could have an important impact on smoking cessation. No statistically significant difference was found between subjects reporting lung disease (tuberculosis pleurisy or pneumonia) and the rest of the survey population with regard to smoking. However there was a higher prevalence of pulmonary disorders among women nonsmokers and women who reported past or current lung disease were less apt to smoke than other women. The prevalence of asthma was higher among women but there was little difference in smoking habits between those who reported asthma and those who did not. Peak expiratory flow values were lower for smokers than nonsmokers and former smokers values did not differ significantly from those of nonsmokers. This finding suggests that the functional damage caused by smoking is at least partially reversible. Smokers experienced considerably more loss of peak expiratory flow values over a period of years than nonsmokers with pronounced declines occuring after age 55 in men and between ages 45-64 in women. Since most smoking cessation in the sample occurred around age 45 it is concluded that such cessation coincided with the emergence of dyspnea which takes 20-30 years to develop in smokers.
