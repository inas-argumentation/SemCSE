Creating a tailored trustworthy space for democracy advocates using hostile host
This paper examines unique challenges faced by citizen-- journalists and democracy advocates. They have specific digital security and privacy needs. Our use case considers citizen-journalists accessing the Internet through Internet cafes in regions controlled by repressive governments. These work in hostile environments countering censorship technologies, such as the great firewall of China, and surveillance tools such as deep packet inspection tools marketed to Syria by Blue Coat technologies. We discuss current tools for protecting our users and their limitations. We then consider how to develop an open source environment that is flexible, adaptive, and protects the user from surveillance, as much as possible. This is a unique example of creating tailored trustworthy spaces designed to work in insecure, hostile environments.
