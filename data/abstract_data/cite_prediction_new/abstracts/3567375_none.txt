The kaliuretic impact of cicletanine compared to hydrochlorothiazide.
E vidence from randomized clinical trials in patients with hypertension demonstrates that treatment with thiazide diuretics are associated with incident diabetes mellitus and other metabolic abnormalities [1,2]. These abnormal metabolic findings associated with use of thiazide diuretics have been attributed to increased kaliuresis and development of hypokalemia that alters pancreatic b cell function and consequent systemic insulin sensitivity. In overweight persons with impaired fasting glucose there is a three to five-fold increased risk for incident diabetes mellitus [3–5]. In this context, participants that are overweight and insulin resistant/prediabetic are especially prone for progression to clinical diabetes while being treated for their hypertension [1,2,4–6]. In the present issue of Journal of Hypertension, Wagner et al. [7] examine the differential effects of thiazides on development of hypokalemia in, this important population utilizing cicletanine, a furopyridine used for the treatment of hypertension. Cicletanine is thought to retain thiazide-type properties attributable to the inhibitory action of its sulphoconjugated (þ)enantiomer on the apical Na-dependent Cl/HCO3 anion exchanger in the cortical diluting segment of the nephron. The authors explore the impact of cicletanine compared to hydrochlorothiazide on sodium and potassium excretion in a targeted population of prehypertensive or stage 1 hypertensive individuals with SBPs of 120–150 mmHg or DBPs of 85–90mmHg that upon entry are overweight (BMI 27).
