A Case Study of Remnant Gastric Ulcer: Eradication of Helicobacter pylori Not Only Improved the Ulcer But Also Decreased p53 Protein Expression
In this paper, we report the experience of a 66 year old man with remnant gastric ulcer 1 year after distal gastrectomy for early gastric cancer. First, H2 antagonist therapy was applied but failed. Next, antibiotics therapy was applied to eradicate Helicobacter pylori. After the treatment, not only was Helicobacter pylori eradicated and the stage of ulcer improved, but also the oncosuppressor p53 protein expressed in gastric mucosa had decreased to undetectable levels. Chronic bile reflux is believed to increase the risk of ulcerative lesions in remnant stomach, but this case suggests that Helicobacter pylori-infection may also contribute to the etiology of remnant gastric ulcer.
