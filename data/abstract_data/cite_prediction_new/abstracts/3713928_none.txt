Bradykinesia: Why do Parkinson's patients have it and what trouble does it cause?
Patients with Parkinson’s disease have a number of motor disorders often summarized as bradykinesia, rigidity, and tremor at rest. Bradykinesia is the most important. It has never been clear that rigidity impairs movement, and tremor at rest may even go away with movement. Bradykinesia is actually a complex phenomenon with many different components, many dopamine responsive, but some not. Dissecting bradykinesia into its parts, there is bradykinesia itself, which refers to slowness, akinesia, which refers to the absence of expected movement, and hypokinesia, which refers to small movements. The concept of akinesia can include the feature of slow reaction time because the movement does not occur when expected.
