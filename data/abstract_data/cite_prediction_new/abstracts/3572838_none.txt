Changes in retinal morphology and glucose utilization in aging albino rats.
Retinal morphology and glucose utilization (GU) were examined and compared in retinas of 1-, 3-, 12-, 24- and 34-month-old albino Fischer-344 rats, reared in a 12-hr light/dark cycle. Progressive degeneration was observed throughout the retina with age but was most rapid and severe in the periphery. The central retina was more severely affected than were the equatorial regions. GU in the light-adapted retinas did not change in the immature rats from 1 to 3 months even though there were significant cell losses in the photoreceptor and inner nuclear layers. From 3 to 34 months, there was a significant 76% decrease in GU which was associated with an 86% decline in mean number of photoreceptor nuclei for the peripheral, equatorial and central retinal regions. Inner layer nuclei decreased slightly during this period. GU in the optic nerve declined slightly after 3 months. GU in the superior colliculus increased from 1 to 3 months and then decreased to the 1 month level.
