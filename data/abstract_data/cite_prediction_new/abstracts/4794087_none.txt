Constitutive Modeling of Normal Strength and High Performance Concrete using Hierarchical Single Surface Model
In this combined experimental and numerical modeling study, normal strength concrete (NSC) and high-performance concrete (HPC) specimens having compressive strengths of 27.6 MPa and 82.8 MPa, respectively, were cast and tested using a High Capacity Cubical Device (HCCD) under simulated 3-D loading, namely, Conventional Triaxial Compression (CTC) and Simple Shear (SS). The Hierarchical Single Surface (HiSS) model developed within the framework of the Disturbed State Concept (DSC), proposed by Desai and coworkers, was used to simulate stress-strain and volumetric response of NSC and HPC specimens. The material constants associated with these models were evaluated from the stress-strain data from the laboratory using a computer program, HiSSD0D1, developed by Desai and co-workers. Also, a computer program (DSC-SST2D), in which DSC-based HiSS models are implemented, was used to analyze the HPC and NSC specimens under simulated loading. It was found that the HiSS model can adequately represent the stress-strain response of HPC and NSC. Generally, both the finite element (using DSC-SST2D computer code) and the model (HiSSD0D1) predictions agree well with the experimental results for both HPC and NSC. Overall, the finite element results were closer to the experimental data than the model predictions.
