The innervation of the adrenal cortex in the sheep, Ovis ovis.
Dogiel (1894) was the first to report the presence of nerve fibres within the adrenal cortex ofman and other mammals. Hollinshead (1936), using cats and dogs, described bundles of nerve fibres passing directly through the cortex to the medulla which degenerated, as did the terminal medullary plexus, after splanchnic nerve section. Other nerve fibres accompanied blood vessels. This description has since been confirmed in many species (Bennett, 1940; MacFarland & Davenport, 1941; see Symington, 1969). Disagreement exists in the literature, however, on the question of direct innervation of the endocrine cells. For example, Alpert (1931) described a dense plexus around adrenal cortical cells in man and Coupland (1965) has described a cholinesterase-positive plexus in the cortex in man, ox and cat, whereas Bennett (1940) found no evidence of such fibres in the cat. Applying catecholamine fluorescence and electron microscopic techniques to the cortex of the mammalian adrenal, Unsicker (1969, 1971) and Garcia-Alvarez (1970, 1972) have demonstrated autonomic axons running within adrenal cortical tissue, which, in some cases, penetrated the basal lamina of the cortical cells and came into close (i.e. 200 nm) contact with their plasma membranes. Similar relationships between autonomic axons and effector cells have been described in other tissues (e.g. smooth muscle, Burnstock, 1970; salivary secretory cells, Hand, 1970). In the course of control experiments associated with an investigation into the role of splanchnic innervation in ACTH-induced hypertension in the sheep, a fine plexus of acetylcholinesterase (AChE)-positive nerve fibres within the adrenal cortex was observed (Shulkes et al. 1975). The present paper describes the distribution of nerve fibres within the sheep adrenal cortex in the normal animal and after surgical denervation of the gland, using silver impregnation, catecholamine fluorescence histochemistry, AChE histochemistry and electron microscopy.
