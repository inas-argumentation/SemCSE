CLINICAL PROGRESS Possibly Unrecognized Forms of Heart Disease
THE TITLE of this presentation might imply that many forms of heart disease were possibly escaping recognition. Perhaps a more hackneyed title, such as "Pitfalls in Diagnosis, "Missed Diagnoses," or "IReflee-tions on Cardiac Diagnosis," would be more appropriate. At the end of the month or semester, one may reflect on his practice and figuratively calculate his diagnostic batting average; the analysis of his errors, minor or major as they mav be, can be a humbling, yet rewarding task. Obviously, all personally unrecognized, or erroneously labeled, forms of heart disease cannot be evaluated during such reflection. It is necessary to have a final diagnosis, obtained either on a subsequent occasion or by a colleague in either a clinical or a pathologic, anatomic setting. General categories of missed diagnloses are: (1) rarities, (2) simulators, (3) complicators, and (4) the emotionally obscured. Rarities These may be unknown to the physician or even yet to be described. Each specialist undoubtedly sees a patient, each week or so, who presents a picture so puzzling that he may wonder if some new syndrome is teasingly waiting to be identified. Are there disease states equivalent in rarity to the carcinoid syndrome, primary aldosteronism, renal artery fibromuscular disease with hypertension, and pheoehromocytoma that are escaping our understanding? Rarities seem to become comnmonplace once accurately described. One must also remember disease states that one may produce ("diseases of medical progress ") of which a neat cardiovascular example is that of the unique type of anemia related to fragmentation of red cells,1' 2 as-sumedly related to their impact on Teflon prostheses surgically placed in the heart. The cardiologist, to justify his appellation, must be abreast of the times in regard to eponyms and syndromes that abut his specialty.
