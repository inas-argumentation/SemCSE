Re: Respiratory failure in pregnant women infected by Swine‐Origin Influenza A (H1N1)
Dear Editor, With regard to Ang et al. report of two cases of pregnant women with severe respiratory distress caused by the H1N1 viral infection, I was concerned that no mention of evaluation of fetal well-being was made; and in particular for the first case, no description of neonatal condition at birth was described. It may have been that because of journal space restrictions these details were excluded, but if so that was unfortunate. When presented with sick pregnant women, evaluation of both patients (the women and her fetus) is critical to correct management. It is well known, as was documented and referenced in this report, that in critically sick pregnant patients, maternal respiratory and cardiovascular parameters improve dramatically on delivery of the fetus. However, when faced with a sick pregnant patient it should not be forgotten that delivery may also be in the fetus’s best interests; for if the mother is hypoxic and under-perfused, then likely the fetus is also. The authors seem to imply that the poor outcome for the first baby, born at 28 weeks, was a complication of prematurity. However, if the fetus had suffered a significant hypoxic insult in utero, then this may in fact have been the primary aetiology. Close and continuous monitoring of viable fetuses in such cases is vital to ensure optimum timing of delivery for both mother and baby.
