The eight (89%) remaining patients experienced failure as follows: expulsion of a broken plug at week 1 (n = 1), abscess at weeks 2 and 4 (n = 3) and recurrence of the fistula at weeks 10 and 23 (n = 4). Our experience of ano-perineal fistula closure with the Gore Bio-A plug has therefore been disappointing. Factors possibly explaining a more favourable result in the series of Ratto et al. might include systematic previous seton drainage, per-operative and postoperative antibiotics, per-operative hydrogen peroxide irrigation and closure of the internal opening. Our experience is worthy of note since most new treatments for anal fistula are reported to result in an initial high success rate, perhaps related to publication bias in which negative results are not interesting enough to be worthy of publication.
