Cost of land management for amenity in multiple use policies.
Summary Multiple use policies seem easy to promote but hard to define. Tree cover on grazing properties can provide amenity to the community but may affect the grazing potential of the land. Increases in tree cover may enhance amenity but lower the land value. In this case then, a socially-desirable balance of grazing and amenity is a desirable multiple use policy. This problem is analysed by (a) establishing the relationship between land value and land characteristics, including tree cover, (b) defining amenity scores and relating them to the same land characteristics, (c) relating the amenity score to land value through the characteristics and so costing amenity increases and (d) discussing possible government policies to enhance the amenity of these woodlands. The empirical analysis is set in northern New South Wales.
