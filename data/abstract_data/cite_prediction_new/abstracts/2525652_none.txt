Symptomatic congenital cytomegalovirus infection in one twin after recurrent maternal infection
Cytomegalovirus (CMV) is the most common cause of congenital infection, with reported incidences varying between 0.2 and 2.5%. 1 In contrast to other causes of fetal infection, such as toxoplasma and rubella virus, preconceptional immunity against CMV provides only partial protection from intrauterine transmission of the virus. 1,2 Congenitally infected neonates are usually asymptomatic at birth, however, 5 – 17% of newborns with initially asymptomatic congenital CMV infection will present with sensorineural hearing loss, chorioretinitis, mental retardation and neurologic defi cits at a later age. 1 Maternal preconceptional immunity reduces signifi cantly the transmission rate of the virus to the fetus and protects the fetus from severe damage. 1,3 Symptomatic congenital infection due to CMV after recurrent infection of the mother has only rarely been reported. Although factors regulating intrauterine transmission are poorly understood, the different course of twin fetuses after primary maternal infection indicates that not only maternal factors but also barriers in the placenta and the fetus play a signifi cant role. 4,5 Intrauterine infection of one or both twins after primary maternal infection has been quite frequently reported in literature, however, only one case of infection after recurrent maternal infection has been described. 6 We report the case of symptomatic congenital CMV infection of one twin, after recurrent infection in a pregnant woman with pre-existing immunity to the virus, and we review the relevant literature.
