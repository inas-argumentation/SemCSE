The ulcer diathesis in perforated duodenal ulcer disease. Experience with 252 patients during a twenty-five year period.
In a review of 252 patients hospitalized for perforated ulcer disease and treated by simple closure technic, a high degree of intractibility of the ulcer diathesis has been found. Sixty-nine per cent of these patients continued to experience ulcer symptoms and 40 per cent subsequently required gastric resection, pyloroplasty, and vagotomy, or gastroenterostomy and vagotomy. The number of patients requiring later operation increased directly in proportion to the length of follow-up study. Approximately half of those patients followed for five years or more after plication have come to definitive surgery, and this need is greater in female patients. In this retrospective study and in a review of the data in the literature, it has been found that definitive procedures for perforated ulcer disease can be carried out in many patients with a morbidity and mortality rate lower than that after simple closure of the leak, and at a risk comparable to that found after operations for other complications of duodenal ulcer. A change in approach in the care of these patients is encouraged; and guidelines for the choice of procedure in treatment of individual cases are suggested.
