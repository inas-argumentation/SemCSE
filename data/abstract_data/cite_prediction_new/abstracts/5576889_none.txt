An analysis of the effects of independent audits on selected financial statement variables
Although audited financial statements are an integral part of the information set used by decision makers, usually the only evidence of an auditor's involvement with these statements is the auditor's report. As a result, critics of the accounting profession and the general public may not appreciate or understand the direct effects that audit-adjusting entries have on reported financial data. Moreover, very little research has been done in the area of audit-adjusting entries because of the confidential nature of audit working papers. This research study examined the recorded audit adjusting entries from 31 audits conducted by three Big Eight public- accounting firms. Financial variables and ratios such as net income, return on assets, return on equity, working capital, current ratio, and debt-to-equity ratio were adversely affected at a significant level by the recorded audit adjustments for the sample companies.
