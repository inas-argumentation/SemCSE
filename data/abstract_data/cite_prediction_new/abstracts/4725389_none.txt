Economics of Organic Paddy Production in Balaghat District of Madhya Pradesh
Organic farming systems rely on ecologically based pract ices such as cultural and biological pest management and virtually excludes the use of synthetic chemicals in crop production and prohibits the use of antibiot ics and hormones in livestock production. The present study was undertaken to assess the production performance and identif ication of various constraints for organic farming in Lalbarra block of Balaghat district, Madhya Pradesh, with the fol lowing specif ic objectives: 1. To study the cost effectiveness and comparative prof itabi l ity of organic and inorganic farming in the study area. 2. To identify various constraints in popularizat ion of organic farming in the study area. 3. To offer suggestions based on results for future prospects of organic farming. The study was confined to Lalbarra block of Balaghat district, Madhya Pradesh. Five organic vil lages namely, Naytra, Katanga, Katangjhri, Khamariya and Piparia were selected randomly. This study covered total 60 farmers comprised of organic and inorganic paddy in the selected area. The data required were collected through survey method in the agricultural year 2011-12. The farm business analysis viz., costs and returns, cost concepts, prof itabil ity concepts techniques were used to analyze the collected data. The major f indings of the study are summarized below:The average size of the family members in organic farms was 6.2 which ranged between 5.7 to 6.7 under different size groups. In case of inorganic farms, average family members ranged between 5.4 to 7.0 under dif ferent size with an average of 6.23 numbers in overal l farm. Average size of land holding were 1.63, 3.27 and 6.12 hectares in small, medium and large size groups with an average holding of 3.67 ha on overal l organic farming. In inorganic farms, average size of land holding ranged between 1.70 to 6.15 ha on dif ferent farmers with 3.78 ha in sample farmers in the study area, respectively. P roportionate net irrigated area was found maximum 77.18 per cent in organic farms against 80.79 per cent in case of inorganic farm. Paddy was major kharif crop grown by both the organic and inorganic cult ivators and accounted 41.46 and 41.21 per cent of gross cropped areas, respectively. The total investment of paddy crop was highest in small size, followed by medium and large size of land holding in both organic and inorganic farms, but total investment of paddy cult ivation was highest in inorganic farms i n the study area. It was Rs.19970, Rs.17962 and Rs.16919 per ha in small, medium and large farms, as compared to organic farms were Rs. 12979, Rs. 11344 and Rs.10688 per ha in small, medium and large farm, respectively. The ratio of operational and fixed cost to total cost was 76.23 and 23.76 per cent in organic farm and from 84.82 to 15.47 per cent was in inorganic paddy. The average yield per ha of paddy crop was found maximum in inorganic farms 29.18q/ha against 19.83 q/ha in organic farms. The price of organic paddy was found relat ively higher than inorganic paddy in the study area. Net income and output-input ratio was higher in organic farm. Thus, incremental net income was only Rs. 1315 per ha realizing that organic farming was economically viable and prof itable over inorganic paddy farming in the study area. major constraints identif ied in popularizat ion of organic farming in the study area were lack of proper guidance and training, lack of government facil it ies, irregular visit of agriculture off icers, lack of testing faci l it ies (soil, product), inadequate risk ability and unavailabil ity of loan facil it ies at right time and quality. Suggestions: On the basis of the f inding of study, following suggestions to popularize are made for the study area.  The wide gap between productivity level of organic farming and attainable yield was observed in the study area decrease by augmenting the productivity level of organic farming. Sincere efforts be made by the extension personnel to motivate the farmers to adopt improved production technology to minimize the yield gap. Krishi Vigyan Kendra should identify the problems of the farmers and feedback and solut ion of constraints be provided in time to the farmers.  Government off icers and other network should be established in each vil lage to produce organic products from farmers. Government should also announce separate support price to the organic product.
