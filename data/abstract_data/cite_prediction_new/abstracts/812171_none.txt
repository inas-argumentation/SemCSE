Thus, the monkey frontal pole cortex further appears to be the functional analog of the human vmPFC.In the study reviewed by Tsujimoto et al., the frontal pole neurons in the monkeys were selective for the two exclusive task sets that the monkeys had to select alternatively, namely ‘make a left versus right movement in response to the go signal’. However, these neurons responded neither to the external cues instructing the monkeys to switch between the two task sets nor to the go signals. They responded only after an action according to the outcome predicted by each task set, given external cues. Moreover, their activity anticipated outcome occurrences and was independent of outcome values. The authors interpreted these findings as reflecting the encoding and retrospective monitoring of chosen goals. I suggest another possible interpretation, that these neurons implemented forward, internal models associated with every task set. Such models learn action outcomes associated with every task set according to stimuli. Computational models [9xUncertainty, neuromodulation, and attention. Yu, A.J. and Dayan, P.
