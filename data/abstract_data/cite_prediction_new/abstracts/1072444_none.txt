Despite the APER and a defunctioning colostomy, the patient continued to have the painful indolent perianal ulcer, with two new areas of ulceration in his scrotum and right groin. It was at this point that a dermatology opinion was sought. Clinically the patient had well-demarcated ulcers around the anus, scrotum and right groin. The ulcers appeared clean, with healthy granulation tissue, and morphologically did not suggest pyoderma gangrenosum. The patient had been on nicorandil 20 mg twice daily for 8 years for angina. The patient’s medical history included diverticular disease and only took occasional analgesic for arthralgia. He was on no other systemic medication. In view of the reported association of perianal ulcers with nicorandil, and after consultation with the patient s primary care physician, the nicorandil was discontinued and isosorbide mononitrate substituted. Six weeks after stopping nicorandil, the scrotal ulcer had healed, and at 12 weeks, there was complete healing of the perianal ulcer. Our patient developed a cutaneous perianal ulcer after surgery of the perianal fistula. It is possible that the perianal fistula was related to the nicorandil. A link between nicorandil and fistulating bowel disease is under investigation. Failure to recognize nicorandil as a factor in the aetiology of anal ulcers may lead to unnecessary surgical intervention. This has been reported in the largest case series of which we are aware. We feel it is important for gastroenterologists and colorectal surgeons to be aware of this association and the high morbidity that can result. Our perusal of the published literature on nicorandilassociated ulcers leads us to the observation that ulceration in patients on nicorandil occurs spontaneously, with increase in daily dosing, or more often, after surgical procedures. As dermatologists, it behoves us to elicit an appropriate drug history before removing perianal warts or skin tags. We are aware of reports of nicorandil-associated oral, perianal, perivulvar, peristomal and cutaneous ulcers. We are also aware of a deep leg ulcer healing after discontinuation of nicorandil, and of reports of gastrointestinal ulcers linked to nicorandil therapy. We are not aware of any previous reports of possible association of a perianal fistula with nicorandil. We are awaiting results of the studies investigating the theoretical link between nicorandil and gastrointestinal fistula formation.
