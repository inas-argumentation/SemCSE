Effect of malnutrition on experimental allergic encephalomyelitis.
Summary One-month-old Lewis rats on low protein-isocaloric diets (malnutrition) since birth failed to develop experimental allergic encephalomyelitis (EAE), while all control animals showed severe EAE 14–21 days after injection of guinea pig spinal cord or guinea pig basic protein emulsion in complete Freunds adjuvant. Two- or 3-month-old or adult malnourished animals were not protected from EAE. Weights of brains and spinal cords of malnourished animal were reduced by a factor of 15–30%, but myelin yielding per gram of fresh weight and myelin proteins were similar in control and malnourished animals. Qualitative differences in myelin proteins of malnourished and control animals were not detected on polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis. It remains to be determined whether changes of myelin encephalitogenic proteins, immune suppression or both factors are responsible for the profound temporary inhibitory effect of malnutrition on EAE.
