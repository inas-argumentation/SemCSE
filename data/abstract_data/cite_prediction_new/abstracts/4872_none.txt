Interactions between a floodway and coastal constructions in Aguila bay (Southeastern Spain)
The influence of eutrophication associated with the presence of coastal constructions (dykes, quays and seaports) and several natural phenomena over the benthic biological communities in a bay of southeastern Iberia (Aguilas, Murcia) is studied in this work. This bay has an area of 6.3 km 2 , where a small floodway, belonging to a drainage system of 49 km 2 discharge. Eighty-five per cent of its shoreline is occupied by an industrial port, and an artificial beach. Due to the semi-arid character of this zone, periodical high intensity rains occur, causing floods with a great quantity of suspended materials that are discharged into the sea through the drainage streams. This significantly conditions the biological functioning of the planktonic and benthic marine populations in the affected littoral zone. The analysis of the sediments, and of the physical and chemical parameters, shows that the constructions built in the bay make it difficult for the fine materials poured into it after the floods to be washed away, thus prolonging the eutrophication associated with natural phenomena. The cartography of the bay clearly shows the effects of these periodical earth-carrying floods on the structure of the Posidonia oceanica meadows, and on the distribution of the fine materials. Comparison of recent aerial photographs reveals regression symptoms of the P. oceanica meadow associated with coastal works. The analysis of the biocoenosis diversity of the P. oceanica and the soft-bottoms shows different states of succession in relation to the degree of perturbation.
