Proper and improper SrRuO3 phase and corresponding itinerant ferromagnetic behavior
Summary form only given. SrRuO<sub>3</sub> is a very important member of layered Ruddlesden-Popper series of Sr<sub>n+1</sub>Ru<sub>n</sub>O<sub>3n+1</sub> compounds; where n (= 1, 2, 3 and ∞) indicates number of RuO<sub>6</sub> octahedral layers per formula unit. Structural, transport and magnetic properties in Ruddlesden-Popper series vary with different values of `n'. For example; Sr<sub>2</sub>RuO<sub>4</sub> (n=1) is a possible p-wave spin-triplet superconductor [1], Sr<sub>3</sub>Ru<sub>2</sub>O7 (n=2) is a bilayered paramagnetic metal with quantum criticality [2], Sr<sub>4</sub>Ru<sub>3</sub>O<sub>10</sub> (n=3) is a structurally distorted ferromagnetic (or metamagnetic) metal [3] and SrRuO<sub>3</sub> (n=∞) is a perovskite structured ferromagnetic metal [4,5]. Due to its itinerant ferromagnetic behavior and ability of easy epitaxial growth on many perovskite substrates, SrRuO<sub>3</sub> is vastly used as a conducting electrode in multilayer device applications, magnetic tunnel junctions, electronic transport tuning, dynamic random access memory applications, switchable acoustic wave resonators, spintronic devices and many more.<sup>31</sup>-<sup>38</sup> The SrRuO<sub>3</sub> single crystals show long range ferromagnetic ordering at 165 K (T<sub>C</sub>, Curie temperature) and low temperature ferromagnetic moment of ~1.6 μB/Ru [6-10]. But, achieving these values experimentally for polycrystalline SrRuO<sub>3</sub> is not easy due to many reasons such as volatile nature of RuO<sub>2</sub>. It has been reported earlier that Ru vacancies created due to the deficiency of Ru is responsible for getting reduced T<sub>C</sub> and magnetic moment. In our experiment, it has been observed that Ru deficiency is leading to the formation of the secondary Sr<sub>3</sub>Ru<sub>2</sub>O<sub>7</sub> phase in the system. We observed that the secondary phase is increasing with extended annealing. The evolution of the secondary phase of Sr<sub>3</sub>Ru<sub>2</sub>O<sub>7</sub> is accompanied with the change of the magnetic moment and resistivity of the system. For a particular processing condition, a single phase SrRuO<sub>3</sub> phase is observed and it's Curie temperature (T<sub>C</sub>) as high as 164 K and low temperature ordered magnetic moment ~1.6 μ<sub>B</sub>/Ru has been achieved, which is almost equal to the values shown by SrRuO<sub>3</sub> single crystal. Additionally, the metal-metal transition and resistivity has been observed to be affected with the presence of Sr<sub>3</sub>Ru<sub>2</sub>O<sub>7</sub> phase and shows lowest resistivity for the system which exhibits best magnetic response. This correlation in ferromagnetism and resistivity is an indirect evidence that ferromagnetism of SrRuO<sub>3</sub> is due to the itineration of electrons. Moreover, a crossover from Fermi liquid (FL) to non-Fermi liquid (NFL) like behavior have been observed for all systems near 40-50 K.
