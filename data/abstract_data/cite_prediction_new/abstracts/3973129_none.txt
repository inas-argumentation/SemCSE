Evidence‐Based Medicine in the Era of Biomarkers: Teaching a New Dog Old Tricks?
The term “evidence-based medicine (EBM)” was coined in 1990 and refers to an attitude of enlightened skepticism, based in the science of clinical epidemiology, that encourages rigorous critical appraisal of the evidence base underpinning medical decision making and clinical claims. In the past 20 years, EBM has established itself as one of the necessary core skills for any clinical practitioner, but does it have any relevance in the laboratory and basic science setting? Although biomarker discovery remains solidly in the realm of the laboratory sciences, we argue here that many of the lessons of EBM can be fruitfully applied in the subsequent steps of biomarker development, biomarker validation, and biomarker application. We review each of these in turn.
