A case of a Korean newborn with IMAGe association presenting with hyperpigmented skin at birth
IMAGe association (intrauterine growth retardation, metaphyseal dysplasia, adrenal hypoplasia congenita, genital anomalies; Mckusick 300290) is a recently reported, multisystemic disorder with broad phenotype [5]. As yet, few cases with IMAGe association have been reported worldwide [1–3]. We report the case of a Korean boy with adrenal hypoplasia congenita (AHC) with IMAGe association (Fig. 1). He was born at the 31st week of gestation to nonconsanguineous parents. He was the first child born to his parents and a family history of adrenal insufficiency was absent. His mother had Grave’s disease for 2 years and her thyroid function was maintained within normal range by propylthiouracil treatment. During the pregnancy, the mother had severe oligohydramnios and received amniotic fluid infusion therapy twice. Severe oligohydramnios was persistent on ultrasonogram monitoring and poor beat-tobeat variability of the fetal heart was also observed. An emergency cesarean section was performed at the 31st week of gestation. The patient had symmetric intrauterine growth retardation with a birth weight of 882 g (<3rd percentile for age), length 35 cm (<3rd percentile for age), and head circumference 24 cm (<3rd percentile for age). He had highly pigmented skin without nevi, bifrontal bossing, lowset ears, bilateral cryptorchidism, and a micropenis (length 1.5 cm;<2 SD of his gestational age) [5]. However, genital ambiguity was not observed. His Apgar score was 3 at 1 min and 6 at 5 min after birth. The patient was placed on artificial ventilation therapy for 7 days after birth. On the third day, acute adrenal insufficiency was evident by hypotension, high urine output (8.1 ml/kg/h), hyponatremia (128 mmol/l), hyperkalemia (6.0 mmol/l), and hypernatriuria (104 mEq/l). His plasma ACTH level was 4,670 pg/ml (normal range 10–60 pg/ml) with 217 ng/ml/h (normal range <2.8 ng/ml/h) of plasma renin activity, 55.1 pg/ml (190– 1,410 pg/ml) of plasma aldosterone, and 6.0 ng/ml (<20 ng/ml) of plasma 17 hydroxyprogesterone. A rapid ACTH stimulation test was not undertaken. Replacement doses of fludrocortisone (0.02 mg/day), a stress dose of hydrocortisone (95 mg/m/day), and sodium supplementation (8 mEq/kg/day) were initiated. On the fourth day, hypocalcemia (6.6 mg/dl) developed. Definite hypercalciuria (urine calcium/creatinine ratio=0.39) was absent. Serum PTH and alkaline phosphatase level were within normal range. He had normal creatine kinase and triglyceride levels, indicating normal glycerol kinase activity. Disproportionally short limbs and craniosynostosis were not found. Irregular and broad metaphyses of the distal femur were observed on skeletal radiograph. An abdominal ultrasonogram did not visualize any adrenal structure, and both testes were found in his abdominal cavity. The findings of nephrocalcinosis and other urinary anomaly were absent. Chromosome analysis revealed a normal male 46,XY karyotype and no mutation was identified in the DAX-1 gene. His serum electrolytes, ACTH level and blood pressure were normalized and skin hyperpigmentation were improved with therapy. The etiology and mode of inheritance of this new association is still unclear [1, 2]. Clinically well-delineated cases will help us to discover a novel gene and molecular pathogenesis and aid our understanding of clinical features.
