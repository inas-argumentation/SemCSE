Management of acute episodes in multiple sclerosis; a preliminary report.
THE PROBLEM STUDIES made several years ago by Brickner and Franklin1and Franklin and Brickner2showed that some of the symptoms of multiple sclerosis could be reduced or abolished promptly with vasodilating drugs. Constrictions seen in retinal arterioles often appeared to be associated with scotomas; both constrictions and scotomas were affected by the drugs. From these and certain related observations the theory was evolved that at least some of the lesions are the consequence of vasospasm,3with or without associated thrombosis (Putnam and others4). Photographs of constrictions will appear in another communication.5 Franklin and I were unable at that time to apply these findings in such a way as to make them of definite therapeutic use.
