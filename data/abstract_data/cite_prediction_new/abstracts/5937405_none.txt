Acute dacryocystitis caused by Pasteurella multocida.
Support for the diagnosis as the primary phospholipid antibody syndrome includes the insidious onset of pallor and visual loss, which would not be expected with temporal arteritis, ischemic optic neuropathy, or collagen vascular disease, and the lack of any other positive tests supporting alternative diagnoses. The aortic valvular disease also is an interesting finding, because a significant number of patients with this syndrome have been reported to have associated valve lesions.' We believe this case most likely represents a primary antiphospholipid antibody syndrome as the cause of this patient's optic atrophy. This syndrome is important to recognize, because treatment with corticosteroids and immunosuppressive agents has been shown to lower antibody titer with some resolution of disease, although clinical results are unpredictable.' This syndrome should be included in the differential diagnosis of optic atrophy.
