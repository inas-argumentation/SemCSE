Hypoparathyroidism Solely Manifesting Otological Symptoms.
A 68-year-old woman complained of tinnitus which was dominant on her left side and dizziness. Physical examination resulted in no remarkable findings, including Chvostek’s and Trousseau’s signs; however, laboratory tests showed hypocalcemia (6.4 mg/dL), hypercalciuria (fractional excretion, 0.34 %), hypomagnesemia (1.9 mg/dL) and hyperphosphatemia (6.2 mg/dL). Cranial CT demonstrated massive calcifications at the basal ganglia and dendate nuclei, and a decreased blood flow was particularly detected at both the left thalamus and caudate nucleus by single photon emission CT (SPECT) (Picture). The serum levels of intact and whole parathyroid hormones were found to be extremely low, and the patient was diagnosed with idiopathic hypoparathyroidism (IPH). The hypocalcemia normalized after the administration of cholecalciferol; however, her symptoms persisted. Recently, a decreased blood flow at specific basal ganglia has been demonstrated to be the causal area of tinnitus (1). Although intracranial calcification occurring with IPH is usually not associated with otological symptoms (2), the left dominant tinnitus seen in this case was likely associated with the radiological laterality. IPH-induced intracranial calcification may be related to otological symptoms.
