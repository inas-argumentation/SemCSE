The Effect of Diagnostic Criteria on the Efficiency of Experimental Clinical Trials
There are two distinct stages in the clinical testing of a new therapeutic or preventive agent. First, an experimental clinical trial is carried out under contrived conditions in order to give the agent the best possible chance of demonstrating its effect in the shortest possible time. This is followed by a community clinical trial carried out under real-life circumstances before a decision is finally made on whether the agent would be of value in the public health field.1 It is clear therefore that an essential feature of an experimental clinical trial is that it should be as efficient as possible, where the term efficiency is used to mean the relative cost of achieving a specific objective.
