May the Road Rise to Meet You: DNA Deformation May Drive DNA Translocation.
Main Text Biological systems are rife with polymer translocation events. From the nucleus to the proteosome to the cytoplasmic membrane, long biological polymers of nucleic acids and polypeptides are pulled and pushed across barriers, shuttled to their required destinations by complex machines. This processive movement is often coupled to other functional events, such as the unwinding of helices or the unfolding of polypeptides, and are thus critical to a broad range of cellular activities. Invariably, complexes that track along polymers, particularly along nucleic acids as part of the repair, maintenance, and utilization of the genome, couple the consumption of chemical energy to rectified linear motion along the polymer substrate.
