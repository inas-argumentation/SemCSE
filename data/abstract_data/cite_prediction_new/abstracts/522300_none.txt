Attitudes and mental illness: consumers and the general public are on one side of the medal, mental health professionals on the other
We are grateful to Takei and Sugihara for their thoughtful comments on the article, and agree that clinicians are often time-pressured and may often diagnose adjustment disorder with depressed mood. In our clinical experience in the USA, we also notice depressive disorder NOS. At this time, minor depression is not a formal DSM-IV diagnosis. Our study shows that this depression is not so minor (1). Interestingly, when comparing psychiatrist’s judgments to the DIS measure used in the Epidemiologic Catchment Area data set, the DIS was more conservative and missed cases of depressive disorder judged to be present by the psychiatrist, but discounted by participants because these occurred in a stressful situation (2). Our results for minor depression may thus be very conservative. Unfortunately, our data set does not directly link stressful events to depressive symptoms, so we cannot specifically study the relationship of adjustment disorders to major depressive disorder. Moreover, a longitudinal analysis considering both minor depression and adjustment disorder and how they relate to major depressive disorder, would be quite challenging, even with appropriate data. Minor depression has continuity with major depressive disorder as both necessitate assessment of mood problems and bodily complaints. Adjustment disorder that combines sad mood with stressful circumstances does not have this continuity with major depressive disorder. We differ with Takei and Sugihara in that we believe that assessing for minor depression can easily be incorporated into clinical practice. Specifically, assessing for stressful events as part of determining an adjustment disorder may be intrusive to the patient and also may be more time consuming for the clinician. On the other hand, depressive symptoms can be initially screened with freely available questionnaires such as the print version PHQ-9 (3), English or Spanish versions (4), or the online version CESD-R (5) and followed up with specific questions by the clinician to determine a diagnosis of minor depression.
