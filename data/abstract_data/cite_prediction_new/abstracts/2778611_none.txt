Occipital artery: a not so poor artery for the embolization of lateral sinus dural arteriovenous fistulas with Onyx
We read with interest the technical note by Chiu et al presenting their single center experience in the treatment of dural arteriovenous fistulas (dAVFs) using doublelumen balloon catheters for liquid embolic agent injection. This case series presents six cases of dAVFs (3 Cognard type IV; 2 type III; and 1 type IIa+b) embolized through the middle meningeal artery (MMA) via a double-lumen compliant balloon (Scepter C; Microvention, Tustin, California, USA). This technique seems useful and effective. However, we think that the navigation of this type of balloon catheter, and especially its inflation in such a small artery, might be associated with a significant risk of complications. As shown in Figure 3 in the paper, this technique may lead to rupture of the MMA that can result in severe complications such as acute epidural hematoma. We do not see a great advantage in using these balloon catheters in the MMA since the Onyx (eV3/ Covidien, Irvine, California, USA) usually progresses easily through this artery towards the shunt point if a sufficiently distal navigation is obtained. However, when distal catheterization of the MMA is not feasible due to arterial occlusion or major tortuosity and/or if the dAVF is predominantly supplied by extracranial arteries like the superficial temporal artery (STA) or the occipital artery (OcA), the use of double-lumen balloon catheters in the abovementioned arteries (STA and OcA) can be beneficial and less dangerous since these arteries present a larger caliber and an extracranial location. The OcA has been reported to be a poor option for the embolization of lateral sinus dAVFs since the arterial feeders from the OcA are transosseous resulting in poor flow control and Onyx penetration into the shunt. The use of a double-lumen balloon catheter with injection of Onyx through the OcA may help to overcome this limitation by increasing proximal resistance and enhancing distal penetration. We used this technique in three cases of lateral sinus dAVFs with satisfactory results. We conclude that our experience, although limited to only three cases, stresses the fact that the use of double-lumen balloon catheters may increase the chances of a successful embolization by overcoming the intrinsic limitations usually encountered during Onyx injection through the OcA or the STA in the treatment of lateral sinus dAVFs.
