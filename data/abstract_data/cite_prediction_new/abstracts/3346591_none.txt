Studies on a group of previously unclassified bacteria associated with meningitis in infants.
The author's purpose in this report is to describe the morphologic, cultural, and biochemical characteristics of a previously unclassified organism that is apparently an etiologic agent of meningitis in infants. I t causes both sporadic infections and welldefined hospital outbreaks. The first strain reported here was received by this laboratory for identification in August 1949. By August 1956, a total of 7 strains had been received, recognized as unusual organisms, and designated as group I la in our collection of late or nonfermenting Gram-negative rods. Five of these strains were from either the blood or spinal fluid of infants. One strain from an adult's throat remains to be the only strain in our collection known to be from an adult. The age of one patient was unknown. From August 1956 to the end of that year, 4 strains were received—all from infants. This apparent rise in numbers increased our awareness of this problem in relation to the age group involved. When an outbreak in a premature nursery of a South Carolina hospital occurred, the group of organisms was already well defined. The report on this outbreak and a second that occurred later has been the subject of a previous report.
