Prospective comparison of three non-invasive tests for pancreatic disease.
The British government supported the WHO code at the World Health Assembly in May 1981 but has failed to introduce the code in this country. Instead the government allowed the Food Manufacturers' Association to produce a voluntary code which omits many of the restrictions on advertising that are in the WHO code. This voluntary code has been criticised by the British Medical Association, the Health Education Council, and the National Childbirth trust. Oxfam opposed its introduction and sought implementation of the WHO code. In April 1983 the European Parliament overwhelmingly passed a resolution calling on the commission and the Council of Ministers to introduce the WHO code in Europe rather than a proposed manufacturers' voluntary code. It is disappointing to find a paediatrician as eminent as Professor Dobbing ignoring the failure of many countries, including our own, to introduce the WHO code and criticising those who have drawn attention to this deficiency and without whose action no codes would exist.
