Elective coronary revascularization, an iatrogenic form of acute coronary syndrome: how can clinicians reduce the risks?
Widespread debate has recently been directed at the question of whether or not cardiac biomarkers such as creatine kinase or troponin T or I should be routinely measured in patients undergoing coronary revascularization. Whereas in the past the long-term consequences of biomarker elevations were ill defined, recent large cohort and clinical trial reports have solidified the short- and long-term prognostic importance of biomarker elevations in patients undergoing percutaneous or surgical coronary revascularization. On the basis of these reports, a legitimate question is whether routine sampling should now be done, and what are the causes and therapeutic responses that relate to this phenomenon? We critically review the literature regarding the prognostic implications of elevated biomarkers after coronary revascularization and make treatment recommendations on the basis of contemporary evidence.
