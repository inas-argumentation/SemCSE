Refractory chronic lupus peritonitis resolved by multitargeted therapy
Sir, A 43-year-old woman was admitted to our hospital for chronic painless ascites. She was diagnosed as systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) with secondary antiphospholipid antibody syndrome based on clinical symptoms and laboratory findings of butterfly rash, bicytopenia, positive antinuclear antibodies, and anti-double-stranded DNA (anti-dsDNA) antibodies (28.6 IU/ml; normal, < 12.0 IU/ml), hypocomplementemia, chronic lupus peritonitis, portal vein thrombosis, acalculous cholecystitis, and pulmonary hypertension (Figure 1(a)). Her SLE Disease Activity Index (SLEDAI) was 26 on admission. To address the severe manifestations of SLE, she was treated with high-dose prednisolone (PSL) at 1mg/kg/d; four monthly intravenous infusions of 1000mg cyclophosphamide (CY) (625mg/m); mycophenolate mofetil (MMF) (2 g/d); and anticoagulants. Oral PSL (1mg/kg/d) was administered for four weeks before tapering by 5mg/d every two weeks until the dose reached 45mg/d. Immediately after treatment, portal vein thrombosis, acalculous cholecystitis, and pulmonary hypertension improved (Figure 1(b)) and the titer of anti-dsDNA antibodies decreased to 3.7 IU/ml. However, the ascites rapidly deteriorated and the hypocomplementemia did not improve. Owing to exacerbation of chronic lupus peritonitis, we could not taper the PSL dose further from 45mg/day to 40mg/d. We considered refractory chronic lupus peritonitis and added tacrolimus (TAC) (4mg/d), which was maintained to target a blood trough concentration of 5 ng/ml at 12 h after dose administration. After treatment, the chronic lupus peritonitis and hypocomplementemia gradually improved and her oral PSL dose could be tapered by 10% every two weeks. The SLEDAI decreased to eight and she was discharged from the hospital. She continued to receive multitargeted therapy as maintenance, and the chronic lupus peritonitis completely resolved without recurrence on day 259 (Figure 1(c)). She had no adverse effects, such as pneumonia, septicemia, gastrointestinal symptoms, and kidney failure during the multitargeted therapy. We plan to taper the MMF dose to 0.75 g/d and the TAC dose to 3mg/day as maintenance treatment. Chronic lupus peritonitis is peritoneal serositis with chronic painless ascites that can last for at least two months and is usually diagnosed after exclusion of other diagnoses, such as congestive heart failure, constrictive pericarditis, nephrotic syndrome, protein-losing enteropathy, peritoneal carcinomatosis, and peritoneal infection. Chronic peritonitis is especially rare in SLE patients and presents with gradually developing ascites despite control of the other lupus manifestations. It often responds poorly and needs moderate to high doses of corticosteroids, in addition to immunosuppressive therapy, such as CY or azathioprine. In our case, chronic lupus peritonitis deteriorated despite extensive treatment with a combination of PSL, CY, and MMF. Refractory chronic lupus peritonitis is a life-threatening condition without an established treatment strategy. In this case, successful treatment of refractory chronic lupus peritonitis was possible by multitargeted administration of steroids, MMF, and TAC. TAC is a calcineurin inhibitor that inhibits T cell activation and has been reported to be effective for lupus nephritis (LN) and systemic symptoms, such as cutaneous vasculitis, leukopenia, and arthritis. On the other hand, MMF is a prodrug of mycophenolic acid, which is a highly selective, noncompetitive, and reversible inhibitor of inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase that suppresses proliferation of both T cells and B cells in response to mitogenic stimulation. The different mechanisms of immunosuppression by TAC and MMF are used for the synergistic effect in LN. Multitargeted therapy with corticosteroids, TAC, and MMF was reported to be effective for patients with active class III and IV LN. The complete remission rate was higher in the group of multitargeted combination of TAC, MMF and corticosteroids than in the intravenous CY group in patients with LN class IV, V, and IVþ V.
