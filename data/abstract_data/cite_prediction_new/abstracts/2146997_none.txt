Black and White Women's Employment Rates: Disaggregated Decomposition in Probit and Linear Probability Models
The working age populations of black and white women in the US differ substantially in terms of their demographic characteristics and in the ways these characteristics relate to employment. The effects of these differences on the probability of employment have not been quantified in the context of nonlinear models. This paper applies Yun's (2004) decomposition technique to probit analysis and estimates the components of employment rate differentials disaggregated by specific characteristics. These components are also estimated by a linear probability model and compared to the probit estimates. Controls for cost of living differences, local labour market conditions and state minimum wages are introduced. Both probits and linear probability models point to educational attainment, minimum wages and unmeasured characteristics as the main determinants of the employment differential.
