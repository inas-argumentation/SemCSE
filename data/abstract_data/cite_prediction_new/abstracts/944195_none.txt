Motor evoked potential polyphasia: A novel endophenotype of idiopathic generalized epilepsy
Editors' Note: In reference to “Motor evoked potential polyphasia: A novel endophenotype of idiopathic generalized epilepsy,” Dr. Valls-Sole argues that the motor evoked potential (MEP) polyphasia seen in the idiopathic generalized epilepsy patient could have been due to muscle contraction and that the amplitude discriminator was set too low to distinguish the MEP responses from background EMG. The authors dispute both points. Drs. Taieb and Labauge and authors Symmonds et al. discuss the clinical features and pathophysiology of chronic lymphocytic inflammation with pontine perivascular enhancement responsive to steroids (CLIPPERS). —Megan Alcauskas, MD, and Robert C.
