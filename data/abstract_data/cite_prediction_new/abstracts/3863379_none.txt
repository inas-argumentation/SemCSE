A Network Architecture for Supporting ParallelComputing over ATMJoan
The objective of our work has been the determination of an adequate network architecture for supporting communications from parallel computing applications over ATM. It is assumed that the underlying ATM network is not addressed exclusively to parallel computing but shared with other networking applications. In addition, we do not attempt to adapt the characteristics of the ATM network to the requirements of parallel computing. Instead, the work is based on the standard deenitions from ITU-T and the ATM Forum. In particular, we have addressed the following issues: (1) the study of the potential performance achievable using ATM in parallel computing; (2) the deenition of the network architecture model; (3) the proposal of a speciic AAL solution addressed to parallel computing; and (4) the adaptation of this solution to the service facilities provided by ATM. With regard to the potential performance achievable using ATM, we have assessed that ATM-based parallel computing environments outperform environments based on other networks such as Ethernet and, depending on the application, the performance can be comparable to that of multi-processors. We have also assessed that the achievement of the maximum performance over ATM networks requires the elimination of the bottlenecks found in the endpoint hosts. As an architectural model, we have decided to consider a network architecture speciic to parallel computing which can be integrated with the network architectures of conventional networking applications. For this architecture, an existing transport-level protocol has been modiied so that it appears to applications as a purpose-speciic AAL. With this AAL, parallel computing applications become more robust and fast, even when the ATM network is highly loaded. Finally, this AAL has been enhanced with further mechanisms that beneet from the various service categories provided by ATM networks. With these extensions, the proposed network architecture succeeds in achieving satisfactory, cost-eeective performance.
