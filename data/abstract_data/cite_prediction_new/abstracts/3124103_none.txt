Quantification of sodium lauryl sulfate irritant dermatitis in man: Comparison of four techniques: skin color reflectance, transepidermal water loss, laser Doppler flow measurement and visual scores
Irritant skin reactions can be evaluated using different scores for the degree of erythema, edema, scaling, and fissuring. This is the time honored approach but implies the disadvantages of lacking objectivity and parametric properties. Skin reaction to some irritants such as surfactants consists of stratum corneum barrier impairment and increased cutaneous blood flow, macroscopically visible as erythema [13]. Techniques for measuring stratum corneum barrier function include electrical measurements [2] and transepidermal water loss (TEWL) measurements. Erythema can be evaluated indirectly by blood plethysmography [8] or laser Doppler blood flow (LDF) measurements [11]. The described methods are non-invasive and correlate well with clinical grading and also with the applied dose of chemical [11, 13]. However, neither technique is suitable for routine clinical use since they are time consuming, expensive and not easy to perform. A direct approach to measure erythema instrumentally uses reflectance techniques that define color by determining a reflectance spectrum [3, 5, 6] or use the standardized tri-stimulus system recommended by CIE (Commission Internationale de l'Eclairage) [14]. However, many reflectance meters are immobile, cumbersome to use on the skin and custom-made.
