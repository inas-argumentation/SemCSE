Editorial for issue 3 of 2014, Journal of Evidence Based Medicine
We are pleased to begin this editorial with another milestone for The Cochrane Collaboration. Since the journal’s second issue of 2014, the 6000th full Cochrane Reviews has appeared in The Cochrane Library. We outline some of the most recent and updated Cochrane Reviews later in this issue. You can also read the words of the authors of recent reviews of the effects of trastuzumab for women with metastatic breast cancer (1), intravenous magnesium sulfate for adults with acute asthma in an emergency department (2), and an overview of interventions to improve safe and effective medicines use by consumers (3). Elsewhere in this issue, there are articles from a range of places, about a range of topics relevant to evidence-based health care. There are new studies of a series of interventions to reduce errors in the pre-analytical phase in a clinical laboratory department and a mixed-methods study from India, which investigates barriers encountered by evidence informed practice-trained physiotherapists in the management of ‘real world’ patients in rural rehabilitation settings. Considering the information that might be used in evidence informed practice, we feature three different types of review this month. A literature review examines the large volume of research on clinical decision support systems for reducing medical error and there is a systematic review of the association between the presence of aortic atheroma and risk of stroke, which identified 12 eligible studies with nearly 4000 participants. While a rapid health technology assessment (HTA) examines helical tomotherapy for the treatment of complex malignant cancer with high-precise radiotherapy, drawing on information from five previous HTAs, 18 controlled trials, and 127 observational studies. Finally, the quality of research is explored in survey of more than 1700 articles from Indonesian researchers published in the three years from January 2008.
