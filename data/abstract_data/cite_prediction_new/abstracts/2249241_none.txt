Pharmacodynamic monitoring of eptastigmine in capillary blood
Cholinesterase inhibitors have been proposed for the past 20 years for the symptomatic treatment of Alzheimer’s disease. The ideal degree acetylcholinesterase (AChE) inhibition (peripheral or central) to be reached in Alzheimer patients is still unknown [1], in part because of the practical and ethical difficulties of making multiple measurements of AChE activity in Alzheimer patients. Eptastigmine is a new, long-acting cholinesterase inhibitor [2–6]. In Alzheimer patients, eptastigmine dose-dependently inhibits cholinesterase activity [7]. In a four week, placebo-controlled study in 103 patients with Alzheimer’s disease, eptastigmine was shown to improve memory and cognitive performance, giving statistically significant results in the Physician Clinical Global Impression of Change and Instrumental Activities of Daily Living Scale [8]. It has been suggested that moderate AChE inhibition (30–40%) with eptastigmine is associated with maximal cognitive and behavioral improvement, while excessive inhibitory activity (>70%) may cause adverse cholinergic events [8]. Thus, selection of the appropriate dose for target AChE inhibition is crucial to optimise the clinical response to eptastigmine. Unfortunately, measurement of eptastigmine concentrations in plasma is complex, due to analytical difficulties and to the rapid and extensive metabolism of the drug [9, 10]. In order to monitor the pharmacodynamic activity of eptastigmine in Alzheimer patients, we have developed a new potentiometric technique for the rapid measurement of cholinesterase activity in venous blood [11]. However, since multiple venous blood samples cannot be obtained from Alzheimer patients, we have adapted the technique for assaying AChE activity in capillary blood [12]. The AChE assay in capillary blood is accurate and reproducible (within-run coefficients of variation < 3%) and can be performed with a completely automated and portable device (CL-10 Micro, Eurochem, Rome, Italy). In the present study we have extended the potentiometric assay to the measurement of BuChE activity in capillary blood samples. After an overnight fast, three healthy male subjects (age 27–48 y, weight 65–74 kg) were given 30 mg eptastigmine as a single oral dose with 250 ml tap water. Capillary blood samples (10 μl) were collected before and 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12 and 24 h after drug administration. Assays were performed within 2 min of finger pricking. Fasting was continued for 6 h after drug ingestion. Average basal AChE and BuChE activities were determined with the subject fasting for the three days before initiation of treatment. AChE activity was measured as previously described [12]. BuChE activity was determined from the pH change produced in the reaction medium by hydrolysis of butyrylcholine catalysed by the BuChE present in plasma. The pH change was measured with the same differential pH apparatus (CL-10 Micro, Eurochem, Rome, Italy) used for determinations of AChE activity, 10 μl whole blood was required. The composition of the original working buffer for AChE determinations was modified for BuChE assays to a) the KCl concentration was reduced from 130 mmol l to 50 mmol l, b) quinidine sulfate was omitted; c) Triton X100 was replaced by 0.5 g l Brij 35 (Bracco, Milan, Italy). The final butyrylcholine concentration, after the addition of the starter to the working buffer, was 10 mmol l. The reproducibility of the method was determined by measuring 20 samples in duplicate: BuChE activity (mean with SD) 4334 (113) U l, CV 2.6%.
