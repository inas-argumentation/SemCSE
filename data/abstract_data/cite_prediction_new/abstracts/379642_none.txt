The Effect of Cohabitation and Marriage on Parental Involvement in Elementary School
Parental involvement has long been a component of federal education policy, dating back to the creation of Head Start in 1965. Recently, the No Child Left Behind Act of 2001 included extensive parental involvement language, mandating that parents be involved in Title I school plans and activities. Given the presence of parental involvement measures in education policy, it is important to understand how family demographics influence the likelihood of parents being involved. Building on previous research, this analysis examines how changes in family structure influence rates of involvement from Kindergarten to 1 grade. Specifically, this study seeks to (1) assess how the addition of an adult cohabitant influences the incidence of involvement; and (2) examine the influence of a parent getting married on household involvement. This analysis relies on data collected from the Early Childhood Longitudinal Study-Kindergarten Class of 1998-1999. To investigate the influence of cohabitation and marriage on parental involvement, linear probability models are estimated. The dependent variables for both aims are six binary parent involvement variablesattendance at open houses, PTA meetings, parent teacher conferences, extracurricular events, volunteer events, and fundraising events. The results indicate that the addition of an adult cohabitant has a negative influence on volunteerism, but a minimal effect on other forms of involvement. The influence of marriage on parental involvement is insignificant. Implications and avenues for future research are discussed.
