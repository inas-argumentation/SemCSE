Long-term Behavioural Aberration produced in Mice by a Pharmacological Agent
HARMALA alkaloids which are derived from plants all contain a β-carboline moiety. These compounds have been of interest because they are hallucinogenic1–3. in addition to possessing other pharmacological properties4. Because of the ease of formation of tetrahydro-β-carbolines from the reaction of tryptamines and aldehydes in mild, aqueous conditions, they have been suggested to be endogenously formed psychotropic agents arid causative factors in some psychoses5. In order to investigate further their effect on animal behaviour, and other pharmacological responses, a series of new terahydro-β-carbolines (tetrahydroharmans) has been synthesized4. One of these was prepared by the Pictet-Spengler reaction from 5-methoxytryptamine and pyridoxal (vitamin B6). The structure of the compound, 1-(3-hydroxy-5-hydroxy-methyl-2-methylpyridyl-4)-6-methoxy-1,2,3,4-tetrahydro-β-carboline (compound 1), is shown in Fig. 1. Thus it contains indole, 5-substituted tryptamine, β-carboline and vitamin B6 moieties combined in one molecule. When compound 1 was injected intraperitoneally into trained rats at dosages useful for behavioural studies of other tetrahydro-β-carbolines (1.5–3 mg/kg) all animals were slowly intoxicated and died within 24–48 h.
