1163 it is well known that cardiovascular events occur more frequently in the morning and that blood pressure (Bp) levels increase on waking in the morning (morning surge). in a previous study, we first defined morning Bp surge (MBps) by ambulatory Bp monitoring (ABpM), and the results demonstrated that MBps in elderly hypertensive patients is associated with silent cerebral infarcts defined by brain magnetic resonance imaging and future clinical stroke events independently of age or average 24-hour Bp level. We also stressed the importance of controlling morning hypertension in clinical practice. Many studies indicate that MBps is a risk factor for cardiovascular disease, independently of 24-hour Bp levels in both hypertensive outpatients and community-dwelling subjects, although a few studies have struck a discordant note. in this debate article, i demonstrate the evidence that MBps and related Bp variability (Bpv) are risk factors for organ damage and cardiovascular events, and i discuss perspectives related to this issue.
