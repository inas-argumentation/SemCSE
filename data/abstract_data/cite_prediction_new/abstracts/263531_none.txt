On the pathophysiological implications of an intracellular renin receptor.
he renin angiotensin system plays an important role on the regulation of arterial pressure, blood volume and cardiac function. Both a circulating and several tissue-localized systems have been identified and able to cleave angiotensinogen by renin to form angiotensin I which is converted to angiotensin II (Ang II) by angiotensin converting enzyme. The presence of local Ang II generation, which has been supported by the beneficial effects of Ang II AT 1 receptor blockers and ACE inhibitors independently of their effects on arterial blood pressure, certainly requires that renin, angiotensinogen and ACE be synthesized locally or taken from the plasma.
