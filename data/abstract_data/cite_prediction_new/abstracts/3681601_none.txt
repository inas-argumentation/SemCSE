PREDICTION OF FIBER MICROSTRUCTURE FOR INJECTION MOLDING : ORIENTATION , DEGRADATION , AND CONCENTRATION
Fiber-reinforced thermoplastic composites open up exciting new possibilities for the green automotive industry, owing to excellent mechanical properties, advantageous weight reduction, and economical fuel consumption. However, fiber microstructure is a very critical issue, including fiber orientation, fiber length, and fiber concentration, that causes anisotropy in mechanical properties and unexpected warpage of molded parts. Thus, this work aims to carry out the detailed prediction of fiber microstructure analysis, based on a classic injection mold filling simulation of the center-gated disk. In particular, we performed long fiber degradation simulation starting from the plastication stage inside the barrel since screw rotation generally leads to serious damage to the fiber as much as in the cavity. This work also investigated the famous shell-core structure of fiber orientation with respect to fiber length and fiber concentration. In addition, the fiber migration behavior is presented by using the predicted fiber concentration distribution. Consequently, the predictive results are in good agreement with related experiments. Such an available simulation method will benefit the design of fiber-filled parts using injection/compression molding.
