Jack Edgar Myers (1913–2006), an algal physiologist par excellence
Jack Edgar Myers was an important figure in photosynthetic research over a period spanning four decades, which is the main theme of this tribute. However, his contributions went well beyond photosynthesis. He was acknowledged as an excellent instructor in both the classroom and in his research laboratory at the University of Texas at Austin, his lifelong academic home. He was a coauthor of an early textbook— ‘‘Pre-Medical Physical Chemistry’’ (Matsen et al. 1949), which emphasized at a college sophomore level the logical conclusions that can come from proper experimental design and quantitative chemical observations. He was an enthusiastic proponent of science education and inspired two generations of children through his science exploration books (e.g., Myers 1967, 1978, 1991, 2000; Myers and Rice 2005) and monthly contributions to the popular magazine Highlights for Children. He was an able administrator, serving as chairman of Biological Sciences at his home University, as president of the Botanical Society of America, the Phycological Society of America and the Society for Photobiology, and as an organizing force of a variety of scientific meetings and conferences. He received various honors for his scientific contributions, including the Charles F. Kettering Award, a Guggenheim Fellowship and an honorary Doctor of Science degree from his undergraduate institution, Juniata College, Huntingdon, PA. His scientific accomplishments were recognized in his induction as a member of the U.S. National Academy of Sciences. Jack was wise, humorous, good natured and always inquisitive. Not the least, he was a remarkable family man. Jack Myers was born in 1913 to Gary Cleveland Myers and Caroline Clark Myers. Subsequently Gary Myers received a Ph.D., served in World War I, became a professor as well as a farmer, and founded the magazine, Highlights for Children, which would encourage generations of children to understand and enjoy science. Jack Myers has written a Personal Perspective (Myers 1996) describing his life prior to his start as a professional scientist ‘‘Country Boy to Scientist’’.
