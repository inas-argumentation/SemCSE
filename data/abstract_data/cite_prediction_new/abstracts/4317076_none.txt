Microregulation and Post-Fordism: Critique and Development of Regulation Theory
There is a diverse and substantial body of opinion contending that a new stage of capitalism began to emerge around the 1970s. It has been given many labels, but post-Fordism seems to have found most favour. The idea of a new stage of capitalism, particularly one with a ‘post’ pre x, has been highly contentious . Also, among those who propose that post-Fordism has come (or at least that Fordism has collapsed), there are competing theoretical justi cations. After a brief description of Fordism and post-Fordism, the regulationist rationale for these stages is critically evaluated. In the light of this two additions to regulation theory are proposed: explicit consideration of strategies and ‘agitation’. Labour process aspects of the regulationist approach are developed with these additions. Finally, alternative perspectives on post-Fordism are interpreted in the light of this augmented regulation approach.
