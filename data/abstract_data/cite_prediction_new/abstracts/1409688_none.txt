Barrier effect of sternum to right ventricle dilatation appearing like letter phi “φ” on MPI
A 46-year-old woman was referred to our clinic for evaluation of new-onset exertional dyspnea. She did not have any of traditional risk factors for coronary artery disease (CAD). Physical examination was unremarkable, except median sternotomy scar due to previous open cardiac surgery in childhood (Figure 1). After initial evaluation, Tc-99m sestamibi myocardial perfusion SPECT imaging (MPI) was performed using a single-day stress/rest protocol. There was no left ventricular perfusion abnormality on MPI. Nevertheless, right ventricle (RV) was clearly visualized both in stress and rest images. In the basal short axis cross-sections, letter phi-like ‘‘u’’ cardiac form with increased RV uptake, marked RV dilatation, and hypertrophy were observed (Figure 2). On echocardiography, systolic pulmonary artery pressure was measured as 40 mmHg. Subsequent cardiac MRI (CMR) (Figure 3) confirmed the increased RV volumes and RV wall thickness. Nevertheless, there was no residual defect or intracardiac shunt detected neither by echocardiography nor by CMR.
