3-Regular Maps on Closed Surfaces are Nearly Distinguishing 3-Colorable with Few Exceptions
A k-coloring of a map M(G) on a closed surface with underlying graph G is said to be distinguishing if no automorphism of M(G) other than the identity map preserves the colors given by the coloring. In particular, if there is a distinguishing k-coloring of M(G) which uses color k at most once, then M(G) is said to be nearly distinguishing ($$k{-}1$$k-1)-colorable. We shall show that any 3-regular map on a closed surface is nearly distinguishing 3-colorable unless it is one of the three exceptions.
