Investment Banking: An Economic Analysis of Optimal Underwriting Contracts
In the corporate finance literature on long-term external financing, thorough discussions are devoted to the analysis of the various characteristics of securities issues. Such topics as the coupon rate, maturity, conversion properties, subscription price and pricing are analyzed extensively. Little analytical attention has been given to the problem of new securities floatations. Several studies have discussed underwriters' behavior. West [20], Ederington [6] and Logue and Lindvall [12] have analyzed underwriter's compensation as a function of various characteristics of the underwriter and the new issue offered. Those analyses have completely ignored the other, and maybe the more important side of the transaction, namely, the issuing firm. In particular, no attention has been given to the following questions: Given the fact that the firm seeks underwriting arrangements, what is the "best" contract between the issuing firm and the underwriter? How do different characteristics of the issuing firm, the underwriter, and the security to be floated, shape the form of the contract? This is surprising, in view of the fact that every corporation and government agency seeking to raise funds in the capital markets is confronted with these decisions.
