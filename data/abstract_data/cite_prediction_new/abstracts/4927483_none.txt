Impact of mitigating tick size on mispricing of index futures in Taiwan stock exchanges
According to the cost-of-carry model, theoretical settlement prices in futures markets are deduced from spot market prices. When actual futures prices differ from their theoretical prices, such mispricing is primarily due to transaction costs occurred in bid-ask spread, margins, interest rate spread, and commission. Alternatively, the magnitude of tick size is positively related to investor transaction cost, directly affecting spot prices and subsequently futures mispricing. This study determines the impact on futures market of tick size reduction policy in Taiwan. Empirical findings show that abridged tick sizes have significantly reduced mispricing in futures indices.
