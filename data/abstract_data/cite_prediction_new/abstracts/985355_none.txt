One hopes that future work on organizing in the city can make these links and develop our understanding of labor’s place in urban space. Despite their minor flaws, read side by side Global Unions and The New Immigrant Workforce offer contrasting but complementary analyses of union renewal. What the two volumes collectively demonstrate is that the labor movement must learn to work at, and very often across, multiple scales to organize the global workforce, be they employed in New York City’s greengroceries and swanky restaurants or the export-processing zones and banana plantations of the global South.
