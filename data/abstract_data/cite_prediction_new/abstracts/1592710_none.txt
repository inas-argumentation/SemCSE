Selective blocking of the nodal sodium channels by ultraviolet radiation
SummaryThe effect of ultraviolet radiation on the excitable membrane of the node of Ranvier was studied. Ultraviolet radiation blocks the sodium channel and shifts the steady state inactivation curve along the voltage axis in the negative direction. The blocking process appears to be highly specific in two respects: 1. no other electrical properties of the nodal membrane such as resting potential, leak conductance and (to a lesser extent) potassium currents are substantially modified; 2. the sodium channel is blocked as a whole, since after irradiation the not-blocked channels function normally (regarding the ionic selectivity and the activation mechanism). Tetrodotoxin does not interfere with the effect of ultraviolet radiation and the photoreaction does not depend on temperature. The action spectrum for blocking of the sodium system has a marked peak in the region of 280 nm wavelength. The potassium system shows no significant peak of UV-sensitivity. Its sensitivity at 280 nm is about a tenth in comparison to the UV-sensitivity of the sodium channels.
