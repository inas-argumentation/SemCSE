The Privatization of Nature Conservation in Russia
An American geographer describes and evaluates the first attempt to establish a large, privately operated nature reserve and sustainable agricultural economy in post-Soviet Russia. A 5,200 ha preserve along the Amur River, known as Murav'yovka Park, was acquired as a 50-year lease from nearby collective farms. The preserve's role as an important breeding area for migratory cranes is examined in the context of international conservation efforts and funding; other functions (e.g., agricultural production, environmental education) also are discussed, based on field observations in 1998. Developments at Murav'yovka are symptomatic of broader problems involved with privatizing nature conservation in contemporary Russia.
