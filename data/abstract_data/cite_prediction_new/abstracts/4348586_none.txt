Intraparietal suture: a normality variable rarely described
We report a case of an infant male (8 months old) who suffered an accidental fall down a staircase. He presented a head injury in the left parietal region. After physical examination, only cephalohematoma was evidenced in the region without other symptoms or disorders. The skull radiography showed an image compatible with unilateral intraparietal suture (Figs. 1, 2). However, due to uncertainty in the diagnosis, we performed a computed tomography (CT scan) in which we observed: (1) a left parietal fracture (Fig. 3) and (2) a 4 mm subdural hematoma in left temporal fossa and confirmed the subgaleal hematoma. The existence of an intraparietal suture is a normality variable rarely described [1, 3]. This can be explained by the presence of two separate ossification centers in the parietal bone. As consequence, sometimes it is difficult to differentiate skull fractures from accessory sutures or vascular grooves. Usually, intraparietal sutures appear to be bilateral and symmetrical. However, unilateral sutures can also occur but this is unlikely in the case of fractures. Generally, sutures are shown in zigzag with interdigitations and sclerotic margins [2]. Besides, skull fractures are radiolucent and non-sclerotic margins are displayed. Furthermore, sutures are linked with the main ones but in the case of fractures these can be presented across the sutures or linked as they approach the suture. In cases of difficult differential diagnosis (skull fracture vs. suture) [1, 4], it is possible to provide a definitive diagnosis by performing a computed tomography (CT scan).
