Utility of gender determination in cell line identity
Dear Editor: Short Tandem Repeat (STR) loci are a class of polymorphic reference markers of great utility in differentiating humans at the DNA level (Edwards et al., 1992). STRs are repetitive microsatellite deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) regions with base repeat motifs of 2 -6 bp. Comparisons at a single STR locus generally yield identical results from 1 in 100 unrelated individuals, however discrimination levels in excess of 1 in 109 may be achieved when several STR loci are multiplexed. The total size of an STR locus is generally under 500 bp, which makes it ideally suited for analysis by polymerase chain reaction (PCR) amplification. Routine screening of STR loci obviates the need for highly purified DNA preparations since relatively crude DNA extracts have been used successfully. The small target size of STR loci also allows successful multiplexing of PCR reactions in a single reaction vessel. Commercial kits are available, which permit the simultaneous amplification of 10 target loci. Use of commercial kits for routine screening minimizes the potential for laboratory error when examining a sample at multiple loci. DNA profiling is one of several techniques employed by the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC) in its rigorous quality control procedures for authenticating human cell lines maintained in its repository. As part of the accessioning process, each cell line in the master cell bank is characterized at eight STR loci using the commercially available GenePrint PowerPlex vl .2 system (Promega, 1998). The level of discrimination for this kit is approximately 1 in 109. This resolving power should prove sufficient to characterize the approximately 2000 human cell lines currently present in the ATCC cell line repository. The utility of DNA profiling is being demonstrated by a number of cell line collections worldwide. Using DNA profiling, Drexler et al. (1999) of the German Collection of Microorganisms and Cell Cultures (DSMZ, Braunschweig, Germany) have demonstrated an incidence of 15% cell line cross-contamination of 117 hematopoietic cell lines received from an original investigator or a secondary source. Saeki and Kudo (1999) of the Cell Resource Center for Biomedical Research (Tohoku University, Japan) used a commercially available STR kit composed of four STR loci to successfully authenticate 63 cell lines in their collection. However, they reached the conclusion that more STR loci will be required as the number of cell lines in the collection increased. Kaplan and Hukku (1998), who have estimated cell line cross-contamination at an incidence of 7%, are strong proponents of DNA profiling as a component of a multipartite cell line monitoring strategy. At the ATCC, an STR DNA profile is established for each accessioned cell line in the master cell bank.
