The facilitatory effects of catechol and phenol at the neuromuscular junction of the cat.
The effects of catechol and phenol on neuromuscular transmission were studied in the indirectly stimulated anterior tibialis muscle of the cat in vivo . Both compounds facilitated transmission as they produced a slight augmentation of the muscle contractions, and antagonized neuromuscular blockade due to d -tubocurarine. It was concluded that the facilitation was due to a presynaptic action since no evidence was found for a postsynaptic or anticholinesterase action. Since the facilitation was not prevented by benzoquinonium it appears that catechol and phenol do not act at the same receptor sites as the anticholinesterase type of facilitatory drug. The facilitatory action of catechol and phenol resembled that of epinephrine in that it could be potentiated by neostigmine and further potentiated by aminophylline. However, since neither phenoxybenzamine nor propranolol prevented the facilitatory action of catechol or phenol, it seems that they do not act at the same receptor site as epinephrine.
