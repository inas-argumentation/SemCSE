Leukocytoclastic vasculitis associated with sulfuric acid inhalation
Cutaneous leukocytoclastic vasculitis (CLV) is confined to capillaries and post-capillary venules of the superficial plexus. The principal clinical lesions in CLV are purpuric papules, although other clinical findings secondary to ischemia, including ulceration, may occur. Causes of CLV include drugs, infection, connective tissue disease, and malignancy. Cutaneous leukocytoclastic vasculitis has very rarely been reported in association with the inhalation of drugs or chemical products. We present an atypical case of leukocytoclastic vasculitis secondary to sulfuric acid inhalation in a 53-year-old man. Complementary examinations ruled out systemic involvement and other causes of CLV. To our knowledge, this is the first case of CLV following inhalation of sulfuric acid to be reported.
