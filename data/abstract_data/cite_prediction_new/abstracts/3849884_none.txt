Sjögren's syndrome: systemic lupus erythematosus and auto-immune hemolytic anemia. Report of a case with prolonged survival.
THIS report describes a patient with Sjogren's syndrome (SS), systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), recurrent auto-immune hemolytic ane¬ mia, a pigment gallstone and cholecystitis. She has been under the author's care from 1958 to 1968. Almost certainly, SLE has been present for at least 37 years and possibly for 48 years. It is difficult to determine from the medical litera¬ ture1 the maximal duration of survival of pa¬ tients with SLE. Brunsting et al.2 mentioned briefly a patient who was proved by skin biopsy to have discoid lupus erythematosus in 1911 and who died of SLE in 1952. McCombs and Patterson3 cited two patients with SLE and survival times of 30 and 24 years' duration. Jessar, Lamont-Havers and Ragan4 mentioned one pa¬ tient who was still alive 20 years after the onset of SLE. Such cases as these and the one to be presented convincingly document the fact that lupus erythematosus is not always an acute or subacute illness progressing relentlessly to a fatal termination but may be a chronic or inter¬ mittent illness consistent with long-term survival.
