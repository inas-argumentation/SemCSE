Communication, according to Maslow's model, becomes a need to be satisfied only when physiological and safety and security needs have been satisfied. However, experience tells us that people have the need to communicate even when their lower-order needs are not satisfied, as evidenced by the behavior of people in concentration camps. It is also well known that solitary confinement is the most dreaded form of discipline in prison. We argue that despite interaction being such a basic need, the glut of communication media has overloaded us to such an extent that the biggest luxury of all is to choose not to interact with others.
