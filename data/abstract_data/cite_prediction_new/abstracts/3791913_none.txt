Transient analysis of Diffusion for charged molecules to model the input process in a stochastic event based simulation framework for the PhoPQ signal transduction system
With biological experiments generating lots of empirical data, the challenge is to develop a modeling paradigm that integrates structural, molecular and genetic data for a quantitative understanding of physiology and behavior of biological processes at multiple scales starting from cell, to tissues and finally to the whole organism. The complexity of the problem motivates the use of computer or ”in silico” stochastic event based modeling approach. We present the discrete event framework of the signal transduction cascade triggered by extra-cellular Mg concentration in the two component PhoPQ regulatory system of Salmonella Enterica serovar Thphimurium. We also present the mathematical formulation for the estimation of statistical parameters of interarrival time of molecules/ions to a cell receptor as external signal. In particular, we present the transient analysis of a diffusion model for charged ions to model the input process in our discrete event simulation structure. I. I NTRODUCTION During the last decade, advancement in high-throughput biological experiments has generated an explosive amount of empirical data on the molecular foundations of biological structures and functions. Complete genomic sequencing of new organisms are been completed and advanced databases which store comprehensive annotations of genomic and protein structures are being developed at a previously unimaginable speed. As more and more data become available, biologists are now looking beyond assigning functions to individual genes. Although individual functions and their mechanisms have been studied and characterized, the next challenge is to understand the complex interactions of biological processes and functions. The complexity increases manifold as we move into higher scales: interaction of large ensemble of cells in a tissue or interaction of tissues in continuum for rhythmic pumping of the heart. The challenge [1], is to develop a comprehensive model integrating molecular and genetic data for a quantitative understanding of physiology and behavior of biological processes at multiple scales starting from the cell, to the tissues and finally to the whole organism. In recent years, researchers from diverse disciplines like physical sciences, mathematics, biological and computational sciences have focused their joint efforts on developing models which capture the dynamics of biological processes [2], [4], [6], [7]. These models can be classified into four types [3] based on temporal and spatial complexities like 1) Quantum Mechanics based model (femtosecond-picosecond; A − 1nm), 2) Molecular dynamics based model (picosecond-nanosecond; 1nm-10nm), 3) Mesoscale Dynamics (nanosecond-seconds; 10nm-mm), 4) Cellular-level/Organ-level simulation. Most of the proposed models have focused on a narrow range of biological components, like the wave model for ventricular fibrillation in human heart [2], and the neural network signaling model to control the onset of sleep in humans [7]. Thus, the models have been tightly coupled with the system under consideration and are incapable of modeling arbitrary interpretations of biological systems. E-Cell [8] and Virtual Cell [9] are two projects which have developed a simulation framework for modeling biological processes. Most of these models are based on solution of a complex set of differential equations and lowdimensional dynamic systems in continuous time domain and thus lacks the capability to accommodate multi-scale abstraction requirements.
