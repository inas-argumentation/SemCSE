Isoelectric focusing of MDH and 6-PGDH from Escherichia coli of diverse natural origins
Electrophoretic analysis of five Escherichia coIi enzymes in 829 clones from 156 fecal samples has indicated a level of genetic polymorphism comparable to that found in many higher organisms (Milkman, 1973, 1975; Lewontin, 1974). This variation could not stem fi'om heterosis, since E. coli is not diploid. Although the spacing of the mobility classes indicated resolution more than sufficient for the required analysis, the importance of the conclusions encouraged us to determine whether isoelectric focusing might resolve further variants. Accordingly, we undertook the reexamination of two enzymes, 6phosphogluconate dehydrogenase (6-PGDH) and malic dehydrogenase (MDH). The 6-PGDH had been the most polymorphic of the original five enzymes; it was also one of the two sharpest. M D H had had the thickest bands; it was also clearly quite polymorphic, but the commonest mobility class had a frequency of 0.928 (Milkman, 1975). The reexamination centered on the most common mobility class in each case, but some of the variant classes were studied as well. In 127 clones, one new 6-PGDH variant was discovered. The commonest M D H mobility class was resolved into two with frequencies that extrapolate to about 0.66 and 0.27.
