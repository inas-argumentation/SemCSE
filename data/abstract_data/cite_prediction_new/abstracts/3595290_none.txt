Levetiracetam improves paroxysmal symptoms in a patient with stiff-person syndrome
Stiff-person syndrome (SPS) is a disorder of the CNS that leads to slowly increasing rigidity predominantly of the axial musculature and painful spasms in trunk and limb muscles. The spasms are often triggered by emotions such as fear and unexpected noises and may be aggravated by secondary phobias.1,2⇓ Therapy focuses on the causal treatment of the autoimmune mechanisms by immunomodulatory agents and on symptomatic treatment enhancing GABAergic transmission.3 Whereas immunomodulation often shows only modest efficacy, enhancement of GABAergic transmission results in significant improvement of axial rigidity, but may be associated with serious side effects. The following case illustrates these therapeutic challenges and demonstrates the successful treatment of paroxysms with the new antiepileptic drug (AED) levetiracetam (LEV).
