A Transaction Cost Perspective on the Organization of European Railways
This paper reveals approaches from the literature on transaction cost economics that could be applied to the recently redrafted European Union (EU) rail structures. The paper discusses the few existing applications of Williamson’s approach to railways, which focused mainly on the United Kingdom (UK) and on rolling stock aspects. The author found no studies measuring transaction cost in railways directly. The paper presents the lessons learned in trying to apply the theory within a wider comparative analysis of Swedish, British and German railways. The author found it important to analyze the entire process chain at the rail infrastructure/operation interface and to address all rail related assets including human capital. This becomes more interesting but also more difficult with the substantially increased heterogeneity and complexity of today’s EU railways.
