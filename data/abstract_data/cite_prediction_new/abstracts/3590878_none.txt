Measuring the Interfacial Behavior of Sugar-Based Surfactants to Link Molecular Structure and Uses
This chapter covers an important category of biobased surfactants, namely, those based on sugar polar groups or comprising nonpolar chains bound to a sugar via the glycosidic bond. We first highlight the strong motivation to adopt such surfactants, which perfectly match environmental and product performance requirements. They can be relatively inexpensive while offering the advantage of being nontoxic and biodegradable. Their use, however, requires a deep understanding of their activity, which demands specialized measurements. Some related techniques used for this purpose have been presented in other chapters of this book. However, others tools that are probably less known and yet most useful to draw correlations between structure and properties are discussed here. Based on fundamental studies conducted by the authors, among the many researchers active in the area, it has been possible to describe the behavior of nonionic amphiphiles at the air/liquid and solid/liquid interfaces. In this respect, this chapter draws on the uniqueness of sugar-based surfactants by comparing them against some of the most typical petroleum-based counterparts, used as reference. For this, structural and interfacial aspects, including packing at interfaces, are discussed and further interrelated with the respective molecular features, which can be used to propose structure-property relationships. We end this chapter by offering a broad picture of the applications of sugar-based surfactants and their prospects in their ever-expanding deployment.
