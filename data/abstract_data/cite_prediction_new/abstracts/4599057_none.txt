Molecular Detection and Characterization of Anaplasma Species in Wild Deer and Boars in Gifu Prefecture, Japan.
Anaplasmosis is a tick-borne infectious disease of humans, domestic ruminants, and companion animals (1). Recently in Japan, human granulocytic anaplasmosis caused by Anaplasma phagocytophilum was first identified in 2 patients by a retrospective analysis (2) although the distribution of this pathogen in domestic animals, wild animals, and ticks had been reported (3–7). Recent molecular analyses indicate possible differences in A. phagocytophilum isolates between humans and Japanese reservoir animals such as deer and boars (8). A subsequent study has suggested the existence of 2 sequence types of A. phagocytophilum: i) the human type has a 16S ribosomal DNA (rDNA) sequence of A. phagocytophilum that is identical to or very similar to that of human isolates; ii) the deer type, which is detected in deer and other animals, and has a sequence that differs from that of human isolates (9). Our review of the literature reveals studies on A. phagocytophilum in wild deer and boars in Japan where researchers have examined animals captured in 20 prefectures from Hokkaido to Kagoshima (4–6, 9,10). Nonetheless, no similar survey has been performed in Gifu Prefecture. This study was conducted to ascertain the prevalence of A. phagocytophilum and other Anaplasma spp. in wild deer and boars in Gifu Prefecture and to characterize those bacteria molecularly. In this study, we examined 76 frozen clot samples from 69 wild deer (Cervus nippon nippon) and 7 boars (Sus scrofa leucomystax) captured in 8 districts of Gifu Prefecture from October 2015 to March 2016.
