Somatic cell mapping of the adenine nucleotide translocator gene family in cattle
Adenine nucleotide translocator [ADP/ATP translocase, (ANT), or ADP/ATP carrier, (AAC)] is the most abundant mitochondrial protein. As an integral component of the inner mitochondrial membrane, it catalyzes the exchange of intramitochondrial ATP for cytoplasmic ADP, consequently controlling the ATP supply of the cell (Klingenberg 1989). Its central role in cellular energy supply suggests that ANT might be regulated in different tissues to fit tissue-specific functional and development requirements. Tissue specificity of bovine ANT activity was first evidenced in immunological studies (Schultheiss and Klingenberg 1984), and subsequently three functional ANT genes and seven pseudogenes have been identified in the human genome (Chen et al. 1990; Houldsworth and Attardi 1988; Neckelmann et al. 1987). The first mammalian ANT gene (pHAT14, T1, or ANT1) was cloned from bovine heart (Rasmussen and Wohlrab 1986), and ANT3 (pHAT18 or T2) was later isolated from bovine kidney and liver (Powell et al. 1989; Walker et al. 1987). ANT2 was cloned from a human fibroblast cDNA library (Battini et al. 1987), and has not yet been cloned from bovine. The predicted amino acid sequences from bovine cDNAs of ANT1 and ANT3 are 89% identical. We report here the assignment of the three ANT genes to two different cattle chromosomes. In this study, genomic DNA was prepared from bovine × rodent hybrid somatic cells, and Southern blot hybridization was done as described previously (Li et al. 1992). The ANT2 probe was obtained from American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). It is a human cDNA, identified as hp2F1 (Walker et al. 1987). The 1.4-kb insert was excised by XhoI and labeled by P-dCTP by the random primer method to specific activity of 109 dpm/mg. The hybridization conditions were described previously (Li et al. 1992). Forty-five percent formamide was used in both prehybridization and hybridization. Final washing was with 2 × SSC at 60°C. Southern blots revealed four EcoRI fragments in bovine genomic DNA (data not shown) when hybridized with the ANT2 probe. Two of the bands, 7.0 kb and 1.95 kb segregated concordantly with bovine Chromosome (Chr) X in hybrid cell lines (Table 1). Two other bands (10 kb and 5.0 kb) were not evident in hybrid cell lines. Primers of ANT1 and ANT3 were designed from published bovine cDNA sequences (Powell et al. 1989), and the intron positions were predicted from human genomic DNAs of ANT1 and ANT3: ANT1U (58CATTGATTGCGTGGTGAGAA38), and ANT1D (58ATAATGATGCCCTGGACCGA-38), ANT3U (58TCCTTCTGGCGGGGCAACCT38), and ANT3D (58TAGACGAAGCACAGGGAAGT38). The polymerase chain reaction (PCR; Rolfs et al. 1992) was performed in 50-ml volume in each reaction tube, using 100 ng of bovine, rodent, or hybrid cell genomic DNA, 1.0 mM upper and lower primers, 200 mM dNTPs, 10 mM Tris pH 9.0, 50 mM KCl, 1.5 mM MgCl2, 0.01% gelatin (wt/ vol), 0.1% Triton X-100, and 1.25 units of Taq polymerase (Promega, Madison, Wis.). The PCR cycled for 30 s at 95°C, 30 s at 60°C for ANT1, at 58°C for ANT3, and 40 s at 72°C for 35–40 cycles in a Perkin-Elmer Cetus Gene Amp 9600 thermocycler. The subsequent reaction products were electrophoresed on 2.5% agarose gels. The primer pair for ANT1 revealed a 397-bp single product (Fig. 1). Concordancy analysis assigned ANT1 to cattle Chr 27 (Table 1). In human, ANT1 was first assigned to Chr 4 by dot blot hybridization with flow-sorted chromosomes and Southern blot hybridization of human–mouse hybrid cell lines (Li et al. 1989). It was then located to 4q35 by fluorescence in situ hybridization (Fan et al. 1992). A large segment of human Chr 4q is conserved relative to cattle Chr 17 (Fries et al. 1993). Therefore, the assignment of ANT1 to bovine Chr 27 reveals an unexpected evolutionary disruption in the comparative maps of human and cattle. The pair of ANT3 primers amplified a 191-bp sequence of Exon II in bovine ANT3 (Fig. 1). With the same bovine × hamster hybrid somatic cell lines, ANT3 was also assigned to bovine Chr X and segregated concordantly with ANT2 (Table 1). Both ANT2 and ANT3 have been mapped on human Chr X.
