The effects of organochlorine compounds on British birds of prey
The great majority of bird of prey eggs collected in Britain in the last ten years have been analysed by us for organochlorine residue. Britain is surprisingly rich in raptors and has the greatest known concentrations of some of these species in Europe. Indeed the Sparrowhawk (Accipiter nisus) in Britain is one of the few birds of prey in the 'developed' world present at sufficient density for a detailed study of the effects of organochlorine residues on reproductive ability to be assessed. About 250 eggs are collected annually from raptor species and these are analysed by conventional methods for organochlorine residues (Newton and Bogan, 1974).
