Recurrent Paratesticular Rhabdomyosarcoma Reported from Nigeria
Paratesticular tumours are uncommon, most being benign lesions whilst malignant tumours are rare'. With the exception of the adenomatoid tumour and papillary cystadenoma (both benign tumours), rhabdomyosarcomas are the next most frequently encountered tumours and are the most common malignant paratesticular tumours-, Rare before the age of 3 years, it accounts for about 7% of childhood rhabdomyosarcomas-, In adults, these tumours are uncommon and few cases have been reported. Paratesticular rhabdomyosarcoma has a good prognosis and a high survival rate in children". In adults, the clinical behaviour and outcome is not well documented", Frequent clinical findings in adults include a scrotal mass, lumbar pain, weight loss, hypercalcaemia and thrombocytopenia the last being due to metastatic disease with bone marrow involvement. Histologically, rhabdomyosarcoma exhibits myogenic differentiation and is characterized by the presence of rhabdomyoblasts consisting of spindle or round cells with brightly eosinophilic cytoplasm in which crossstriations may be present", Five histological types have been described embryonal, alveolar botyroid, embryonal sarcoma and spindle cell rhabdomyosarcomas. The prognosis is more favourable in spindle cell, botyroid and embryonal rhabdomyosarcomas with a 10year survival and IO-year disease-free intervals at 80%, 64% and 83%, respectively". Our patient presented with a stage III disease (TNM)5 and had a local recurrence 8 weeks after the first surgery. Microscopy of cut sections of the tumour showed the presence of spindle, round, oval and fusiform tumour cells, making histological classification difficult. The prognosis for a stage III disease is about 37% but good results have been achieved by radical orchidectomy with high ligation of the spermatic cord using an inguinal approach and by the use of adjuvant chemotherapy using vincristine, actinomycin-D and cyclophosphamide for up to 18 months. We have added complete excision of the scrotal wall on the affected side because of tumour infiltration due to the recurrence. The value of transillumination as a clinical tool is worth mentioning. In blacks, this physical sign is not a totally reliable test due to skin hyper-pigmentation. However, it should always be carried out as even ifpoor transillumination is obtained it would still differentiate hydrocele from solid testicular and paratesticular tumours. Our patient has had 12 monthly courses of adjuvant chemotherapy without further recurrence. Evaluation of the retroperitoneal lymph nodes could not be carried out due to limited facilities.
