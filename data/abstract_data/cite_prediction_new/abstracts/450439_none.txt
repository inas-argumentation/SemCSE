Statistical analysis comparing these frequencies with those widely accepted in the literature (60%) [3]1, showed a significant P-value in favor of our patients for most of the time points evaluated. At 730 (2 yrs) ± 210 days of follow-up, the 6 remaining patients reported phantom limb sensations, although only two patients complained of actual pain. Statistical analysis of the VAS scores regarding amputation site [above (n = 6) or below the knee] showed no difference in phantom limb pain or in stump pain. Our results suggest that a continuous perioperative sciatic nerve block provides effective analgesia before and after lower limb amputation, and that it may lower the incidence of phantom pain long-term. The present study bears the limitations of an observational, nonrandomized study without a control group.
