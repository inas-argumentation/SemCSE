Management of refractory fluid retention with a combination of L-arginine monohydrochloride and mercurials.
FLUID retention presents a problem in the management of many diverse clinical conditions. It is most commonly associated with congestive heart failure and Laennec's cirrhosis, but may also be present in patients with diffuse or localized carcinomatosis that manifests as pleural effusion, ascites, lymphedema of extremities or generalized edema, and may be very difficult to mobilize. In many cases the fluid retention can be adequately controlled by the administration of mercurial diuretics,1 chlorothiazide and related compounds2 or carbonic anhydrase inhibitors.3 However, not uncommonly patients with fluid retention may not respond to these agents from the beginning or may become refractory . . .
