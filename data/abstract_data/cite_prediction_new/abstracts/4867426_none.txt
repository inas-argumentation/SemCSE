Importantly, researchers from the Pediatric Heart Network have published several studies on longer term growth and neurodevelopmental outcomes from neonates and young infants who were enrolled in the Single Ventricle Reconstruction and the Infant Single Ventricle Trials. In one study, this group found that aggressive nutritional support improved weightfor-age z scores at 14 months follow-up (3). However, neurodevelopmental measures were independently associated with other growth measures, such as head circumference z scores and height trajectory (14). In another study, low weight-for-age z scores had inconsistent risk factors over time suggesting that tailoring nutritional interventions to different stages of palliation and individuals might be the best approach (15). Clearly, growth measures have an important role in the shortand longterm outcomes of infants with CHD and are not just numbers to follow. The detailed work of Li et al (9) highlights this fact and helps guide us in how we might approach these challenges in the ICU.
