In vivo incorporation of dietary ( 14 C)cholesterol in rat serum lipoproteins and transfers in connective tissue.
In rats in isotopically steady state, the incorporation of dietary [14C]cholesterol in α- and β-lipoproteins is very different. β-Lipoproteins incorporate 30 times more of dietary cholesterol than α-lipoproteins. Most of the cholesterol in several connective tissues (skin, aorta, subcutaneous, polyvinyl sponge connective tissues) derives from β-lipoproteins. The turnover of cholesterol in sponge tissue is 2-fold higher than in the aorta, and 4-fold higher than in skin. The experimental conditions presented allow the measurement of the transfer of cholesterol and lipoproteins between blood and connective tissues.
