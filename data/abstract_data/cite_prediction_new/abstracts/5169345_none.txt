Migraine and hemorrhagic stroke: association or causation?
Hemorrhagic stroke can be a devastating disease. Although some risk factors have been defined such as hypertension, much remains to be learned about triggers for this disease and their prevention. Hemodynamic factors, genetics, and systemic diseases will likely form some of the elements of an individualized predictive model. Although building such a multivariate model remains a distant goal because of the daunting task of identifying and quantifying the relative risks of numerous variables, analyzing some of these variables separately might enhance the way we currently look at hemorrhagic stroke prediction and prevention. Migraine has been suspected as a risk factor for hemorrhagic stroke, but a definitive relation has not been clearly established and contradictory results have been published over the past several decades (2-4). To further refine knowledge in this area, Sacco et al. conducted a meta-analysis with the aim to quantify the relationship between migraine and hemorrhagic stroke. This meta-analysis was published in the October 2013 issue of Stroke (1).
