Editorial: BiP, From Putting Out Fires to Fanning the Flames in Rheumatoid Arthritis
RA is an autoimmune disease characterized by chronic inflammation of the synovial lining of joints and systemic manifestations, including the production of autoantibodies (1). Most individuals with genetic susceptibility to RA do not develop the disease, indicating that environmental factors are necessary for a breakdown in immune tolerance. Both cellular and antibody-mediated immune responses contribute to disease pathogenesis in RA. Previous studies have established that autoantibodies to BiP, or immunoglobulin binding protein, are present in a majority of RA patients (2,3). Native or citrullinated BiP protein can serve as an antigen for T cell expansion and the differentiation of T cell effectors (4). The role of BiP in autoreactive immune responses is a subject of controversy, however. On the one hand, administration of BiP has been reported to be of therapeutic benefit (3); however, other studies have shown that BiP drives inflammatory T cell proliferation and differentiation (2,4). In this issue of Arthritis & Rheumatology, Shoda and colleagues set out to identify BiPderived HLA–DR4–restricted epitopes in order to map peptides from BiP antigen that might contain stimulatory properties for inflammatory or regulatory T cell responses in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from RA patients (5).
