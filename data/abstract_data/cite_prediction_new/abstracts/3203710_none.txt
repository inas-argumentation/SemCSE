Multiple Eruptive Dermatofibromas Localized Exclusively to the Buttock
• In May 1989, a 23-year-old woman was admitted to our institute with numerous nodular lesions localized to the left buttock. These lesions were either single or clustered; in particular, they were linearly arranged, tending toward the lumbosacral region. The nodules were round or oval in shape, ranging from 0.7 to 2 cm in diameter, having a color that varied from purplish-red to brown, with poorly defined margins. The surface was smooth or slightly desquamated (Fig. 1). On palpation, the nodules had a parenchymatous-hard consistency; furthermore, lateral compression of each nodule produced a central depression. At history-taking, the patient stated that the lesions appeared eruptively 2 years before, without any preceding trauma, and that they were intensely pruriginous. Family and past medical history did not reveal anything of significance. Laboratory tests and instrumental investigations all gave results within normal ranges. The histopathologic examination showed epidermal hyperplasia with hyperpigmentation of the basal layer. The dermis appeared to be involved in its whole thickness by a proliferation of spindle-shaped elements, which were arranged in fascicles, at times with a whorllike structure (Figs. 2,3).
