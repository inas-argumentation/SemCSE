In utilizing a cost-effectiveness model, and not a cost-utility model, they failed to capture the true impact of alloimmunization on health. The model overestimated the costs of antigen matching and testing, while underestimating the cost savings incurred by continuing transfusion therapy. As a society, a premium is placed on transfusion safety. Prospective matching strategies, at any cost, make transfusions safer for patients with SCD. Caution must be taken if one concludes from the model proposed by Kacker and colleagues that prospective antigen matching is not cost-effective.
