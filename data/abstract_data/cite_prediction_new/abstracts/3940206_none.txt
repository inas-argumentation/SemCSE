Influence of Fibrin Glue on Seroma Formation after Modified Radical Mastectomy: A Prospective Randomized Study
To the Editor: Seroma is an abnormal collection of sterile serous fluid at the operation site beneath the skin flaps (1). The reported incidence of seroma formation varies widely between 15% and 81% following operations for breast carcinoma (2,3). Seroma formation was defined as inability to remove participant drain by postoperative day 10 because of high output (drain seroma) and ⁄ or the need to aspirate of fluid after removal of the drain (4). There are several factors implicated in the formation of seroma after modified radical mastectomy (MRM) because of extensive dissection that damage several blood vessels and lymphatic and the subsequent oozing of blood and lymphatic fluid from a large raw surface area (5,6). Also presence of a large dead space beneath flaps, constant chest wall movement because of respiration, and shoulder use create shearing forces that delay flaps adhesion (7). Many surgical procedures have been proposed to decrease its incidence, such as the use of multiple drains, post-mastectomy shoulder immobilization, compression dressing, sclerotherapy or tetracycline, prolonged suction drainage. The optimal ways to reduce the incidence of seroma formation are unknown (5,8). In animal models and some clinical studies, it has been stated that fibrin glue reduces seroma formation. For this reason, our study was planned to evaluate the effect of fibrin glue on seroma formation and time of drain removal after MRM. Fifty patients were randomized at end of the operation to avoid possible treatment bias, into two groups fibrin studied group and control group. Fibrin glue (8 mL) was sprayed on the surgical area with Y canula in fibrin treated group. Followed by external compression for 10 minutes in both groups. Drains were left in places until the drainage for the preceding 24 hours was less than 30 mL. In the fibrin treated group, the frequency of seroma formation was 4 ⁄ 25 (16%), while it was 9 ⁄ 25 (36%) in control group. The mean duration of axillary drainage was 9.88 ± 1.56 days in fibrin group and 11.62 ± 2.68 days in control group (p = 0.04). The mean total drainage volume was 770.48 ± 70.81 in fibrin treated group and 1089.51 ± 75 in control group. Also the drainage was significantly lower at D1, D3 and D7 in fibrin glue treated group (Table 1). The volume of aspirated fluid after removal of drain was significantly less in fibrin treated group than control group (150 + 75.90 versus 253.51 ± 129.5, p = 0.002).
