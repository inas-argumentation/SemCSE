Equal opportunities in the professions: a research agenda
The paper identifies gendered power relations as a key issue for research into equal opportunities in the professions. It brings research in history and sociology to bear on each other. It argues that from the early modern period particular hegemonic masculinities were institutionalized in the changing power relations of the State, universities and professions, from which women were marginalized as the teachers of the poor. Following a major restructuring of Church, State and university relations, women were admitted to the universities and professions in the late nineteenth century. While the route to professional gentleman through the public schools and universities was institutionalized by the First World War, the position of women changed little, with most graduate women studying in the Arts’ faculties and becoming teachers.
