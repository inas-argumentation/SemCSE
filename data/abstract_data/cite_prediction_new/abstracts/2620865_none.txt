An inducement-contribution approach to corporate financial reporting
The public attitude to companies' responsibilities is changing. They are no longer seen by society as the instruments of their shareholders alone, but as organizations existing within society with responsibilities to society. More particularly, recent legislation and institutional and professional pronouncements are coming to recognise the rights of all organisational participants to corporate reports, and not solely the shareholders. A framework is suggested to accord with these wider responsibilities of corporate reports, in which inducement-contribution theory is used to demonstrate the reality and legitimacy of the organisational rights of all a firm's participants. The concept of organisational surplus is proposed to describe the fruits of the organisational synthesis which, depending on the participants' relative market inducement-contribution positions, may be negotiated for over a period of time. A formal layout for participant's reports is also suggested which clarifies the position for each participant class with regard to the firm as a whole. It is argued that the system proposed would remove the bias inherent in the formal structure of the present reporting system, which implies a special position for equity shareholders that does not accord with socio-economic reality.
