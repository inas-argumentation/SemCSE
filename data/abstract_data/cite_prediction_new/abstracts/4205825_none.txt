Cystathionine β-synthase in the central nervous system of the shore crab Hemigrapsus sanguineus
94 Hydrogen sulfide (H2S) is synthesized endoge nously from L cysteine due to the catalytic activity of cystathionine β synthase (CBS) [1]; along with nitric oxide (II) and carbon monoxide, H2S is one of the gas eous brain neuromodulators of the vertebrate animals and man [2, 3]; these compounds are involved into inter and intracellular regulation of various physio logical functions [4, 5]. Some regulatory functions known in mammals have been recently found out to be also characteristic of invertebrates. Among these func tions is H2S participation in the mechanisms of mem ory and learning [6], which testifies indirectly to H2S functional significance in evolution. However, despite the increasing number of the published data on orga nization of the H2S signaling in the nervous system of vertebrate animals, the evolutionary aspects of this problem remain unstudied. It is still unclear how ancient is this signaling system phylogenetically and what are the principles of organization of this system in the central nervous system (CNS) of invertebrate animals. H2S and sulfides have been identified in peripheral organs and tissues of several taxonomic cell clusters of invertebrate animals [7, 8]. In their nervous system, synthesis of endogenous H2S has been dem onstrated only by the biochemical methods [6]. Any reliable data have not been so far reported on the pres ence and distribution of the H2S synthetizing neurons in the CNS of invertebrate animals.
