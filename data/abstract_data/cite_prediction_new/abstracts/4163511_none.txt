The patient was admitted to the stroke care unit of our hospital and received atorvastatin and aspirin. Pazopanib was discontinued, and the patient left the hospital after 2 days. Discharge medications included as follows: eprosartan 600 mg once daily (QD), aspirin 100 mg QD, and omeprazole 20 mg QD. The patient did not suffer sequels and was advised to keep an active lifestyle. The Naranjo nomogram revealed a probable correlation (score of 5), and the case was reported to the Spanish Pharmacovigilance System. The influence of antiangiogenic drugs in the development of arterial thrombotic events has not been extensively addressed in clinical trials, although recent meta-analysis of randomized control trials with oral antiangiogenic tyrosin-kinase inhibitors showed a significant increase in the risk of arterial thromboembolic events (ATEs).2,3 It has been proposed that these drugs can cause the abnormal apoptosis of the vascular endothelial cells which can activate coagulation in some patients but the exact mechanism remains unknown.1 Cancer itself has also been associated with an increase of the thromboembolic risk, but this remains unclear in sarcoma patients because it has been rarely addressed, although it can be inferred that the endovascular origin of angiosarcoma may play a role.4 Although it has been assumed that low-dose aspirin can be recommended for the prophylaxis of ATEs in high-risk patients (history of ATEs or age .65 years) with a small increase in the risk of bleeding,5 the management of patients with no cardiovascular risk factors arises as a challenge. In conclusion, we report the first case of a soft-tissue sarcoma patient who developed a TIA during treatment with pazopanib. The patient was considered at low risk because no other risk factors (lifestyle or cardiovascular comorbidities) were present. More studies are needed to assess the appropriateness of thromboprophylaxis in patients treated with antiangiogenic agents, where the risk of thrombotic events may be underestimated.
