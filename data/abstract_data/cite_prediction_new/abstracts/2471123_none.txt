Title of Paper - Entrepreneurship and Learning - A Stakeholder Model of Entrepreneurship Research, Education and Training Authors:
Central to the sustainable development of innovation in the new economy is the role of entrepreneurship and learning. Some effort has been made (Drucker, 1996, Castells, 1996, Tapscott, 1997, Scott, et al, 1997, The DTI, UK, 1998, Mitra and Matlay, 2000) to capture the essence of the entrepreneurial and innovation process which underlines the growth of new economy. Yet there is only a limited amount of evidence to comprehend the critical roles played by different stakeholders, especially government, higher education and industry to make strategic and operational connections to sustain the development of the new economy, define the social forms of organisation to make the new economy, and develop appropriate research, education and training programmes to support such effort. This paper is based on on-going research by the two authors on the critical proccessual nodes – learning, innovation and knowledge management– that bring together key stakeholders to foster entrepreneurship. The authors argue that these three nodes interact with each other on a continuous basis through growing, knowledge-based businesses, proactive government and ‘relevant’ research and development and training offered by higher education institutions. The paper identifies issues of ‘connectivity’, ‘convergence’, ‘paradox’ and ‘complexity’ all of which form the essence of the learning process in today’s economic climate. These issues also underlie the relationship between industry, government and higher education and their connected roles in sustaining economic competitiveness and social development. These ideas and concepts inform the development of current and forthcoming entrepreneurship research, education and training programmes managed by the Enterprise Research and Development Centre of the Business School, University of Central England which have ben designed to meet both student and SME learning and developmental needs. Using research evidence the authors posit a theoretical model for the better understanding of the entrepreneurship process, especially entrepreneurship research, education and training in the new economy.
