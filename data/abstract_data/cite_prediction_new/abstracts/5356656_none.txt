Hodgkin transformation of newly diagnosed small lymphocytic lymphoma in the gastrointestinal tract
Hodgkin transformation of chronic lymphocytic leukemia/small lymphocytic lymphoma (CLL/SLL) is a rare but well described entity, usually occurring several years after the diagnosis of CLL/SLL has been made. The most frequent presentation is with B-symptoms and lymph node enlargement and the most common sites of involvement are the lymph nodes and bone marrow. The gastrointestinal tract as the site of transformation has not been reported. We report an 87-year-old man with a WHO performance score of 0 who presented to the emergency department with haematochezia, on a background of known haemorrhoids, previously treated with sclerotherapy. The only notable history was of a low level IgM monoclonal protein, detected many years earlier at another center, which had been thought to represent a monoclonal gammopathy of uncertain significance (MGUS). The paraprotein had been stable over many years. A bone marrow biopsy had never been performed. Examination was reported to be normal except for the presence of internal haemorrhoids on proctoscopy. His full blood count (FBC) demonstrated a normocytic anemia (hemoglobin (Hb) 6.6 g/dL). The lymphocyte, neutrophil, and platelet counts were normal. The patient was transfused on cessation of bleeding and was discharged on oral iron supplements with arrangements made for follow-up in the surgical out-patients. He remained well until 1 year later when he again presented with haematochezia and anemia (Hb 7.8 g/dL); the remainder of the FBC was again normal. On examination he had no evidence of hepatosplenomegaly or palpable adenopathy. A colonoscopy demonstrated haemorrhoids, but no other pathology. A computerized tomographic (CT) scan, however, showed circumferential small bowel wall thickening with marked external iliac and retroperitoneal lymph node enlargement. He subsequently underwent a retroperitoneal lymph node biopsy, under CT guidance, which showed effacement of normal nodal architecture by sheets of small lymphoid cells, which were CD20þ, CD5þ, and CD23þ, consistent with a WHO diagnosis of CLL/SLL. A bone marrow aspirate and trephine at this time was concordant with the lymph node histology. The blood film showed no lymphocytosis. Immunophenotyping of the peripheral blood lymphocytes by flow cytometry was not performed. The patient was transfused and discharged home with planned hematology follow-up for SLL and gastroenterology follow-up for the small bowel thickening found on CT. He was re-presented, however, as an emergency a few weeks later with severe abdominal pain, fever, rigors, and cardiovascular collapse. On examination he had peritonism. Laboratory investigations demonstrated neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and a coagulopathy consistent with disseminated intravascular coagulation. A contrast-enhanced CT of the abdomen showed bowel perforation with free fluid and gas in the peritoneal cavity.
