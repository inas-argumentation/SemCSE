Are antipsychotic drugs potentially chemopreventive agents for cancer?
Sir, Some data in the literature have shown a low occurrence of some types of cancer among schizophrenic patients and, a long-term therapy with high-dose antipsychotic drugs (phenothiazines) seems to account for the only signi®cant association [1, 2]. Most carcinogens require metabolic activation by the host to express optimal mutagenic activity. Hence, the involvement of human cytochrome P450 (CYP) enzymes in the metabolism of environmental toxic compounds such as a ̄atoxin B1 and nitrosamines, found in cooked foods, tobacco smoke, synthetic fuels, and agricultural chemicals, seems to be partly responsible for the association between oxidative capacity and cancer susceptibility in humans [3]. Accordingly, the polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon-inducible CYP1A family, which consists of the structurally related isozymes CYP1A1 and CYP1A2, metabolically activates a large number of procarcinogens to reactive intermediates that can interact with cellular nucleophiles and can ultimately trigger carcinogenesis [4]. However, epidemiological studies have shown that frequent consumption of fruit and vegetables is associated with low risk of various cancers [5]. This protective eect has been attributed in part to ̄avonoids, mainly through interaction with the CYP system to reduce the activation of procarcinogenic substrates to carcinogens [6]. Indeed, the dietary constituent resveratrol is claimed to act as a protective agent against cancer [7]. Conversely, the hypothesis of a chemopreventive eect for cancer based on inhibition of human CYP metabolic activities by drugs has received little attention in the literature. Nevertheless, the polymorphic CYP2D6 is involved, at least in part, in the metabolism of most antipsychotic drugs so far investigated, namely, haloperidol, perphenazine, thioridazine, zuclopenthixol, remoxipride, sertindole, risperidone and olanzapine among others [8, 9]. Additionally, the role of the human CYP1A2 in psychopharmacology is increasingly acknowledged, and this enzyme participates in the metabolism of the antipsychotic drugs clozapine, olanzapine and probably thioridazine [9, 10]. Antipsychotic drugs are often used for years, and enzymatic inhibition does not disappear even during long-term administration. Therefore, a putative mechanism of chemoprevention in psychiatric patients may be related to the modulation by antipsychotic drugs of CYP enzymes involved in mutagen activation and also elimination. To test whether olanzapine, an atypical antipsychotic, is also an inhibitor of CYP1A2 activity, we conducted a study in eight schizophrenic patients (four males and four females), aged 24±70 years [11].
