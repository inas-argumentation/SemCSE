Radiation chemistry. V. Some aspects of the biochemical effects of ionizing radiations.
Instead of speaking on selected details of the biological and biochemical effects of ionizing radiations I thought it might be more appropriate to give you a brief account of how research on this problem has developed in the Christie Hospital during the last decade. When I entered this branch of science the general view was prevalent that only the cell nucleus was responsible for the effects of radiation. All attempts at linking up isolated systems and substances and metabolic changes with radiation effects gave either equivocal or negative results. In particular, enzymes were definitely ruled out from having anything to do with radiation effects because they had proved to be by far too insensitive. In spite of this discouraging verdict I wished to convince myself how far this claim of the resistance of enzymes was true.
