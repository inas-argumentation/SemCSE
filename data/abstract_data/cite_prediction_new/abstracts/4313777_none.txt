Center‐Based Differences in Liver Quality: Why Do They Exist?
Deciding whether to accept a particular donor liver for an individual patient occurs many times daily all around the country. Often the decision is relatively straightforward, particularly if the liver is of high quality. However, if the liver is of suboptimal quality, the decision is more complex with consideration first as to whether the organ is usable and second whether the organ is appropriate for the designated recipient. In this issue, Volk et al. (1) report on organ utilization patterns deciphered by analysis of the Scientific Registry of Transplant Recipients database. They found that donor quality, as estimated by the donor risk index (DRI) (2) varied widely across transplant centers in the United States. The variability was greatly but incompletely explained by differences in recipient disease severity, as estimated by the Model of End-stage Liver Disease (MELD) score, the United Network for Organ (UNOS) region or the organ procurement organization. The authors identified modest but significant differences at the level of the transplant center. Large volume centers and centers with local competition were more likely to utilize higher DRI livers and placed them preferentially into lower MELD recipients. The question is why do transplant center characteristics drive differences in organ utilization and donor– recipient match practices?
