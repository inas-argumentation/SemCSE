An epidemiologic study of breast cancer and benign breast neoplasias in relation to the oral contraceptive and estrogen use.
A retrospective case-control study of oral contraceptive (OC) and other estrogen use in malignant or benign breast disease patients less than 65 years of age was conducted. Ever-usage of OC and estrogens was slightly less common among breast cancer patients up to 49 years of age compared with their controls. In older patients no differences in estrogen use was seen. Cystic disease patients had significantly lower rates of ever- or long-term OC usage; this was not seen with cases of fibroadenoma. There was a considerably higher proportion of ever pregnant and ever parous women among patients with cystic disease and fibroadenoma. Menopausal women were less frequent than expected among cystic disease patients. Factors other than menopause ordinarily associated with breast cancer were not found associated with benign breast neoplasia. Though these findings indicate a decreased risk of benign breast neoplasias and no relation to breast cancer risk with OC use the results of other studies suggesting increased risk of developing benign and malignant breast neoplasms in long-term users of estrogen-containing preparations call for continued monitoring of the effects of these preparations.
