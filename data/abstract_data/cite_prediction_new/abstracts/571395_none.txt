Enzyme Activity as a Function of Age in the Human Erythrocyte
SABINE (1940, 1951) showed that the cholinesterase activity of erythrocytes containing a high proportion of reticulocytes was higher than normal. She also showed that the greater the proportion of reticulocytes, the greater was the enzyme activity. A similar correlation between the erythrocyte cholinesterase activity in the rat and the percentage of reticulocytes has also been reported by Pritchard (1949). These observations can be taken to mean that the cholinesterase activity is at a high level at the reticulocyte stage and subsequently falls to a lower level in mature erythrocytes. In this paper evidence is presented which supports the theory that the enzyme activity of human erythrocytes is a function of their age. Erythrocytes contain a number of enzymes, and three which are present in high concentration and easy to measure are cholinesterase, catalase and glyoxalase. First, we have studied the activities of these three enzymes in the erythrocytes of normal human subjects in order to distinguish between three components of variance: the error of estimation, the variation of activity in any one subject at different times, and the variation between different subjects. Secondly, Sabine’s work has been confirmed and extended to include catalase and glyoxalase, and an attempt has been made to calculate the absolute enzyme activity of reticulocytes. Thirdly, the change in cholinesterase and catalase activity of donor erythrocytes has been measured after transfusion, the donor cells being identified by differential agglutination.
