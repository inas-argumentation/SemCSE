However, it is the second most frequent thromboembolic complication of nephrotic syndrome in children and was found in 25–30% of children who underwent systematic ventilation–perfusion scintigraphy. The treatment should consist of anti-thrombotic agents alongside with immunosuppressive drugs. In children over 2 months of age with an initial thromboembolism, treatment with intravenous heparin or LMWH is recommended. Continuing anticoagulant therapy is suggested for at least 6 months using vitamin K antagonists, or alternatively LMWH. Treatment needs to be individualised. Thrombolytic therapy is recommended only in cases of bilateral RVT with various degrees of renal failure. Intravenous heparin can be also combined with AT-III concentrate. Long-term anticoagulation with oral warfarin has been also reported as beneficial in paediatric SLE patients. In conclusion, thromboembolism can be one of the first manifestations of SLE, particularly when nephrotic syndrome occurs. Paediatricians, rheumatologists and nephrologists should be aware of this fact. Prompt diagnosis, and rapid and effective treatment are essential for patient prognosis.
