Coaching early‐career educators in the health professions
Editors’ note: Clinical educators (or teachers) come from a wide range of professions, disciplines and backgrounds. Many fi nd themselves involved in education almost by chance – being in the right place at the right time – without any specifi c career expectations or goals. Others choose an academic path or pursue an interest in education because of past experiences, role models and the joy of interacting with motivated learners. This toolbox article acknowledges this diversity and, although addressed to potential and actual coaches of early-career educators (ECEs), should be of interest to ECEs themselves. It offers practical guidance on what coaching entails, and has a set of pertinent case studies that illustrate that ‘one size does not fi t all’. ECEs may be new to a particular clinical environment or they may have practised in that space for some time; however, as in other situations of transition and new responsibilities, orientation is important for everyone, together with an understanding of what the new role entails. The authors recommend regular meetings between coach and ECE, with discussion of professional development opportunities and de-briefi ng. An experienced clinical educator who takes on the coaching of a less-experienced colleague is likely to benefi t not only the ECEs but also, indirectly, the learners.
