The effect of moisture and storage temperature on a Salmonella senftenberg 775W population in meat and bone meal.
SALMONELLAE have frequently been found as contaminants in animal byproducts, other feed ingredients, and in mixed feeds (Allred et al., 1967; and Morehouse and Wedman, 1961). Little information is available on the interrelationship between storage temperature and moisture level of these dry feed products and contaminating salmonella populations. High water activity (aw) of a substrate (Scott, 1953), denned as the relative humidity (RH) of an atmosphere with which a product is in equilibrium, is required for microbial multiplication. Scott (1953) noted that 14 strains of Staphylococcus aureus grew in media having aw’s of 0.860 or greater at 30°C. However, the growth rate and cell yield were reduced substantially when the aw was less than 0.940. In a similar study, Christian and Scott (1953) reported that salmonellae grew in liquid media having aw’s between 0.945 and 0.995, while in foods the aw required for growth was slightly less.
