Long-term tamoxifen therapy: can an antagonist become an agonist?
Antiestrogen administration is standard therapy for selected groups of women with breast cancer in the adjuvant setting (1). Large, well designed, randomized, controlled trials have confirmed the benefit of tamoxifen in extending both disease-free and overall survival when used for up to 5 yr (2). The question remains whether longer-term administration might produce even greater benefit. A major trial in the United States, the NSABP B-14 study, was designed to answer this question and compared 5 with 10 yr of tamoxifen administration. Recently (November 30, 1995) the National Cancer Institute mailed a Clinical Announcement to 22,000 physicians indicating that this study did not find 10 yr superior to 5 yr, and consequently women should receive tamoxifen for no more than 5 yr (3). Surprisingly, patients receiving tamoxifen for 10 yr experienced a shorter disease free survival than women given the drug for only 5 yr. This National Cancer Institute (NCI) announcement also reviewed a smaller unpublished Scottish trial showing nearly identical results. The Clinical Announcement commented that the “troublesome possibility that continuation of tamoxifen beyond 5 yr may actually be detrimental cannot be unequivocally resolved by these data.” If a detrimental effect is ultimately substantiated, the question arises whether tamoxifen can be transformed from an antagonist to an estrogen agonist on breast cancer tissue with long-term use. Precedent for this phenomenon derives from studies in model systems of breast cancer. Tamoxifen is an antiestrogen initially utilized in the treatment of recurrent or advanced hormone-dependent breast cancer. Randomized clinical trials demonstrated efficacy equal to other hormonal therapies, a lack of significant side effects, and ease of administration. Additional trials then examined whether tamoxifen might be useful in the adjuvant setting to prevent or delay recurrence of breast cancer after initial surgical therapy. Meta-analyses of multiple studies comparing placebo with 2 yr of adjuvant tamoxifen clearly demonstrated benefit with extended disease-free survival as well as an increase in overall survival (2). The striking finding was that administration of tamoxifen for only two yr resulted in benefits to patients that lasted at least 15 yr. Seven percent more women receiving tamoxifen were alive 15 yr after starting therapy than those given placebo. These findings suggested that with nearly 200,000 women diagnosed with breast cancer per year, the number of lives saved with adjuvant tamoxifen could be substantial.
