International collaborative study to establish the World Health Organization 2nd International Standard for Fibrinogen Concentrate (09/242): communication from the SSC of the ISTH
The 1st International Standard (IS) for Fibrinogen Concentrate (98/614) was established by the World Health Organization (WHO) in 2000, with an assigned clottable protein potency of 10.4 mg per ampoule and an assigned total protein potency of 15.0 mg per ampoule [1], in order to standardize potency estimations of fibrinogen in plasma-derived therapeutic concentrates, which are primarily used in the treatment of bleeding in patients with congenital or acquired fibrinogen deficiencies [2,3], or are components of fibrin sealants routinely used as hemostatic agents [4,5]. Because of low stock numbers, an international collaborative study was organized to calibrate a replacement for the 1st IS, with the primary objective of a robust transference of the thrombin clottable fibrinogen (clottable protein) and total protein unitages between the 1st IS and the proposed replacement 2nd IS. A follow-up field study was also carried out in order to assess the suitability of the new standard, and the present article describes the value assignment of the replacement preparation, using data from both studies.
