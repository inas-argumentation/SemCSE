A 0.7-V 0.43-pJ/cycle Wakeup Timer Based on a Bang-Bang Digital-Intensive Frequency-Locked-Loop for IoT Applications
A 40-nm CMOS wakeup timer employing a bang-bang digital-intensive frequency-locked loop for Internet-of-Things applications is presented. A self-biased <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$\Sigma \Delta $ </tex-math></inline-formula> digitally controlled oscillator (DCO) is locked to an RC time constant via a single-bit chopped comparator and a digital loop filter. Such highly digitized architecture fully exploits the advantages of advanced CMOS processes, thus enabling operation down to 0.7 V and a small area (0.07 mm<sup>2</sup>). Most circuitry operates at 32<inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$\times $ </tex-math></inline-formula> lower frequency than the DCO in order to reduce the total power consumption down to 181 nW. High frequency accuracy and a <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$10 \times $ </tex-math></inline-formula> enhancement of long-term stability is achieved by the adoption of chopping to reduce the effect of comparator offset and 1/f noise and by the use of <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$ \Sigma \Delta $ </tex-math></inline-formula> modulation to improve the DCO resolution. The proposed timer achieves the best energy efficiency (0.43 pJ/cycle at 417 kHz) over prior art while keeping excellent on-par long-term stability (Allan deviation floor <20 ppm) and temperature stability (106 ppm/°C).
