Nerve root and sciatic trunk enlargement in Déjérine-Sottas disease: MRI appearances
A 23-year-old female presented with slowly progressive weakness of all limbs since infancy. There were severe predominantly distal weakness and atrophy of the upper limbs and slight weakness of the lower limbs. All tendon reflexes were absent. The family history was negative for neuromuscular disease. The cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) protein was elevated (154 mg/dl). Right median and ulnar motor nerve conduction velocities were 6.1 and 7.4 m/s. Sural nerve biopsy confirmed loss of myelinated fibres (2303/mm 2) and showed typical onion bulb formations. The chest radiograph revealed bilateral rounded soft tissue density masses at the apices (Fig. 1). MRI showed marked enlargement of cervical and lumbar spinal nerve roots and revealed that the masses seen in the chest X-P were enlarged nerve roots; it also demonstrated enlarged sciatic trunks (Fig. 2). Nerve biopsies of the enlarged portions were not performed. Fig. 1. Bilateral rounded soft tissue density masses (arrows) at the apices
