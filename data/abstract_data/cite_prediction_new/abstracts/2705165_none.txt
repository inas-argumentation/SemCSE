Potato production in the context of world and farm economy
The potato is a relatively stable part of the diet of European and North American people. As incomes have risen, the consumption of potatoes per head has remained constant or declined slowly. At the same time, history as well as household food expenditure surveys, show that the poorer section of the community obtain a greater proportion of their calories from potatoes than do richer people. Indeed, the ability of the potato to support a large number of people on a small acreage of land is a means of withstanding population pressure. In prosperous times accompanied by yield increasing technology then the acreage of potato shrinks, leaving land for use in some more profitable way. At the same time, capital intensive technology, which is both a cause of and a response to a reduction in the farm labour force, produces larger and more specialized production units. The same phenomenon can also be observed in processing units as higher incomes increase the demand for more convenience foods so that the potato has more services added to it before it reaches the housewife.
