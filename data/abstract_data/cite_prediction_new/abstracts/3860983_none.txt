The Home-Based Businesses of Madison County: Creating Categories and Assessing Local Mechanisms for Local Self-Development
Home-based businesses as a strategy for local self-development are explored here. A two-year qualitative study of home-based businesses in Madison County (NY) was used as a case study to raise a number of theoretical and substantive questions about home-based businesses. We argue that the home-based business sector consists of five types of businesses: (1) professional service entrepreneurs (2) manual labor services; (3) light-manufacturers, (4) crafters, and (5) direct consumer sales. We conclude by arguing that home-based businesses are worth studying because they can be a viable part of rural self-development strategies. However, the home-based business sector is diversified, and hence development strategies will typically have to be multi-tiered. In making this argument, we also raise a number of methodological questions about the challenge of studying home-based businesses.
