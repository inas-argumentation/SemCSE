Cortisol secretion in acidotic and nonacidotic juvenile diabetes mellitus.
An attempt was made to determine whether increased cortisol production contributes to the pathogenesis of juvenile diabetes mellitus. Cortisol secretion rates (CSR) were estimated by the in vivo isotope dilution method in 10 children with newly diagnosed diabetes, who were not acutely ill and whose venous blood pH was 7.33 and plasma CO 2 content was 20 mM. per liter. The mean and range of their cortisol secretion rates were no different from those of normal control children. Hyperadrenocorticism apparently plays no role in the pathogenesis of juvenile diabetes mellitus. However, newly diagnosed diabetic children who were acutely ill in diabetic acidosis (venous blood pH ⪯7.30 and plasma CO 2 content ⪯19 mM. per liter) had elevated cortisol secretion rates.
