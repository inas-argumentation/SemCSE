Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons in oysters from coastal waters of the Lebanon 10 months after the Jiyeh oil spill in 2006.
In July 2006, the power station at Jiyeh on the coast of the Lebanon was attacked by the Israeli military. More than 10,000 tonnes of heavy fuel oil were released into the Mediterranean Sea, causing a slick which polluted ca. 120 km of coastline to the north of the plant, as far as the border with Syria (UNDP/MoE, 2007). Some of the oil sank, carpeting the seabed and causing concern about possible long-term damage to fisheries and the environment generally. An additional 55,000 tonnes of fuel oil was thought to have burned in the tank farm adjoining the power station, depositing oil droplets in a 5 km radius around Jiyeh (Steiner, 2006). During 10–12 May 2007, approximately 300 days after the spill, samples of oysters (Ostrea edulis) were collected from six locations along the Lebanese coast (two to the south of Jiyeh and four to the north) and analysed for total hydrocarbons and a range of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons. A total of 15 wild oysters were collected from each location, comprising three replicates of five individual oysters from each of the six sites.
