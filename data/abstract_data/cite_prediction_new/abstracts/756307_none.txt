Suppurative peripheral arthritis in inflammatory bowel disease.
Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is a chronic condition affecting the gastrointestinal tract, resulting from environmental triggers in a genetically susceptible host (1). Crohn disease (CD) is characterized by transmural granulomatous inflammation that may affect any region of the gastrointestinal tract, whereas ulcerative colitis (UC) is characterized by mucosal inflammation spreading from the rectum proximally. Extraintestinal manifestations are common in both conditions and may precede the onset of intestinal symptoms. Arthritis has been reported as the most common of these manifestations and may be divided into peripheral arthritis, sacroiliitis, and ankylosing spondylitis (2,3). Peripheral arthritis generally affects the large joints of the lower extremities (knees, ankles) with a benign clinical course. Few reports in the literature have described suppurative arthritis as a manifestation of IBD, and reports of erosive arthritis involving the hips are rare.
