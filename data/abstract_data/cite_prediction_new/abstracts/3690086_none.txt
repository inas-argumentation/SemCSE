Evaluation of the pancreaticobiliary ductal systems by intraductal US.
The technical evolution of EUS has led to the development of small-diameter (~2 mm) intraductal ultrasound (IDUS) probes that can be passed through standard endoscopes directly into the bile and pancreatic ducts. IDUS probes are useful for evaluating a variety of biliary and pancreatic disorders (Table 1). Acoustic coupling is optimized by the tubular anatomy of both ducts, which are fluid filled and normally only slightly larger in diameter than the probe itself. These probes image at higher frequencies (12-30 MHz) than dedicated echoendoscopes and therefore provide greater image resolution (0.07-0.18 mm) compared with dedicated echoendoscopes.1 Experience with IDUS was first reported by Martin et al.2 and Silverstein et al.3 in 1989. The small diameter, flexibility, and image quality offered by these devices makes them ideal for evaluating a variety of biliary and pancreatic diseases, luminal stenoses in particular.
