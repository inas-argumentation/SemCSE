EFFECT OF REGIONAL CHARACTERISTICS ON CRASH INJURY 1 SEVERITY OF LOCAL BUSES USING A HIERARCHICAL ORDERED 2 MODEL 3 4
1 As the importance of public transportation increases, the management of bus-involved crashes 2 has become a crucial issue for traffic safety. Currently, however, there are relatively few studies 3 on crash severity for buses in Korea. The present study investigated factors influencing injury 4 severity of local bus-involved crashes identified based on using commercial vehicle crash data 5 for a five-year period from 2010 to 2014 in Korea. In order to determine unobserved regional 6 effects on crash severity, a hierarchical ordered model was applied to the analysis. Individual 7 crash characteristics were set to lower level variables and regional characteristics were adopted 8 as upper level variables. At the lower level, the factors affecting severity of local bus injury 9 included vehicle speed, vehicle age, road alignment, surface status, road class and traffic light 10 installation as found in previous studies. At the upper level, ratio the factors included road 11 pavement, emergent medical environment, traffic rate of compliance and ratio of elderly in the 12 community. There was a 5.1% unobserved variation between regions from the intraclass 13 correlation analysis. The validity of a hierarchical model for local bus crashes was verified by 14 applying the same model on other types of long-distance buses and it appeared that there were no 15 regional effects. This study found a regional effect for local bus crash severity and thus this 16 factor is important when developing prevention plans to reduce local bus crashes. These results 17 contribute to the study of traffic safety. 18 19
