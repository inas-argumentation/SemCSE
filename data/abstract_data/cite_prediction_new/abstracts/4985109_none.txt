The response of desalivate rats to a time-dependent food reinforcement schedule
Eight experimentally naive female rats, made desalivate by surgical ligation of the salivary ducts, were exposed to a time-dependent food reinforcement schedule in which one Noyes 45 mg food pellet was presented each 60 sec to each animal, independently of the behavior in which the animal happened to be engaged. Relative to pre and postexperimental condition baselines, all animals increased their water consumption in the time-dependent food reinforcement schedule condition. The increase in water consumption was attributable to an interaction between number of food pellets consumed prior to the initiation of a drink burst, and to the frequency with which drink bursts were initiated. These results were interpreted to indicate that the instatement of schedule-induced polydipsia is primarily attributable to the eat-then-drink sequence of behavior characteristic of both normal and desalivate rats, and that the maintenance of schedule-induced polydipsia is the result of the experimenter-imposed determination of meal size under which the rat must labor. It was hypothesized that schedule-induced hyperphagia should be as readily produced in species that normally exhibit drink-then-eat sequences of behavior as is polydipsia in animals that normally engage in eat-then-drink behavioral sequences.
