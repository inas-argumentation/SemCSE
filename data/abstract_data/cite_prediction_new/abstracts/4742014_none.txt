New Compact Accommodometer to Measure Accommodation Amplitude.
To the Editor: I de et al 1 evaluated a compact design of accommodometer in an observational case series. The authors highlight that the major advantages of this accommodometer are its size, fast speed, and convenience of use in clinical settings. Further, they also compared compact accommodometer with the conventional accommodometer in their study. Overall, with the accuracy of adjustment within 1 cm, compact accommodometer seems to be a promising device for ophthalmic diagnosis. However, a few points need to be clarified. It is noteworthy that an accommodometer is a subjective instrument used for the measurement of accommodative amplitude. It would therefore be prudent to discuss the validity of measurements by the conventional accommodometer. Did the authors use any rationale in obtaining 3 measurements in every case? It is known that an accommodometer does not offer high reproducibility because it relies on the subjective responses of the patients. Reproducibility data would be desirable for any such instrument. Alternatively, both subjective and objective assessments of accommodation would be ideal for confirming the authenticity of the results. Moreover, although the authors have mentioned that there was a statistically significant difference in measured accommodative amplitude between both instruments, they did not discuss the probable reason for the same. This difference may be attributed to the disparity in instrument design, range of measurement, or relation to the learning curve. Also, in the second part of the study where the authors have measured difference in accommodation between smokers and nonsmokers, comparison data between the 2 machines would have been more useful as well as convincing. With different study populations in 2 studies, the evidence becomes weaker as acknowledged by the authors. It is therefore imperative that further studies with a larger sample size are planned to validate the data generated from compact accommodometers. Future studies can also look at the comparison of measurement of accommodative amplitude between compact accommodometer and other diagnostic modalities.
