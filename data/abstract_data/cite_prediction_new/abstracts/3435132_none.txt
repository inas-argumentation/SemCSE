Economics of Globalization in Higher Education: Current Issues in Recruiting and Serving International Students
The internationalization of higher education refers to the process of integrating an international, intercultural, and global dimension into the mission, goals, and delivery of higher education. The goals of internationalization are diverse, ranging from increased awareness of the mobility and educational needs of international students to the need for enhanced cooperation among nations and postsecondary institutions in developing more internationally-focused curricula and improving the capacity, knowledge production, and research capabilities of higher education systems within developing countries. A number of barriers to the achievement of these broad higher education goals have emerged in recent years including challenges in increasing international student recruitment, institutional competition regarding the push for increased international academic prestige, rapid expansion without concern for quality, and “brain drain.” This chapter examines some of these issues with the goal of fostering campus leaders committed to creating institutional climates that promote collaboration, increased access, exchange, and preparing young adults who are more globally and culturally aware of economic, political, social, and historical issues.
