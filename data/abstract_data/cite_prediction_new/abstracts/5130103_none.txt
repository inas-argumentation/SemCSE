Treasury Rules OK? The Further Evolution of a British Institution
Her Majesty’s Treasury is like an old-fashioned villain in an Edwardian melodrama—booed whenever it makes an appearance on stage in suitably dark and suspicious black, and thereafter assumed to be the culprit behind all unpopular or cataclysmic events. This image has a long pedigree. In W. H. Greenleaf’s monumental study of British politics he devotes a chapter to the ‘Department of Departments’ (Greenleaf 1987) responsible for managing the rise of collectivism and in constant struggle for control of Whitehall. Do recent studies of the Treasury and its political masters, chancellors of the exchequer, cast new light and insights into a department which continues to fascinate? BJPIR: 2004 VOL 6, 121–128
