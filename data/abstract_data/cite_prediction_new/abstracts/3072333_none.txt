D-Aspartic Acid Regulates the Release and Synthesis of LH and Testosterone in Human
Summary Background: D-Asp is an endogenous amino acid present in nervous and endocrine tissues. During the embryonic life, it is involved in the development of the nervous system, whereas in the adult animal it is implicated in the synthesis and release of hormones from major endocrine glands, such as pineal, pituitary and testes. Methods: A group of 12 healthy men, aged 27-67 years was administered an oral solution of sodium Daspartate every morning after breakfast for 12 days. Similarly, another group of 10 healthy men, aged 25-64 years, received the same drug but without sodium D-aspartate (placebo). Serum LH and testosterone baseline levels were determined before the beginning of treatment and 1, 5, 10 and 20 days after the end of treatment. Comparative in vivo and in vitro experiments were conducted on rats to assess the amount and duration of D-Asp accumulated in testes and other tissues and the molecular mechanisms of action. Findings: Eleven out of 12 patients (91.66%) showed a significant increase in serum LH and testosterone levels after treatment with sodium D-aspartate. LH increased by 46% (P<0.0001) on day 1 and by 14.4% (P<0.001) on day 5 after the end of treatment. Testosterone increased by 45.5% on day 1 (P<0.00001), by 30.6% on day 5 (P<0.0001) and by 12.8% on day 10 (P=0.001). No substantial increases in LH and testosterone were observed in the placebo. Experiments on rats demonstrated that D-Asp had the same effect as in human in inducing LH and testosterone synthesis and release. Indeed, its presence was still evidenced in the pituitary and in testes 5 and 10 days, respectively, after the end of treatment, thus inducing an increase in LH and testosterone. Lastly, D-Asp also acted on the Leydig cells, eliciting not only the synthesis of testosterone but also an increase in the activity of cytochrome P-450, the enzyme that converts testosterone in 17β-estradiol. Interpretation: D-aspartic acid is implicated in the regulation of the release and synthesis of LH and testosterone in human and in the activation of aromatase activity.
