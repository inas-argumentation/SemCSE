Diabetic retinopathy studied by fluorescein angiography.
THE INTRODUCTION OF RETINAL ANGIOGRAPHY has given the physician the opportunity to study and permanently record morphology and physiology in the living ocular vascular system. Specifically, flow and permeability in the retinal and choroidal vessels can now be correlated with anatomical changes. Other publications have described the normal fluorescein pattern," 9 the changes that occur in mass lesions of the macular hypertension,5 and occlusive disease of the carotid and retinal vessels.9 The purpose of this paper is to report our preliminaiy observations in the study of a small of diabetic patients, to compare these observations with those of others, and to indicate the possible value of this technique in the study of diabetic retinopathy.
