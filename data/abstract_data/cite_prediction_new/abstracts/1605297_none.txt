Letter to the Editor: Seasonal Pattern of Births in Patients with Optic Neuritis
Monosymptomatic optic neuritis (MON) is an acute inflammatory demyelinating disease of the optic nerve, with acute onset of symptoms and remission occurring spontaneously or following corticosteroid therapy. In most cases MON is considered idiopathic, and after the exclusion of various vascular, hereditary, metabolic, toxic, infectious, inflammatory, or neoplastic etiologies (Rodriguez et al., 1995). As many as 38% of patients with idiopathic MON will develop multiple sclerosis (MS) within 10 yr (Beck et al., 2003). MS appears to be a complex trait with susceptibility controlled by genetic and as yet unknown environmental factors, with the environment influencing risk on a population level (Marrie, 2004). Several studies have addressed the issue of whether the life-time risk of developing MS might be influenced by season of birth. Indeed, cumulative available evidence indicates an excess of births in spring, specifically in May, and a deficit of births in November, among MS patients (Sadovnick and Yee, 1994; Templer et al., 1992; Torrey et al., 2000; Willer et al., 2005). The twofold excess of concordance found in dizygotic twin over non-twin siblings (Willer et al., 2003) and a maternal parent-of-origin effect in MS susceptibility (Ebers et al., 2004) also may be indicative of an environmental factor effective at the time of conception or during gestation. The seasonal birth pattern of MS patients in a German population and, more specifically, of MON has not been previously investigated. We retrospectively evaluated the birth dates of 308 MON patients (286 men and 22 women), mean age (+SD) 34+ 19 yr, referred between 1995 and 2004 to the Department of Neurology at the University of Regensburg, a tertiary care center serving eastern Bavaria. Prior to analysis, the frequency of births per month of the patients was normalized (number of MON births multiplied by 100,000/number of total births) to the overall birth rates per month in Germany (Federal Statistical Office, Wiesbaden, Germany). Month of birth was allocated to seasons (Winter: December, January, February; Spring: March, April, May; Summer: June, July, August: Fall: September, October, November), and median and non-outlier extreme values were calculated.
