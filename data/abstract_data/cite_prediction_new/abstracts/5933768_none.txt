INFLUENCE OF MICROHABITAT HETEROGENEITY ON GENE FREQUENCY DISTRIBUTION AND GAMETIC PHASE DISEQUILIBRIUM IN AVENA BARBATA
Since its introduction into California some two-hundred years ago the slender wild oat (Avena barbata) has become established in a wide variety of habitats. While it is generally restricted to regions with a Mediterranean climate, A. barbata may be found on sites whose environments range from rich, mesic agricultural conditions to infertile, xeric ridgetop conditions. In some areas such as the Napa Valley of Northern California A. barbata may be found growing in environmentally diverse habitats which are located in close physical proximity to one another. Using five genetically determined allozyme loci, Hamrick and Allard (1972) demonstrated that spatial variation in allele frequencies was closely associated with environmental variation in Napa Valley populations of A. barbata.
