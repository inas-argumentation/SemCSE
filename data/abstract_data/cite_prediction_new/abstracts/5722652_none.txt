Re: Optimal Settings for the Noncontact Holmium:YAG Stone Fragmentation Popcorn Technique.
The authors assessed various laser parameters to determine the optimal settings for stone fragmentation in the “popcorn” procedure. They performed an experimental study with artificial hard stones of 4–5 mm in size and 1900 HU in density. For each test, four stones were placed in a special tube and treated with a flexible ureteroscope through an access sheath with a view of performing “popcorn” fragmentation. Tests were done with 273and 365-mm laser fibers for a period of 2–4 min and using various combinations of parameters (5–60 W, 0.5–40 Hz) associated with a short or long laser pulse. Stones were weighed afterwards on an electronic balance, and a median stone reduction of 50% was the criterion for success. The factors that were significant in predicting successful “popcorn” fragmentation were a longer time, high energy, a long pulse, a frequency of 20 or 40 Hz, and the use of 273-mm fibers. Multivariate analysis demonstrated that the best combination associated with success was a small laser fiber size, a longer pulse, and a longer working time.
