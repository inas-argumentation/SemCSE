X-linked recessive inheritance of IHP seemed well established, although none of the affected male subjects had children so thai mate-to-male transmission could be excluded or their daughters could be shown to be carriers. Peden suggested that sex-linked transmission might be the most common pattern of inheritance tbr IHP presenting in infancy. Our findings support Peden's conclusions. X-linked recessive inheritance seems certain for IHP in the kindred reported herein. Although we too did not have the opportunity to observe absence of male-to-male transmission or an obligate carrier state for IHP in the daughters of affected men to support sex-linked inheritance, IHP was confirmed in male members of three generations and almost certainly affected infant boys in four generations. There was no history of seizures or other problems to suggest IHP in female family members. Normal circulating calcium, inorganic phosphate, and immunoreactive parathyroid hormone values in the propositi's mother further supported sex-linked recessive inheritance; her unremarkable biochemical values and normal X chromosome morphology suggest that carriers cannot be readily identified. Provocative tests, however, such as ethylenediamine tetraacetic acid infusion, 4 were not used to study parathyroid hormone reserve.
