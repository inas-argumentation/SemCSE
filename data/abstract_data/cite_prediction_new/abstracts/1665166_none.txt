Metabolism rate, biochemical and endocrine alterations in male whirler mice.
The present investigation reports differences in locomotor activity, behavior, metabolism rates, white blood cell counts, biochemistry and endocrine function of recessive, homozygous male whirler mice vs. the phenotypically “normal” heterozygous littermates. Homozygous whirlers, a waltzing-type mutation, show syndromes of rapid, circling locomotor activity, head-shaking and deafness. The mice are extremely nervous and excitable. The 2 male groups were obtained from matings of phenotypically “normal” heterozygous females to whirler males. Various studies were undertaken at 12–16 weeks of age. Body weights of the whirler mice were consistently and significantly lower than comparative values of the heterozygous mice. At 12 weeks, locomotor activity was significantly higher in the whirlers. At the 13th and 14th weeks, oxygen consumption rates obtained respectively under free-moving and semi-restrained conditions were significantly higher. Total leukocyte counts in the whirlers were significantly lower at the 15th and at 16 weeks. Autopsy after rapid decapitation, revealed significant increases in the relative adrenal and heart weights but seminal vesicle weights were significantly lower. Biochemical analyses revealed significantly higher levels of plasma and adrenal corticosterone in the whirler mice sacrificed at 16 weeks. Conversely, liver glycogen and liver phosphorylase values were significantly decreased. Plasma glucose levels were markedly lower ( p = 0.07). The present investigation confirms prior findings of heightened metabolism rates, adrenocortical activity and hypogonadal function in the whirler vs. heterozygous mice. The significant and/or marked decreases in liver glycogen, liver phosphorylase and plasma glucose indicate profound differences in the carbohydrate metabolism and utilization processes of the homozygous whirlers. These alterations, among other possibilities, are most likely a function of the frequent spontaneous bursts of exercise, increased excitability and heightened metabolism rates of the whirler mice.
