Evolution of Labor Productivity in Industry Following Economic Reforms: A Comparative Structural Analysis between India and Mexico
By constructing a database with annual series for production, value added, employment and labor productivity on 90 categories from 1994 to 2007 in Mexico's case, and from 1998 to 2007 with 123 categories (at four digits of the ISIC rev. 3) for India, this article compares the structural transformations that took place in the industrial sector of these countries after similar economic reforms. The results show a relative process of restructuring and productive modernization in India with a certain time lag and a more questionable evolution in Mexico due to an excessive specialization dependent on multinational dynamics. The calculations corroborate also what other authors have demonstrated for both cases: widening disparities among the various industries as well as a concentration of modernization in a reduced number of activities. Finally, a shift-share type methodology reveals that in neither case we can confirm the existence of a structural change bolstering efficiency levels.
