AN ANALYSIS OF SEASONAL PRICING SCHEMES FOR MANUFACTURING MILK
The seasonal supply pattern of milk is determined by the typical lactation curve of the cow and the calving pattern of dairy herds. The lactation curve typically rises to a peak about 4-6 weeks after calving, and then falls almost linearly until drying off about 10 months after calving. If herd calving dates are highly concentrated, then the overall milk supply pattern will be highly peaked. This is true of the Irish manufacturing milk sector where the peak month's supply has been 12-16 times greater than the trough month's supply in recent years. Seasonally differentiated milk pricing is the basic means by which more evenly distributed supply patterns are encouraged. Irish manufacturing milk firms have offered a number of different schemes over the last decade, and these are evaluated by reference to a detailed milk supply model. The basic objective of the schemes in relation to which the evaluation is made is taken from an economic study of seasonality. This study concluded that a shift away from calvings in the March-May period towards Jan-Feb calvings would lead to an optimum calving pattern. The various incentive schemes can be classified in relation to the period over which incentives are offered and the presence of a herd quota arrangement. Six schemes, broadly representative of those currently being offered, are assessed. With regard to schemes without a herd quota arrangement, it is shown that those which offer incentives over a comparatively short period would be more effective than schemes in which payments are spread over many months. Arguments for and against the inclusion of herd quota arrangements are also discussed.
