Prostate size: association with disease severity and risk of postsurgical progression
Median survival for patients with androgenindependent prostate cancer is only 9–12 months, and no standard therapy has been established. Vaccination with antigens from prostate cancer cells is an attractive strategy, but the most frequently studied antigens are poor immunogens. Previous studies have indicated that the PROTO-ONCOGENE product HER-2/neu might play a role in promoting prostate cancer by independently stimulating the androgen-receptor pathway. In this study, Hueman et al. investigated whether E75 peptide (an IMMUNODOMINANT EPITOPE of HER-2/ neu that binds the human leukocyte antigen [HLA]-A2 allele) mixed with granulocyte– macrophage colony-stimulating factor is a safe, effective and simple vaccine therapy. After undergoing prostatectomy and risk assessment, 27 patients were enrolled, all of whom were disease-free but at a high risk of biochemical relapse. Patients were HLA-typed, and HLA-A2+ patients (n = 17) were vaccinated. HLA-A2– patients (n = 10) were followed prospectively as the control group. HLA-typing revealed that prognostic factors were worse for the HLA-A2+ patients. Two years after vaccination, however, the HLA-A2+ group had a disease-free survival rate similar to that of the HLA-A2– group. All HLA-A2+ patients showed in vivo and in vitro phenotypic and functional immune responses. The vaccine was found to be safe, with only minor toxicities observed. These findings suggest that an E75 vaccine might affect prostate cancer recurrence rates, if HER-2/neu-expressing high-risk patients are vaccinated before their PSA levels increase. Katherine Sole
