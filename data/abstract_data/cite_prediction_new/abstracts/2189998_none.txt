These robot control programs can be saved on disk and loaded into memory to be executed when required. Figure 1 shows that two oocyte rigs can be simultaneously perfused by the robot. The Hamilton HV3-3 valve located just above the platform allows for either or both to be perfused with buffer. The robot cannot, however, perform different experiments on each rig simultaneously. The sample tracings obtained using hand vs. robotic transfer of perfusion tubing are shown in Figure 2. In this experiment, Xenopus oocyte nuclei were injected with 0.3 ng/30 nL human glycine α1 receptor subunit cDNA, and the oocytes were treated as described in Mihic et al. (1). Figure 2 shows two sets of tracings using 50 μM, 75 μM and 100 μM glycine, one obtained after transferring the perfusion tubing by hand, and the second using the robot. The peak heights observed were virtually identical. The use of robotic techniques as well as multiple oocyte rigs allows for more rapid data acquisition, subject of course to the nature of the experiment and the quality of oocytes. It may be especially useful for the automated testing of large numbers of compounds on defined receptors. Furthermore, this inexpensive robotic technique can be used not only for Xenopus oocyte perfusion, but in any assay system involving the pumping of various buffers. A schematic of the rotary table Lucite disk drill holes and the executable code of our robot control software may be freely obtained upon request.
