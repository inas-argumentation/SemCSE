The cytoskeleton of nerve cells in the process of differentiation
According to contemporary data, the cytoskeleton of living cells is a dynamic system which influences their functional properties to a significant degree. Above all, the state of the cytoskeletal elements in the nerve cells conditions extreme heterogeneity of forms and dimensionswith the characteristic dendritic and axonal processes, and the geometric characteristics of neurons is closely tied to their physiological characteristics. The contractile elements of the cytoskeleton participate in cytoplasmic transport of various substances through the neuron. The cytoskeleton is important because it supports growth processes in the cells, and it participates as well in cellular migration in the developing brain. The increasing complexity of the structure of neurons, and the acquisition by them of the definitive "adult" form in the period of preand early postnatal development is evidently conditioned both by the genetic program inherent in the given neuron, and by the functional interactions of neighboring neurons. Since the form and dimension of the nerve cell are determined by the state of the eytoskeletal elements, processes taking place in the tyroskeleton lie at the base of the changes in these indices in the process of development. It is evident, therefore, that investigation of the structure of the cytoskeleton of nerve cells and of its changes in the process of differentiation is very important for the understanding of the morphofunctional properties of neurons.
