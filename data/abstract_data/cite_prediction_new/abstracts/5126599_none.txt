A New Concept of Immunoregulation and its Application to Leukemia and Lymphoma 1-2
It is a reasonable assumption that the induction of malignant lymphoproliferative diseases is an event triggered during physiological activities of lymphoid tissue; i.e., it represents an abnormal result of the normal immune response. This concept, which is supported by both experimental results and clinical experience, must now allow for the rapidly growing body of evidence that oncogenic viruses are etiologic in many kinds of tumors, including malignant lymphoproliferative disease. For example, oncogenic RNA viruses have been implicated in almost all examples of experimentally induced and spontaneous lymphomas and leukemias of rodents. Thus, any experimental model of these disorders must take into account the role of oncogenic viruses. The graft versus host reaction is a condition which incites in the graft an immunologically specific reaction to antigens in the host. Under certain circumstances this reaction culminates as a malignant lymphoma (1). Moreover, there is recent evidence that during this kind of immune response latent oncogenic viruses are activated (5). The graft versus host reaction is unique in this regard, because no other immune response thus far tested activates latent oncogenic viruses. In this presentation I wish to illustrate these findings and to speculate on the broader significance of the graft versus host reaction. Graft versus host reactions are most easily induced by the administration of spleen cells from inbred mice to their F I hybrids. In this situation, the hybrid recipient is genetically unable to respond to parental antigens and accepts the graft without any conditioning. By contrast, immunocytes in the spleen cell inoculum respond to antigens in the hybrid, thereby initiating the graft versus host reaction. Acute graft versus host reactions occur in infant or x-irradiated recipients, and they are rapidly fatal. Very chronic graft versus host reactions develop when mature recipients are used, and it is these animals which develop malignant lymphomas. The neoplastic transformation is preceded by a sustained hyperplasia of lymphoid tissue, most notable in the spleen, but also present in lymph nodes. Histologically, the lymphoid tissue is initially reactive and contains numerous « activated» lymphocytes; it then takes on a very pleomorphic appearance, which is difficult to diagnose.
