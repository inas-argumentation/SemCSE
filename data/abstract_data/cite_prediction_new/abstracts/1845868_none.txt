Bed boards may be placed under the mattress, or the patient may be transferred to the floor. Complications may follow external cardiac massage. These include fractures of ribs and sternum,hlceration of the liver and spleen, and pulmonary bone-marrow emboli (Baringer et alii, 1961; Morgan, 1961; Bynum et alii, 1963; Yanoff, 1963). Among the patients treated, rib fractures were common, while fracture of the sternum occurred in three. In one patient, massage had been excessively vigorous and : prolonged, and resulted in multiple rib fractures and laceration of the liver with extensive Intra-abdominal hlemorrhage. During massage, compression of the sternum should be firm, but excessive force must be avoided. The sternum should be depressed 3 to 4 em. with each stroke, but the whole weight of the operator's body is not necessary to maintain an adequate circulation. The hands should be kept just above the lower end of the sternum, and compression over the epigastrium or abdominal viscera must be avoided.
