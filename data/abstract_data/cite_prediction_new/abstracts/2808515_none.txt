A comparison of sarcoplasmic reticulum function in fast and slow skeletal muscle using crude homogenate and isolated vesicles.
Sarcoplasmic reticulum vesicles (FSR) were isolated from relatively homogeneous muscle samples representative of type I, IIA, and IIB fibers and Ca 2+ uptake (V max and total capacity) determined. Crude homogenates of these same fiber populations were also assayed for Ca 2+ uptake and the results compared to the FSR values. Both techniques produced qualitatively similar results and demonstrated distinct fiber type differences in both the rate and extent of Ca 2+ uptake. The results obtained support the contention that the crude homogenate technique accurately reflects the activity of the sarcoplasmic reticulum.
