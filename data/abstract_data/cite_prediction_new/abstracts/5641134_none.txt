The determinants of students' financial awareness—Some UK evidence
This study examines the extent to which variations in the performance of undergraduate students in a test of basic accounting and financial knowledge is explained by periods of exposure to university courses in accountancy, and related subjects, and also by a wide range of additional variables. These include, among others, age, gender, educational background, pre-university study of accountancy, work experience and investment holdings. Two distinct groups of students are used in this study. The first consists of mainly first-year BSc Econ students with no formal university-level tuition in accountancy. The second is made up of third-year students specialising in accountancy subjects. Both univariate and multivariate analysis is employed to identify the significant inter-temporal determinants of the ‘financial awareness’ of students.
