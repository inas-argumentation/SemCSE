Influence of the encapsulated clusters on the electrochemical behaviour of endohedral fullerene derivatives: comparative study of N-tritylpyrrolidino derivatives of Sc3N@I(h)-C80 and Lu3N@I(h)-C80.
Trimetallic nitride endohedral fullerenes have emerged as the most extensively studied compounds among endohedral metallic fullerenes (EMFs) in recent years. Within this family, endohedrals with an Ih-C80 cage have attracted most attention because of their exceptionally high yields and potential for a wide range of applications. These fullerenes exhibit interesting properties which are dramatically different from those of the conventional EMFs and many techniques have been used to explore their unique properties. Among them, electrochemistry, due to its high sensitivity, plays an important role in the determination of the electronic structures of endohedral fullerenes and their derivatives. However, the redox properties of these fullerenes and their derivatives remain largely unexplored and not fully understood. The electrochemistry of metallic nitride EMFs, especially for M3N@Ih-C80, show significant differences depending on the nitride clusters. The redox behavior of M3N@C80 fullerenes have been intensively studied in recent years and most of them show similar redox patterns with irreversible reduction processes. 7–10] Only very recently Popov and coworkers reported that TiSc2N@Ih-C80 exhibits reversible reduction processes. Although the cause of irreversibility remains unclear, tremendous progress has been made in this field. Both intermolecular and intramolecular rearrangements have been proposed to rationalize the irreversible reduction processes. 12–13] For the derivatives, the redox properties vary for different adducts. Popov et al. found that addition of the CF3 group on the cage of Sc3N@C80 significantly changes its redox properties. Our previous studies with monoadducts of M3N@Ih-C80 showed similar complex electrochemical behaviour but they seem to follow a general trend. It was found that the reversibilities of the monoadducts depend dramatically on the location of the addend. [5,6] adducts exhibit reversible reduction processes whereas [6,6] adducts exhibit irreversible reductions. This trend was observed for the monoadducts of Er3N@Ih-C80 and Y3N@Ih-C80. However, due to the difficulty of preparing a [6,6] pyrrolidino-Sc3N@Ih-C80 product, the full comparison of the electrochemical behaviour of different regioisomers with different metallic nitride cluster fullerenes has not been reported. The electrochemical behaviour of a [6,6] pyrrolidino-Sc3N@Ih-C80 has never been reported and the effect of the metallic nitride clusters on the redox properties of these derivatives has also not been observed. Herein, we report for the first time the electrochemical behaviour of the [5,6] and [6,6] isomers of Sc3N@Ih-C80 and Lu3N@Ih-C80 containing the same addend. The four monoadducts were isolated by HPLC and characterized by NMR spectroscopy, MALDI-MS and electrochemistry. Sc3N@Ih-C80 and Lu3N@Ih-C80 were synthesized using a modified Kr schmer–Huffman method. 18–19] The as-produced soot was Soxhlet-extracted with CS2. Pure Sc3N@Ih-C80 and Lu3N@Ih-C80 were obtained by eluting a solution of the isomeric mixture through a silica gel column with a small amount of tris(4-bromophenyl)aminium hexachloroantimonate layered inside, which selectively oxidized and immobilized the D5h isomer on the column. Isomerically pure samples of 5.0 mg Sc3N@Ih-C80 and 2.1 mg Lu3N@Ih-C80 were isolated and dried under argon. The monoadducts of tritylpyrrolidino Sc3N@Ih-C80 and Lu3N@Ih-C80 were synthesized by the previously reported method. A Buckyprep-M column was used to isolate the products. The resulting HPLC profiles for the two samples were shown in Figures S1 and S2 (Supporting Information). Each fraction was collected and analyzed by matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization mass spectrometry (MALDI-MS) to determine the composition. The fractions with retention times around 15 min were assigned as the pyrrolidinofullerene monoadducts and the fractions with retention times around 30 min were characterized as the unreacted pristine M3N@Ih-C80 (M = Sc and Lu). To further isolate and investigate the regioisomers of the monoadducts, a second-stage HPLC separation was conducted using a Buckyclutcher column. As shown in Figure 1, two peaks appear within the same retention time range in the chromatograms of both Sc3N@Ih-C80 and Lu3N@Ih-C80 products, which indicates that in both cases, two regioisomers, [5,6] and [6,6] , were produced. To further analyze the structures in each fraction, they were characterized by C NMR spectroscopy. As shown in Table 1, fraction 1a shows two peaks at 65.59 and 57.58 ppm, which indicates that fraction 1 a is a [6,6] isomer. Fraction 1 b, as expected, shows a single peak at 62.58 ppm, which is typical for a [5,6] isomer. In the case of the monoadducts of Lu3N@Ih-C80, the C NMR spectrum of 2 a shows a single peak at 68.29 ppm, which indicates that it is the [5,6] isomer although it has the same retention time on the Buckyclutcher column.
