Effect of pulsatile pulmonary artery pressure on distribution of blood flow in isolated lung.
An isolated lung preparation has been used to compare the topographical distributions of blood flow in the lung during pulsatile and non-pulsatile perfusion. It was found that the pattern of blood flow (measured with radioactive xenon-133) was altered during pulsatile perfusion and that more blood passed through the upper zones of the lung. As the frequency of the pulmonary arterial pressure waves was increased from 0.03 to 3 c/s the differences in the flow pattern diminished, and the level to which blood rose in the lung decreased. At the lower frequencies blood rose to a height approximately 70% of the mean to peak pulmonary artery pressure pulse above the mean. In the upper frequencies this was reduced to about 30%. It is suggested that the transmission of the pulmonary artery pressure pulse by the blood vessels of the lung is frequency dependent and that this alters the distribution of blood flow in the lung.
