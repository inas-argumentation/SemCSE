Bowel symptoms in patients with primary Sjögren's syndrome
Sjögren’s syndrome (SS) has been characterized as an autoimmune epithelitis with lymphocytic infiltration of exocrine tissues at multiple sites leading to glandular dysfunction (1). Gastrointestinal symptoms and dysmotility have been described in systemic sclerosis (2–6), mixed connective tissue disease, and polymyositis-systemic sclerosis overlap (6). However, gastrointestinal symptoms may be underestimated in clinical practice and gastrointestinal function in SS remains to be studied. Accordingly, the aim of the present study was to describe gastrointestinal symptoms in a well-defined group of patients with primary Sjögren’s syndrome (PSS) and to compare them to a control group. Thirty-five patients with PSS were identified from the out-patient clinics at the Departments of Rheumatology at Aarhus University Hospital and Bispebjerg Hospital in Denmark. All patients fulfilled classification criteria for PSS according to the American–European consensus group (1). An 18item questionnaire including the Cleveland Constipation Score (7) extended by information about faecal incontinence, use of laxatives, abdominal bloating, and impact on social activities and quality of life was mailed to all patients. Results from the group of PSS patients were compared with results from a control group of consecutive persons with comparable gender distribution and all at least 50 years of age who visited their doctor for any reason.
