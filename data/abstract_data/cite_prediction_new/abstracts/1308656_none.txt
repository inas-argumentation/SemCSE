Ongelyke kanalisering van gesondheidsorg: die stand van siekteversekering in Suid-Afrika
The unequal distribution of health care: the condition of health insurance in South Africa. This article's point of departure is that the health care system was, and to a great extend still is, influenced and in some aspects even determined by the evolution of health insurance in South Africa. It is submitted that health insurance can be used as indicator to trace the stance of the provision of medical services in the present-day situation. A prominent feature of the pluralistic South African society is the reflection thereof in a similar pluralistic health care system. This plurality of opposing interest groups in the health insurance market is of course a direct consequence of the predominantly free-market-orientated society in which we live. This situation can lead and has led to an unproductive complexity and scattering of the health insurance system with a corresponding lack of cooperation and uniformity in this field.
