Plaque‐like cutaneous mucinosis after joint replacement
To the Editor, Localized cutaneous mucinosis may arise after joint replacement.1 We report a case of plaquelike cutaneous mucinosis associated with monoclonal gammopathy that developed months after knee replacement. An 81-year-old woman had an erythematous infiltrated plaque on her right knee that lasted for 9 months (Fig. 1). This lesion appeared 8 months after total knee replacement surgery for osteoarthritis and caused discomfort and a burning sensation. She did not have other cutaneous lesions or prosthesis dysfunction. Histopathological examination revealed enlarged interstitial spaces that were occupied with mucin. There was also a lymphocytic infiltrate with vascular dilation (Fig. 2A). Alcian blue and colloidal iron stains (Fig. 2B) showed diffuse deposition of mucin in the upper dermis.
