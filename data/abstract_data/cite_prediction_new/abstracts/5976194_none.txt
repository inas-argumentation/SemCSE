Head and neck manifestations of Hay-Wells Syndrome
A 13-month-old white female was referred to the Otolaryngology service for assessment of the external auditory canals and tympanic membranes following the extension of a severely desquamative skin condition to both auricles (Fig 1). At birth, the child was noted to have ankyloblepharon (fusion of the eyelids) and a cleft of the secondary hard palate and soft palate. Other findings included dystrophic nails, mild hypohydrosis, urethral meatal stenosis, and a soft tissue band connecting the labiae minora. Based on these features, a clinical diagnosis of Hay-Wells syndrome was established. The child also suffered from a progressive desquamation of the scalp. This condition ultimately involved the skin of the auricles and external auditory canals, hindering assessment of the tympanic membranes. Meticulous wound care and several split-thickness skin grafting procedures have resulted in only modest success. The head and neck manifestations of Hay-Wells syndrome are presented in this brief clinical report.
