Using Logistic Regression to Guide Enrollment Management at a Public Regional University
Although numerous studies of college choice for private colleges and research universities have enabled administrators to more effectively manage enrollments, relatively few such efforts have occurred at comprehensive state universities. In college choice studies, student characteristics, institutional characteristics, and college contact variables have all been found to influence students’ enrollment decisions. In this study, logistic regression analysis was used and two choice models were developed and compared to investigate the effects of student characteristics, institutional characteristics, and college influence factors on the decision to enroll at a public regional university for two enrollment years, 1993 and 1995. The second model that included institutional influence variables yielded an Aldrich-Nelson pseudo R 2 value (Aldrich & Nelson, 1984) of .147 in contrast to a value of .061 for the first model. Implications for enrollment management in comprehensive state universities are discussed.
