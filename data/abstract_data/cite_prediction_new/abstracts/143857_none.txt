Short‐cut diagnostic tool in cystinosis: Bone marrow aspiration
Cystinosis is a rare metabolic genetic disorder caused by a mutation in cystinosin lysosomal cystine transporter (CTNS). The diagnosis of nephropathic cystinosis (NC) is made by observing corneal cystine crystals and/or measuring the cystine content of leukocytes. CTNS mutation analysis confirms the diagnosis of cystinosis, but leukocyte cystine measurement and CTNS analysis have not been widely available, and cystine crystals in the cornea may not be apparent in the first months of life. Cystine crystal deposition can be seen in the bone marrow earlier than corneal deposition, in patients with NC.
