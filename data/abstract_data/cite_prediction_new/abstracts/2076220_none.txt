The implication of acid concentration in lipid phosphorus determinations.
The effect of acid concentration in lipid phosphorus determinations by the ascorbic acid reducing method and aminonaphthosulfonic acid reducing method, was reinvestigated in the presence and absence of silica gel G using sulfuric acid, perchloric acid, and a 1:1 mixture of the two acids. The concentration range of each acid or acid mixture within which a constant phosphorus heteropoly blue reading can be obtained with a given concentration of phosphorus is presented for both methods of phosphorus assay. Although the pattern of the three acid effect curves was different, the plateaus (the adequate concentration or volume ranges) of the curves lay in the same pH range of the reaction mixture with the three acids and by the two methods of phosphorus determination. The acid effect was the same with or without silica gel when carefully controlled corrections were made for the amounts of silica gel used. The importance of the proper amount of acid in phosphorus color development is particularly emphasized when perchloric acid or an acid mixture containing perchloric acid is used for digestion of lipids or scrapings from TLC plates because appreciable quantities of perchloric acid may be lost unless the digestion conditions are controlled. Necessary precautions for digestion procedures are discussed.
