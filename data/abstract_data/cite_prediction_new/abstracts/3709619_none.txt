The micromorphology of zeiotic blebs in cultured human epithelial (HEp) cells.
Cultured HEp cells undergoing interphase zeiosis have been observed by phase contrast and electron microscopy. Study of a selected cell by electron microscopy was facilitated by a method for identifying the chosen cell during phase contrast microscopy and the process of fixation and preparation for thin sectioning. The zeiotic bleb apparently begins as a localized weakening of the cell membrane followed by extrusion of cytoplasm and rough endoplasmic reticulum. Morphological evidence indicates that retraction of the bleb begins with alterations of the membrane of the endoplasmic reticulum which is accompanied by a change of the surrounding cytoplasm to the sol state. The membrane of the endoplasmic reticulum disappears leaving an aggregation of what appears to be polysomes in a pool of electron transparent matrix enclosed by a ring of denser cytoplasmic matrix and the cell membrane.
