High-resolution, real-time-geometry video acquisition
[Zhang et al. 2004] developed 3-D face scanning systems that measure dynamic geometric shape. However, capturing, reconstructing and displaying the geometry video in high resolution is difficult for such a system. [Zhang and Huang 2004; Zhang 2005] developed a high-resolution, real-time 3-D shape measurement that can measure the relative 3-D dynamic geometry in real time. But the system can not acquire absolute coordinates in real time. This paper presents an improved version of the latter system that overcomes the shortcomings of the existing systems. It captures high-resolution absolute 3-D geometry videos in real time. The coded patterns are sent to a DLP projector and projected in B/W mode; the images are captured by a high-speed CCD camera synchronized with the projector at 90 fps. 3-D geometries can be obtained from 2-D images in real time. Since the data acquisition speed is so fast, the process of motion can be captured accurately. The system is able to obtain absolute coordinates, hence, the geometric shape and position changes can be measured accurately. Due to the unique nature of the coding method, we demonstrated that the system can capture both geometric shape and position changes smoothly within a depth range of 700 mm. The system is robust to ambient light, surface reflectivity variations, color variations, etc. The measurement can be performed with extremely dark images, although the noise is larger. It is less sensitive to image defocusing. We successfully demonstrated that the system can measure hair, heavily bearded faces, dark faces, sweater with strong texture variations, and extremely blurred faces.
