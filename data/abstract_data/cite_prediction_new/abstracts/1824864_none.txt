Dental Research: Strategic Alliances and the Issue of Quality
President Wei, Officers of Divisions and Sections, distinguished guests, ladies and gentlemen. I am honored that I have been elected to the Presidency of the IADR and that I join a line of five other Presidents from Toronto. It also gives me special pleasure to be the first President of the IADR elected from the IADR Neuroscience Group. My presentation today, and indeed my Presidency over the next year, will focus on two particular challenges that lie ahead for the IADR, and for dental research per se. In preparing my presentation to focus on the challenges, I first briefly reviewed those that have faced the international dental research community in the past. Just 25 years ago, when the IADR held a symposium on the occasion of its 50th anniversary, the issues and challenges foremost in the minds of the leaders in dental research at that time included research funding, dental manpower, and the interface of research with education and the profession (see Orland, 1973). Judging from the recent IADR survey of its membership, plus my own recent experiences, I believe that this interface is still of such central importance and has acquired new and critical dimensions that it requires our special attention. Another concern of my own is the quality of dental research. I will briefly outline why I believe that these two areas are critical issues, and how the IADR is strategically positioned to help address them.
