Ten-year survival rates of 92% have been documented in patients with tumours of less than 6.5 cm diameter (Enzinger and Smith, 1976), while the survival decreases to 29% in cases with more than four mitoses per HPF (Pandy et al., 1997). With regard to haemangiopericytomas affecting gynaecological sites, uterine HPCs are the most frequently reported and to date, 64 such cases have been published (Guercio et al., 1992). In contrast, vulval haemangiopericytoma are extremely rare and only eight cases have been reported so far in the international literature (De Sousa and Lash, 1960; Reymond et al., 1972; Mariani et al., 1998). The main issue in the management of this case is the extent of surgical excision considered as adequate. The recommended primary therapeutic approach usually involves wide excision of the tumour and radical vulvectomy has been advocated in highly malignant cases (Pandy et al., 1997). However, the patient made an informed choice to decline further excision of the surrounding vulval tissue. This may be justified, as no malignant features were noted on histology, but nevertheless the uncertain malignant potential of this tumour and its incomplete excision margins made prognosis in this case difficult to estimate. The authors have not found such a situation being reported previously in the literature. The patient is currently under 6-monthly review in the gynaecology clinic.
