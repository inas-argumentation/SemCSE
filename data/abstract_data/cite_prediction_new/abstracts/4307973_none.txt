The redistribution of body sodium in women on long-term oestrogen therapy.
175 oophorectomized women were treated with 40 mcg of mestranol or a placebo for 1 to 3 years to determine the effect of estrogen on packed cell volume (PCV) serum albumin serum and urine osmolality inulin space total exchangeable sodium and serum electrolytes. Mean serum sodium and chloride were significantly lower (p less than .001) in mestranol treated women as were PCV (p less than .001) and mean serum albumin (p less than .001). Mean osmolar excretion was significantly higher (p less than .02) in mestranol-treated women and inulin space was significantly lower in the mestranol-treated women (p less than .02). It is concluded that the estrogen induced hemodilution by water retention with secondary sodium redistribution rather than by sodium retention. Increased activity of antidiuretic hormone by estrogen is a suggested mechanism.
