Arteriomesenteric duodenal compression syndrome. Its association with peptic ulcer.
Thirty-two cases with the diagnosis of arteriomesenteric duodenal compression syndrome are reviewed. Nineteen of the patients (59 per cent) have associated peptic ulcer disease. Detection of the syndrome depends on a high index of suspicion, especially in patients with peptic ulcer or debilitating disease. Current methods of management are reviewed, and evidence suggests that the choice of treatment must be strictly individualized. When peptic ulcer surgery is contemplated, consideration must also be given to alleviation of the distal duodenal obstruction.
