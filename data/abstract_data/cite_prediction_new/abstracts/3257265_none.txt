Intravenous corticosteroids for systemic mastocytosis.
To the Editor.— We read with interest the article by Guzzo et al 1 on the use of potent topical steroids for urticaria pigmentosa in the February 1991 issue of theArchives. Because of their earlier work 2 and reports that systemic corticosteroids cause regression of mast cells in animals, 3,4 we treated a patient with symptomatic systemic mastocytosis with intravenous corticosteroids. Report of a Case.— A 43-year-old woman presented with a 1-year history of numerous daily episodes of flushing and a 2-month history of a generalized brown papular eruption. She complained of recent palpitations, gastrointestinal upset, near syncope, inability to concentrate, irritability, and depressed mood. Physical examination revealed reddish brown papules scattered over the trunk and proximal extremities. Darier's sign was present.
