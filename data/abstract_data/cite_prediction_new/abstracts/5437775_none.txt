Why does a higher repseonse rate to chemotherapy correlate poorly with improved survival?
One of the major objectives of cancer investigators conducting clinical trials of new chemotherapeutic regimes is to increase the objective response rate compared to a standard treatment program. Preliminary evidence suggesting possible improvement is provided by comparison of Phase 2 trial results of patients with the same disease and similar pretreatment characteristics. However, definitive statements about improved response rate require the conduct of randomized controlled Phase 3 trials to be certain an apparent increase in response observed in the Phase 2 setting is not simply the results of inadvertent favorable bias. Unfortunately, even with evidence of clear improvment in objective response (i.e. enhanced tumor cell kill in a larger percentage of patients), there is often minimal or no impact of this improved response on overall survical (Rapp et al. 1988; Cellerino et al. 1991; Ganz et al. 1989; Cellerino et al. 1988; Woods et al. 1989).
