Attachment and composition of skeletal muscles in relation to their function.
The distinction between so called spurt muscles and shunt muscles, as postulated by McConaill (1949), is based on simple goniometric calculations with reference to the geometry of muscle attachments. These calculations bear some essential errors, which undermine the spurt-shunt muscle model completely. Earlier criticisms of this theory were based on conceptual objections without pointing to fundamental pitfalls in calculations. In the present paper the geometry of muscle attachments and the relation contractile/non-contractile length of the muscle are brought in relation to the muscular torque at different angles between the bones of insertion of that muscle. The torque is represented in a torque-angle diagram. The torque is estimated as a function of: 1. (1) the angle between the bones; 2. (2) the relation between the distance of the attachments of the muscle to the fulcrum; 3. (3) the length fraction of the muscle consisting of contractile tissue; 4. (4) the properties of the isometric length-tension diagram of the contractile tissue. A distinction between different categories of muscles cannot be reached with this model, but it is possible to characterize any muscle on the basis of the size, shape and relative position of the curve, which represents the torque of the muscular force in relation to the angle between the bones.
