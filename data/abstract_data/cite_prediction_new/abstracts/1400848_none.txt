The effects of total acute ischaemia on the structure of the rabbit retina.
The effect of total acute ischaemia on the structure of the rabbit retina has been studied by raising the intraocular pressure above systemic systolic blood pressure for varying periods. The retina as a tissue was readily affected by ischaemia with the individual cells exhibiting varying tolerances to ischaemia. The visual cells were the most vulnerable with changes evident after short periods of ischaemia. The retinal pigment epithelium and the Muller cells were the least susceptible to ischaemic insults with the inner retina exhibiting an intermediate tolerance.
