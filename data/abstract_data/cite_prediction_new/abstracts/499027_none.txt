The impact of the EPA's TRI disclosure program on state environmental and natural resource expenditures
My study examines whether the EPA's disclosure of toxic releases into the environment on a state-by-state basis influenced subsequent state spending on environmental and natural resource programs. Based on arguments from the public policy literature (see, e.g., Peters, 1996. pp. 131–132; Treadway, 1985. pp. 11–15; Gray, 1983. pp. 3–26) it is hypothesized that states with a greater apparent pollution problem would be more likely to increase resource allocations for the environment as a result of the information disclosure. An analysis of expenditure information from Comprehensive Annual Financial Reports for 42 states found that, controlling for differences in states' environmental responsiveness and major increases in environmental spending in the year previous to the analysis period, states with a greater apparent pollution problem based on 1988 TRI disclosures, increased their 1989 and 1990 resource allocations to environmental and natural resource programs more than states with lesser apparent problems.
