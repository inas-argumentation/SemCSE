Merkel cell carcinoma, Kaposi's sarcoma, basal cell carcinoma and keratoacanthoma: multiple association in a patient with chronic lymphatic leukaemia
SIR, I would like to point out a potential source of confusion that turned up recently in my practice. A 70-year-old woman presented to her general practitioner with a small pigmented lesion on her abdomen which he curetted off and submitted for histology. He was surprised to get the report back of a basal cell carcinoma, but as there was nothing left he agreed to observe it. When the lesion appeared to be recurring at the same site she was referred to me. I found a seborrhoeic wart, which I treated with liquid nitrogen. Further investigation revealed that the lesion had been reported by the consultant pathologist as a basal cell papilloma but that there had been a typographical error. I have previously encountered this problem of confusion, either on tape recorders or over the telephone, between basal cell papilloma and basal cell carcinoma. In this particular instance no harm was done, but plainly there could be serious consequences to this sort of confusion. We have agreed in future always to use the term seborrhoeic wart, which, although it sounds less scientific than basal cell papilloma, is much less capable of confusion.
