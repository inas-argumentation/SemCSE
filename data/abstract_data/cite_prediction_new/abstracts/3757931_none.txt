The first reorganisation of the CPSU central committee apparat under Perestroika
THE ATTEMPT BY MIKHAIL GORBACHEV to reform the Soviet Communist Party (CPSU) apparat, particularly its all-powerful Central Committee (CC) apparat, was inextricably tied to the conduct of the economic and political reforms of perestroika and determined many of their successes and failures. Indeed, reform making is policy making, and it must pass through the stages of decision, conception, bargaining and implementation and is subject to the vicissitudes of political conflict. Any assessment of the suitability of a reform's design and strategy and any explanations for its success or failure therefore should examine the constraints on reform making, both structural and 'intentional': the range of choices configured by the demands imposed on the rational calculations of decision makers from different powerful interest or political groups and tendencies within a polity.1 It is insufficient to look exclusively at the political intentions of reform's friends and foes2 or to focus only on the explanatory value of such structural constraints as the multinational and 'partyised' nature of the Soviet state or the hypercentralised structure of its economy.3 The combined power of the conservative opposition to reform (an intentional constraint) and the power of entrenched bureaucracies (a structural constraint) must be taken into account when assessing the reasons for the successes and failures of perestroika. The more conservative and entrenched the bureaucrats, the more difficult it is to implement reform and the less likely it is to produce the outcome the originators of reform seek: a more effective system and maintenance of their power. In the Soviet case, no reform could be implemented without the strong commitment of the party apparatus, if not especially then including the central apparat, the only mobilisational instrument capable of orienting both the state machinery and society toward reform. There were essentially two reorganisations of the CC apparat during Gorbachev's tenure as General Secretary. The first was initiated in autumn 1988 and was being executed virtually right up to the XXVIII Congress, after which a second reorganisation was implemented. This article examines the first reorganisation as it was conceived and implemented, in an attempt to understand better the obstacles to successful reform in the USSR better.
