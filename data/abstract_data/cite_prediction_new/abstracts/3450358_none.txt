Change in activity of fibroblast mitotic inhibitor from mouse embryo cells aging in vitro and undergoing spontaneous transformation
A mouse embryo fibroblast culture was established. At the 14 population doubling level (PDL) the phase III culture nearly died out, followed by the establishing of a culture with apparently unlimited lifespan and decreased serum dependency but on in vivo malignancy in newborn syngeneic recipients. A tissue-specific reversibly acting mitotic inhibitor MW 30–50 000 was extracted from washed cells of subconfluent cultures during the 5th, 8th, 14th and 21st PDLs and tested for effect on incorporation of tritiated thymidine on subconfluent cultures in their 5th, 8th and 21st PDLs. The inhibitor activity extracted from a given number of donor cells increased between 3 and 12 fold with increase in PDL and became potent against cultures at all PDLs whereas extract from the lowest PDL culture was effective only against low PDL target cells. It is concluded that accumulation of mitotic inhibitor accompanies in vitro aging and occurs independent of the mechanism determining the finiteness of in vitro mitosis.
