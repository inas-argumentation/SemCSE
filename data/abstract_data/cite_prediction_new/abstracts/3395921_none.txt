Fear and loathing in NATO: The Atlantic alliance after the crisis over Iraq
Transatlantic relations have been badly strained by the dispute over the US policy on Iraq. The divisions within NATO in February 2003 and subsequent relations between members, however; are a reflection of the alliance not having been able to define an agreed role for itself. Underlying this problem are three deep‐seated issues that hark back into the cold war era that have yet to be resolved: the role of the US in Europe; Europe's role in the common defence; and NATO's role ‘out of area’. The success or failure of NATO's current efforts to transform itself will impact directly on these three issues and on the future role of the alliance, particularly in the eyes of the US.
