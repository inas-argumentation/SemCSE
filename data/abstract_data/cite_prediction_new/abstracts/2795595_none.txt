Pretibial Myxoedema and Thyroid Acropachy. Possible Dermatitis Herpetiformis
Mrs B C, aged 66 History: First seen in July 1969 complaining of loss of weight for one year and anxiety. Also had highly irritating rash on the shoulders, hips and knees. She was diagnosed as having mild thyrotoxicosis and treated with radioactive iodine. Clinically and biochemically she has been euthyroid since. History and distribution of the rash suggested dermatitis herpetiformis; trial of dapsone 50 mg twice daily caused the rash to 'melt away' but it recurred each time treatment was stopped. Two months after treatment with radioactive iodine, presence of pretibial myxcedema affecting legs, feet and hands was noted. This has been progressive (Fig 1). Thyroid acropachy on X-ray of the hands and feet was seen in October 1971; it was then also found that dapsone was causing haemolysis. Until recently she was unwilling to have her eruption investigated, but in January 1973 she started on a gluten-free diet and recently she has been able to reduce her daily dose of dapsone by half.
