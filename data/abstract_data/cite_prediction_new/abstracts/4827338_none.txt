Effect of temperature on space charge and periodic grounded DC tree in cross-linked polyethylene
In order to investigate the effect of temperature on space charge and periodic grounded DC tree in cross-linked polyethylene (XLPE), space charge measurement and periodic grounded DC tree experiment were conducted on XLPE samples in the temperature range from 20 to 80 °C. The space charge measurement shows that the polarity and amount of space charge accumulated varies with temperature, and the apparent trap-controlled mobility of space charge during depolarization becomes larger at higher temperature. The periodic grounded DC tree experiment shows that the initiation ratio, length and width of electrical tree all basically increase with temperature. However, some electrical tree widths decrease at 80 °C. In addition, there are some significant differences in initiation and growth between electrical trees under different polarities, which may be related to the difference in injection and transfer between electron and hole. The initiation and growth of periodic grounded DC tree is believed to be related to the charge detrapping at the instant of short circuit, and the promotion of tree initiation and growth at higher temperature is explained by the easier charge detrapping which can be confirmed from the space charge measurement, as well as the weaker electrical mechanical strength of XLPE.
