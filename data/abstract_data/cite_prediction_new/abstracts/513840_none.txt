Achalasia-microcephaly syndrome: a further case report.
The pregnancy was complicated with gestational diabetes, which was managed by diet control and metformin. Antenatal scans did not reveal any abnormality. She was born at 39 weeks by emergency caesarean section because of foetal distress, with a birth weight of 2.54 kg (second–ninth centiles). Her head circumference at birth was not recorded. She did not require resuscitation after birth. At 6 weeks, feeding difficulties and recurrent vomiting were noted. The vomiting was nonprojectile, nonbilious, and occurred with every feed. Her weight dropped below the 0.4th centile, and she was commenced on nasogastric feeds. Barium swallow investigation revealed oesophageal dilatation and a functional obstruction at the lower end of the oesophagus. The child was treated with repeated oesophagogastroduodenoscopy balloon dilatation of the oesophagus and with reflux medication. Her feeding difficulties persisted and she required laparoscopic Heller’s myotomy with Thal fundoplication at the age of 2 and repeated balloon dilatations thereafter (11 in total). Although the patient is currently orally fed, she continues to have recurrent swallowing difficulties.
