Effects ofRS-albuterol on the development of antigen-mediated airway hyperreactivity in guinea pigs
[32-Adrenoceptor agonists are widely used to treat bronchospasm in patients with asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. Although most of their pharmacologic activity resides in the R-enantiomer (1-5), commercially available [32-agonists are marketed as racemates. Use of P2-agonists has been associated with increased morbidity and mortality in asthmatics (6). In sensitized guinea pigs, administration of RS-albuterol (1.0 m g / k g / d ) for 6 d via an osmotic minipump induced increased airways' responsiveness to several bronchospastic agonists, including histamine and leukotriene-C 4 ( L T C 4) (7). Also, in control and sensitized guinea pigs, chronic administrat ion of racfenoterol significantly increased airways' responses to acetylcholine both in vivo and in vitro (8). The purpose of this study was to determine whether sustained administration of RS-albuterol via an implanted osmotic minipump enhanced the increased airways' responsiveness to histamine and LTC 4 s e e n in guinea pigs that had been passively immunized with antiovalbumin antibody-containing serum ((~-OA-Ab) and challenged by inhalation of ovalbumin (OA) (2%) containing aerosols.
