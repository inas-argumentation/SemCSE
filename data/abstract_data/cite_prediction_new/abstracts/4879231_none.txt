Learning, Growth and Climate Feedbacks Does the Fat Tail Wag the Dog? *
We study the effect of potentially severe climate change on near-term optimal climate change policy, accounting for learning and uncertainty in the climate system. In particular, we study the possibility that a doubling of greenhouse gasses causes very high temperature changes (a high climate sensitivity). We build an integrated assessment model to test how fat tails in the distribution of the climate sensitivity caused by uncertainties in the climate system affect economic growth and abatement policy. In addition, we examine whether and how fast uncertainties could be diminished through Bayesian learning. Preliminary results indicate that while learning is slow, fat tails diminish quickly if no climate disaster exists, since observations near the mean provide evidence against fat tails. However, the larger the true climate sensitivity, the slower the fat tails diminish. Thus, if the true climate sensivity is large, fat tailed uncertainty diminishes more slowly.
