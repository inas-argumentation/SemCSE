Epithelial hyperplasia is responsible for the compensatory enlargement of remaining thyroid lobe after thyroidectomy
In their recent study, Pustelnik et al. reported postoperative growth of the remaining thyroid lobe after hemithyroidectomy for benign nodular goiter [1]. The authors employed magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), the gold standard technique for measuring thyroid size, which could demonstrate a small but significant increase of the unresected lobe by 17% in size 6 months after surgery. Thyroid enlargement potentially can be contributed by various mechanisms, including epithelial hyperplasia, epithelial hypertrophy, retention of colloid, and stromal remodeling with edema, accumulation of extracellular matrix and stromal fibers. Hyperplasia could be the most likely mechanism, however, it is known that proliferation in the adult benign thyroid is exceedingly low [2]. To address this issue, we compared the proliferative activity of thyroid follicular cells in the first lobe excised during lobectomy or hemithyroidectomy for thyroid tumors (L1) with that in the second lobe removed on completion thyroidectomy (L2). Inclusion criteria considered a presence of sufficient amount of benign thyroid tissue not involved by the tumor in L1, and a time period of at least 4 weeks between two surgeries. Sixteen thyroid cancer samples, including 12 papillary, 3 follicular, and 1 medullary carcinoma from 14 females and 2 males (mean age 50 ± 10.5 years) were enrolled from our recently described cohort [3]. The corresponding L2 specimens were retrieved from the archive of the Department of Pathology, King Chulalongkorn Memorial Hospital, Bangkok, Thailand. A mean time between the surgeries was 10.1±7.3 weeks, range 4–35 weeks. Immunohistochemical staining with the anti-Ki-67 antibody, clone MIB-1 (Dako, CA), was performed on paraffin sections using a Dako Autostainer Link 48 (Dako, CA). The number of Ki-67-positive cells was counted automatically on digitized slides using the Aperio Imagescope software (Aperio Technologies, CA). A mean of 2675 ± 934 nonneoplastic epithelial cells was scored per each specimen, and a resulting Ki-67 labeling index (LI) was expressed as a number of Ki-67-positive cells per 1000 follicular cells. Consistently with the previous studies, we found a low Ki-67 LI in the benign thyroid tissue of L1, 2.3 ± 1.3 (0.9–4.9). However, proliferation activity in L2 was significantly higher, 13.2 ± 8.4 (1.8–37.9, p < 0.0001 by paired t test), shown in Fig. 1a–c. There was an average 6.2-fold increase in proliferation rate between L1 and L2, which negatively correlated with a time between the surgeries (R = − 0.62, p = 0.01; Spearman’s correlation), Fig. 1d. In addition, there was a correlation between Ki-67 LI in L1 and L2 of the same patient (R = 0.51, p = 0.04; Pearson correlation). No age-dependent associations were found. We found unexpectedly high proliferation rate of follicular epithelium in remaining thyroid lobe after thyroidectomy, especially prominent in 4–6 weeks after surgery, which is likely responsible for the compensatory enlargement of the organ. A study of Pustelnik et al. was focused on benign goiter [1]; however, the results of our study initiated by their publication may have implications for thyroid cancer. Interestingly, that majority of papillary thyroid carcinomas demonstrate Ki-67 LI less than 10, which is lower than mean LI in L2 in our cohort [4]. Moreover, Ki-67 LI over 10 was associated with the worse disease-free survival in papillary cancer [4].
