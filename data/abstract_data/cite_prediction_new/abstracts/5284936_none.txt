Should Class be Supplanted by Distribution as Core Category of Analysis and Policy? Observations, taking the German Labour Movement as a Point of Departure
The concept of ‘class’ is highly contested in economic, political and sociological analysis. A half-way appropriate epistemology of class would insist that its de nition be seen to alter according to historical or other contexts. There cannot be an immutable de nition of a word as ‘meaning’ when social understandings of that meaning alter over time, alter between linguistic , social and economic cultures. ‘Class’ thus shares with all words the ambivalence of shifting meanings, particular when that meaning is abstract, derived from attempts to assemble concrete but differing phenomena (rocks, animals,  sh) into categories with some shared characteristics. The ‘assembling’ of groups of people into convenient categories for economic or sociological analysis involves a further degree of abstraction, where the shared characteristics are in turn dependent on ambivalent de nitions of qualititative or quantitative distinctions : the congruence of profession, source of income, income level, belief system, of blood line etc. The concept ‘class’ and its embeddedness in varying systems of thought or academic theories thus suffers from an ambivalence which cannot be ignored. The purpose of these opening remarks is not to introduce an overriding sense of analytical relativism or indeed fatalism but simply to underscore the banality of change in material reality and in discourse, a banality which takes no dogmatism to assert as undeniable. It is not irrelevant that German is the original language of modern class analysis and that Germany produced the most highly organised labour movement in Europe before the First World War. The rhetoric and practices of German Social Democracy were always strongly imbued with the category of class, however tightly or loosely it was de ned. Social identity for the bulk of the urban proletariat was strongly de ned in terms of membership of the ‘workers’
