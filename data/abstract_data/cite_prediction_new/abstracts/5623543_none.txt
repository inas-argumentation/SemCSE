PROFILE - The Valencia Conference: Reinvigorating the Barcelona Process?
Since the year 2000, EU officials have been speaking of a need to ‘reinvigorate’ the Barcelona Process. The motives are both to address the damage brought to the Euro-Mediterranean Partnership (EMP) by the collapse of the Middle East Peace Process, and to remedy some of the weaknesses deriving from the Barcelona Conference of November 1995. These weaknesses have included a lack of clear and specific plans regarding the cultural dimension of the process. Whereas the first chapter of the Barcelona Declaration, delineating a political and security partnership, had a practical focus on the pursuit of agreement on a Charter on Peace and Stability, and the second chapter, devoted to an economic and financial partnership, had as its centre-piece the goal of a Euro-Mediterranean free trade area by 2010, the final chapter, referring to a partnership in social, cultural and human affairs, remained vague and offered little in the way of promoting an ongoing, structured dialogue, or enhancing real co-operation in the various policy spheres involved. Within four years of the Barcelona Conference, concerns about ‘stagnation’ of the process were being voiced even by many of its strongest supporters. Against a background formed by a deteriorating situation in the Middle East, hopes for agreement even on an outline of the proposed security charter began to fade. Even in relation to the second chapter there was frustration at the slow pace of several negotiations on new EuroMediterranean association agreements and over the pace of MEDA (the EMP’s financial package) disbursements.
