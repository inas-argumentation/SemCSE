Collective Domain Modeling across Clinical Trials Standards : Needs , Challenges , and Design Implications
Objective: Develop a shared clinical research domain analysis model using the consensus of the developers of existing representative clinical trials standards. Design: Engage major stakeholders from distributed clinical research communities and standardization agencies to model the clinical trials domain collectively. Common concepts for clinical research are identified from free text definitions, discussed in the group, and represented in the Unified Modeling Language (UML). Results: The Biomedical Research Integration Domain Group (BRIDG) experimented with a method for user-centered standards development. The resulting BRIDG domain analysis model has been adopted by the Health Level Seven (HL7) and the Clinical Data Interchange Standards Consortium (CDISC) in their standardization initiatives. We reported our early experiences with the needs and challenges for collective domain modeling efforts. Overall, it is important to support group awareness and capture design rationale for the collaborative participants. Face-to-face meetings are indispensable for building trust and sustaining collaboration among domain experts. UML appropriately supports modularity but also introduces ambiguity to the model. Version control for the evolving information sources and shared model remains challenging.
