Editorial Comment to Clinical analysis of the PADUA and the RENAL scoring systems for renal neoplasms: A retrospective study of 245 patients undergoing laparoscopic partial nephrectomy
Nephron-sparing surgery has consistently become the main treatment option for renal tumors over the past decade. As a consequence of this major paradigm shift, the debate of the urological community has been centered on the best indication for partial nephrectomy (PN), which is technically complex, especially when being carried out in a minimally-invasive fashion. Besides patients’ characteristics, the surgical indication is related to the complexity of the renal mass undergoing treatment. The introduction of scoring systems for renal tumors has changed the way investigators present their PN outcomes. The main attractive feature of these systems is the possibility of providing a single number (score) incorporating the main anatomical characteristics of the tumor. Intuitively, this has greatly facilitated the comparisons among different institutions, ultimately providing a shared language to use when presenting and discussing data. The fact of the scoring systems being attractive is demonstrated by the fact they have been extensively adopted in the field of nephron-sparing surgery, for open and minimally-invasive PN, including ablative therapies. In this issue of International Journal of Urology, Zhang et al. report a study investigating the “clinical significance” of both the PADUA and RENAL scoring systems in patients undergoing laparoscopic PN. The found both tools to be predictive of change in renal function at multivariate analysis. In contrast, there were no significant associations between these scoring systems and the occurrence of complications or tumor stage. They also found the two systems to be significantly correlated, and to be reproducible. The findings of the study partially mirror those from previous reports, confirming the validity of these scoring systems in the contemporary management of renal masses. One should keep in mind that tumor characteristics do not represent the only factors to consider in the equation. Patients’ characteristics need to be considered, including previous surgical history, comorbidities, baseline renal function and body habitus. Furthermore, surgical expertise, and availability of instruments and technology represent other de facto determining factors. Overall, the challenges of a nephron-sparing procedure are related to multiple elements. In other words, renal tumor scoring systems unquestionably facilitate comparison between reported series, which was, until recently, a very complicated exercise. However, as clinicians, we do consider much more than just the tumor we are asked to treat. As previously done for radical prostatectomy, the concept of composite outcomes has also recently been introduced for PN.
