U.S. Fisheries Management and Economic Analysis: Implications of the Alaskan Groundfish Controversy
Fisheries management under the Magnuson Fisheries Conservation and Management Act (MFCMA) has focused on treating symptoms of open-access inefficiency (e.g., overharvesting and overcapitalization) with little regard for economic efficiency objectives (Huppert). While the seven national standards under Section 301 of the Act include "promoting efficiency" (standard 5) and "minimizing costs" (standard 7), the same standards preclude economic allocation as the sole purpose of management. Cost-benefit analysis of fishery management alternatives has been required since 1981 under Executive Order No. 12291 and National Marine Fisheries Service (NMFS) guidelines for regulatory impact reviews (RIR). The record, however, on conducting cost-benefit analyses is mixed and, in practice, there have been few examples where cost-benefit studies played an important role in decisionmaking. Recently the U.S. Department of Commerce (DOC) rejected part of an Alaskan groundfish management plan by the North Pacific Fisheries Management Council (NPFMC) because "the disapproved measures would violate national standard 7 and Executive Order 12291" (U.S. DOC). In addition, an internal audit by the DOC recommended rejection of the entire plan because documentation supporting the plan "did not include an appropriate benefit-cost analysis" (Office of the Inspector General (OIG)).
