The Primacy of Patient-Centered Outcomes in Tracheostomy Care
The comprehensive review of the literature by NakaradaKordic et al. [12] provides a much-needed perspective about what is known and what questions remain in tracheostomy care, as seen through the eyes of patients. Whereas the most heavily cited literature on tracheostomy has focused narrowly on the technical aspects of tracheostomy, improving the outcomes that matter to patients has taken center stage. We now know that how patients are cared for after tracheostomy has far more profound implications than selection of technique. It is with this realization that all clinicians should carefully consider how tracheostomy affects the lives of patients. This is not an area where the healthcare establishment has always done well by its patients. A defining moment in tracheostomy care was the publication of the National Confidential Enquiry into Patient Outcomes and Death (NCEPOD) on tracheotomy care in the UK in 2014. The study brought to light shortfalls in patient care and urgent need for more coordinated, patient-centric approaches to care [1]. This work led to a number of key initiatives and helped galvanize the international community, coinciding with a growing emphasis on patient-reported outcomes of surgery and calls for multidisciplinary, team-based care. The review by Nakarada-Kordic et al. [12] brings the patient voice to the fore, framing the challenges faced by patients with a tracheostomy. The authors sought to focus their literature review on adult tracheostomy, but many lessons also speak to challenges and opportunities in pediatric tracheostomy care. What is most needed is a call to action—clear guidance on how to move beyond the current state.
