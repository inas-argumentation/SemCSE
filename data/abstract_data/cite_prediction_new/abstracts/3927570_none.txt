CS 364 A : Algorithmic Game Theory Lecture # 13 : Potential Games ; A Hierarchy of Equilibria ∗
Last lecture we proved that every pure Nash equilibrium of an atomic selfish routing game with affine cost functions (of the form ce(x) = aex + be with ae, be ≥ 0) has cost at most 5 2 times that of an optimal outcome, and that this bound is tight in the worst case. There can be multiple pure Nash equilibria in such a game, and this bound of 5 2 applies to all of them. But how do we know that there is at least one? After all, there are plenty of games like Rock-Paper-Scissors that possess no pure Nash equilibrium. How do we know that our price of anarchy (POA) guarantee is not vacuous? This lecture introduces basic definitions of and questions about several equilibrium concepts. When do they exist? When is computing one computationally tractable? Why should we prefer one equilibrium concept over another?
