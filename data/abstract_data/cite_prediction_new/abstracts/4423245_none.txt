The impact of different levels of muscular force on the contingent negative variation (CNV).
Publisher Summary This chapter discusses the impact of different levels of muscular force on the contingent negative variation (CNV). The form, amplitude and spatial distribution of the sustained potentials developing during the foreperiod of a reaction time experiment and referred to as the CNV are now well agreed. Yet the functional significance of the CNV or even its correlation with either behaviour or psychological functions remains a matter for debate. Originally the CNV was thought of as having a priming function, at the cortical level, which facilitates the preparation process for an action to S2. Such a proposal suggests the CNV as an output control device. Faced with these contrasting and yet not necessarily mutually exclusive views, and to examine the possibility that the CNV reflects some aspect of one of the important parameters of movement, it was decided to examine, and introduce changes in, isometric muscular force during the foreperiod to observe changes in the behaviour of the related EEG potentials.
