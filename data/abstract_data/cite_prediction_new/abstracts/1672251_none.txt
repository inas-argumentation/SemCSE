In dubious battle: bleeding versus ischemic events
Bleedings have become a major clinical issue since the work by Kinnaird et al. suggesting that they were associated with short and long-term mortality [1]. Before these findings, the main goal of the therapy was to reduce ischemic events through the development of more potent antithrombotic agents. Since the demonstration of the high clinical cost of bleedings in acute coronary syndrome patients, focus has shifted to promoting an optimal but not excessive antithrombotic therapy to prevent ischemic events without increasing bleedings. Accordingly, recent trials of antithrombotic agents in ACS have used ischemic events as primary endpoint and bleedings as secondary in order to enable evaluation of the net clinical benefit [2, 3]. A major advance in the field was the publication of a new classification of bleedings to further promote unskewed comparisons between trials. The Bleeding Academic Research Classification (BARC) was published in 2012 and correlates with the clinical outcome [4, 5]. The current international guidelines therefore advocate the use of the CRUSADE score to evaluate the bleeding risk, similarly to the GRACE score for ischemic events, in order to help tailor therapy and help determine the risk benefit ratio of therapies [6]. The guidelines advocate careful assessment of the risk of ischemic and bleedings events in order to determine the pharmacological and technical therapeutic strategy and improve the clinical outcome. In recent years the biological efficacy of P2Y12-ADP receptor blockade, or platelet reactivity (PR), was shown to be a powerful factor determining ischemic and bleedings events during therapy with P2Y12-ADP receptor blockers [7]. A large body of evidences demonstrated that PR inhibition determined the risk of ischemic events following PCI in acute coronary syndrome patients. This strong relationship was mainly observed in patients treated with clopidogrel which has a mild biological efficacy. In parallel, Sibbing et al. demonstrated that a low level of PR under clopidogrel was associated with a high rate of bleedings [8]. They were the first to evidence the therapeutic window of P2Y12-ADP receptor blockade which had been hypothesized by Gurbel a few years before. In fact, they identified a link between high and low onclopidogrel PR with respectively ischemic and bleeding events. Their findings were replicated and prompted the publication of a consensus document endorsing the concept of a therapeutic window of P2Y12-ADP receptor blockers [7]. In the present issue of the journal, Shan Li and colleagues have performed a very interesting study assessing the predictive value of on-clopidogrel PR for bleedings in ACS patients undergoing PCI [9]. They also estimated the potential improvement of the predictive value of the CRUSADE score in addition to PR. The hypothesis was that on-clopidogrel PR may add to the current CRUSADE score to improve the ability of the physician to predict bleedings.
