Decay Distance and Residence Time of an Air Pollutant for Different Atmospheric Thermal Stability Categories
In this work, we represent an analytical treatment for estimating the decay distance of an air pollutant and consequently, its residence time (RT) as it moves horizontally in downwind direction. The treatment to derive the decay distance is based on analytical solution of the steady-state atmospheric dispersion equation. Two important physical processes are included in our description, dry deposition and capping inversion. The mathematical expressions obtained have been applied to predict the decay distance and RT of some radioactive emissions from a hypothetical nuclear accident for a nuclear power plant in India. The calculation has been performed for different atmospheric thermal stability classes. The values of RT for the considered radionuclides could be compared with their half-lives to identify whether the isotope remains an air borne pollutant or it will be removed to the ground surface.
