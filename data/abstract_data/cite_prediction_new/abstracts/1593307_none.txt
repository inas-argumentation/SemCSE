Towards a formal analysis of discourse structure and interpretation
Text studies is a very wide-ranging and complex field involving studies of the social and political content and context of verbal production and the reproduction of the discourses of power, authority, control, and disenfranchisement. In this discussion, however, I will limit my remarks to a more technically focussed, purely linguistic and cognitive set of issues which for me as a linguist lie at the heart of text studies: namely the techniques of discourse organization and interpretation. For our purposes, here, I will take discourse to be a complex exchange involving conventional linguistic and non-verbal signs. The sub-field of Text studies concerned with modelling this intersubjective semiotic exchange I will term discourse understanding. The goals of discourse understanding research in my view are twofold: firstly, to determine descriptively, if not explanatorily, adequate principles and formalisms for assigning proper semantic interpretations to the words, phrases, clauses and sentences which make up the observable surface structure of language in use; and secondly, to provide compositional semantic mechanisms for integrating the interpretations of semantically and pragmatically related surface forms with one another. The eventual achievement of full discourse understanding depends on the successful solution of a complex of issues involving discourse segmentation, discourse segment sequential organization and discourse segment interpretation. In addition, continued development of sentential syntactic and semantic treatments to provide the discourse component with structural, semantic and pragmatically relevant information is of crucial importance to the eventual success of discourse understanding.
