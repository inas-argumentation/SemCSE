Induction of amebiasis in tissues of white mice and rats by subcutaneous inoculation of small free-living, inquilinic, and parasitic amebas with associated coliform bacteria.
Amebas from the genera Acanthamoeba, Vahlkampfia, Hartmannella, Entamoeba , and Endolimax were isolated from water samples or from fecal contents of various snakes and lizards and cultured with their associated bacteria on Neff's agar slants. Saline suspensions of trophic and encysted amebas and bacteria were injected intraperitoneally into white laboratory mice, or intramuscularly into white laboratory rats. The animals were sacrificed after 6 weeks, and trophic and encysted amebas were identified from the coelom, lung, liver, lymph nodes, and kidney of the mice, and from the coelom, lung, spleen, liver, peritoneum, kidney, lymph nodes, blood, and brain of the rats. Cortisone-acetate-treated rats gave no significant increase in infectivity over nontreated rats. The significance of infection of mammalian hosts by free-living, inquilinic or poikilothermic parasites is discussed.
