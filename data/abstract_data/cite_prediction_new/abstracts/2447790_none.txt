Angiogenesis in rheumatoid arthritis: implications for future therapeutic strategies
A characteristic feature of the synovium early in rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is an increased density of sub-lining capillaries and post-capillary venules within the developing pannus. The number of synovial blood vessels has been found to correlate with synovial cell hyperplasia, mononuclear cell infiltration and the Ritchie articular index of joint tenderness [76]. The stimuli which control the formation of blood vessels in RA have been the subject of a large number of studies, leading to the demonstration that several growth factors play a key role in RA, and that modulation of blood vessel formation may regulate disease progression. This chapter will focus on the mechanisms of angiogenesis with particular reference to RA, and the potential for development of future therapeutic strategies targeted specifically at new blood vessel formation.
