Vesicular exanthem and stomatitis. Report of an epidemic due to Coxsacke virus Group A, Type 16.
A NEW and easily recognized clinical syndrome is caused by Coxsackie virus Group A, Type 16. The essential features of the illness, which was first described in Toronto, Canada, in the summer of 1957 by Robinson, Doane and Rhodes,1 were a disseminated maculopapular and vesicular exanthem together with a stomatitis similar to that in herpangina. The outbreak remained a unique occurrence until 1959, when epidemics were described in Birmingham (England),2 California, Colorado and Hawaii.3 In each of these outbreaks, the Coxsackie virus Group A, Type 16, was isolated.
