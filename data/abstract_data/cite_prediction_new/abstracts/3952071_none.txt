Induction of pulmonary adenomas in strain A mice by substituted organohalides.
The abilities of 28 organohalides to induce lung tumors in strain A mice were investigated. The compounds included 10 monochlorinated and 18 monobrominated derivatives of alcohols, esters, ethers, carboxylic acid, ketones, and amines, containing 2 to 4 carbon atoms. Four organohalides produced an elevated lung tumor response which was significant by both Student9s t test and the ϰ 2 test. These tumorigenic compounds are 2-bromoethanol, 2-chloro- N,N -dimethylethylamine hydrochloride, 3-chloropropionic acid, and 4-chloro-1-butanol. Each of these compounds contains a nucleophilic substituent, and in each case, a molecular conformation may be envisioned in which the nucleophilic substituent stabilizes the transition state of the biological alkylating agent. These substituted derivatives appear to exert tumorigenic activity at lower doses than similar unsubstituted compounds. Five organohalides produced an elevated lung tumor response which was significant by only one of the statistical tests used. These borderline tumorigenic compounds are 3-bromopropionic acid, 3-bromopropylamine hydrobromide, ethyl chloroacetate, 3-chloropropene, and 3-chlorobutyric acid. Four of the 10 organochlorides tested and 15 of the 18 organobromides tested showed no significant tumorigenic activity. The inactivity of the organobromides relative to the organochlorides may be due to the greater general toxicity of the brominated compounds toward strain A mice. Of three acyl halides tested, none was tumorigenic.
