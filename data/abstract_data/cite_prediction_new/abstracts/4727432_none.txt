Management of pain associated with metastatic epidural spinal cord compression: use of imaging studies in planning epidural therapy
Direct or metastatic spread of malignancy to involve the epidural space can lead to spinal cord compression and paraplegia. Compression of the spinal cord by metastatic tumor is a common neurologic complication of advanced cancer.1 Epidural metastases are most often associated with lung, breast, prostate, kidney, and hematologic malignancies in decreasing order of frequencies.2 Patients with epidural metastases causing spinal cord compression present with back pain with or without radicular pain followed weeks to months later by onset of neurologic deficit.2 Neuropathic pain associated with partial compression of the spinal cord and exiting nerve roots can be extraordinarily difficult to manage. We describe a man with metastatic lung cancer involving the epidural space and intractable neuropathic pain. An implanted epidural catheter was used to successfully manage his pain. We focus on the usefulness of imaging studies to effectively manage epidural analgesia in the presence of metastatic cancer involving the epidural space.
