Should animal ribosomes be expected to participate in an "error catastrophe"?
Kirkwood & Holliday's (1975) rebuttal to Hoffmann's (1974) statistical assessment of the stability of the translation apparatus is considered. A discussion of the fidelity characteristics of animal ribosomes is made which bolsters Hoffmann's conclusion that age-related appearance of altered protein is not likely a result of ribosomal infidelity.
