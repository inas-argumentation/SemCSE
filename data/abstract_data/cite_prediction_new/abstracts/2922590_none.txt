Tissue Engineered Disc Replacement . Long term outcome in a rat spinal model
INTRODUCTION Degeneration of the intervertebral discs (IVD) in the human spine is a common and frequent occurrence. It is associated with symptomatic presentations in patients with cervical of lumbar complaints. In the US in excess of 200,000 surgeries per year are performed to either replace the IVD, currently with a mechanical construct, or to fuse the adjacent vertebral bones. Mechanical replacements are known to suffer from wear and implant failures common to non-biological replacements, whilst fusion may lead to adjacent level disease due to the loss of a motion segment. Recent years have demonstrated successes in the tissue-engineering field (1, 2) which may lead to a more biological approach for discal replacement. In the last few years, we have reported on our fabrication of a composite cell-seeded tissue engineered construct composed of a nucleus pulposus (NP) and annulus fibrosus (AF) analogue. We have previously published our medium term outcomes of our in-vivo model in the rat caudal spine(1), we present the long-term outcomes at the maximal survival of the model.
