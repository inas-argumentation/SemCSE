Box 20345, Houston, TX 77225-0345 Weell over 30 years have passed since Sir Russell Brock' proposed the idea of functional obstruction to the left ventricular outlet. In 1958, a year after Brock's proposal, Donald Teare2 published the pathological findings encountered in 8 hearts with severe but asymmetric hypertrophy affecting the interventricular septum and, because of cellular disarray, likened the findings to those of a "muscular hamartoma." During that same period, Spodick and Littman3 emphasized the malignant nature of idiopathic hypertrophy, once symptoms had appeared. Braunwald4 detailed the physiologic and clinical features of obstructive cardiomyopathy and concluded that there was a dynamic obstruction to the left ventricular outlet during systole. Although more than 25 years have passed since Braunwald's presentation, there is still some disagreement concerning the presence or absence of obstruction,j-9 the role of hypertrophy as primary or secondary, and the role of coronary blood flow in causing pain and ventricular dysfunction. There is general agreement that resistance to left ventricular filling occurs with a decrease in ventricular compliance and in ventricular distensibility, a point emphasized by Goodwin. 10 Hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy (HOCM) is characterized clinically by systolic anterior motion of the mitral valve and the presence of asymmetric interventricular hypertrophyusually characterized by a septum thicker than the left posterior ventricular wall. These findings are not specific to HOCM, but are almost always found in patients with obstruction. Similarly, the histologic appearance of myocardial fiber disarray occurs in a high percentage of patients with HOCM,"12 but also occurs in patients with other forms of secondary ventricular hypertrophy. Whether this histologic appearance is related to the degree of severity of hypertrophy or to a specific disease is debatable. Another characteristic histopathologic feature of hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy is increased myocardial fibrous tissue, which most commonly and most extensively involves the interventricular septum.'3 There seems little doubt that the anterior leaflet of the mitral valve and hypertrophy ofthe interventricular septum are somehow related in this disease. The question is whether one causes the other. The possible combinations of abnormalities associated with HOCM make it difficult to find a homogeneous group of patients for study. In particular, the degree of left ventricular outflow obstruction, the presence or absence of mitral regurgitation, and the presence or absence ofcoronary artery disease complicate the analysis of ventricular function. The abnormality of cardiac function can be related to: left ventricular outlet obstruction; resistance to left ventricular filling; mitral valve regurgitation; associated coronary artery disease; and, possibly, to functional myocardial ischemia and wall stress factors produced by regional contraction differences (midcavity obliteration).
