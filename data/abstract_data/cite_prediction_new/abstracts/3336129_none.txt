Reply to the letters to the editor regarding the elevated body mass index is a risk factor associated with possible liver cirrhosis across different etiologies of chronic liver disease: Methodological issues.
First of all, we would like to thank Erfan Ayubi and his colleagues for the thoughtful comments on our work regarding the Elevated body mass index is a risk factor associated with possible liver cirrhosis across different etiologies of chronic liver disease. They are right in commenting that the sparse data bias can be addressed by sophisticated statistical methods such as penalized logistic regression model which provide less biased ORs. Therefore, we provided the multivariate penalized logistic regression results in Supplementary Table 1e3 to provide less biased ORs and 95% CIs results for the models. However, we disagree with the authors that the relative large P-values (P < 0.2) must be considered as criterion for variable inclusion into the multivariable model. There are many possible ways to use significance testing to reduce covariates inmultivariate analysis. By setting smallerp-value (using p < 0.05 instead of p < 0.2) in multivariate model selection, we could avoid the inclusion of weak covariates or non-covariates. Since our study aimed to avoid type II error rather than type I error based on the study purpose, we used the narrow p-value (<0.05) to include more important variables in final model in order to reduce type II error. Finally, we have examined the multicollinearity relations between included independent variables using variance inflation factor (VIF) and tolerance index. As shown in Supplementary Tables 4e6, the multicollinearity statistic results show that the included independent variables exhibit no significant collinearity between each other in the multivariate logistic models.
