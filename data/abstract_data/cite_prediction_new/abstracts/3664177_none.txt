The binding of purified factor VIII/von Willebrand factor to collagens of differing type and form.
In view of the evidence for the involvement Factor VIII/van Willebrand factor (FVIII/VWF) in to the subendothelial surface (1,2,3), it is not that this protein may mediate in the interaction of the plasma protein the adhesion of platelets unreasonable to consider between platelets and collagen. However, studies directly aimed at establishing the existence of an interaction between FVIII/VWF and collagen have yielded conflicting results. Nyman, examining several commercial collagen preparations of largely type I in composition, including collagen of calf skin and bovine tendon, found that some preparations could bind with FVIII/VWF whilst others were unable to do so (4,5). On the other hand, Legrand et al (6) have reported that purified type III collagen binds readily but that type I is largely ineffective and this permits the possible implication that the activity observed sometimes by Nyman could be attributed to the variable presence of type 111 in commercial collagen preparations. In this communication we report similar binding of FVIII/VWF to a variety of collagens, including both the interstitial collagens types I and III, basement membrane collagen (type IV) and collagen type V. Collagens types IV and V are both located in the subendothelial space and may, along with collagens I and III play an important role in the interaction between platelets and the subendothelium (7,8,9). Binding has been measured by estimating the adsorption of 1251-labelled FVIII/VWF to the surface of collagen-coated wells. We have also confirmed the binding of FVIII/VWF to collagen by measuring the adsorption of the labelled protein to a dispersion of collagen fibres. In contrast to Nyman, we find rapid binding to collagen (Type I> fibres of bovine tendons. This is in accord with the recent findings of Santoro (lo), reported since completion of the investigations described herein, of a reduction in FVIII/VWF activity following the exposure of plasma to fibres of collagens types I, II and III.
