The influence of scale form on relevance judgments
This paper reports the results of two studies. The first compared ranking and category rating procedures for measuring relevance of documents to information requirement statements. The comparison measure was number of reversals; the condition where document-requirement pair A is measured as more relevant than pair B by one procedure and as less relevant than pair B by the other. As compared to category rating, ranking produced three times the expected number of reversals. The results are explained in terms of a “cascaded distortion process” that can affect any procedure which arbitrarily restricts distribution shape. The second study compared the stimulus range and anchoring sensitivities of a nine-point category scale and a magnitude-ratio scale procedure. Results from the category scale were more consistent and more as predicted. Magnitude ratio results were distorted by unrepresentative scale moduli selected by about one-sixth of the judges, a condition which may be correctable. Suggestions for improved anchoring procedures are discussed in light of the findings for the anchoring treatment.
