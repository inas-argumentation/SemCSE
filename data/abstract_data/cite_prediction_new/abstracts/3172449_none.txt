Electrophoretic characteristics and membrane receptors of lymphocytes in primary immunodeficiency diseases.
Electrophoretic characteristics of the blood lymphocytes and membrane receptors of B and T cells were studied in 12 patients with primary immunodeficiency diseases. In sexlinked agammaglobulinemias, the majority of lymphocytes had a high electrophoretic mobility (EPM). In patients with variable hypogammaglobulinemia, the presence of B cells was confirmed both with the various B cell-markers and the electrophoretic mobility. In a severe combined immunodeficiency with a large amount of blood lymphocytes bearing μ and δ chains, the majority of the lymphocytes had a very low mobility. In one patient, the nondetection of B cell markers contrasting with the presence of low mobility cells suggested an abnormal B cell maturation. This study in well-defined immunodeficiency syndromes argues for a relationship between fast moving cells and T lymphocytes, slow moving cells and B lymphocytes.
