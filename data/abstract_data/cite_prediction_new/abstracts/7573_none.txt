Retrograde peroxidase tracing of neurons combined with transmitter histochemistry
Recently LaVail and LaVai121 have introduced a most powerful technique into neuroanatomy by which the cellular origin of axon terminal networks can be traced. Horseradish peroxidase (HRP) is injected stereotaxically into a specific brain area. The enzyme is taken up by nerve endings and transported retrogradely via the axons to the cell bodies as originally shown by Kristensson et ai.19. The peroxidase is then visualized in the cell bodies histochemically as described by Graham and Karnovsky 9. When injected into a certain brain area the peroxidase is in all probability taken up into a heterogenous population of nerve endings using different types of transmitter substances. In this paper we describe a technique by which pathways, retrogradely traced with peroxidase, in addition are characterized histochemically with regard to their neurotransmitter. The present approach is based on a combination of the LaVail technique with immunohistochemistry but other methods such as autoradiography or the formaldehyde fluorescence (Falck-Hillarp) methodS, 6 should be possible to adopt for the same purpose. Male rats (Sprague-Dawley, body weight 150 g) were used. 0.4/A of a 33 ~ solution of HRP (Sigma, Grade VI) in distilled water or saline were injected stereotaxically into the head of the caudate nucleus. Twenty-four hours later the rats were perfused with ice-cold 4 ~ formalin for 20 min and subsequently immersed in the same fixative for 4 h and rinsed over night in 0.1 M phosphate buffer (pH 7.2) with 5~o sucrose added. Cryostat sections, 30-40/zm thick, from the forebrain and the ventral mesencephalon, including the substantia nigra containing the cell bodies projecting to the neostriatum 1, were processed for immunohistochemistry according to the indirect technique a,~4. Antibodies to tyrosine hydroxylase (TH), the first enzyme in the catecholamine (CA) synthesis, prepared from human phaeochromocytoma 26 were used to identify dopamine (DA) neurons (see refs. 11 and 12).
