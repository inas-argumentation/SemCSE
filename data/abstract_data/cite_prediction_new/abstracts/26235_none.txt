Major Urban Projects and the People Affected: The Case of Casablanca's Avenue Royale
Major urban projects, which are a part of today's metropolitan development, whether in the North or the South, are rooted in the pursuit of internationalization and of economic and financial gain. Such projects seek to present to the world , an image of a vibrant city, attractive to international and national companies, businessmen and financial capital, and offering luxury tourism. But these projects give rise to conflict when they confront the people who live within the areas to be developed. They obscure the social and economic realities, and deny the multiplicity of material and symbolic relationships that people have built with their homes, their work places and their localities. The Avenue Royale project in the heart of Casablanca illustrates such conflicts precisely. Some 60,000 people from modest or poor backgrounds, are affected by being moved to the city's distant suburbs.
