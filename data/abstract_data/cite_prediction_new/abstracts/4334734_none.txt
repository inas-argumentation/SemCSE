Relapsed subcutaneous panniculitis-like T cell lymphoma: role of haploidentical hematopoietic stem cell transplant
Dear Editor, Subcutaneous panniculitis-like Tcell lymphoma (SPTCL) is a rare type of primary cutaneous T cell lymphoma. Here, we report a case with relapsed SPTCL achieving long-term remission after haploidentical allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplant (haplo-HCT)with informed consent from the patient. A 42-year-old woman presented with fever, rash on forearm and eyelid with skin ulceration. Skin biopsy revealed atypical mononuclear lobular pannicular infiltrate with epidermal ulceration. Cells were uniformly positive for CD8, Granzyme B, Beta F1, focally positive for TIA 1 and CD4 and negative for CD56 and gamma-delta TCR. (Figure 1) PCR for T cell receptor (TCR) gene rearrangement was positive. Her past medical history was notable for lupus panniculitis involving the torso and upper extremities for 5 years. On diagnostic work up, CTscan showed splenomegaly and borderline lymphadenopathy in the left axilla and right hilar region. Bone marrow and left axillary lymph node biopsies were negative for lymphomatous involvement. She was found to have nephrotic range proteinuria with creatinine of 1.4 mg/ dl. Renal biopsy was consistent with diagnosis of minimal change disease. The patient achieved complete remission and resolution of proteinuria with 6 cycles of cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisolone (CHOP) regimen. Her disease relapsed within 3 months of treatment. She received salvage chemotherapy with ifosfamide, carboplatin, and etoposide (ICE) regimen and achieved radiological remission after 2 cycles. In absence of full HLA-matched donor, she underwent haplo-HCT from her brother. Her conditioning was with fludarabine, cyclophosphamide, and total body irradiation (200 cGy). Post-transplant cyclophosphamide, mycophenolate, and tacrolimus were given for graft vs. host disease prophylaxis. Post-transplant course was unremarkable and immunosuppressive medications were weaned off by day + 122. She continues to maintain 100% donor chimerism and disease-free status at the last follow-up, 20 months after the haplo-HCT. Most patients with SPTCL follow an indolent course with 5-year overall survival (OS) rate of 80%. Patients presenting with hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis, skin ulceration, or systemic involvement have an aggressive clinical course [4]. Currently, most physicians treat patients with multidrug chemotherapy (e.g., CHOP) or chronic immunosuppression with steroids, cyclosporine, or methotrexate. Autologous HCT (auto-HCT) can be considered in selected cases and may provide longer duration of disease control. In a series fromMayo Clinic of 31 SPTCL cases, three patients had auto-HCT and two patients underwent matched allo-HCT. HCT recipients had better survival compared to rest of the cohort (median OS 44 vs. 5.2 months) [3]. Allo-HCT can potentially lead to cure but is associated with significant risks of morbidity and mortality. Two patients with relapsed SPTCL, who underwent fully matched related and unrelated allo-HCT after myeloablative conditioning had a relapse-free survival of 14 and 16months, respectively [1, 2, 5]. To best of our knowledge, ours is the first reported case of relapsed SPTCL treated with related donor haplo-HCT. In conclusion, while most patients with SPTCL will do well and follow a relatively indolent course, some can follow an aggressive course characterized by early relapse.
