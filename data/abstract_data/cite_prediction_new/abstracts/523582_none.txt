Level, trend, and variability of blood pressure in childhood and adolescence.
T he report in this issue of Circulation by St. George and associates1 presents results resembling an analysis of data from the Muscatine Study published in this journal in 1984.2 The subject of both reports is the concept of tracking of blood pressure during childhood and adolescence, specifically as measured by the serial assessment of the percentile rank of each individual within his or her respective age and sex group at each of several examinations, scheduled at 2-year intervals.
