Coronary ectasia: manifesting as acute coronary syndrome
A 56-year-old woman presented with 2 days history of anginal chest pain. No risk factors for coronary artery disease, except family history in paternal uncle. Cardiac examination was normal. ECG showed ST segment depression in leads V1–V6. Echocardiography showed regional wall motion abnormality in left anterior descending artery (LAD) territory with adequate left ventricle ejection fraction. The patient's troponin T test was positive according to reference laboratory standards. The patient was diagnosed as having acute coronary syndrome-non-ST segment elevation myocardial infarction.
