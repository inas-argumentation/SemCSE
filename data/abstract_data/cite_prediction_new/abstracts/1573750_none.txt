Thyroid feeding: a forgotten treatment for psoriasis
Sir Byrom Bramwell (1847–1931) was a physician who practiced in Scotland, ultimately becoming president of the Royal College of Physicians of Edinburgh. His Atlas of Clinical Medicine published in 1896 by Constable in Edinburgh contained a remarkable case series of patients with psoriasis. It had previously been observed that patients with cretinism had a startling improvement in the quality of their skin after being fed with thyroid glands. This led Bramwell to postulate that ‘thyroid feeding’ may also be beneficial to patients with psoriasis. He reported a case series of 20 patients to whom he fed thyroid glands to treat their psoriasis. There was no standardization as some patients had mild disease while others were more severely affected. The thyroid supplementation was varied for the patients, some receiving minced raw gland, some dried extract and some liquid extract. Dosing was variable with patients treated later in the series receiving a hundred-fold increase in dose compared with earlier subjects. Results
