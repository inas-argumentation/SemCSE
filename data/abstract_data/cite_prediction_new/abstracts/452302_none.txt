Sciatic cross-reinnervation of normal and dystrophic muscle in parabiotic mice: Isometric contractile responses of reinnervated tibialis anticus and triceps surae
Recent reports suggest that the central or peripheral nervous systems, or both, may be the gene-controlled sites determining the functional and histopathological alterations characteristic of murine muscular dystrophy. A surgical technique of cross-innervating leg muscles of 129B6F 1 mice joined in parabiosis was used to test the regenerative responses of normal and dystrophic muscles to “dystrophic” and normal sciatic nerve inputs. The twitch and tetanic responses of tibialis anticus and triceps surae of normal mice, reinnervated by sciatic nerves of dystrophic littermates, were within the ranges of tensions of normal control muscles. The active mechanical responses of tibialis anticus and triceps surae of dystrophic mice, reinnervated by sciatic nerves of normal littermates, retained the weakened contractility typical of dystrophic control muscles. Normal muscle contractility was dependent on the phenotype of the reinnervated muscle rather than that of the transposed nerve. It is concluded that neither central nor peripheral neural mechanisms are responsible for the degeneration and reduced contractile responses of skeletal muscles which are undergoing the pathological changes of murine muscular dystrophy.
