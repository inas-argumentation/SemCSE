Subclavian To Internal Carotid Artery Substitution
In 1950, Webster and his associates’ described excision of the internal carotid artery for occlusive disease. Eastcott and co-w orkers2 were the first to re-establish normal carotid flow by excision of the diseased segment and anastomosis of the internal to the common carotid artery in 1954. Since then, procedures for vein graft, by-pass and endarterectomy have been reported.3-10 De Bakey et al.ll presented a series of 80 patients operated upon for occlusive carotid artery disease. Three operations were used; namely, endarterectomy, endarterectomy and patch graft and end-to-side by-pass graft. The symptoms of 65 patients (85 per cent) were relieved. The number of failures of individual operative procedures due to thrombosis is not stated and cannot be assumed from the clinical status of the patients as reported since, as case 2, a second side may thrombose, yet the patient remains asymptomatic.
