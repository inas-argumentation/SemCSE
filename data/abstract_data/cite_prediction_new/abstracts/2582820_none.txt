Crossreactivity with other flaviviruses, malaria, leptospirosis, and RF can lead to false-positive rates of up to 70% (10). As such, patients with lupus or early rheumatoid arthritis may be mistaken as having DF. Our hospital uses the Panbio IgM and IgG immunochromatography kit. The kit’s analytical specificity was examined in patients not infected with dengue. Three out of 20 serum samples from RF-positive patients were positive for dengue IgM but none for dengue IgG. All 10 samples with ANA were negative for dengue IgM and IgG indicating no cross-reactivity (11). Dengue NS1 antigen can be detected with 92% sensitivity and 100% specificity within 9 days of illness. Non-immunologically based tests, such as reverse transcriptase (RT)-PCR for viral RNA from serum collected within 5 days of fever (Figure 1), are also accurate in confirming dengue infection, particularly in individuals with autoantibodies. It was necessary to exclude DF before escalating immunosuppression for lupus flare in our patient, optimally with dengue antigen or RT-PCR. Although dengue IgM alone has a high positive predictive value in our endemic region, we should be aware of its limitations. We propose a diagnostic algorithm to guide diagnosis of dengue infection in circumstances of autoantibodies, atypical disease manifestation or progression (Figure 1).
