Balanced pericentric inversion 8(p23q13) in a child with rhizomelic chondrodysplasia punctata and his mother
Sirs, We have read a review of rhizomelic chondrodysplasia punctata (RCDP) by Wardinsky et al. in Clinicol Genetics (1990) 38, 84-93. Patient 1 had a normal karyotype, but there was no reference to the cytogenetic study in the other four case reports nor in the review of the literature. We have seen one male case, suspected by ultrasound at 25 weeks gestation, in a 2:year-old, gravida 1 mother and her 25-yearold unrelated husband, because of shortened humeri and femora and periarticular stippling. There were no exposures to medications (i.e. Warfarin), alcohol, tobacco, or drugs. An elective cesarean section was performed at 40 weeks of gestation because of breech presentation. Birth weight was 2630 g (3rd percentile), but birth length was impossible to measure due to flexion contractures of the lower extremities. Apgar scores were 9 at 1 min and 9 at 5 min. Abnormal physical findings included high-pitched cry, unusual facies with a broad nasal bridge, bulbous nasal tip, hypertelorism with upward slanting of palpebral fissures, lymphedema of the cheeks, proximal shortening of limbs with multiple joint contractures, camptodactyly with a single proximal flexion crease in all fingers, and bilateral transverse palmar crease. Xray of the skeleton revealed symmetric shortening of humeri and femora, with metaphyseal flaring and stippling of the epiphyses of the ankle, knee, hip, elbow and shoulder joints. Lateral spine radiographs showed coronal vertebral clefts of all cervical and thoracic vertebrae (Fig. 1). There was also bilateral hip dislocation.
