Trade Liberalization and Mexico's Dairy Sector: A Spatial Economic Analysis
Mexico was one of the world's biggest importers of dairy products the last decade. In the early 1990s, nearly 20% of the country's dairy consumption was supplied by imports, mostly milk powders used for social programs whose exports were subsidized by the US and EU. Mexico's dairy trade policies will undergo substantial changes due to the NAFTA and GATT agreements. Whereas NAFTA provisions lower trade restrictions for US products, GA IT allows Mexico to increase tariff rates on most dairy products. A spatial price equilibrium model with a high degree of regional and dairy product disaggregation is used to predict prices, dairy processing activity, and dairy product imports in 2000 under NAFT A and GAIT, compared with a continuation of 1992 trade policies. Dollar-denominated producer prices are predicted to be as low as 50% oftheir 1992 levels, whereas retailer prices fall some 10 to 25%. Dairy processing in Mexico is predicted to increase under trade liberalization, due to the greater availability of dairy components. As expected, trade liberalization is predicted to increase imports of milk powders previously subject to import controls, but imports of final products are less under NAFT A and GAIT than under 1992 policies. Thus, dairy processing companies and consumers appear to benefit from dairy trade liberalization, but dairy producers will face additional competitive challenges in the future.
