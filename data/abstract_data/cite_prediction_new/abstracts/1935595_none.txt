Alcohol Delamination of Corneal Epithelium in Recurrent Granular Dystrophy.
Granular dystrophy is an autosomal dominant disorder characterized by a deposition of drop-like, snowflake, or ring-shaped opacities in the cornea. A mutation in the Big-H3 gene with transcription of abnormal transforming growth factor beta (TGF-b1) results in the deposition of aggregates of keratoepithelin with other proteins both within and in between the basal and suprabasal cells of the corneal epithelium. The deposits progress to involve the stroma and cause visual impairment usually requiring corneal transplantation by middle age. Granular dystrophy is known to recur in the corneal grafts within 3 to 4 years. The deposits usually start in the epithelium; centripetal migration of cells from the limbus can explain the increased density of the deposits often seen at the center, at times in a vortex pattern. Accumulation of deposits in the epithelium and stroma can affect vision. Attempts to treat recurrence by various means such as electrolysis, surgical keratectomy and excimer laser phototherapeutic keratectomy have proved to be of limited success with some adverse consequences, such as haze and hyperopic shift after phototherapeutic keratectomy. A repeat corneal transplant is regarded as the definitive treatment for recurrence. We treated seven eyes with early symptomatic recurrent granular dystrophy (RGD), by alcohol delamination of the corneal epithelium (ALD). Over a 15-year period, ALD was carried out on 14 occasions without the need for a repeat corneal graft in any eye. Three females and 1 male with granular dystrophy requiring corneal transplantation in both eyes were retrospectively studied. Except for the left eye of case 2, both eyes of the remaining cases developed vision impairing RGD and required ALD to restore vision. The patients’ age ranged from 32 to 57 years (mean, 44.5) at the time of corneal transplantation. Clinical details related to slit lamp examination, in vivo confocal microscopy (Heidelberg Retina Tomograph II-Rostock Corneal Module, Heidelberg Engineering, GmBH, Dossenheim, Germany) and patient demographics were ascertained and documented (Fig 1AeL and Table 1 available at www.aaojournal.org). The duration to recurrence from first corneal graft was between 3 and 8.5 years (mean, 5.4). Five eyes had penetrating keratoplasty (PK) and 3 had deep anterior lamellar keratoplasty (DALK). There was no difference between PK and DALK in relation to the time of onset of recurrence. Tissue planes created by keratoplasty such as the graft bed in DALK and the graftehost junction in PK, could provide directional guidance as a result of reduced resistance or altered nature of collagen in the scar tissue. We noted a preferential deposition of granular material in the DALK interface and along suture tracks and arcuate incisions made to manage post-PK astigmatism (Fig 1I, J). Both of these surgical planes are known to be plugged with epithelial cells migrating from the surface. Pre-ALD in vivo confocal microscopy demonstrated abundant granular deposition in the graft epithelium (Fig 1C, G). This would support the role of the epithelium in the pathogenesis of RGD. Superficial epithelial recurrences can affect vision and induce symptomatic corneal erosions. Thus, removal of the affected epithelium would help both vision and symptoms. We performed ALD as described previously. Briefly, the central epithelium was covered with 20% alcohol for 30 seconds, using an 8-mm optical zone marker. Excess alcohol was removed and the loosened epithelium peeled off by gentle brushing with a dry swab or crescent blade. A bandage contact lens was inserted and the eye was treated with drops of chloramphenicol minims 0.5% weight/volume 4 times a day and prednisolone minims 0.5% 4 times a day (Bausch & Lomb, Kingston, UK) for 2 weeks. Alcohol facilitates the removal of the granular material-laden epithelium without any direct effect on the deposits. The ALD consistently cleaves the epithelial sheet at the basement membrane, leaving behind a smooth bed clear of any subepithelial debris. This facilitates good adhesion of the basal cells of the renewing epithelium to the underlying intact Bowman’s membrane, which is not breached by ALD. Tissue samples thus obtained were subjected to transmission electron microscopy (Jeol1010, Jeol UK Ltd, Herts, UK). Transmission electron microscopy confirmed the intraepithelial location of granular deposits in (Fig 1D, H). The newly migrating epithelium was devoid of granular deposits and gave the patient clear vision for several months or years (Fig 1A, B, E, F, IeL). Two eyes needed >3 ALDs, which were performed between 2and 3-year intervals. The timeline of episodes of ALD and consequent visual improvement is given in Table 2 (available at www.aaojournal.org). Despite multiple episodes of ALD, no patient experienced symptoms of recurrent corneal erosions, graft rejection, or delayed epithelial wound healing. Six eyes retained 6/6 best-corrected visual acuity and 1 eye retained 6/9 best-corrected visual acuity until the last followup (Table 2; available at www.aaojournal.org). Based on our experience we recommend ALD as a simple and relatively inexpensive means of dealing with RGD. This intervention has to be done in the early stages of RGD when the deposits are predominantly intraepithelial. For this reason, patients who have been grafted for granular dystrophy should be monitored closely for early recurrence so that ALD can be done, as opposed to risking advancement of the recurrence into the stroma, which may require anterior keratectomy or repeat grafting. Besides being minimally invasive, it can be carried out multiple times with restoration of good vision. The epithelial defect created by ALD healed within 7 days in all cases on every occasion. It does not seem to increase the risk of graft rejection and avoids the need of multiple grafts over the lifetime of the patient, thus contributing to reduced risk of rejection, if and when a graft is required. Like other treatment options for RGD, ALD does not prevent recurrences. In patients who present late with widespread RGD in the transplanted cornea, a repeat PK or DALK would be the favored option.
