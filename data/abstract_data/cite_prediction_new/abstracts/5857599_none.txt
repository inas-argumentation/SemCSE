A Survey of Two Hundred Patients and an Assessment of the Value of Intra-Arterial Therapy
1 Peripheral Vascular Clinic, The United Liverpool Hospitals. Intermittent claudication indicates an inadequate supply of arterial blood to contracting muscles. It provides, moreover, a gauge of the severity of the arterial deficiency-the smaller the supply of blood the earlier will pain develop during walking, and the more slowly will it disappear when the patient rests. A sequence of 200 male patients with intermittent claudication has been observed in the Peripheral Vascular Clinic at the David Lewis Northern Hospital for a minimum period of 12 months, from three points of view-etiology, clinical assessment, and response to treatment. During the same period we saw only 7 women suffering from claudication. They have been excluded from the series under consideration, not only because the symptom is so rare in women, but because we are not certain that the condition in the two sexes is exactly comparable. The 200 patients reviewed in this investigation are an unselected group of consecutive male patients attending the Clinic on account of intermittent claudication.
