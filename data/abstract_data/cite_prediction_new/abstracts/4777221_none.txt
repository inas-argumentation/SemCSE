The right oculomotor nerve paralysis was marked by a dilated right pupil and a limitation of adduction, elevation, and depression of the right eye. There is a previous report4 of a unilateral third nerve palsy and prenuclear vertical gaze palsy. In bilateral third nerve palsies such a s in our patient, the precise location of t he lesion causing th i s th i rd nerve palsy, namely nuclear or fascicular, cannot be established on the basis of clinical examination or neuroimaging studies. The paucity of additional neurologic signs favors the hypothesis of a nuclear lesion, since oculomotor fascicular lesions a re generally associated with the involvement of neighboring mesencephalic nuclei and pathways. Patients with mesencephalic hematoma usually present with impaired vertical eye movements, impaired consciousness, pyramidal tract dysfunction, and abnormalities of coordination. ‘J There are occasional reports of isolated oculomotor signs in relation to a midbrain hematoma, such as a unilateral fascicular third nerve palsyfi and a bilateral ptosis with left inferior rectus weakness7 To our knowledge, this is the first case of a complete bilateral third nerve palsy due to a mesencephalic hematoma without associated neurologic signs. A cavernous angioma was probably responsible for the hematoma in our patient. A spontaneous regression of the third nerve palsy occurred in our patient after the resorption of the hematoma.
