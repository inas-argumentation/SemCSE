Are home‐based enterprises ‘worthwhile’? The informal sector with reference to social security
This article arises from a countrywide survey on ‘affordable social provision’. Since people's ability to provide for the contingencies in their lives usually depends on their involvement in the formal employment market, the question may be asked to what extent the increasing numbers of those not directly connected with the world of work can make provision for themselves. In order to determine that, the possibilities for the generation of income outside the formal market have to be established. In this article people's views on the economic viability of certain activities that can be undertaken at home are analysed. Making and selling of handiwork and clothing were largely seen as economically worthwhile, while services such as child minding, small‐scale removals, etc, were most often mentioned under the heading ‘worthwhile for other reasons’.
