High-flow nasal cannula versus nasal continuous positive airway pressure in the management of apnea of prematurity.
To the Editor —

Sreenan et al1 should be complimented for publishing a study that has important practical implications. There is no doubt that if one could establish that high-flow nasal cannula (HFNC) oxygen is as effective and safe and better tolerated than nasal continuous positive airway pressure (nCPAP), it would make the administration of continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) simpler and cheaper. However, I wish to discuss certain aspects of the study which, I feel, have not been adequately addressed by the authors.

The authors enrolled the subjects only after they had already been on nCPAP for at least 24 hours. The need for this pre-enrollment nCPAP and the upper limit of its duration are not mentioned. This is despite the fact that the natural course of the disease could change over this duration and the pre-enrollment nCPAP does not serve any purpose in the study.
