UVB exposure is also advised in vitiligo patients; however, a very low serum level of vitamin D was noticed in these patients. Primary predisposing skin defect affecting melanocyte adhesiveness and basal keratinocytes may affect the quantity and the quality of the 7-dehydrocholesterol in the skin cell membrane, which is the primary precursor of vitamin D in the skin. The chronicity of infections such as leishmaniasis may explain the lowest mean of vitamin D level. Patterns of vitamin D deficiency differ according to the disease, and such comparative studies must be performed prospectively. This may open further perspectives in the vitamin D supplementation based on the type, activity, and severity of dermatoses.
