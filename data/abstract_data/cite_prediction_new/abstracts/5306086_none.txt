Fostering appropriate behaviour in rehabilitant orangutans ( Pongo 5 pygmaeus )
16 Rehabilitation centres in Indonesia and Malaysia accommodate displaced orangutans (Pongo 17 pygmaeus and P. abelii) and aim to facilitate their release into the wild by developing in them 18 the skills that are necessary for survival. Regular forest excursions are provided but their 19 efficacy in improving learning of appropriate behaviours is unknown. We observed forty 20 rehabilitating orangutans from the Orangutan Care and Quarantine Centre during three forest 21 excursions each to determine whether their behaviour fostered the development of survival 22 skills. In total 38% of their time was spent in locomotion, particularly quadrupedal arboreal 23 travel (13%), walking (8%), climbing (7%) and vine-swinging (4%). 26.5% of their time was 24 spent 5 m or more from the ground, at heights up to 25 m. Arboreal activities were more 25 2 common early in the excursions and interaction with care-givers more common later (hour 1: 26 0.3% of time; hour 5: 0.9% of time). Animals of lower body weight were significantly more 27 likely to engage in arboreal movement, locomotion in general, eating of bark and leaves, and 28 social play, and less likely to eat insects. Those that had been at the Centre the longest were 29 less likely to perform arboreal activities and significantly more likely to be found standing 30 and at ground level, than those that were there for a shorter time. During this study, many 31 forest food items were consumed, particularly leaves and fruit, but also invertebrates and 32 bark. Little time was spent in sexual behaviour, tool use, nest building or socially-mediated 33 learning, but social play occupied almost 6% of their time. We conclude that regular 34 excursions into the forest are likely to assist in the development of locomotion and feeding 35 skills for survival in rehabilitating orangutans, but special attention is needed to encourage 36 nest building, social activities and arboreal activity.
