In vitro response of human pulmonary macrophages with volcanic ash: a morphological study.
The eruption of Mount St. Helens resulted in an exposure of a large population to respirable size dust particles. The toxicity of this dust was of considerable concern. An analysis of the respirable size fraction from three collection sites confirmed that the predominant components were aluminosilicates. The in vitro model was used in the present study to assess the interactions of lavaged human macrophages with sized ash particles. Following incubation periods with the ash of either 5 min, 1 hr, 2 hr, or 18 hr, the cultures were fixed for morphological study. The cell interactions with the ash were studied by light microscopy, scanning electron microscopy, and transmission electron microscopy. Further clarification as to particulate composition was achieved with X-ray energy-dispersive systemsin terfaced with the electron microscopes. The macrophages from both volunteer smokers and non-smokers readily phagocytized the ash particles and over the time course of this study, displayed no morphological changes attributable to cytotoxic influences of the ash.
