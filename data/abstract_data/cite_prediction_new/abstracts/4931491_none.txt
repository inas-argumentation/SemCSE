Discussion: Tracing changes in atmospheric sources of lead contamination using lead isotopic compositions in Australian red wine Chemosphere (2016) 154, 40-47. Authors Kristensen LJ, Taylor MP, Evans AJ.
I looked forward to reading this paper aswebeganaprovenancing investigation at CSIRO in the late 1980's with the former Roseworthy Agricultural College (Grape and Wine Research Unit) and the Australian Wine Research Institute (AWRI) in response to possible fraud in the source of wine but got side-tracked with more critical studies on tin-lead capsules and other health issues related to wine. The paper purports to use lead (Pb) isotopes and strontium (Sr) isotopes measured by inductively coupled mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) to trace changes in Australian redwines over a 60 year period from 2 wine regions in South Australia. Similar studies using Pb isotopes were undertaken by, for example, Lobinski et al. (1994), Larcher et al. (2003), Rosman et al. (1998), Medina et al. (2000) andMihaljevi et al. (2006) and for Sr by Almeida and Vasconcelos (2003), Petrini et al. (2015) and Rummel et al. (2008) in orange juices. Some basic details of sampling would have been useful such as how many vineyards were sampled, and how many vintages were sampled from the one vineyard? There are fundamental flaws in the paper both in data and interpretation.
