An extension of the simple analytics of segmented grain markets and the case for liberalization
This note extends the model used by Roemer (1986) to analyze the effect of maintaining a low consumer price in the grains market by the government. It is shown that Roemer's result that government intervention is unlikely to have an adverse impact on domestic output is not valid if some other, different grain-purchase schemes of the government and the possibility of smuggling are incorporated in the analysis.
