Ultrastructural, cytochemical and biochemical changes occurring during syngeneic macrophage-lymphoma interaction in vitro.
Immune peritoneal macrophages (IPM) obtained from DBA/ 2 mice hyperimmunized against syngeneic L 5178 Y lymphoma differed from normal macrophages (NPM) in that they were larger, they exhibited more small lysosomes in the Golgi region and they had a higher intracellular acid phosphatase activity. When cultures of IPM and NPM were mixed with the lymphoma cells at a ratio of 40:1 , there was an increase in the number of small lysosomes reaching a peak level at 2 hr. Although this occurred in both macrophage types the actual increase was greater in IPM and was followed by lysosomal fusion. There was no detectable biochemical increase of acid phosphatase in either IPM or NPM before 6 hr. At a 1:1 ratio, adhesion of L 5178 Y cells was rapid and strong, although there was no specific ultrastructural damage to the tumour cells before 24 hr. The data are discussed in relation to the immunological specificity of target cell growth inhibition by IPM and the changes taking place in the macrophages.
