D e s p i t e progress in pharmacological treatment, mortality rates for patients with advanced heart failure remain as high as 50% annually. In 1998, 4.7 million persons in the United States had heart failure, and cardiovascular disease was the leading cause of death for persons more than 65 years old. Treatment choices for heart failure include pharmacological therapy and surgical intervention. Clinical trials established that mortality can be reduced with the use of certain medications, such as β-blockers, angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors, and spironalactone. These drugs affect the responses of the sympathetic nervous system to heart failure, regulate neurohormonal progression of the disease, and decrease circulating volume.
