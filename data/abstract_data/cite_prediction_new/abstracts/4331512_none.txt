Isolation, purification, and properties of the lipid-linked intermediates of O-antigen biosynthesis.
Lipid-linked intermediates involved in the biosynthesis of the Salmonella newington O-antigen have been prepared by incubating EDTA-treated cells with uridine diphos-phate- d -galactose (UDPGal) or UDPGal and thymidine diphosphate- l -rhamnose, in an in vitro system. Galactose-P-lipid and rhamnosyl-galactose-P-lipid, isolated by butanol extraction of the appropriate incubation mixture, have been purified by DEAE-cellulose column chromatography. EDTA-treated cells have been prepared from cells grown in the presence of 14C-glucose, 32P-inorganic phosphate, 1-14C-leucine, 35S-inorganic sulfate, and 2-3H-glycerol, but only in the case of 14C-glucose and 32P-inorganic phosphate was the isotope incorporated into the isolated disaccharide intermediate. The disaccharide-P-lipid contains rhamnose, galactose, and phosphate in a molar ratio of 1:1:2. No glycerol was detected. Results of alkaline and acid hydrolysis indicate that the two phosphates may be linked, forming a pyrophosphate bridge between the sugars and the lipid. The structure of the lipid moiety is still unknown. The disaccharide-P-lipid has a molecular weight of approximately 1000.
