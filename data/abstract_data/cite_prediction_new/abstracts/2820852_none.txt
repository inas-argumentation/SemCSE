Editorial Comment: Does gut-derived inflammation enhance pain signaling following chemotherapy in a Toll-like receptor 4-dependent manner?
Regimen-related toxicities are universally underappreciated and often seen as the trade-off for cancer remission. Although research efforts into supportive care in cancer have seen significant improvement, regimen-related toxicities continue to be viewed as biologically independent, but simultaneous events, perpetuating the silo mentality that typically exists within the supportive care domain. This approach ignores global observations that regimen-related toxicities occur in symptom clusters [1] that point to commonalities in their underlying biology or, at the least, overlapping mechanisms. Despite this, it is becoming increasingly recognized that commonalities exist between neurotoxicity (specifically the symptom of pain) and gut toxicity, two important and dose-limiting side-effects of chemotherapy. In light of a recently published review of the literature [2], we propose a paradigm shift in the approach to these toxicities, suggesting that peripheral, gut-derived inflammation enhances pain signaling through central glial activation.
