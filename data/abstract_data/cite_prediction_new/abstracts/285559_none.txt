The chromosome number of the yeast Hansenula wingei observed in mitosis and meiosis.
Four chromosomes were observed at mitosis in haploid as well as diploid strains and four bivalents were observed at meiosis in the diploid. Therefore it is assumed that a) the haploid number of chromosomes in H. wingei is four and b) somatic pairing occurs in the diploid. Genetic analysis of six genes in H. wingei defined only two linkage groups, each with three markers (Crandall and Richter, 3 973). These preliminary results suggested that H. wingei might have a small number of chromosomes. Therefore, cytologicaI studies were performed to see whether the chromosomes could be visualized. Diploid strain VD and haploid strain VIA used for the cytological studies were obtained from Dr. A. Herman (USDA Northern Regional Laboratory, Peoria, Illinois). Diploid cultures were sporulated on malt extract agar (2.5% malt extract and 3% agar) at 25 C. Monolayers of cells were grown on YG agar plates (0.7% Difco Yeast extract, 3.0% glucose, 0.5% KH2P04 and 2% agar) for 6-7 hr at room temperature. Cutouts of agar were placed cells down on coverslips, smeared with fresh egg white and gently pressed. This film of cells was immediately plunged into freshly prepared fixative (formalin: glacial acetic acid: 95% alcohol: water, 5:5:50:40). Ten min of fixation was followed by 1 min in 95% alcohol, storage in 70% alcohol, hydrolysis with 1 N HC1 at 60 C for 6 min, rinses in tap water, brief soaking in 60% acetic acid and staining with aceto-orcein for 20 rnin (1% synthetic orcein, Fisher Scientific Co., Fair Lawn, N.J., in 60% acetic acid, filtered). The stained films were mounted over a drop of stain, excess liquid was removed without pressure by touching filter paper to the edges, and the preparation was sealed. Slides were photographed 1-2 days later.
