Rethinking Mitral Paravalvular Leak Closure: Surgical Approach or Plugging the Holes?
SEE PAGE 1946 P aravalvular leak (PVL) is the most common form of nonstructural valve dysfunction observed after a heart valve replacement (1). The reported annual incidence of PVL ranges between 0.2% and 1.4% per year after mitral valve replacement (1–3). Most patients with mild-to-moderate PVL remain asymptomatic with no clinical consequences (4). However, patients with severe PVL can present with cardiac failure and hemolysis, impairing functional capacity and long-term survival (5). Currently, surgical correction remains the gold standard therapy for symptomatic PVL and is typically performed in patients with severe congestive heart failure (CHF) and/or refractory hemolytic anemia, as it has been reported to improve symptoms and long-term outcomes compared with medical therapy (5,6). Nevertheless, surgical repair is associated with high morbidity and mortality as well as a high incidence of recurrence in the long term (7). Hence, the surgical choice remains limited to a specific patient population, leaving a significant number of patients in need for alternative therapies (8). Percutaneous transcatheter reduction of PVL has emerged as an alternative for patients who are deemed unsuitable for surgery (9).
