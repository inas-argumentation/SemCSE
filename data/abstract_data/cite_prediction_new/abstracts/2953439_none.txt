Comments on ‘Subinsular vascular lesions: an analysis of 119 consecutive autopsied brains’
Sir, We read with great interest the article by Dr Tomimoto and colleagues [1] in the January 2007 issue of European Journal of Neurology, on subinsular vascular lesions. The authors stated in their introduction: insular arteries arising from the M3 segment of the middle cerebral artery . Readers should know that a total of 75 to 104 insular arteries originating from the M2 segment of the MCA provide the arterial supply of the insula according to Türe et al. [2]. The M2 segment of the MCA is accurately the insular segment [3–5]. The authors also stated in their conclusion that subinsular T2 hyperintensities rarely correspond to ... cerebral hemorrhage,... . Haemorrhage or bleeds in the brain at the acute or chronic phase contain either intracellular methemoglobin or hemosiderin deposits that appear hypointense on T2 weighted imaging, precisely on gradient echo MR images [6,7]. Thus, except in the hyperacute phase of hematoma (oxyhemoglobin) isolated hypersignals on T2 weighted sequences are definitely not signs of haemorrhage. References
