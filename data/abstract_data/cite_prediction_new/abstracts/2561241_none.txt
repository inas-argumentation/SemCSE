Cutaneous seeding following percutaneous ethanol injection therapy for hepatocellular carcinoma.
A 77-year-old Japanese female was referred to our department because of a “knot” on her waist. Her family history was unremarkable. At the age of 65, she was diagnosed as having liver cirrhosis and hepatocellullar carcinoma (HCC), and was treated with percutaneous ethanol injection therapy (PEIT). Four months prior to our first examination, she had undergone a second PEIT and had had a 30 day-history of the forementioned eruption at the location in which the needle had penetrated when the second PEIT was conducted. Before the second PEIT, a biopsy was not performed. Computed tomography demonstrated a single lesion 10 mm in diameter at S6 and angiography revealed a slight vascularity of the tumor. Physical examination revealed an asymptomatic, reddish papule on her right side waist (Fig. 1A). Serum -fetoprotein was 8.4 ng/ml (normal range: below 10.0 ng/ml). Suspecting needle track seeding of HCC, we resected the papule. Histology showed a wellcircumscribed intradermal lesion composed of discrete clusters separated by fibrous septa in the dermis (Fig. 1B). The clusters consisted of tumor cells with clear cytoplasm and various sizes of nuclei. Mitoses were also seen (Fig. 1C). These findings were consistent with HCC, although bile production was not seen. Thereafter the patient received one session of transcatheter arterial infusion therapy and three sessions of PEIT. One year later, neither a recurrence of the HCC nor cutaneous lesions were seen. PEIT is a local treatment in which ethanol is percutaneously injected into the HCC (1).
