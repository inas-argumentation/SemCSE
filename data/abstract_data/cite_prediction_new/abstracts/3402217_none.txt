Deposition of microbial strains in public resource centres: safeguarding valuable resources for academic and applied research.
The Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) has recognized Biological Resource Centres (BRCs) to be one of the key elements for strengthening sustainable international scientific infrastructure. During the inaugural workshop on BRCs in Tokyo (1999) BRCs were defined as consisting of “service providers and repositories of the living cells, genomes of organism, and information relating to heredity and the functions of biological systems. BRCs contain collections of culturable organisms (e.g., microorganisms, plant, animal and human cells), replicable parts of these (e.g., genomes, plasmids, viruses, cDNAs), viable but not yet culturable organisms, cells and tissues, as well as databases containing molecular, physiological and structural information relevant to these collections and related bioinformatics”. Microbial BRCs, the advanced, qualityand research-driven versions of traditional microbial Culture Collections, are recognized as being instrumental in the accession, maintenance and provision of resources, and through these activities they underpin their importance for the development of R&D in life sciences and advances in major areas of health, agriculture, environment and biotechnology. Their role in science and society is highlighted in the brochure “Biological Resource Centres: Underpinning the Future of Life Sciences and Biotechnology” (http://www.oecd.org/ dataoecd/55/48/2487422.pdf). In order to support ex-situ repositories and international networking among BRCs the OECD BRC Task Force recommends that governments, policy makers, and other stakeholders help to facilitate access to high quality biological material to support R&D programs (Smith, 2011). The European Commission has recognized the importance of this issue by including the “Microbial Resources Research Infrastructure” (MIRRI) proposal on the European Strategy Forum for Research Infrastructures (ESFRI) road map, and by financing its preparatory phase (2012e2015).
