Feline leukemia virus infections: the significance of chronic viremia.
Cats that became naturally infected with feline leukemia virus (FeLV) were examined for levels of virus excretion in saliva and duration of induction period before disease development. Viremia persisted for periods of up to 52 months before disease development. Cats that developed non-neoplastic diseases had persistent viremia for a mean period of at least 2 y, while cats that developed leukemia or lymphoma maintained viremia for a mean period of at least 17 months. Healthy viremic cats excreted a mean of about 2 × 105 infectious units of FeLV/ml saliva while infected leukemic cats excreted a mean of 4 × 104 infectious virus/ml saliva. An epidemiologic model for the distribution of FeLV in the cat population is proposed.
