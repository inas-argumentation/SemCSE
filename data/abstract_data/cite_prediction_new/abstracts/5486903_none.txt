Diabetic Foot Health Education and Amputation Prevention
Diabetes is a metabolic condition caused by an inability of the body to control the sugar levels within the bloodstream. Approximately 24 million people in the United States have been diagnosed with diabetes and there are an additional 60 million people considered to be prediabetic. These prediabetic individuals have elevated blood glucose levels and are likely to develop diabetes within the next 10 years. At current rates and estimates, one in three children born today will be diagnosed with diabetes. While many people recognize that diabetes poses a risk for health problems (e.g., heart disease, high blood pressure, and stroke), fewer people recognize that diabetes increases the likelihood of lower extremity amputation by 40%. The increased risk of amputation is a result of slowly progressive complications of elevated blood sugar levels on the arterial, musculoskeletal, integumentary, and nervous systems. These complications are often unrecognized by patients and physicians because the early stages of disease are relatively unobtrusive. A diabetes-related amputation occurs every 30 seconds, and 85% of these amputations are precipitated by a minor foot injury such as a blister or a callus. These injuries are often ignored because the diabetes causes peripheral neuropathy (nerve damage to the feet).
