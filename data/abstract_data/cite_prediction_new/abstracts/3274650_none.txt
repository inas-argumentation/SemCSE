ADPKD may be complicated by cardiovascular disease, including hypertension, left ventricular hypertrophy, and cardiac valvular disease [4] . There may be an additional vulnerability to atherosclerosis in that subjects with ADPKD may have an increased prevalence of diabetes after kidney transplant [5] . This clinical finding, which does not appear to relate to total dose of prednisone or age, has a mechanistic support in studies that show insulin resistance in subjects with ADPKD and normal kidney function [6] . In addition to showing reduced glucose tolerance and higher baseline plasma insulin concentrations in ADPKD compared to normal controls, this work also showed decreased fluorescence anisotropy in the leukocytes of subjects with ADPKD compared to normal controls. In fact, insulin sensitivity was directly and significantly correlated to fluorescence anisotropy, and thus inversely correlated to membrane fluidity. This finding also occurs in non-insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus [7] . Thus, the increased cell membrane fluidity of ADPKD could explain its association with glucose intolerance. The association of gout and uric acid nephrolithiasis with ADPKD is well known, but largely unexplained [8, Identification of polycystins 1 and 2 has marked an increasingly detailed understanding of autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD). So, too, have cellular studies advanced the mechanistic knowledge of cyst formation [1] . These and many other studies may result in useful therapies for ADPKD, but they do not explain other important aspects of ADPKD including its metabolic complications of gout, nephrolithiasis and impaired glucose tolerance. I propose that increased cell membrane fluidity occurs in ADPKD, and that it may explain many features of that condition ( fig. 1 ). Membrane fluidity denotes the intramembrane mobility of membrane-associated proteins. It may be measured by fluorescence anisotropy, which uses diphenylhexatriene or other fluorescent molecules as intramembrane probes. That enables a spectrofluorometer to test the physical properties of the tested material. Anisotropy, the property of differing physical behavior according to the direction and orientation of testing, is inversely related to fluidity in this setting. When within the cell membrane, the probe ‘senses’ the motion of lipid acyl side chains, thus showing fluidity. Thus, for example, there appears to be an inverse relation between red blood cell membrane cholesterol-to-phospholipid ratio and its fluidity [2] .
