Circadian rhythms in the concentration of thyroid hormone in the plasma of normal calves.
SUMMARY On 60 occasions blood samples from seven normal calves were collected at hourly intervals for periods of at least 24 h and the levels of circulating iodoproteins were determined. In both natural and controlled environments the presence of sinusoidal rhythms in iodoprotein concentration has thus been fully established : the rhythms are discussed in relation to factors which may control their appearance and form. When the controlled lighting patterns paralleled the sequence of night and day, the acrophase of the 24-h rhythm fell approximately in the middle of the dark period. However, when the lighting pattern was reversed (dark period 09.00–21.00 h), the time of appearance of the acrophase changed significantly so that it occurred just inside the new light period. Whether a rhythm could be detected or not, the mean concentration of thyroxine, over a 24-h period, remained almost constant, as did the amplitude of the 24-h rhythms.
