Two cases of agranulocytosis associated with cocaine use: is this phenomenon becoming more prevalent?
Levamisole was first introduced in 1972 as immunomodulatory therapy for rheumatoid arthritis, metastatic carcinoma and immunodeficiency states (1, 2). Ten years later, cases of drug-related agranulocytosis occurred and levamisole was removed from the market for humans (2, 3). In 2008, unexplained cases of agranulocytosis emerged in cocaine abusers in the USA (4) and it was then reported that the cocaine samples were contaminated by Levamisole (5). Here, we describe two cocaine abusers, hospitalized with agranulocytosis in May–June 2009, the first case was a 22-yr-old man admitted with fever. He was not on any medications. Laboratory results showed: WBC, 2.45 · 10 ⁄m, neutrophils (<1%), prolonged uncorrectable partial thromboplastin time (PTT) of ¢¢51.3. Blood chemistry, autoimmune profile, levels of vitamins and iron and whole body CT scan were all normal. Bone marrow aspiration and flow cytometry of cells showed a lack of maturing myeloid cells and granulocytes with maturation arrest (Fig. 1A,B). He received antibiotics and filgrastrim, recovered quickly and was discharged from hospital. One month later, he had a car accident, was admitted again but quickly discharged with a normal WBC ⁄neutrophils count. However, 1 d later he was re-admitted with high fever and agranulocytosis [WBC, 0.78 · 10 mm, neutrophils: (1.76%.)]. He now admitted using cocaine the evening before. He was re-treated with antibiotics and filgrastrim and discharged 9 d later with normal WBC ⁄neutrophils counts. The second case was a 55-yrold known HIV carrier and chronic drug abuser admitted for dyspnea. She was not taking any medications but admitted using cocaine during that week. Laboratory tests showed agranulocytosis – WBC 0.770 · 10 ⁄mm, (0.5% neutrophils). Bone marrow revealed a marked decrease in maturing myeloid cells and absent neutrophils (Fig. 1C). She received intravenous antibiotics and filgrastrim and was discharged quickly with a normal WBC ⁄neutrophils count. In December 2009, the CDC reported 21 individuals seen with cocaine-induced agranulocytosis in North America during the period November 2008–November 2009 (5).
