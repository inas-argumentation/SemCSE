Is Pain Control Important in Oncologic Surgery and Why?
Early, correct and satisfactory management of perioperative pain in cancer surgery is very important. It requires teamwork: surgeons and nurses carry out the pain management protocols under the supervision of an anesthesiologist. This is the concept of the Acute Pain Service (APS) where anesthesiologists, surgeons, nurses and patients work together and where regular recording takes place of pain intensity just like other relevant parameters for patient assessment. A Pain-Free Hospital is a project where the patient, and not the disease, has the central role and where the goal is qualified medical assistance. It also means that postoperative analgesia is a must. APSs have broad consensus and very high costs in the US l . In Europe APS must be less expensive: a model based on an anesthesiologist supervising surgeons and nurses is also successfuI2-4• The anesthesiologist establishes the most suitable analgesic protocol for each patient, checks its efficacy and possible side effects, coordinates research, and provides training. Surgeons and ward nurses carry out the protocols and record the pain intensity scores using an easy method such as the Scott-Hutchinson visual analogue scale (VAS). Pain relief can also be recorded by a verbal scale (very good, good, quite good, not good): it is a marker of the patient's satisfactions.
