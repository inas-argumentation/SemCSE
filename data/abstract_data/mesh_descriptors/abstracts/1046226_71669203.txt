7 Surgery of liver tumours
Abstract The liver is a segmental organ that allows resection through anatomically defined planes. The surgical management of an intrahepatic lesion, discovered either during investigation of hepatological symptoms or coincidentally, must involve an approach to investigation that carries a minimum risk and does not compromise subsequent excision of the lesion. Biopsy of an intrahepatic lesion found at laparotomy is essential, but attempts at early tissue diagnosis by percutaneous biopsy of operable tumours may lead to unnecessary morbidity and tumour spread. Preoperative studies often allow a firm pathological diagnosis to be made and ultrasonography, CT scanning and arteriography can be used to fully assess operability. Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the commonest primary liver cancer and is often found in association with cirrhosis and in patients with inadequate functional hepatic reserve.
