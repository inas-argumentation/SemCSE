New Neurogenes in Mammals
Currently, the attention of biologists to the problems of neurogenetics and behavioral genetics has markedly increased, especially after the discovery that the specific features of animal and human behavior are determined predominantly by genes [1, 2]. The genetic determination of these characteristics is based on the fact that these genes control the processes of neurogenesis. The development of the central nervous system, which determines individual behavioral response patterns, is genetically predetermined [3–5]. In view of this, the problem of identification of certain neurogenes and determination of the role of each of them in the development of the brain and the role of the corresponding parts of the central nervous system in the organization of behavior has arisen [6]. Mice, whose genetics has been well studied, are the most convenient mammalian object for this purpose. In particular, a new family of neurogenes ( d4 ) was found in them [7–10]. This family, also found in the Drosophila [11], is apparently universal. In this study, we continued our earlier work on the determination of the new genes that are differentially expressed in the mouse brain during ontogeny [12, 13]. The methods of differential display and subtracting hybridization, used in this work, were described earlier [14]. The genomic sequences were identified using the commercial database Celera (www.celera.com). In situ hybridization was performed as described in [15]. The study was performed with 15-day-old embryos.
