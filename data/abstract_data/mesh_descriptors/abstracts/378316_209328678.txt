Iatrogenic Kaposi sarcoma in a patient treated with ruxolitinib: A case report
Dear Editor, Kaposi sarcoma (KS) is a rare lymphoangioproliferative disease associated with human herpes virus 8 (HHV-8). Immunological status plays a crucial role in the development of KS, and wellrecognized immunosuppressive agents, mainly corticosteroids and cyclosporin, may trigger the iatrogenic variant of KS (iKS). Conversely, fewer cases have been reported of iKS in patients treated with biologic medications. We herein report a case of iKS in a patient treated with ruxolitinib, a Janus kinase (JAK)1 and JAK2 inhibitor. A 70-year-old man presented with a few angiomatous nodules, macules and plaques on the lower limbs (Fig. 1), and a single exophytic nodule on the soft palate. Histopathological examination was consistent with KS, showing bundles of neoplastic spindle cells in the dermis, separated by slit-like spaces containing erythrocytes; with positive immunoreactivity for HHV-8. Nine months before, he had started therapy with ruxolitinib for his myelofibrosis; hence, the diagnosis of iKS was made. HIV test, laryngoscopy and relevant examinations for lymph node or visceral involvement were negative.
