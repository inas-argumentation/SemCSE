Increased translation in liver involves a supernatant factor following laser irradiation.
1. The incorporation of [ 14 C]phenylalanyl-tRNA into peptide was increased markedly in cell-free preparations obtained from the liver of rats obtained 2 days following continuous or pulsed CO 2 laser irradiation at a wavelength of 10 600 A and energy of 150 J. The increase was smaller with lower energy and in liver obtained 1, 3 or 7 days following laser injury. 2. The factor responsible for the 2-fold increase in peptide synthesis was found in the pH 5 supernatant fraction of liver from the laser irradiated rats and evidence was obtained that it was not associated with initiation factor activity, endogenous mRNA, aminoacyl-tRNA or translocation. 3. There was an increase in the pH 5 supernatant-dependent binding of aminoacyl-tRNA to control liver ribosomes when the supernatant was obtained from the liver of irradiated rats. When control liver supernatant was used for binding, the addition of purified elongation factor 1 (EF 1 or transferase I) ★ increased binding to the level that was obtained using supernatant from liver of laser irradiated rats.
