[Cardiovascular complications, lipoprotein metabolism and oral contraceptives].
It is well known that the relative risk of cardiovascular complications is increased in women using oral contraceptives (OCs). Relative risks of venous thromboembolic and the much more rare arterial thromboembolic disorders have been found to be related to the estrogen dose in OCs. Thromboembolism is 2-5 times more common in users of OCs with estrogen doses of 100 mcg than in those containing 50 mcg. Risk is unrelated to duration of use and disappears with discontinuation of use. The reported number of venous thromboses has declined in recent years as estrogen content in OCs has been reduced. Other factors such as age and abdominal and pelvic surgical intervention favor thromboembolism and OCs augment preexisting risks. OCs increase the small risk of cerebral thromboses and hemorrhages in young women. Some studies have found no relation to estrogen dose while others have found greater frequency in users of higher dose progestins. Risk factors include migraine hypertension smoking and hypercholesterolemia. OCs are an independent risk factor and interact synergistically with other factors. Myocardial infarcts are the most frequently observed cardiovascular complication and usually result from thrombosis or atherosclerotic lesions. Risk factors in which intervention is impossible include age male sex and a family history of coronary disease at young ages. Major risk factors in which intervention is possible include hypertension hypercholesterolemia smoking and low level of high density lipoprotein (HDL) cholesterol. Minor risk factors permitting intervention include hypertriglyceridemia diet diabetes obesity sedentary life style electrocardiographic anomalies type A personality renal insufficiency and others. OCs may accelerate the process of atherosclerosis and their effects on myocardial infarct may persist after discontinuation of OC use. OCs also may influence other risk factors for myocardial infarct. Blood pressure is elevated in OC users but only a few develop hypertension with family history a frequently observed factor. Pill-induced hypertension is usually reversible; it appears to be related to the dose and type of progestins. OCs diminish glucose tolerance but the effect is minimal in most women. The effect is also related to the dose and type of progestin. Smoking is an important cardiovascular risk factor especially after age 30 and increases the cardiovascular risks of OC use. Primary hyperlipoproteinemia is considered a contraindication to OC use. Estrogens increase very low density lipoprotein (VLDL) and HDL cholesterol but triglyceride levels remain within normal limits for most OC users. Synthetic progestins derived from 19-nortestosterone diminish VLDL levels and increase low density lipoprotein (LDL) levels with the action depending on dose and type of progestin. Progestins also decrease HDL levels with effects depending on dose and type. The overall effect is to increase the atherogenic index. The effect of an OC on lipids depends on its relative estrogen and progestin content and on the type of progestin. Norgestrel has greater effects on lipids than norethindrone.
