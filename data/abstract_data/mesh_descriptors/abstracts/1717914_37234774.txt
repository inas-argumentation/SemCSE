A symptom questionnaire for hypertensive patients.
A self-administered symptom questionnaire is described for use in hypertensive patients. The questionnaire was employed in a hypertension clinic in order to quantify the complaints of the treated patients, and was posted to the patients prior to a clinic attendance. There was an 82 per cent return rate without a reminder and the questions were well answered with a completion rate in excess of 93 per cent of all questions, apart from those on sexual intercourse (76–83 per cent completion rate). The questionnaire was also sent to a control group of normotensive subjects. The treated hypertensive patients complained more of sleepiness, postural hypotension, dry mouth, nocturia, diarrhea, slow walking pace, impotence and failure of ejaculation, than did the normotensive controls. Headache and depression were more common in women than men at all ages. These two symptoms were reduced in the elderly. The walking pace ‘compared to other men or women of your age’ fell with increasing age and nocturia was an increasing complaint in the elderly. The male frequency of sexual intercourse fell with increasing age, and the complaints of impotence and postural hypotension tended to increase with age. The older patients complained less of sleepiness. Negro patients slept less hours than Caucasian patients and complained more of weakness in the limbs. They also reported an increase in headache, diarrhea, nocturia and dry mouth. Increased levels of blood pressure were associated with weakness of the limbs and morning headache, and an elevation in lying blood pressure was associated with nocturia. It is concluded that the questionnaire is a useful research tool in studies of hypotensive agents.
