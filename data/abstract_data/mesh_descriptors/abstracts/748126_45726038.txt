We implemented our studies with the same criteria to estabhsh the diagnosis of asthma and allergic rhinitis as well as to perform the skin priek test. The skin prick test was performed on all subjects, according to methodology and criteria proposed by the Italian Society of Allergy and Clinical Immunology (5), and with a common panel of allergens (3, 4). Sensitization was considered when there was a relationship between history and skin prick test positivity (i.e., wheal diameter > 3 mm). Subjects with just one positive skin prick test were considered monosensitized; on the other hand, subjects with two or more positive skin prick tests were considered polysensitized. Statistical analysis was performed by the chi-square test, confidence limits of the relative risk being calculated at 95%. The distribution of lifetime asthma and allergic rhinitis rates, and their association in 1981 and 1995, respectively, are shown in Table 1. The prevalence of asthma was significantly decreased in the second observation (P<0.05), while the prevalence of its association with rhinitis was increased {P<0.05). Polysensitized subjects significantly increased in 1995 in comparison with 1981 {P<0.Q5). Mite allergen appeared to be the most important causal allergen: mite cutaneous positivity increased (P<0.05); there was an increase in polysensitized subjects (P<0.001). The pollen sensitization decreased in total (P = 0.001) and for monosensitized (P<0.009) subjects. Pet sensitization showed an iticreasing trend {P<0.05); only one patient was monosensitized. The most important finding of this study was the increasing trend of the association of asthma with allergic rhinitis over the period studied. This finding confirms the trend of a growing worsening of allergic diseases, and it is similar to that of our previous survey on conscripts (3). Other findings of the present study show that indoor allergens are predominant in the present context and there is an increasing trend of polysensitizations. These findings are also consistent with the conscript observations (3). In other words, it would seem that the epidemiologic role of allergic diseases is growing both as prevalence and spectrum of clinical features and sensitizations. A possible explanation of these findings is the increasing pathogenetic role played by environmental factors (1). In fact, it is well known that allergic diseases are genetically infiuenced (1), but they appear clinically when aggressive factors break the immunologic equilibrium, allowing sensitization to occur (2). Among these factors, air pollution, viral infections, and changes in the indoor microenvironment (such as heating methods, thermal proofing devices, the introduction of pets, etc.) may play a critical role. In fact, this study suggests that indoor allergens are the major cause of allergic respiratory diseases, probably because they are perennial. In conclusion, it clearly appears that asthma and allergic rhinitis are two pathologic conditions causing increasing economic costs in terms of health care.
