Effects of sequential extraction of histone proteins on structural organization of avian erythrocyte and liver nuclei.
Nuclei from chicken erythrocytes and liver were isolated in saline and histone proteins were fractionally dissociated by sequential acid extraction. At each stage nuclei were fixed both at the pH of extraction and at neutral pH, embedded, sectioned and examined by electron microscopy. Unextracted nuclei showed 200 A diameter chromatin fibrils with gross distribution similar to that in situ. Removal of histone I from liver nuclei caused irreversible change to tightly intermeshed fibrils of approx. 100 A diameter. In similarly extracted erythrocyte nuclei the chromatin was tightly packed and fibrillar substructure was not visible after glutaraldehyde fixation. Removal of both histones I and V from erythrocyte nuclei revealed fibrils up to 100 A in diameter. Extraction of further histones from both nuclei gave loosely packed fibrils of variable diameters down to 20 A. Despite these marked changes in fine structure the gross distribution of heterochromatin in the nuclei was not markedly changed during extraction. Removal of loosely bound histones (I and V) caused essentially no change while extraction of tightly bound histones (IIb1, IIb2, III and IV) induced loosening within the heterochromatin masses, but the general distribution of the masses was still recognizable. It is concluded that all histone fractions contribute to the integrity of the 200 A chromatin fibrils found in fixed preparations. Histone proteins appear to be only partially responsible for the gross secondary organization of chromatin; non-histone proteins and the nuclear membrane are probably also important for such structure.
