The Effect of CPAP on Vascular Function and Cardiac Structure in Diabetes and Sleep Apnea: A Randomized Controlled Trial.
RATIONALE
Although both type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) are independently recognized as risk factors for cardiovascular disease, little is known about their interaction.


OBJECTIVES
We hypothesized that T2DM and OSA act synergistically to increase vascular risk, and that treatment of OSA would improve vascular reactivity in patients with T2DM+OSA.


METHODS
Cross-sectional study of 141 adults with T2DM, OSA, T2DM+OSA, and controls, followed by a 3-month, parallel-arm, randomized, placebo-controlled trial comparing active and sham continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) in 53 adults with T2DM+OSA. Endothelium-dependent macro- and micro-vascular reactivity (flow-mediated dilation (FMD) of the brachial artery and acetylcholine-induced dilation of forearm micro-vasculature, respectively) and cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) to assess left- and right-ventricular mass/volume.


RESULTS
Mean+SD FMD was 6.1±4.0%, 7.3±3.6%, 6.8±4.5%, and 4.8±2.9% in controls, T2DM-only, OSA-only, and T2DM+OSA, respectively. We observed a significant T2DM*OSA interaction on FMD, such that the mean effect of OSA in those with T2DM was 3.1% (95% CI 0.6 - 5.6) greater than the effect of OSA in those without T2DM. Three months of CPAP resulted in a mean absolute increase in FMD of 0.3% (95% CI -1.9 - 2.5); primary endpoint), with a net improvement of 1.1% (95% CI -1.4 - 3.6) amongst those with adherence ≥4 hours/night. A significant T2DM*OSA interaction was found for both LV and RV end-diastolic volume, such that OSA was associated with a 22.4mL (95% CI 3.2 - 41.6) greater LV end-diastolic volume and 23.2mL (95% CI 2.6 - 43.8) greater RV end-diastolic volume in those with T2DM compared to the impact of OSA in those without T2DM. We observed a net improvement in LV end-diastolic volume of 8.7mL (95% CI -7.0 - 24.4).


CONCLUSIONS
The combination of T2DM+OSA is associated with macro-vascular endothelial dysfunction beyond that observed with either disease alone. CPAP for 3-months did not significantly improve macro-vascular endothelial function in the intent-to-treat analysis; however, CMR results suggest that there may be a beneficial effect of CPAP on LV diastolic volume. Clinical trial registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT01629862).
