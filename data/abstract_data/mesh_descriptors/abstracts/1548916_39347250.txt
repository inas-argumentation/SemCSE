Elevated sympathetic nerve activity: the link between low birth size and adult-onset metabolic syndrome?
Evidence of increased sympathetic nerve activity constitutes a tempting explanation for the relationship between low birth weight and increased risk of cardiovascular disease in adulthood. However, regulation and function of the sympathetic nervous system is highly complex, as shown by the fundamental role of sympathetic activity in the regulation of cardiovascular and metabolic properties. Therefore, it is insurprising that, in subjects born small for gestational age, decreased [2], unchanged [3] and increased sympathetic nerve activity [1,4] have been reported. Several factors of influence have to be addressed to elucidate the relationship between alterations in sympathetic nerve activity, low birth weight and adult-onset cardiovascular disease.
