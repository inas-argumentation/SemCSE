Cerebrovascular nodules in a patient with leukostasis
A 68-year-old man was admitted to the hospital with an uncategorized, Philadelphia chromosome-negative myeloproliferative disorder associated with leukocytosis, anemia and thrombocytopenia. His WBC count on admission was 290,000 per mm 3 , and oral hydroxyurea was started on Day 2 in hospital, when the WBC count was 392,000 per mm 3 . On Day 3, the WBC count was 323,000 per mm 3 , and the patient became confused and eventually stuporous. A cerebral CT scan demonstrated multiple hyper-dense nodules surrounded by edema. Therapeutic leukapheresis was initiated on Day 4; no sedimenting agent or other drugs were used. Two procedures were performed, on Days 4 and 5. The patient's WBC count fell from 316,000 per mm 3 prior to the first apheresis to 69,000 per mm 3 on Day 6. The hemoglobin level went from 8.9 g per dL percent to 6.6 g per dL, and the platelet count from 71,700 per mm 3 to 37,700 per mm 3 . Despite the intervention, the patient became comatose on Day 7, and died on Day 10. An autopsy was not performed. Leukostasis occurs in patients with high blast counts due to aggregates of leukemic cells in vessels, especially in the lung and brain. In patients with very high WBC counts (> 300,000/mm 3 ), these leukemic cells can destroy vessel walls and invade white matter to form nodules, often surrounded by hemorrhage. Because hydroxyurea takes at least 24 h for an effect, early cytoreduction by leukapheresis may be useful for the symptomatic patient. However, leukapheresis may not be able to reverse the more advanced pathology of intracerebral leukemic nodules, which could have contributed to the death of our patient.
