Misleading chest X-ray findings following the insertion of a breast expander.
Figure 1 Chest x-ray showing fluid level within a left sided breast expander. A 57 year old woman was diagnosed with a left sided high grade ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS). She underwent a skin sparing mastectomy and immediate implant only reconstruction at Addenbrooke’s Hospital, Cambridge. The patient is an ex-smoker with no history of respiratory disease who suffers with ankylosing spondylitis and osteoarthritis of the right knee. She underwent a left mastectomy and a subpectoral expander (McGhan 150 SH) was inserted. The expander was filled with 250 mls of saline in theatre, the port was tunnelled subcutaneously and two drains were inserted. The procedure went well and was uneventful with no intraoperative issues. Post-operatively she received a standard course of oral antibiotics and thromboprophylaxis; including TED stockings and 40 mg of enoxaparin daily. On the second postoperative day, the patient developed a dry cough and desaturated to 91% on air for a short period of time. She was seen by the on-call doctor and her oxygen saturation returned to normal after a short course of oxygen therapy, deep breathing and repositioning on the bed. The most likely diagnosis for this episode of desaturation was atelectasis but in order to exclude a pneumothorax or a chest infection following her surgery a chest x-ray was requested. The chest x-ray was performed and it was reported by the Consultant Radiologist as having features suggesting a left hydropneumothorax (arrow right) with a drain projecting over the left chest (arrow left) (Figure 1). The right lung was clear. Despite the chest x-ray appearances the patient remained clinically well. There had been no further episodes of oxygen desaturation or coughing, and the patient had not complained of any chest pain or shortness of breath. Her oxygen saturations remained above 98% on air.
