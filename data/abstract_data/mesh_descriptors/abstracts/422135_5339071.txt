Researchers wrestle with spread and control of emerging infections.
HIV/AIDS in Russia HIV is sweeping through the northwestern region of Russia, according to new findings reported here by researchers from Norway and the Russian Federation, and the unfolding epidemic is causing alarm among public health experts. Previously, officials with the United Nations Programme on HIV/AIDS (UNAIDS) had warned of an explosion of HIV in other areas of Russia. The country’s first reported HIV epidemic emerged in 1996 in the Baltic port city of Kaliningrad, mostly among male injecting drug users (IDUs). Researchers then noted a similar explosive surge of new HIV infections in other regions, including St Petersburg and surrounding Leningrad county, as well as parts of the central Russian Federation. Within 4 years, the infection swept across the country to more than 30 cities, and health officials feared further spread ( JAMA. 2000;284:3113-3114). Now, researchers have discovered a new region in Russia experiencing a dramatic surge in HIV infection: Murmansk, a far-northern county of about 1 million inhabitants near the Barents Sea. This outbreak is fueled by unsafe injecting practices among IDUs, and because of Murmansk’s location, there is concern that the unfolding epidemic will be spread not only within this part of Russia but also across nearby borders to Norway and Finland by drug users and sex workers, said Egil Bjorlow, MD, of the Norwegian Institute of Public Health, in Oslo.
