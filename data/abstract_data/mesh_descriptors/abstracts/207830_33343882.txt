EFFECT OF MIDBRAIN STIMULATION ON A VISUAL RESPONSE.
Abstract Recordings were made from neural elements in the cat's lateral geniculate body with micropipette electrodes. Retinal stimulation was performed with flashes of light; mesencephalic, with electrical pulses. With the latter, two interaction patterns were demonstrated. One was facilitation of the visual response by stimulating the mesencephalic reticular formation and was characterized by an increase in the evoked discharges; the other was occlusion of the visual response by stimulating the mesencephalic lemniscus. These two interactions closely resembled the patterns obtained with simultaneous application of light and cutaneous-nerve stimuli.
