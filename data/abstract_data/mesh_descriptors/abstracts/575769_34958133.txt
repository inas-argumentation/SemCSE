Cancer detection with human peripheral lymphocytes: a word of caution.
In this paper the validity of two proposed general tests for early cancer detection is questioned. These tests, the macrophage electrophoretic mobility (MEM) test and the related structuredness cytoplasmic matrix (SCM) test, are based upon the interaction of isolated human peripheral lymphocyte preparations with protein extracts derived from any human tumour. Evidence is presented to indicate that the MEM procedure is unlikely to have an immunological basis as proposed by the originators of the test. An alternative mechanism is suggested that is based upon non-specific morphological and biochemical changes that occur during the isolation of lymphocytes from the peripheral blood of patients with clinically diagnosed cancer compared with normal individuals. The SCM test is discussed in relation to these observations.
