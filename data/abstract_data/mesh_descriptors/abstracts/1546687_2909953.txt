Glycogen metabolism in fetal and postnatal rat brain: influence of birth
NEWBORN animals of species known to be developmentally immature at birth often possess higher levels of cerebral glycogen than found in adults (MAENPM & RAIH~;, 1968; MAYMAN & TIJERINA, 1971; EDWARDS & ROGERS, 1972). Unlike glycogen in liver, the functional significance of glycogen in brain is poorly understood, although its potential as an energy source may, in part, explain the greater resistance of young animals to hypoxia and cerebral ischemia (COXON, 1970; MAYMAN & TIJERINA, 1971). A pronounced decrease in cerebral glycogen levels during the first 24 h of postnatal life in rats delivered vaginally has been previously demonstrated (VANNUCCI & DUFFY, 1974), but neither the time course nor the underlying biochemical mechanisms of the depletion were investigated. This study was undertaken to explore more thoroughly glycogen metabolism during the perinatal period by measuring changes in cerebral glycogen and related metabolites in fetuses and in developing postnatal rats particularly during the initial 24 h after birth.
