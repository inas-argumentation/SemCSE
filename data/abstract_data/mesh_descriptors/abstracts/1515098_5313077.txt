Insulin resistance correlated with the severity of liver histology in Japanese NAFLD patients: a multicenter retrospective study.
To the Editor: We read the article by Jung et al1 with great interest. In their study, they demonstrated the correlation between metabolic-related biochemical evaluation and the histologic activity of the liver in nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) patients. Their results showed that the severity of hepatic fibrosis was strongly correlated with the serum aspartate aminotransferase/alanine aminotransferase ratio and highdensity lipoprotein cholesterol level. However, their study did not show a significant correlation between insulin resistance (IR) and the severity of both hepatic histologic inflammatory grading and fibrosis staging in NASH patients. They stated several limitations in their study. First, some NASH subjects were retrospectively enrolled and thus selection bias was not completely eliminated. Second, they applied nonhistologic criteria to clinically diagnose NASH in subjects who did not receive a liver biopsy, which may incur diagnostic verification bias. Finally, the study was a relatively small-scale study carried out at a single center, which is possibly a limiting factor with respect to generalizing these results. Therefore, they speculated that a large-scale study to maximize the generalization of these results should be performed in the future. Homeostasis model assessmentInsulin Resistance (HOMA-IR), calculated using the fasting plasma glucose and fasting insulin, is frequently observed in patients with NASH,2 and measurement of the fasting insulin is clinically important for simple and accurate prediction of NASH in nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) patients.3 However, an association between the severity of IR and that of liver histologic inflammation and fibrosis is not well established in NAFLD patients. Neuschwander-Tetri et al4 reported a significant positive correlation between HOMA-IR and the severity of liver fibrosis in patients with NAFLD, based on the results of a multicenter retrospective cohort study in America. Recently, in Japan, we reported a large-scale prospective study in biopsyproven NAFLD patients who were enrolled between 2002 and 2011 at institutes affiliated with the Japan Study Group of NAFLD (JSG-NAFLD), which included 10 hepatology centers.5 Using a multicenter database, 1365 biopsy-proven NAFLD patients were investigated. We examined the HOMAIR value based on the degree of the following 5 liver histology criteria: (1) steatosis grade, (2) lobular inflammation grade, (3) ballooning grade, (4) fibrosis stage, and (5) NAS score. Our results showed that HOMAIR significantly increased according to the degree of all 5 criteria. Briefly, the results are as follows: (1) steatosis (3.61±1.77 in grade 1, 4.19±1.97 in grade 2, 4.94±2.23 in grade 3; P< 0.01); (2) lobular inflammation (2.63± 1.47 in grade 0, 3.69±1.59 in grade 1, 4.20±1.79 in grade 2, 4.69± 2.16 in grade 3; P<0.01); (3) ballooning (2.65±1.22 in grade 0, 3.78±1.79 in grade 1, 4.89±2.14 in grade 2; P<0.01); (4) fibrosis (2.68±1.12 in stage 0, 3.46±1.63 in stage 1, 4.01± 1.74 in stage 2, 4.32±1.72 in stage 3, 4.60±2.10 in stage 4; P<0.01); and (5) NAS score (2.32±0.10 in NAS 0 to 2, 3.55±1.72 in NAS 3 to 4, 4.70± 1.96 in NAS 5 to 8; P<0.01). Liver biopsy can be used as a tool to confirm NASH; it can reveal the histologic activity of steatosis, inflammation, and fibrosis, and it is frequently used for diagnosis as the gold standard tool to determine the degree of NASH in patients.6 As the authors pointed out, the differences of HOMAIR according to the differences in the degree of liver histology that were obtained using liver biopsy have been reported in only a few studies worldwide.1,4 Therefore, these findings remain controversial. An investigation that includes many institutions from many countries where liver biopsies are performed may be necessary. Although there were some limitations of selection bias, diagnostic verification bias, and the effects of a small-scale study, Jung and colleagues conducted a valuable investigation, which evaluated both HOMA-IR and the degree of biopsyproven liver histology. Following Jung et al,1 our results showed a significant correlation between HOMA-IR and the liver histology grade severity, stage, and NAS score in a Japanese multicenter study. With the global increase in the incidence of obesity and diabetes, Jung and colleagues’ and our findings will become increasingly important. Additional information from other institutions from many countries may also be needed to clarify the association between HOMA-IR and the degree of liver histology. The contents of this letter show the correlation between IR and the liver histology severity, and it may become a more precise means for noninvasively diagnosing NASH in NAFLD patients or to evaluate the severity in NASH patients.
