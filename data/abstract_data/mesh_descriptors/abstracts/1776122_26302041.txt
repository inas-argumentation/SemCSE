REDUCTION OF CHLOROMYCETIN AND RELATED COMPOUNDS BY ESCHERICHIA COLI
Smith and Worrel (1949, 1950b) have demonstrated that the nitro group of chloromycetin is essential for the antibiotic properties of the drug. Bacterial reduction of the nitro group results in lose of bacteriostatic action and enables the organism to overcome the action of the drug and resume normal growth. An explanation of how the nitro group may exert its action on microorganisms has been suggested recently by Egami, Ebata, and Sato (1951). They indicate a competitive reaction between the nitro group of chloromycetin and nitrite for the active centers of nitrite reductase. Nitrite inhibits the reduction of the nitro, and similarly chloromycetin inhibits the reduction of the nitrite. Various investigations on the metabolismof the nitro group of chloromycetin and related compounds have suggested that the ease with which the nitro group was reduced may be related to both the configuration of the molecule and the composition of the aliphatic chain attached to the benzene ring. These observations suggested that the D (-) threo isomer of chloromycetin might exhibit a distinct pattern of reduction of the nitro group which could be related to the competitive reaction with the nitrite and which might possibly explain the antibiotic activity of this isomer which would distinguish it from the other inactive forms of chloromycetin.
