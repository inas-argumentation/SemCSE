Microvascular pressures in the isolated, perfused dog lung: comparison between theory and measurement.
Measurements by direct micropuncture of subpleural pulmonary vessels in the isolated, perfused dog lung indicated that 46% of pulmonary vascular resistance is in the anatomical capillaries. Are these data applicable to the whole lung? We applied parallel tube, sheet flow, and capillary network models to calculate the pressure drop for Zone III conditions, as in the micropuncture experiments. We used published data for the following capillary parameters: volume, 50 ml; blood flow, 20 ml/sec; viscosity, 2 cP; length, 510 μm; mean thickness, 4.8 μm; vascular-space/tissue ratio (for the sheet-and-post model), 0.92; width (for the tube flow models), 10 μm. The capillary network and distensible sheet-and-post models predict that the fraction of total resistance due to the capillaries should be 45 and 50%, respectively. Since the models are for the whole lung, the agreement between theoretical and measured values suggests that pleural surface microvascular pressures may be representative of microvascular pressures in the interior of the lung and that capillaries may account for about half of the total resistance to flow. Uncertainty in capillary parameter values, however, render such conclusions somewhat tentative. Improved measurements of capillary volume, path length, and thickness would greatly aid our understanding of pulmonary blood flow.
