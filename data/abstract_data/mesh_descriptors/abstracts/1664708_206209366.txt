“CTA and contrast-enhanced MRA are equally accurate for localizing deep inferior epigastric perforator flap arteries e a systematic review” should be read not only for what it reveals, but also for seeing how it raises the bar for transparency. Unless you recently read the JPRAS0 Guide for Authors, you would be unaware that it has changed and that this change is a seismic event for improving the quality of systematic reviews. Previously, JPRAS gave no guidance to authors submitting a systematic review. Now, authors must attest in their Methods section that they used the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) statement. This systematic review has surpassed JPRAS0 new requirement because the authors have gone beyond merely making an attestation. They exceeded JPRAS0 requirement by including both a completed copy of the PRISMA checklist and their work product in their online supplement.
