Combinative treatment of transarterial chemoembolization, celecoxib and lanreotide in unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma.
Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) reveals a pessimistic prognosis due to its abundant blood supply and highly invasive characteristics. In actual fact, the majority of patients are usually diagnosed at advanced stage, which makes curative therapies impossible [1], and only noncurative therapies such as transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) can be employed. However, neoangiogenesis after TACE exerts negative effect on the overall survival of these patients [2,3]. Our preclinical studies implied that transarterial embolization combined with cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) inhibitors and somatostatin analogues, prolonged survival of animals significantly by decreasing tumor weight and metastasis, promoting encapsulation and inhibiting angiogenesis [4,5]. Meanwhile, we made a successful translation of this regimen in the clinical treatment for unresectable HCC. In July 2008, a 49-year-old male patient was admitted to our hospital with the complaint of right quadrant abdominal pain for three months. He had a 15-yearhistory of chronic hepatitis B and a 20-year-history of alcoholism. His blood tests showed the positive hepatitis B virus (HBV) surface antigen and anti-core antibody with a HBV DNA level of < 200 IU/mL and a alapha fetoprotein (AFP) level of > 1210 ng/mL (normal value, nv: < 8). Other laboratory test data were as below: total bililubin 1.4 mg/dL (nv: < 1.6), alanine aminotransferase 39 IU/L (nv: < 55), aspartate aminotransferase 44 IU/L (nv: < 46), albumin 33.9 g/L (nv: 35—55) and prothrombin time 10.4 s (nv: 9.6—12.8).
