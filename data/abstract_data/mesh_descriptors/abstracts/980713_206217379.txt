An unusual case of a "hepatic" tumor.
Question: A 56-year-old woman presented to our clinic with a mass located in her upper abdomen, in association with a 3-kg weight loss over 1 month. Furthermore, 1 year previously, the patient was diagnosed as having a hepatic hemangioma (Figure A); she was managed conservatively without any specific treatment. On physical examination, the abdomen was soft without tenderness. A palpable firm and elastic mass about 9 7c m was detected near the epigastrium. The laboratory findings were unremarkable except abnormal liver function, including the levels of total bilirubin (2.2 mg/dL; normal 0.2‐1.2), direct bilirubin (1.5 mg/dL; normal, 0.4), aspartate transaminase (133 U/L; normal, 37), and alanine transaminase (150 U/L; normal, 41). The serum markers for viral hepatitis B and C were negative. An abdominal ultrasonography and subsequent magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed a huge hepatic tumor in the left lobe of the liver (Figure B) with anterior lower chest wall adhesion or invasion. The tumor size increased markedly over 1.5 years. What is your diagnosis?
