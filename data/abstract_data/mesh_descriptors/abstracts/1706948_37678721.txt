A STUDY ON BACTERIAL SENSITIZATION IN THE INFECTIVE ASTHMA
Infective asthma is regarded as a bronchial asthma syndrome which starts and develops on obvious relation to infections of the respiratory organs. Infective asthma can occur independently or with exogenous allergic bronchial conditions (mixed infective and exogenous allergic asthma). It can also co-exist with chronic bronchitis, in w ĥich case there is the possibility of bronchial, peribronchial and alveolar alterations which constitute the anatomical substrate of the pulmonary emphysema (infective asthma complicated by bronchitis, bronchiolitis and pulmonary emphysema) {Ricci cl al. 1963). As Zamtssi & Alazzci say (1961), it is sometimes very difficult "to distinguish on the clinical plane between infective asthma and the other infectious respiratory symptoms capable of sustaining a similar symptomatology, usually presenting all the symptoms of asthmatic bronchitis". Herxheimer (1961) and Orie (1961) too stress the difficulty of discriminating, both on the clinical and etiopathogenical plane, between the various asthmatic forms of bronchitis.
