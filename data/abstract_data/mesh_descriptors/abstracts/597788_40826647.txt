Effect of radiographic contrast medium and bile on antimicrobial activity
Sir, Surgery and endoscopy of the biliary system may be associated with local and systemic complications such as wound infection and septicaemia, especially in the presence of bacteriobilia (1). Numerous trials have been performed to determine which antibiotics act prophylactically to prevent these complications (2, 3). All antibiotics tested significantly reduced postoperative wound infection: the 20-25 % infection rate found in controls (without prophylaxis) fell to 4 5 % after antibiotic prophylaxis. The spectrum of these antibiotics must include pathogens occurring at the site of infection, e.g. Escherichia coli, Klebsiella aerogenes, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, or Streptococcus faecalis (4), which frequently cause such septic complications. It has recently been proposed that antibiotics should be added to the contrast media and directly injected into the biliary system before endoscopy (5). Before this can become a routine procedure, however it is necessary to determine whether the antibiotics are compatible with the contrast media and bile. We determined the antimicrobial activity of gentamicin, cefuroxime and mezlocillin in the presence of meglumine iothalomate, a frequently used contrast medium, and bile. The MICs of gentamicin (David Bell Laboratories), mezlocillin (Bayer) and cefuroxime (Glaxo) for a strain of Escherichia coil isolated from a biliary T-tube drainage were determined first in nutrient broth, and subsequently in equal volumes of doublestrength nutrient broth with meglumine iothalomate or bile. Antibiotic-free bile obtained from a T-tube drainage of a patient was filtered, sterilized and stored at 4 vC. An inoculum of 5 X 10 s CFU/ml of Escherichia coli and doubling dilutions of antibiotics from 100 mg/1 to 0.15 mg/1 were used. The MICs of the three antibiotics were identical in both the presence and absence of bile. The MIC for gentamicin was 0.3 rag/l; mezlocillin 3.1 mg/1 and cefuroxime 0.7 mg/1. The strain of Escherichia coli did not grow in the presence of meglumine iothalomate. Killing curves for Escherichia coli were also determined in the presence of each antibiotic at a concentration of 100mg/1 and of equal volumes of double-strength nutrient broth with either meglumine iothalomate or bile. Samples of 0.1 ml taken at 2-h intervals were spread onto blood agar plates and then incubated at 37 ~ to determine viable counts. Within 2h the viable counts decreased to less than 10 CFU/ml in the presence of antibiotic, antibiotic and bile, or antibiotic and contrast agent, and to approximately 103 CFU/ml in the presence of meglumine iothalo. mate alone. Finally each of the antibiotics was mixed with bile and the contrast agent and kept overnight. The biological activity of the mixture was assessed by a plate assay procedure. The solutions were mixed to give a concentration of 100 mg/1 of antibiotic and diluted two to tenfold before assay to give readable zones. The amount of antibiotic detected after incubation overnight with either bile or contrast agent was within 10 % of the control value of 100 mg/1. These findings clearly demonstrated that neither bile nor the contrast agent affected the biological activity of the antibiotics, thus confirming and extending the findings of Jendrzejewski et at. (6). The frequently used prophylactic antibiotics gentamicin, mezlocillin and cefuroxime are compatible with both bile and the contrast agent, and can therefore be added to radiographic contrast media before endoscopy of the biliary system.
