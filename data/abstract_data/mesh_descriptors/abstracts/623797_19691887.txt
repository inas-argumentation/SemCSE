Commentary on: "The percutaneous trampoline platysmaplasty: technique and experience with 105 consecutive patients".
In their article, Dr. Mueller and his coauthors describe a novel percutaneous or “closed” approach to the submental area with convincing long-term follow-up. Their procedure borrows from and cleverly expands on several previously well-described techniques. As implied by the authors, contour improvement by liposuction of the submental and submandibular area occurs not only due to fat removal but, just as importantly, because of the unique ability of neck skin to contract once released from its platysmal attachments. Spanning or “woven” sutures, popularized by Giampapa and others,1-3 provide an underlying support or “hammock” effect for the soft tissue above the sutures. The technique described by Mueller et al features a more extensive “back-and-forth” weave with the addition of suture anchoring to the mandibular osteocutaneous and platysmal-auricular ligaments.
