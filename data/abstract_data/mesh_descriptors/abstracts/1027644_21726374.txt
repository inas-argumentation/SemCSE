As before, infection with neither Pseudomonas aeruginosa or Staphylococcus aureus was associated with changes in FEV1% predicted. Use of standardized protocols and rigorous statistical analysis were strengths of the study. For example, evaluation of sputum quality showed that 91% of the 132 sputum samples met acceptability criteria and effects of gender differences were well controlled. Inclusion of a validation cohort of 10 additional patients with CF confirmed the relationship between baseline NE and lung function decline (mean [SE] annual change in FEV1% predicted 1⁄4 21.07 [0.82]) and the ability of an increased sputum NE to predict a more rapid decline in lung function (c-index 1⁄4 0.76). Acknowledged limitations included the single-center study design, inclusion of children with only normal or mildly abnormal lung function, and relatively few patients (35 in the main cohort, 20 and 15 in the “rapid-” and “slow-decliners” groups). Further, data obtained from the study visits were also used in treatment decisions, which likely affected outcomes. Notwithstanding, results confirm findings from a large retrospective review of spontaneously expectorated sputum biomarkers measured in several CF clinical trials, which included data from 269 patients with CF aged 9 to 54 years with FEV1% predicted ranging from 25 to 120 and showed that NE was negatively correlated with FEV1% predicted (correlation 1⁄4 20.35; 95% confidence interval [CI]: 20.46, 20.22) (10). In that study, patients with CF who differed in their elastase measurements by 0.5-log differed in their FEV1% predicted values by 27.3 (95% CI: 29.7, 24.6). It further showed a significant longitudinal association of sputum NE and lung function with a decline in FEV1% predicted of 22.9 (95% CI: 25.0, 20.9) per 1-log increase in elastase. The present study results confirm another study in which older, untreated patients with CF had increased BAL biomarkers of airway inflammation (NE, neutrophil numbers, IL-8) compared with patients receiving DNase therapy (13). Taken together, emerging data from these studies provide strong support for the use of sputum biomarkers of airway inflammation in monitoring lung disease in patients with CF over a wide range of lung severity and ages. Specifically, they provide the means to predict which patients will experience a rapid deterioration and therefore require earlier intervention. Identification of biomarkers associated with clinically significant exacerbations of CF lung disease would be of use in more objectively identifying individuals in need of urgent antimicrobial therapy. Future studies will also need to determine the “minimal clinically meaningful difference (MCID)” in sputum biomarker measurements that correspond to a change in clinical status, and define specific cutoff thresholds for individual sputum biomarkers that identify “rapid decliners.” These studies also have implications for the role of proteases in the pathogenesis of CF lung disease and identify novel molecular targets for therapy, specifically, neutralization of NE, for example, a-1 antitrypsin augmentation therapy (14).
