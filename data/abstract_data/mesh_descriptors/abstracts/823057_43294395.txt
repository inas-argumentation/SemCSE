Is poor weight management a failure of primary care?
This column is a short story not about innovation but about the failure or lack of innovation in primary care today. Since my adolescence, I have been somewhat obsessed with not being overweight. Today at 47, I weigh 157 lb and my body mass index (BMI) is 24.8, which places me in the “desirable” weight category for men (the recommended BMI range for men and women by the National Institutes of Health is 18.5–24.9).1 A person’s BMI is used by many primary care providers and researchers to classify individuals as underweight, acceptable (or desirable) weight, marginally overweight, overweight, severely overweight, or obese.
