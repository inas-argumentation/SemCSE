Dyspareunia From Large Retained Fetal Bone Diagnosed by Pelvic Ultrasound
A 20-year-old G1P0010 presented with deep, sharp dyspareunia since a 22-week elective abortion that she underwent 2 years prior to presentation. She used only depot medroxyprogesterone for contraception, but a pelvic ultrasound from an outside facility noted the presence of an “intrauterine device.” Repeat transvaginal ultrasound revealed a 16 3 3 mm hyperechogenic structure within the uterine cavity, suggestive of retained partial fetal bone (Figure 1A). This corresponded to a 14-week gestational age femur. Speculum exam revealed a palpable foreign body just distal to the external cervical os. Touching this foreign body reproduced the patient’s dyspareunia. An attempt to remove it with alligator forceps under ultrasound guidance was unsuccessful, and the patient was scheduled for a hysteroscopy. The bone was removed hysteroscopically and appeared grossly to be a partial femur (Figure 1B). The patient reported complete resolution of her dyspareunia. Close to 700,000 elective abortions were performed in the United States in 2012, only 1.3% of which were after 21 weeks’ gestation. The risk of any retained products of conception after a secondtrimester surgical abortion is under 1%, with the risk of retained bone likely much less. Retained fetal bone after an elective abortion has been described as an uncommon cause of gynecologic complaints, but dyspareunia accounts for only 1.1% of these. Fortunately, ultrasound is useful in these rare cases. Ultrasound makes the diagnosis in almost two-thirds of cases, and in uncommon presentations like dyspareunia, symptom resolution occurs in greater than 80% of patients after the removal of the retained bone. This case documents a retained fetal bone presenting as dyspareunia. This case also underscores that a patient’s history can affect the interpretation of imaging studies. Including “patient with dyspareunia, on depot medroxyprogesterone for contraception” may have shortened the time from diagnosis to treatment by excluding the possibility of this foreign body being read as “intrauterine device.”
