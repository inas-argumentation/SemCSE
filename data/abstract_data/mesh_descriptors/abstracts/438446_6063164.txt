The use of oral fluid to determine HIV-1 prevalence rates among men in Mexico City.
Oral fluid samples collected using the OraSure HIV-1 oral specimen collection device were recently approved by the US Food and Drug Administration for HIV testing. To validate the performance of such samples obtained with the OraSure device the authors sampled both serum and saliva obtained by laboratory personnel from 134 known HIV-1-positive persons using the Abbott recombinant anti-HIV-1 second generation ELISA to test for potential detection of false-negative results. All were positive for both serum and oral fluid and confirmed by Western blot. In a 1992-93 population study among sexually active men aged 15-60 years in Mexico City 369 paired samples and 1215 unmatched oral fluid samples were obtained for HIV-1 testing for a total of 1584 persons. On the basis of 503 paired samples 134 from the known HIV-positive persons and 369 from the population survey the sensitivity and specificity of HIV-1 testing in oral fluid using the Abbott recombinant test were calculated to be 98.5% and 100% respectively. There were two HIV-positive results among the 1215 unmatched oral fluid specimens but only one was confirmed by Western blot. One man was confirmed HIV-positive among the 369 paired samples. Therefore two of 1584 persons tested were found to be infected with HIV-1 an estimated prevalence of 0.126% among sexually active men aged 15-60 in Mexico City during 1992-93. A 1987 probability survey estimated a prevalence of 0.04% among sexually active men in Mexico City. HIV-1 seroprevalence among blood donors in Mexico is 0.09%. The authors believe oral fluid samples are ideal for use in field surveys to determine the prevalence of HIV infection.
