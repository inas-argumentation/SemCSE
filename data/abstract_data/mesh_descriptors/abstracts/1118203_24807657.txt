Measurement of the hormones of the renin system in hypertensive patients.
The renin-angiotensin-aldusterone system has emerged as an important factor in blood pressure regulation and in the blood pressure elevation of certain hypotensive patients. Peripheral renin measurements are useful for differentiating primary from secondary aldosteronism, for subdividing hypertensive patients into different treatment protocols, and as a screening test for identifying patients with unilateral renovascular hypertension. Renal vein renin measurements are key diagnostic aids in identifying the presence of unilateral or bilateral renal ischemia and in evaluating the degree of reduction in blood flow to the unilateral ischemic kidney. Renin methods involve an enzymatic incubation followed by radioimmunoassay of the formed angiotensin I. Inactive renin comprises a major portion of the renin in plasma. It can be activated by cold (+6 to −4°) or by acidification to below pH 1.0, conditions that should be avoided prior to the incubation step. To be used as a screening test plasma renin methods must he sufficiently sensitive and specific to explore the subnormal range. Non-specific substances in plasma can displace 125 I-angiotensin from angiotensin antibodies and their concentration is difficult to assay accurately. They comprise a major portion of the angiotensin I in low renin samples but become a negligible fraction after an 18 hour incubation and can then be ignored. Prolonged incubations require prolonged angiotensinase inhibition which can be achieved simply by incubating renin at the p11 optimum (5.7) in the presence of EDTA and PMSF. The angiotensin I RIA provides no unusual problems. A flow diagram is presented in which renin measurements are used for the diagnosis of secondary forms of hypertension and for initiating treatment in the majority of patients with essential hypertension.
