Accumulation of amines in the isolated perfused rabbit lung.
An isolated blood-perfused rabbit lung was used to study the steady-state accumulation of imipramine, amphetamine, chlorcyclizine and methadone. As a function of drug concentration in the blood, the accumulation by the lung was resolved into a saturable component and a linear component. The amines were mutually inhibitory with respect to accumulation. The inhibition between amphetamine and imipramine was noncompetitive, whereas that between imipramine and desipramine was competitive. This difference indicates separate mechanisms for the accumulation of amphetamine and imipramine. Inhibition of amphetamine accumulation by a series of fluorinated amphetamine derivatives indicated that inhibition (and hence uptake of the inhibitor) was directly related to the degree of protonation of the nitrogen atom. The accumulation of various other amines was inferred from their ability to inhibit amphetamine or imipramine uptake. The reversible nature of the accumulation was indicated by the ability of a second amine to displace another already accumulated in the lung. As a result of these steadystate analyses, mechanisms involved in the accumulation of amines by the lung were proposed, and the toxicological and pharmacological implications were discussed.
