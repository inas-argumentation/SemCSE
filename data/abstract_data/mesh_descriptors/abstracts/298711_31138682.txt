Induction in rats of uterine epidermoid carcinomas by plastic and stainless steel intrauterine devices.
Abstract Stainless steel and polyethylene intrauterine devices were placed bilaterally in two groups of 100 female juvenile Wistar rats. Within 2 years, 6 epidermoid carcinomas were found associated with stainless steel devices and t were found associated with polyethylene devices; no epidermoid carcinomas were found in 400 control animals. The relevance of these findings to the use of intrauterine contraceptive devices in women is considered.
