Dermoscopy of primary cutaneous intravascular large B‐cell lymphoma
Intravascular large B-cell lymphoma (IVLBCL) is a rare extranodal non-Hodgkin lymphoma where clinical diagnosis is usually difficult. There are not previous reports on dermoscopy features of this infrequent type of lymphoma. Dermoscopy findings in other primary cutaneous B cell lymphoma include white roundish areas, arborizing or serpentine vessels, and salmon-colored background. We present a 73-year-old man, with asymptomatic diffuse erythematous skin lesions on his flank where dermoscopy shows yellowish-salmon colored areas as well as some irregular and dotted vessels that are more evident using contact dermoscopy, thus leading to a clinical suspicion for B-cell lymphoma. Skin biopsy confirmed IVLBCL. We provide the first case of IVBCL dermoscopy features, that provide further insight regarding the cause of this orange-salmon hue in different types of B-cell lymphomas involving the skin, probably due to a vessel dilatation combined with the presence of atypical lymphoid cells either surrounding or within the blood vessel.
