Current issues and new directions in Psychology and Health: The genetics of obesity–What is the role for health psychology?
Obesity is one of the world’s great health threats, contributing to chronic disease, poor quality of life and premature mortality and threatening to overwhelm health care services. Obesity also increases society’s need for food and its dependence on motorised transport and is associated with reduced productivity, adding ecologic and economic burdens. Current estimates point to rising obesity trends worldwide, with estimates of over 1 billion people obese by 2030. In response to this health threat, understanding the factors that contribute to people eating more than they need has become a major focus for health psychology research, as papers in this issue illustrate (Farrow & Tarrant, 2009; Hilbert et al., 2009; Lawrence et al., 2009). This editorial focuses on the issue of how health psychologists can respond to what is known about the genetics of obesity.
