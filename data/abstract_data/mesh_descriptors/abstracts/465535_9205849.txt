Oxygen consumption of nonfiltering dog kidneys
SummaryThe renal oxygen consumption (VO2) was measured in anesthetized dogs under control conditions, after occluding the ureter and loading the animals with hypertonic mannitol solution to abolish glomerular filtration, and after retrograde filling of the whole renal tubular system with oil to obtain a different model of nonfiltering kidney.The VO2 of the stop-flow mannitol-loaded kidney and of the oil-blocked kidney equalled 3.10±(SE) 0.30 and 4.02±0.52 ml/min.· 100g kidney weight, respectively. Both these values were significantly higher than the basal VO2 of 1.51±0.12 ml/min·100 g estimated under control conditions by extrapolation of VO2 on GFR regression line to zero filtration rate.The elevation of basal VO2 in the stop-flow mannitol-loaded kidney was probably dependent on an outward transport of sodium which balances a passive back influx of this ion into tubular lumen. Such an influx may be the consequence of increased transepithelial sodium concentration gradient and possibly also of a greater permeability of the stretched peritubular cell membrane in dilated tubules. It is proposed that high VO2 of kidneys rendered nonfiltering by oil blockade of the tubules is related to a massive back influx of sodium into tubular cells across cell membranes damaged by oil.
