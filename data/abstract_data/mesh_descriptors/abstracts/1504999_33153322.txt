AA genotype of single‐nucleotide polymorphism c.834+7G>A in GAS6 gene (rs8191974) is not protective for acute myocardial infarction
Sir, GAS6, the product of growth arrest specific gene 6 (GAS6), belongs to the family of vitamin K-dependent proteins. GAS6 protein shares 45% structural homology with coagulation factor protein S, but lacks its anticoagulant effect [1]. GAS6 regulates growth and cell differentiation in adult tissues and during embryo development [2]. GAS6 is expressed in and released by many cell types such as endothelial cells (EC), vascular smooth muscle cells (SMC) and macrophages [3]. GAS6 promotes its cellular effects binding to TYRO3, AXL and MERTK tyrosine kinase receptors (named TAM receptors). In blood, GAS6 mediates inflammation by increasing interaction between EC, leucocytes and platelets [4] and regulates vascular homoeostasis and platelet aggregation, as demonstrated in GAS6 / knocked out mice. Indeed, platelet from GAS6 / mice requires higher concentrations of collagen or ADP to be activated and aggregated. Thus, animals are correlatively protected against thrombosis in several models of arterial and venous thrombosis [5]. GAS6 also plays an important role in vessel wall response to damage and clearance of apoptotic cells by mean of a bridge between phosphatidylserine on apoptotic vascular SMC and MERTK receptor on phagocytic cells [6]. Human GAS6 gene is localized on chromosome 13, and its exon–intron structure has been determined in 2004 [7]. It is composed of 15 introns and 18 exons. Thus, allelic variants seem to be associated with stroke. As the result, only the c.834+7G>A SNP in intron 8 of GAS6 gene (rs8191974) was found to be significantly associated with stroke, with a protective role of the AA genotype [7, 8]. Until now, only one clinical association study evaluated the potential association between acute coronary syndrome and c.834+7G>A GAS6 gene polymorphism and its relation with GAS6 plasma level [9]. A confirmed protective effect of the AA genotype was found in a population of patients composed of angina pectoris, unstable angina pectoris and acute myocardial ischaemia. All these genotype studies, as well as GAS6 plasma phenotype evaluation, suggest an important role of GAS6 in the pathophysiology of cardiovascular diseases and particularly atherothrombosis [10]. The aim of the present case/control study was to test the association between GAS6 rs8191974 polymorphism and patients suffering from acute myocardial infarction (AMI), a restricted and well-defined population.
