Photo quiz. Abdominal pain in a veterinarian with cysts in the liver.
A 44-year-old male veterinarian from the Kyrgyz Republic has been living in the Czech Republic for the last 18 months. He has a history of vomiting, nausea and epigastric pain which always lasted approximately one hour. The vomiting and epigastric pain worsened, and would persist for several hours, so he was hospitalised in May 2005. Physical examination revealed stable vital signs; the abdomen was soft with mild epigastric tenderness. Liver function tests showed elevated serum values: total bilirubin 32 mmol/l (range 0-20), alanine transaminase 3.65 mkat/l (range 0-0.8), aspartate transaminase 6.23 mkat/l (range 0-0.65), alkaline phosphatase 2.67 mkat/l (range 0-2.13), and g-glutamyl transpeptidase 7.44 mkat/l (range 0-1.1). Other laboratory investigations were C-reactive protein 4 mg/l (range 0-8), white blood cells 6.3 x 10/l (range 4.0-10.0), eosinophils 0.16 (range 0.00-0.05) and S-IgE total >2000 IU/ml (range 1.0-200.0). Surgical examination ruled out any acute abdominal event. CT scanning of the abdomen was performed (figures 1 and 2) which showed multiple multilocular septic cystoid lesions with large calcifications in the right liver lobe and lobus quadratus, cholecystolithiasis and one cyst in the core of the left kidney.
