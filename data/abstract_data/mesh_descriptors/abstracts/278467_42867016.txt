Stereospecific binding of etorphine in isolated neural cells and in retina determined by a sensitive microassay
After the first demonstration of stereospecific binding of levorphanol, a morphine congener, in brain tissuO, numerous reports were presented on differential binding of narcotic drugs in the presence of an agonist and its pharmacologically inactive enantiomer, e.g., levorphanol and dextrorphan. Stereospecific binding, reflecting interaction of narcotic drugs with opiate receptor, was described in various preparations from brain la,15,19 and in myenteric plexus of the guinea pig ileum 15, a tissue containing a dense neural network sensitive to narcotic drugs 11. Opiate receptor binding was apparently absent in non-neural tissuO 5. A study focusing on the phylogenetic distribution of opiate receptor binding 12 revealed abundant binding in brains of various vertebrates and the absence of such interaction in brain tissue of invertebrates. Recently, the effect of isolation techniques on opiate receptor binding in neuronal and glial fractions from brain was investigated 2. In the present report the cellular differentiation of opiate receptor binding in brain was determined by measuring stereospecific interaction with etorphine, the most potent morphine-like analgesic, in isolated neuronal and glial cells. Stereospecific binding was also investigated in retina, a CNS tissue containing several specialized types of neurons with no reported sensitivity towards narcotic drugs. A separate goal of this study was to re-evaluate and optimize experimental conditions for determining stereospecific binding of narcotic drugs in small amounts of biological material. In view of the recent evidence for an endogenous opioid peptide, present at low concentration in brain 4,~° and the pituitary 2°, the availability of a sensitive microassay for opiate receptor binding becomes of particular interest. Neuronal perikarya and the glial cell fraction were prepared as described previously 7-9, according to the method of Sellinger et al. is, from cortices of 18-20-dayold, male, Sprague-Dawley rats. The procedure avoids exposure of the tissue to histolytic enzymes and accomplishes tissue disintegration by mechanical disruption using nylon cloths of decreasing pole sizes.
