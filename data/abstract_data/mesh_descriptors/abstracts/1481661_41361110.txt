Thoma et al. utilized the new Vacutainer tubes in a study aimed at identifying an analysis technique for accurately measuring serum levels of tricyclic antidepressants. Contact between blood sample and newly formulated rubber stopper did not appear to alter serum tricyclic antidepressant concentration.' Our study is the first to evaluate the effects of red-top rubber stoppers on lidocaine protein binding. Results showed that protein binding does not change when exposed to the red-top stopper in Vacutainer tubes, and sodium citrate also does not appear to alter lidocaine protein binding. The percent lidocaine free fraction was high in both parts of the study, most likely because of the low concentration (31 mg/dL) of alpha-acid glycoprotein concentration in our subject. Routledge et al. showed that the free fraction of lidocaine is inversely related to the concentration of alpha-acid glycoprotein." Anticoagulants, such as heparin, have been noted to reduce the extent of protein binding of quinidine.' The potential exists that this effect may also occur with other basic drugs such as lidocaine, but this did not prove to be so in our study. Citrated free fractions did not differ significantly from controls. Statistical power analysis using the change in protein binding as the response variable (a =0.05, (3 =0.05) indicated that 13 samples would be needed in each study group. 7 Because of limited volume in each sample and laboratory error, this number was not achieved. With the sample size of six or greater obtained in the first part of the study, the probability of making a type II error would be 40% ({3 = 0.40). In the second part of the study, the sample size in each group was much smaller, ranging from two to four. Thus, the ability to correctly reject Ho' if it is really false, is less t~an 50 percent ({3 = 0.50). In conclusion, although our sample size was small, our results seem to indicate that the new formulation of red-top rubber stoppers do not interfere with protein binding of lidocaine. Second, the addition of sodium citrate to whole blood does not appear to alter the protein binding of lidocaine. SUSAN C. SORRELLS, Pharm.D.
