Adenovirus in the sinuses of an asthmatic patient with apparent selective antibody deficiencies.
On two separate occasions 6 weeks apart, adenovirus type I was cultured from frontal and maxillary sinus tissue removed at surgery from a 37 year old man with bronchitis, reversible obstructive airways disease and pansinusitis. Cultures of nasopharyngeal and bronchial washings as well as samples of stool and urine failed to yield adenovirus. Subsequent immunologic evaluation revealed normal cell-mediated immunity, phagocytic and complement function. However, humoral immunity appeared selectively abnormal. Since viruses failed to grow in 30 surgically-removed sinus tissue samples from 22 similar patients, and since this is the first reported case in which an adenovirus or any other virus has been grown from chronically infected sinus tissue, we suspect that persistent virus infection in chronic sinusitis is an unusual event. In this case an immunologic deficiency may have been associated.
