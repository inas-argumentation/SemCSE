Myocardial infarction in young women on oral contraceptives.
3 case studies of transmural myocardial infarction and the implication of oral contraceptives in its occurence are presented. All 3 of the women studied had been taking oral contraceptives; 2 were heavy cigarette smokers and the third had a recent family history of myocardiac infarction. These cases increase the suspicion that oral contraceptives may potentiate myocardial infarction in the presence of heavy smoking coronary artery disease hypertension hyperlipidemia diabetes a family history of heart disease and other known risk factors. The prognosis for patients who discontinue oral contraceptives and cigarette smoking may be better than that for patients who have suffered myocardial infarction due to other causes. It is advised that doctors discontinue prescription of oral contraceptives for patients who complain of chest pains.
