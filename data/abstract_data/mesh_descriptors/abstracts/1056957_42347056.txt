This parameter has not been analysed in previous studies. Both Hann et al. and Dave et al. reported that mucosal involvement is associated with significant progression, which was not seen in our study. However, in common with these two studies, we found that the Koebner phenomenon was associated with progressive disease. Njoo et al. reported that patients with experimentally induced koebnerization had significantly higher disease activitiy than patients with no koebnerization. We found that leucotrichia was not associated with progression of vitiligo, in common with previous studies. The limitations of the present study include possible recall errors by patients about the development of new lesions, spreading of lesions and koebnerization. Our assumption in defining progressiveness/stability in terms of duration may not always be correct, as disease activity may recur even after periods of quiescence. In conclusion, certain clinical characteristics of nonsegmental vitiligo may be relevant in predicting its course. The presence of vitiligo vulgaris, onset in the upper body, and positive Koebner phenomenon seem to be predictors for progressive disease. Further studies are warranted to collect follow-up data on patients who have achieved stability of their vitiligo to see how long the disease can be expected to remain quiescent and what factors influence this outcome.
