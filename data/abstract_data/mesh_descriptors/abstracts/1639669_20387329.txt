Effect of a single dose of sublingual captopril in hypertensive patients
Recent studies have demonstrated that chewing perforated nifedipine capsules is an effective method for promptly lowering the blood pressure. However, the fall in blood pressure is frequently associated with reflex tachycardia and flushing [1, 2]. The administration of captopril lowers blood pressure levels in all kinds of hypertension, but oral captopril takes about 60-120 rain to achieve its maximum effect [3], and its absorption may be delayed by food [41. Experience with captopril given sublingually to produce an acute fall in elevated systemic blood pressure is documented here. Fifteen patients, 10men and 5women, mean age 55+8years, presenting with essential hypertension (WHO Phase II), judged clinically to require rapid therapy because of headache and dizziness, as well as precordial opression (6 patients), were included in the study. Four patients had discontinued clonidine 48b beforehand, 7 patients were untreated, and the others were refractory to treatment with 3-4 medications. Systolic and diastolic blood pressures were significantly elevated 188 + 5/127 _ 8 mmHg). The patients dissolved one tablet of captopril 25 nag under the tongue. The dissolution time (3.6 + 1.1 min) was determined by the patient, who was asked to indicate the moment when there was the impression that the tablet was no longer present. None of the patients were fasting. Blood pressure and heart rate were measured at various times over a 2-h period, while the patient remained sitting. Five to seven min after the administration of captopril the blood pressure started to decrease. The decrease was significantly greater in patients with the clonidine rebound syndrome (20 (range 10-30)/20 (range 14-30) mmHg; a drop of 15%) than in the other two groups (5 (range 0-10)/ 7 (range 5-15) mmHg). After 15 min, the mean decrease in blood pressure was similar in the 3 groups (28 (range 14-35)/25 (range 8-45) mmHg). At 30 min the mean decrease was 38 (range 28-50)/ 28 (range 20-35) mmHg, and 12 of the 15 patients (80%) had a blood pressure within the normal range. At 60 min blood pressure was 150.0_13/92.8+4.1mmHg. The hypotensive effect was maintained until the end of the observation period 2h (160.0+7/94+4mmHg). The mean heart rate, 15 min after the administration of sublingual captopril, had increased on average by t0beats/min (from 77.3+12.2 to 88.0_+7beats/ min). The results confirm the recent observation by Tschollar and Belz [5], who demonstrated the rapid hypotensive effect of sublingual captopril in 6 patients in hypertensive crisis. The present observation indicates that sublingual captopril is more effective than oral captopril [3], and that it is a safe method to lower elevated blood pressure. The efficacy of sublingual captopril is similar to that of nifedipine [1, 2], but the lack of side effects suggests that captopril should be preferred for the treatment of hypertensive crisis. The present results justify a large controlled study of sublingual captopril in hypertensive emergencies.
