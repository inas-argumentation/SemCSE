α‐FETOPROTEIN (AFP) AND α2‐MACROGLOBULIN (α2‐M) AS THE MARKERS OF DISTINCT RESPONSES OF HEPATOCYTES TO CARCINOGENS IN THE RAT: CARCINOGENESIS
The temporal increase of a-fetoprotein (AFP) in the course of hepatocarcinogenesis has been studied intensively, while only a few data are available on q-macroglobulin (a,-M)’ in hepatocarcinogenesis and the results of these studies are controversial. Some authors have not found any correlation between serum AFP and q M in the course of carcinogenesis induced in rats by 4-dimethylaminoazobenzene,2 3I-methyl-4-dimethyl aminoazobenzene (3’-Me-DAB),’ fluorenylacetamide, and diethylnitr~samine.~ On the other hand, temporal waves of G-M were described in parallel with those of AFP during precancerous stages and tumor growth induced by 3’-Me-DAB? These authors proved the site of synthesis mainly in tumor and also in host liver, but another investigator’ found a,-M absent in hepatocellular carcinoma. The relation of q M synthesis to the cellular events in liver tissue during malignant transformation has not yet been clarified. Therefore, we previously studied6 the cellular basis of a,-M expression in adult liver cells using “one-hit” treatment of rats with carcinogens. The a,-M showed random distribution among the regular liver structures around the areas of necrosis and in areas of regenerating hepatocytes. Dynamics of q M during the initial stage of intoxication resulted from the interference of acute phase reaction and de now synthesis. In the present study, the dynamics of AFP were compared with those of q M and acute-phase proteins represented by Winzler’s seromucoid (SM) fraction during hepatocarcinogenesis in the rat induced by dimethylnitrosamine (DMNA) or 3’-MeDAB. It was shown that only SM discriminates for the toxic and inflammatory changes in the early stage of the process. Serum q M did not correlate with AFP in the 3‘Me-DAB and DMNA experiment. The random distribution of q M among liver cell populations provided a basis for the random patterns of serum q M changes in DMNA-induced carcinogenesis.
