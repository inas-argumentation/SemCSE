MECHANISM OF ACTION OF ALDOSTERONE WITH PARTICULAR REFERENCE TO (Na+ K)‐ATPase *
Evidence suggests that aldosterone regulates sodium transport through DNAdependent synthesis of RNA and consequently increases the de n o w synthesis of one or more proteins. 1-4 Although no specific aldosterone-induced protein has been isolated or identified, several theories have been advanced to account for the cellular mode of action of this p r ~ t e i n . ~ ? ~ The energy theory states that aldosterone increases the activity of one or several metabolic steps, leading to an increased rate of synthesis of ATP; while the permease theory proposes that aldosterone increases mucosal surface sodium permeability by increasing the synthesis of a carrier protein, thereby increasing the quantity of sodium available to a sodium-limited pumping mechanism. Both the permease and energy theories incorporate the concept of an active sodium pump that is regulated by an increased availability of either sodium ions and/or ATP. The biochemical manifestation of this active sodium pump is (Na + K)-ATPase (ouabain-sensitive, ATP phosphohydrolase, EC 3.6.1.3.).' The induction of synthesis of (Na + K)-ATPase by aldosterone would constitute a third possible mechanism. Chronic aldosterone treatment results in a significant increase in (Na + K)ATPase activity in the microsomal fraction isolated from adrenalectomized rat However, since changes in electrolyte excretion preceded changes in (Na + K)-ATPase activity following adrenalectomy and aldosterone treatments,8>11*12 it was concluded that the enzyme was not primarily responsible for the antinatriuresis. Since (Na + K)-ATPase is difficult to isolate in the pure form, enzyme preparations employed by previous investigators may not have detected subtle changes in activity due to steroid treatment. In the present investigation, the aldosterone effect was correlated with changes in (Na + K)-ATPase in kidneys of adrenalectomized rats by following the enzyme activity after isolation by differential and sucrose density gradient centrifugation. The involvement of (Na + K)-ATPase in the aldosterone-inductive process was further ascertained by a dual isotopic technique followed by polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis. An aldosterone-induced increase in amino acid incorporation was localized to a particular area of the polyacrylamide gel and subsequently identified as the (Na + K)-ATPase by specifically labeling with (y32P)-ATP and I4C-NEM.
