Effect of Taurine on 45Ca2+ Accumulation in Rat Brain Synaptosomes
In recent years, evidence has accumulated suggesting an involvement of taurine in synaptic transmission in the CNS. Taurine is highly concentrated in synaptosomes (Agrawal e t al., 1971; Sellstrom et al., 1975) as well as in synaptic vesicles (de Belleroche and Bradford, 1973; Rassin e t al., 1977), and it is released from isolated nerve endings upon electrical stimulation as well as by depolarizing KCl concentrations (de Belleroche and Bradford, 1973; Sieghart and Heckl, 1976). In synaptosomes a specific uptake system, showing the properties of a highaffinity transport mechanism, has been demonstrated for taurine (Hruska et al., 1976). The strong inhibitory action of taurine on neuronal activity (Curtis e t al., 1968; 1971), has suggested a possible neurotransmitter role for this amino acid; alternatively, taurine has been implicated in the modulation of ionic fluxes, particularly in those of potassium and calcium ions in the brain and in the heart (Welty et al., 1976), and the possibility of a similar role a t the synapse might be considered. Recently, we have found that taurine markedly decreases 4sCaCI, accumulation into the subcellular fractions of the chick retina (Pasantes-Morales et al., 1979); the present work was undertaken in order to investigate if taurine has the same action on brain nerve endings and, thus, could be involved in a widespread phenomenon regulating calcium fluxes at the CNS synapsgs.
