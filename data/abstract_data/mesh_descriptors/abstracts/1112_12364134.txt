Purification of Phenytoin‐Binding Site from the Brain
As only a little about the action mechanism of antiepileptic drugs is known, we have proposed to study the binding site of the drugs. Recently a high-affinity and saturable binding site of phenytoin (DPH), the most frequently used antiepileptic drug, has been reported in the rat brain homogenate.' In an attempt to purify and characterize the DPH-binding site, the DPH-coupled affinity gel was employed. To prepare the affinity gel, Sepharose 6B was activated with 1,4-butanediol diglycidyl ether at 25OC in the presence of 0.2N NaOH, and was coupled with DPH .Na in aqueous dioxane at 5OOC. Binding activities were assayed by an ultrafiltration method using :'H-DPH. The binding activities per mg protein of crude mitochondria1 and microsomal fractions in the rat brain were observed to be three times higher than that of the soluble fraction. When treated, the crude mitochondrial fraction with 1% Triton X-100, about 4075 of binding activity and 50% of protein were solubilized. The solubilized DPH-binding protein was applied to the affinity column giving several-fold purification at the present step. Further purification and characterization of the DPH-binding protein are in progress.
