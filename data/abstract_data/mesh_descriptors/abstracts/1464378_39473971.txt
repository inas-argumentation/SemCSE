Extensive tufted angioma of the left arm in a 47‐year‐old woman
A 47-year-old otherwise healthy Japanese woman presented with a rapidly growing skin lesion over the left upper arm and forearm. The lesion was first noted as an asymptomatic small reddish skin lesion on the left upper arm when she was 3 years old. It had not enlarged nor regressed and had remained almost unchanged in size for more than 40 years. However, it suddenly began to enlarge 2 years before her presentation to us and became tender. The lesion extended rapidly thereafter, covering a large area of her left arm. On physical examination, multiple, red to dark-brown, indurated, macules and plaques with irregular borders were seen, which involved most of the left upper arm and a significant portion of the forearm (Fig. 1a,b). They were tender to palpation and warmer than the surrounding area. There was focal hyperhydrosis without hypertrichosis. The patient experienced paroxysmal episodes of swelling and pain of the affected limb, which were associated with restricted cubital joint mobility. Histological examination of a biopsy specimen taken from the lesion revealed multiple separated cellular lobules scattered throughout the dermis in a cannonball distribution (Fig. 2a). Each lobule was composed of aggregates of endothelial cells that surrounded small capillary lumina (Fig. 2b). Endothelial cells in each lobule showed no conspicuous atypia. A diagnosis of tufted angioma was made. After discussion of treatment options, the patient opted for no intervention. The size of the lesion has so far remained unchanged during a follow-up period of 2 years. Tufted angioma, also known as angioblastoma of Nakagawa, is a rare, benign cutaneous vascular tumour characterized histologically by circumscribed angiomatous tufts and lobules throughout the dermis in a cannonball appearance. Clinically, this tumour arises as slowly enlarging erythematous macules and plaques that typically occur on the neck and upper trunk. The lesions are often described as tender to palpation and may be associated with focal hyperhydrosis or hypertrichosis. They usually develop in early childhood, and more than half of the reported cases occur during the first 5 years of life. (a)
