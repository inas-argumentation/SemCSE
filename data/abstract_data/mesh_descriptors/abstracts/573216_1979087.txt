Binding of transfer RNA and arginine to the arginine transfer RNA synthetase of Escherichia coli.
The arginine transfer RNA synthetase of Escherichia coli is protected against thermal denaturation by its substrates arginine and tRNAArg but not by ATP, alone or in combination with the other substrates. The protection given by transfer RNA alone requires 20-fold higher concentrations above pH 7 than below, whereas the protection by arginine requires less at higher pH values but does not show a sharp transition at any pH value. Modification of the 3′-terminal adenosine eliminates catalytic activity but does not affect the binding or degree of protection. Stepwise removal of groups from the 3′-hydroxyl end of the transfer RNA shows that the three terminal nucleotides do not participate significantly in binding but that the phosphate that links the cytidine residues plays a role in increasing the resistance of the protein to denaturation. The concentration of arginine required to protect the arginine transfer RNA synthetase is decreased by the presence of tRNAArg. In contrast to the protection by the tRNA alone, the “synergistic protection” requires that the 3′-adenosine be intact. Esterification of arginine to transfer RNA does not increase the protection given by the RNA alone and eliminates synergistic protection.
