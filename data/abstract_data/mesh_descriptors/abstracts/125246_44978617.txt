High-energy phosphate and mitochondrial Mg-2 plus-stimulated ATPase activities in ischemic and nonischemic areas of the myocardium with acute experimental coronary occlusion.
The interrelationship between changes in activity of mitochondrial Mg2+-stimulated ATPase and changes in the levels of high-energy phosphate compounds in ischemic and nonischemic areas of the left ventricle was studied in dog hearts with experimental myocardial infarction (2 h). In the ischemic areas significantly (P < 0.01) decreased high-energy phosphate content and increased lactate and pyruvate levels were found in comparison with both control hearts and nonischemic areas of infarcted hearts. A drop in Mg2+-stimulated mitochondrial ATPase activity was found to be characteristic of the ischemic areas whereas in nonischemic regions increased activity of the enzyme could be observed. Likewise decreased contents of ATP, creatine phosphate and total adenine nucleotides, and increased pyruvate content (P < 0.01) were found in the nonischemic myocardium in comparison to controls. Changes in nonischemic areas possibly reflect a compensatory hyperfunction of non-infarcted parts of the myocardium, with an expressed role of FFA in metabolic regulations.
