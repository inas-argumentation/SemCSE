Selenium in Cancer Prevention: Clinical Issues and Implications
Cancer is the second leading cause of death in the United States, resulting in more than 1 million deaths per year, and is projected to overtake heart disease as the leading cause of death (1). The total annual cost of cancer care in the United States is estimated to reach $200 billion in the year 2001 (2). Prevention strategies for cancer are critical to decreasing patient suffering and lowering cancer care costs (3). Cancer chemoprevention, defined as the use of one or more chemical agents to prevent or delay the occurrence of cancer, would appear to offer huge potentials for benefiting public health (4). For example, if the incidence of epithelial cell cancers, which accounts for 90% of cancer deaths in the United States, could be reduced by even 15%, this would prevent 73,000 deaths annually (5) and could save the U.S. economy nearly $25 billion.
