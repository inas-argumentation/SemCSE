Evoked Effects of PGE2 and PGA2 on Lipid Fluidity and Ca2+‐Stimulated ATPase of Walker‐256 Tumor Microsomal Membranes
Prostaglandins (PGs) of the E and A series have been shown to cause growth inhibition of cultured cells and to induce differentiation in several cases.’.2 It has been suggested that PG-induced growth inhibition is mediated by intracellular cyclic AMP.’ Additionally, Ca’+-dependent adenylate cyclase has been found to be stimulated by calmodulin (CaM), thus increasing the intracellular level of CAMP in cancer ceIIs.4 In our previous studies on the effect of PGE, and PGF, on the activity of some membrane-bound enzymes, we indicated that these compounds can alter the membrane fluidity, causing functional changes in the allosteric properties of integral enzyme^.^^^ This report focuses on the modulations of lipid dynamics and lipid-protein interactions in microsomal membranes, isolated from Walker-256 tumor, induced by PGE, and PGA, using as functional parameter the activity of the integral enzyme Caz+-stimulated ATPase.
