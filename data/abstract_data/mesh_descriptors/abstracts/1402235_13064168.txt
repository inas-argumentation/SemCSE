The role of proteinases in the treatment of HIV infection.

 Throughout the mid-1980s, there existed little treatment which materially affected the survival of people with HIV/AIDS. However, the advent of highly active antiretroviral therapy (HAART) and an understanding of the importance of using drugs in combination revolutionized the survival outlook for many HIV-positive individuals. Although the long-term impact of the treatment is unknown, it is clear that over the short term, opportunistic infections and death are prevented and patients' quality of life can be maintained. The development of proteinases was the most important reason for this therapeutic revolution. This development is a fine example of the importance of basic research and designer drug development, although the best role for the use of proteinase inhibitors remains to be determined. The author discusses when to begin therapy and what such therapy should be. Compliance with the drug regimen and toxicity are considered, followed by a review of non-proteinase containing regimens, whether proteinases work in antiretroviral-experienced patients, and patients who present later in the disease course.
