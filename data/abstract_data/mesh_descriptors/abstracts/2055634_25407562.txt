Obesity susceptibility CART gene polymorphism contributes to bone remodeling in postmenopausal women
Dear Sir, It was recently established by Elefteriou and colleagues [1] in mice that at the level of the central nervous system leptin-regulated neural pathways play a role in the control of bone remodeling and may contribute to bone loss. Conversely, it was suggested that hypothalamic neurones expressing CART (cocaineand amphetamine-regulated transcript) may counter leptin-induced bone resorption [1]. However, these data obtained from mice apparently contradict the general perception that obese women with high circulating leptin have high bone mineral density (BMD). We previously reported that the functional CART promoter SNP-3608C allele was protective against morbid obesity and related alterations in circulating lipids [2]. In the present analysis, we genotyped 801 postmenopausal Caucasian women (60–85 years old and mean BMI =26.6±3.6 kg/m, range 16.6–44.0) (PERF study) [3] for this functional DNA polymorphism, and we investigated independent associations of genotypes with BMD at the forearm and total hip following adjustment for the influence of age and/or lnBMI. None of these participants were smokers and/ or ever users of any form of medication with an established effect on body composition (hormone replacement therapy, bisphosphonates, SERMs, etc.). No association with BMI was observed when comparing genotype and allele distributions between the subgroup of obese (n =124, BMI ‡30 kg/m) and non-obese women (n =464, BMI £ 27 kg/m) (data not shown). The main findings were a significant independent association between SNP-3608T>C and forearm BMD, with the C allele carriers (TC+CC) showing significantly higher forearm BMD than TT carriers (Fig. 1). After adjustment for age and BMI, the standardized residues values accompanying the TC+CC and TT genotypes were 0.054±0.97 and )0.18±1.06, respectively ( P =0.006). However, no association was found with total hip BMD, suggesting a stronger association of the SNP with cortical bone mass than with trabecular bone mass (total hip). A possible additional explanation for the association of the polymorphism with forearm, but not femoral BMD could be the fact that the forearm is not a weight-bearing bone, while femoral BMD is influenced by BMI and exercise. In conclusion, our data show that the CNS-expressed CART promoter SNP-3608 C allele—which is protective against morbid obesity and atherogenesis—is also associated with higher BMD, which seems in line with a role for the CART gene in the regulation of bone homeostasis in humans. The apparent discrepancy with clinical data related to a lower risk for osteoporosis in obese women can be addressed by the well-known leptin signaling resistance associated with obesity and by the excessive endogenous estrogen production in fat mass. In this context, Elmquist [4] also reported that marathon runners with very low leptin levels have high risk of fractures in the long bones, but this paradox may be elucidated if CART has a high role in the control of cortical bone mass in humans and is down-regulated in leptin-deficient states. The present study in a genetically homogenous Danish population of postmenopausal women brings an additional element to the central physiological effect of CART neurons in inhibiting bone loss and provides the first human validation of the plausible contribution of this gene in the regulated pathway of bone remodeling.
