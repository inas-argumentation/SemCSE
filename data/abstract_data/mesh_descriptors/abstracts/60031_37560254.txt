Comment on "Short-term effects of particulate matter on total mortality during Saharan dust outbreaks".
Dr. Tobias et al. (2011) studied the effect modification of Saharan dust outbreak on the association between particulate pollutants (fine particulate and coarse particulate) and all cause mortality in Madrid, Spain. The authors concluded that during Saharan dust outbreaks PM10–2.5 had stronger adverse health effects on all cause mortality than non-dust periods. However, some methodological issues should be noted in this study. First, there was inconsistency in the analysis of effect modification between Methods and Results sections. According to the results, the study did stratified analysis, for Saharan dust days and non-dust days, but they did not mention in theMethods section, there is good example and reference for this (Figueiras et al., 1998); particularly, in the casecrossover study, when doing subgroup analysis, the author should give detailed information about the approaches to choosing controls, especially,when the time series data in each stratumwere not continuous. Second, in this study, the authors gave a figure, which vividly told that the effects were stronger on non-dust days than that in Saharan dust days in the single-pollutant model, for both fine particles and PM coarse, which was supported by the descriptive results illustrated in this paper, “there were 59.6 daily deaths during Saharan dust days and 61.5 during non-Saharan dust days”. However, in the multi-pollutant model, they found that the effect pattern reversed, and it will be better to give a figure like Fig. 1 to make the result more clear.
