Our patient probably has familial leiomyomatosis cutis et uteri, also known as Reed's syndrome.3,6,11 The typical family history for Reed's syndrome is several women with early hysterectomies.6 The inheritance pattern is autosomal dominant with incomplete penetrance.11 Women in these families may have multiple cutaneous leiomyomas. Approximately 50% have uterine leiomyomas, compared with about a 10% incidence in the general population of women in the same age group.1,6 Women with multiple cutaneous leiomyomas need periodic gynecologic evaluation.5,6,11 If uterine leiomyomas are present, the patient should be informed that a hysterectomy may be necessary at some point.5,11 For some women, this information can help with family planning. To our knowledge, zosteriform leiomyoma cutis has not been previously reported.
