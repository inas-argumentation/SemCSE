Effects of isologous and nonisologous nucleic acids on the ageing of normal human fibroblasts in vitro.
Summary Normal human fetal fibroblasts were treated in vitro with isologous nucleic acids that were extracted from the same ovum and added to the medium for a number of transfers, and with nonisologous human nucleic acids that were extracted from the placenta of another individual and given once by the calcium method of Graham and Van der Eb (1973). After treatment ended the proliferation rate (cycle rate) and death rate of the cell strains were registered at intervals by a new simple method based on cell count, mitotic index and mitotic time. It was confirmed that normal (untreated) strains have a high cycle rate and a high death rate when young; when growing old they have a relatively greater decrease in the death rate compared to the cycle rate. Isologous nucleic acid lead in 3 out of 4 experiments to an increase of the cycle rate for many transfers after the treatment ended and, for the two strains so tested, to an increase of the life-span. Nonisologous nucleic acids produced a gradual decrease in the cycle rate from the 7th transfer after treatment and a probable decrease of the life-span. When the nonisologous nucleic acids were moderately sheared the life-span was definitely decreased. The results point to an effect of the DNA of the nucleic acid extracts on the transcription mechanism. A preliminary report has been published (Mtllgren, 1975) on atempts to delay the well-known ageing and ultimate death of normal human fetal fibroblasts in vitro by adding isologous nucleic acids to the culture medium, i. e. nucleic acids extracted from the same ovum. Four to 18 transfers after treatment ended the treated cells still had a higher cycle rate than the controls and after 18 transfers also a higher death rate. The aim of this work was to confirm the preliminary results, to compare them with the effect of nonisologous human nucleic acids, and to test a possible effect on the life-span of human fibroblasts of added isologous and nonisologous human nucleic acids.
