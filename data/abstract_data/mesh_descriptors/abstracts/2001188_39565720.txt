Progesterone metabolism in adenocarcinoma of the endometrium.
Progesterone-4- 14 C was incubated with homogenates of adenocarcinoma of the endometrium. The steroid products resulting were isolated by thin-layer chromatography, radioassayed, and 5α-pregnan-3,20-dione and 20α-OH pregn-4-en-3-one were identified after recrystallization. The tumors converted progesterone to the same products as were found after incubations with normal endometrium. The pattern of metabolism in well-differentiated adenocarcinomas was closely related to proliferative endometrium where 5α-reductase is the predominant activity. (Poorly differentiated adenocarcinomas display unpredictable patterns of progesterone metabolism.) Malignant tissues appear to be deficient in 20α-dehydrogenase and convert a higher percentage (19 per cent) of progesterone than proliferative endometrium (14 per cent). The data can be interpreted to suggest that 5α-reductase activity is dominant in glandular cells, and 20α-OH dehydrogenase activity is dominant in stromal cells.
