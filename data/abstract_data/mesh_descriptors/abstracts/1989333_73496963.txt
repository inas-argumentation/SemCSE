Partner notification and contact tracing must accompany provider-initiated counselling and testing in population screening for HIV infection in Nigeria
Sir, Nsirim et al. have shown the efficacy of routine opportunistic screening for HIV infection when the sero-prevalence is high (1.5% of the catchment population) as recommended in the WHO 2015 HIV Testing Guideline, by increase in the pickup rate of previously undiagnosed HIV infection from 34 cases to 158 cases. This is not surprising, as the case detection rate increase was due to routine screening of all attendees, irrespective of signs and symptoms. We are, however, concerned about the absence of partner notification and contact tracing in the report. In our audit report on partner notification done in rural East Anglia, UK, from 1997 to 2004, the partner notification rate was 51.7% (95% CI: 38.4–64.8%), which compared well with the 69% partner notification rate in Victoria, Australia and 49% reported from Glasgow, Scotland, respectively. In a recent study we conducted in the rural Niger Delta region of Yenagoa, Nigeria, the partner notification rate was 62% (95% CI: 55.9–69) among 260 respondents, comprising 75 males with a median age of 40 (IQR: 34.5– 48) years and 185 females with a median age of 34.5 (IQR: 29.5–41.5) years. The odds ratio of men disclosing was 3.91 (95% CI: 1.82–7.1) compared to women in the cohort. Partnerships of more than 90 months duration disclosed more readily than those of less than 30 months duration and women including pregnant mothers were less likely to disclose. Had Nsirim et al. incorporated partner notification and contact tracing in the 32 primary and secondary healthcare facilities in the study the case detection rate would have significantly increased, including appropriate documentation of spousal screening of antenatal pregnant patients and other named contact(s). Strengthening the local guideline to incorporate routine opportunistic screening would increase case detection, but most importantly ensuring partner notification and contact tracing would further increase case detection, reduce the pool of infectivity, and bring to care those deserving to be treated and make 90–90–90 in 2030 a reality in eliminating new HIV infections.
