INDUCED DELAYED HYPERSENSITIVITY REACTIONS AND MALIGNANCY
In 1965 Gross (1) reported his experiences with induced delayed hypersensitivity reactions (DHR) to dinitrochlorbenzene (DNCB) in healthy adolescents and in patients with chronic non-neoplastic diseases, active tuberculosis or neoplasms. He found that 96.7 per cent of the adolescents (age range 13-17, mean age 15.1) reacted positively compared with 62 per cent of the patients with chronic non-neoplastic diseases (age range 40-95, mean age 72.8), 50 per cent of the patients with active tuberculosis (age range 27-80, mean age 54.4) and 14.3 per cent of the group with neoplasms (age range 59-96, mean age 78.5). The foregoing report also indicated that there was a regression with age in the capacity to develop DHR and that in patients with neoplasms there was a significant reduction in DHR’s when compared with non-neoplastic subjects of comparable age. Furthermore, a study of 5 patients with surgically treated breast cancers and 1 with epithelioma of the skin who were tested from two months to ten years after surgery revealed good DHR’s in 66 per cent (mean age 54). Irrespective of the explanation for these findings, it seemed desirable to see whether the DHR to DNCB could be used clinically, after surgery, to indicate the presence or absence of malignant disease. This could be determined by presurgical testing and follow-up testing a t intervals. If a negative reaction before operation became positive within several months after operation, more exact relationships between malignancy and the immunological response could be established. Groups of patients vary considerably in their make-up, and conclusions drawn from results obtained in. one group may not be valid for another group. For this reason we studied the D H R to DNCB in Domiciliary members, to obtain control data on the reactivity of various age groups for comparison with data on a hospitalized population with malignancy but with relatively comparable social and economic backgrounds.
