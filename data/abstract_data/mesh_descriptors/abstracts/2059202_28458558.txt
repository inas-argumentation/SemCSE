For malignant melanoma the picture is more complicated. Results from animal models suggest that exposure to UVA as well as UVB may be important which presents a further complication. Since UVA levels are not strongly influenced by ozone this would mean that ozone depletion would have little influence on the part of the overall risk of malignant melanoma that was attributable to UVA rather than UVB. Therefore, this study was limited to squamous cell carcinoma and basal cell carcinoma. Traditional mathematical models based on regression analysis using the epidemiological data of skin cancer have some uncertainties. UVB exposure is influenced by human behaviour, exposure types and time, therefore we need to define the actual human exposure scenarios allowing for these influencing factors. Moreover, factors of aetiological significance other than UVB flux have not been included in our analysis because appropriate detailed data are not available at present. To our knowledge, this study is first to demonstrate an estimate of skin cancer incidence in Asia due to ozone depletion. Dermatologists need to recognize the influence of environmental factors in determining the mortality and morbidity of their patients. So far, there has been little interest in skin cancer morbidity due to depletion of ozone layer because of the low incidence of skin cancer in Asian population. Our study estimated considerable increases of skin cancer morbidity due to depletion of ozone layer in Korea. Therefore it will promote public awareness of climate change in Asia and enlighten the Asian countries to cooperate on the global climate change conventions.
