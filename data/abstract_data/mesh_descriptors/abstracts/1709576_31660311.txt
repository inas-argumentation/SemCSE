A spermatid factor inhibits cAMP and calcium signaling in Sertoli but not Leydig cells.
Publisher Summary Both follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and androgens exert stage-specific regulatory influences on the rat seminiferous epithelium. The ability to stimulate cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) production by FSH is both age and stage dependent. In rats and humans, FSH-responsive cAMP production is low in the fetus and the adult but is high in the immature testis. To understand the relationship of spermatogenic cells to altered responsiveness to FSH by Sertoli cells, this chapter uses a coculture system to investigate the possible signals utilized by a germ cell factor(s) that could affect responsiveness of Sertoli cells to FSHT. Immature testes were used to provide Sertoli cells that were highly responsive to FSH while not yet exposed to the putative paracrine factors of later-stage germ cells in vivo. The studies presented in the chapter indicate that spermatid factor(s)-induced changes in the Sertoli cell are accompanied by alteration(s) in the adenylyl cyclase signaling system, possibly at the level of the guanine nucleotide regulatory subunit, G i .
