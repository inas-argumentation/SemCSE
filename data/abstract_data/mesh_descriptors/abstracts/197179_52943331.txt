Complete response to pembrolizumab after initial progress in a patient with metastatic uveal melanoma
Uveal melanoma (UM) is the most common primary malignancy of the eye in adults and represents 3 % of all melanomas. About 34 % of UM patients develop metastatic disease within ten years of diagnosis. Although anti-programmed death-1 (PD-1) and anti-cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA-4) antibodies have revolutionized the treatment of cutaneous metastatic melanoma [ 1, 2 ] , their role in UM therapy still needs to be determined [ 3 ] . Ipilimumab, a CTLA-4 inhibitor, appears to have limited clinical activity in metastatic UM patients [ 4 ] . Reported response rates for the PD-1 inhibitors pembrolizumab and nivolumab vary between 0 and 37 %, indicating that larger cohorts are needed to determine the clinical activity of these agents [ 3, 5–7 ] . A 57-year-old male presented to our ophthalmology clinic with gradual deterioration of the visual acuity of his left eye in May 2015. Ultrasonography of the left eye revealed a dome-shaped tumor of the choroid measuring 13 mm in diameter and 4 mm in thickness, without ciliary body involvement or extraocular extension. Uveal melanoma was diagnosed based on the clinical appearance and ruthenium-106 plaque brachytherapy was performed in June 2014. Routine follow-up abdominal ultrasonography and subsequent magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) in September 2015 showed more than 30 liver metastases with diameters of up to 1.5 cm (Figure 1 ). Computed tomography (CT) of the head, chest and abdomen showed no other signs of malignancy. In accordance with the AJCC Cancer Staging Manual (8 th edition) the disease was classifi ed as stage IV uveal melanoma (T2a N0 M1a). We initiated intravenous therapy with pembrolizumab 2 mg/kg every three weeks. In December 2015, after four cycles of pembrolizumab, tumor staging revealed progression of some but not all liver lesions. Some new lesions had developed (Figure 1 ). A 20 % overall increase in extent of the metastasis was noted. Up to this point no side effects were reported by the patient and the treatment was continued. In March 2016, after eight cycles of pembrolizumab, the patient developed vitiligo on the dorsum of both hands and forearms (Figure 2 ) and MRI showed a mixed response to treatment of the liver metastases. No additional lesions were detected, but some of the known metastases had increased in size while others had decreased. The total tumor burden had not changed. During the next nine months of treatment, the liver metastases gradually disappeared. The patient developed leucotrichia on the right eyebrow and eyelashes but did not suffer from any other side effects. More importantly, no tumor recurrence has been detected so far and the patient is still free of disease after 39 cycles of pembrolizumab. Several studies have reported that metastatic melanoma patients treated with checkpoint inhibitors initially showed disease progression but ultimately achieved a delayed disease response [ 8–10 ] . In general, immunotherapies elicit various response patterns, including an atypical response characterized by an increase in tumor burden that precedes shrinkage of the tumor. This could be explained by a transient immune-cell tumor infi ltrate (pseudoprogression) or continued tumor growth until the establishment of a suffi cient immune response [ 11 ] . Tumor assessment based on response evaluation criteria in solid tumors (RECIST) may underestimate the benefi t of immunotherapy, as it does not take into account this particular response pattern. Such an underestimate may lead to premature termination of treatment based upon apparent progression of the initial disease [ 8 ] . Assessment of the response pattern to immune checkpoint inhibitors with the immune-related RECIST (irRECIST) paradigm may be more accurate than with standard RECIST, because of its different approach to disease progression.
