The glucose 6-phosphate metabolic crossroads in brain. Studies at the enzyme level.
The enzymes involved in glucose 6-phosphate metabolism are functionally cytoplasmic. The activities of these enzymes have been measured in optimal in vitro conditions in rat and calf brain. No significant variation in these activities has been found in extracts from different regions of calf brain. The competitive inhibition by NADPH of glucose 6-phosphate dehydrogenase and 6-phosphogluconate dehydrogenase and the failure in finding other regulatory effectors suggest that the NADPH/ NADP ratio is the main factor which controls the operation of the pentose cycle in brain tissue. No inactive forms of UDPG pyrophosphorylase could be detected in brain. Glycogen synthetase was found mainly in the glucose 6-phosphate dependent form and glycogen phosphorylase mainly in the b form. These studies substantiate at the enzyme level the preferential utilization of glucose in brain by the glycolytic pathway.
