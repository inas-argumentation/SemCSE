G2677T and C3435T Genotype and Haplotype Are Associated With Hepatic ABCB1 (MDR1) Expression
O of drug therapy many times involves circumvention of the body’s defense mechanisms. P-glycoprotein (P-gp), a member of the large adenosine triphosphate–binding cassette superfamily of transport proteins, is the product of the human multidrug resistance gene 1 (MDR1, ABCB1). Localized to the apical (luminal) surface of organs and tissues with excretory or barrier functions such as intestine, kidney, and blood-brain barrier, P-gp safeguards the body by limiting oral absorption and distribution and by facilitating renal excretion of drugs. P-gp is also found in the hepatobiliary tree, where it is expressed by hepatocytes at the biliary canalicular front and by epithelial cells in small biliary ductules at the apical (luminal or central canal) surface. Consequently, P-gp is believed to alter the hepatic drug elimination by reducing oxidative and conjugative drug metabolism secondary to modulation of intracellular substrate levels and/or enhancing biliary excretion. For example, studies using mdr1 knockout mice demonstrate that the hepatic extraction ratio of dual CYP3A and P-gp substrates, for example, vinblastine, paclitaxel, doxorubicin, and erythromycin, was significantly increased. However, this paradigm did not apply to all dual CYP3A/P-gp substrates since the hepatic extraction of tacrolimus was greatly decreased in mdr1 knockout (KO) animals. Independently, P-gp can enhance hepatic extraction by excreting drug directly into the bile. Indeed, of P-gp at the canalicular pole of hepatocytes by drugs such as the calcium channel blocker verapamil or the immunosuppressant cyclosporin A can block excretion of substrates into bile, resulting in decreased clearance and increased drug exposure. Considering the importance of P-gp in hepatic elimination, it is likely that variable expression and function of hepatic P-gp contributes significantly to the large interindividual variability in the disposition of drugs whose elimination relies heavily on the liver. For example, African Americans have higher hepatic clearance for numerous drugs as compared to Caucasians despite having lower hepatic CYP3A4 activity, which suggests differences in hepatic P-gp activity may significantly affect drug disposition. A number of genetic variants of the ABCB1 gene have been described, but unfortunately, a causal link between single loci genetic variation (eg, C3435T) and disposition of P-gp substrates remains elusive. Recently, it has been suggested that haplotype analysis at multiple single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs; ABCB1 G2677T and C3435T) in lieu of unphased genotype data be used to reconcile these differences.
