SEPARATION OF AUSTRALIA ANTIGEN AND SOME BACTERIAL PROTEINS BY ISOELECTRIC FOCUSING IN POLYACRYLAMIDE GELS *
Isoelectric focusing is a separation method that is specifically applicable to the resolution of molecular species differing in charge only. Separation should thus not be influenced by difference in molecular size by “molecular seiving” in the gel. Not until very recently has nonrestrictive pore size for electrofocusing in acrylamide gels been systematically studied.l All methods for gel electrofocusing have until now been performed at a h a 1 voltage of approximately 20-30 V/cm and run for 3-24 hr. This study was initiated to investigate the possibility of making separation faster by using a higher voltage and to study the migration of large particles, such as Australia antigen (Au antigen; hepatitisassociated antigen, HAA), in large-pore polyacrylamide gels.
