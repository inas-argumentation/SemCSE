Are Estring® and Vagifem® equally effective and safe for the treatment of urogenital atrophy in breast cancer patients on aromatase inhibitor therapy?
Sir d The urogenital side-effects of aromatase inhibitors are well recognised, and can have a significant negative effect on quality of life. Safety issues around the use of oestrogen-based therapies in this patient population remain unanswered [1,2]. Our group has conducted a trial examining the effectiveness and safety of Vagifem versus Estring in the treatment of significant urogenital symptoms in women on aromatase inhibitors with significant urogenital symptoms despite the use of a local nonoestrogen-based moisturising therapy [3]. Patients with significant and persistent urogenital symptoms despite a 1 month run-in period of topical nonhormonal moisturiser (Replens) were randomised to treatment with either Estring or Vagifem for 12 weeks. Validated quality of life (FACT-ES) and sexual health questionnaires, maturation index, vaginal pH and serum oestradiol were administered to assess the efficacy and safety of local oestrogen-containing treatment at weeks 2, 4, 8 and 12. Because of extremely low accrual of patients the study was closed prematurely. Here we report data on seven patients who received the treatment. Of these, three patients were treated with Estring and four with Vagifem. For all seven patients who completed the trial, the mean FACT-ES score improved from 133 at baseline to 147 at week 12 (P ¼ 0.056). The maturation index improved in all patients except one. Serum oestradiol levels rose from an undetectable level at baseline in all patients by week 2, but returned to undetectable or near undetectable levels by week 12 (Figure 1). As accrual would not have enabled the primary study objective to be answered, the study was stopped early. In this small population of women with significant symptoms of urogenital atrophy despite topical nonhormonal therapy, both Estring and Vagifem effectively improved both quality of life and vaginal mucosal maturation index. The clinical impact of the transient elevation in serum oestradiol levels and a comparison of the two agents cannot be answered by the current study and warrants further investigation.
