Assignment1 of TLL1 and TLL2, which encode human BMP-1/Tolloid-related metalloproteases, to chromosomes 4q32→q33 and 10q23→q24 and assignment of murine Tll2 to chromosome 19
Bone morphogenetic protein-1 (BMP-1) and Tolloid (TLD) are prototypes of a family of metalloproteases with important functions in morphogenesis. BMP-1 processes procollagens I– III to yield the major fibrillar structures of vertebrate extracellular matrix (Kessler et al., 1996). Drosophila Tolloid and related vertebrate proteases Xenopus xolloid and zebrafish Tolloid act in dorsal-ventral patterning during embryogenesis, by liberating TGFß-like molecules from latent complexes (Marqués et al., 1997; Piccolo et al., 1997; Blader et al., 1997). The phenotype of mice homozygous null for the Bmp1 gene suggested genetic redundancy and led to isolation of cDNAs for the related, but genetically distinct protease mammalian Tolloid-like 1 (mTLL-1) (Takahara et al., 1996). We have previously mapped the murine mTLL-1 gene Tll1 to chromosome 8 (Takahara et al., 1996) and showed it to be necessary for proper formation of the mammalian heart (Clark et al., 1999). Here we map the human mTLL-1 gene TLL1. In addition, PCR with degenerate primers corresponding to sequences conserved in the metalloprotease domains of known BMP-1/TLD proteases has led to isolation of human and mouse cDNA sequences for a novel mammalian BMP-1/TLD-related protease designated mammalian Tolloid-like 2, or mTLL-2 (Scott et al., submitted). Here we map the chromosomal positions of the human and mouse mTLL-2 genes, TLL2 and Tll2.
