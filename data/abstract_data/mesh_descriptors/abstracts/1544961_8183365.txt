Sexually transmitted diseases services in societies with limited resources.
A number of issues concerning the delivery of sexually transmitted disease (STD) services by means of specialist units have arisen. Some of these issues include the following: the rapid spread of HIV and effectiveness of efficient STD control; failure of traditional STD clinical models to provide adequate services; perceived need to integrate sexual and STD management programs with reproductive health programs within the primary health care (PHC) system; and the importance of integrating STD/HIV prevention and care programs. These problems are widely evident in developing societies where STD control is becoming a major concern. In some developing societies, there has been a complete integration of STD services within the PHC system. Within this setting, STD services can be intensified through sexual health promotion and education of the general public attending the PHC system, through outreach activities to sex workers, and through STD screening in prenatal and family planning clinics. Although these programs cannot be instituted in some developing countries without external funding, these services are generally cost-effective. Thus, integration of STD/HIV prevention and care into an efficient PHC system will likely be the best strategy for STD prevention and control.
