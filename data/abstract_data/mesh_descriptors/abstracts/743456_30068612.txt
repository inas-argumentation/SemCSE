Bowel evisceration through a ring pessary
A 56 year old postmenopausal woman who had previously undergone posterior repair and sacrospinous fixation of the cervix for uterovaginal prolapse presented with recurrence of the prolapse in January 2003. She was keen to avoid a vaginal procedure having suffered from dyspareunia following the first repair. A total abdominal hysterectomy and a bilateral salpingo-oophorectomywere performed in May 2003. The immediate post-operative recovery was relatively uneventful although she was admitted with signs and symptoms of pelvic infection twice within the following 10 weeks. These episodes were managed conservatively with antibiotics. She presented again in January 2004 with a recurrent vaginal wall prolapse. Pelvic examination revealed a cystocoele, rectocoele and a vault prolapse. The vault appeared healthy. A ring pessary was inserted and she was placed on the waiting list for sacrospinous fixation of the vault and repair. One month later, she presented to the Accident and Emergency Department with the complaint of a sudden appearance of a mass extruding vaginally following a sneeze and severe abdominal pain. A 20-cm loop of small intestine was visible external to the introitus (Fig. 1). The bowel loop demonstrated visible peristalsis and appeared viable. The previously inserted ring pessary was felt at the top of the vagina and a complete defect in the vault was noted. On abdominal examination, marked tenderness was noted without any guarding or rigidity. The vital signs were stable and bowel sounds were audible on auscultation. The ring pessary was removed and the loop of bowel reduced with the help of moist saline pack. Complete reduction was not possible. An abdominal radiograph showed no evidence of intestinal obstruction. She was managed with parenteral antibiotics, analgesia, followed by a laparotomy through a midline incision. A knuckle of small bowel was seen prolapsing through the opening in the vaginal apex. The prolapsed bowel was pulled back through the aperture and placed within the peritoneal cavity. The vaginal vault was very thin, friable and poorly vascularized and was repaired with interrupted sutures. Further corrective procedures were not attempted at this point in view of the poor quality of vaginal tissue. Post-operatively, the patient developed pulmonary embolism and paralytic ileus, which were treated appropriately. She was discharged after two weeks. At the time of writing, she is awaiting sacrocolpopexy.
