Streptokinase IV Protocol in Acute MI Patients
TO THE EDITOR: A 75-year-old male post-myocardial infarction patient with angina was complaining of the lack of relief of chest discomfort from isosorbide dinitrate 2.5 mg sublingual tablets. The tablets were placed under his tongue as directed, but he noticed that they would not dissolve and could be retrieved without any apparent loss of size or change in shape. The patient also complained of xerostomia, which was traced to the concurrent administration of the antiarrhythmic disopyramide phosphate 150 mg q6h. 1 However, relief could be obtained from isosorbide dinitrate sublingual administration if taken approximately six hours after the disopyramide phosphate or if the disopyramide phosphate was eliminated. This demonstrates that there may be a clinically significant interaction between sublingual medications such as isosorbide dinitrate and nitroglycerin' and anticholinergic medications that diminish salivary secretions necessary for drug dissolution. In this instance, it seems clear that when the anticholinergic influence of disopyramide phosphate was diminished or eliminated by missing a dose, the effectiveness of the vasodilating drugs was observed because the salivary secretion level improved to effect rapid dissolution. It is our concern that this interaction could conceivably involve several categories of pharmacological agents that possess anticholinergic effects, such as antidepressants, antipsychotics, antihistamines, and antispasmodics. An investigation of the problem is underway using laboratory animals to determine a better approach to avoid the interaction.
