The ®broblast growth factors (FGF) are a large family of polypeptides with a molecular mass of 17±34 kDa. In vertebrates 22 members have been identi®ed. They play an important role in embryonic development, angiogenesis, in the nervous system and in tissue repair (Ornitz & Itoh, 2001). Although redundancy in FGF function is known, each member exhibits a characteristic expression pattern. Some of the ®broblast growth factors have tumour-transforming capacity. Five FGF-tyrosine kinase receptors have been found that are alternatively spliced in a tissue-speci®c manner. They contain two to three immunoglobulin-like domains and a heparan sulphate proteoglycan binding site. Interaction with heparan sulphate proteoglycan is required for ef®cient activation of the FGF receptors. Mutation of FGF receptors is associated with severe bone abnormalities, e.g. in achondroplasia (mutation in FGFR3), Pfeiffer, Crouzon, Apert and Jackson±Weiss syndrome (mutation in FGFR1 or FGFR2) (Wilkie et al, 1995; Burke et al, 1998).
