Practice Patterns in the Use of Completion Imaging after Carotid Endarterectomy.
BACKGROUND
The use of intraoperative completion imaging (CI) (completion carotid duplex or angiography) to confirm the technical adequacy of carotid endarterectomy (CEA) remains a matter of controversy. The purpose of this study is to describe vascular surgeons' practice patterns in the use of CI after CEA, and to study the association between CI and postoperative stroke/death and high-grade restenosis (>70%).


METHODS
Patients who underwent CEA without concomitant procedures in the Vascular Quality Initiative (VQI) database between 2003 and 2018 were included. Surgeons' practice patterns were defined based on the distribution of CI use among annual CEA cases per surgeon. Multivariable and Cox proportional hazards models were used to study the association between different practice patterns in the use of CI and perioperative and 1-year outcomes after CEA.


RESULTS
Out of 98,055 CEA cases, 26,716 (27.3%) were performed with CI. Compared to cases where CI was not performed, CI was associated with increased rates of immediate re-exploration (3.5% vs. 0.9%, OR: 3.84, 95%CI: 2.74-5.38, P<0.001), return to the operating room (RTOR) (1.6% vs. 1.2%, OR: 1.24, 95%CI: 1.08-1.42, P<0.01) and longer operative time [median (IQR) 105 min (82-132) vs. 119 min (92-148), P<0.001]. Out of 1,920 surgeons in our cohort, 45% never perform CI, whereas 26% rarely performed CI (for ≤ 20% of annual CEA cases), 9.5% performed it selectively (21% to 79% of annual CEA's) and 19.6% used CI routinely (≥80% of annual CEA's). Rarely performing CI had higher rates of immediate re-exploration (6.5% vs. 0.9%, OR: 7.2, 95%CI: 5.7-9.2, P<0.001), in-hospital stroke (4.0% vs. 1.1%, aOR: 3.4, 95%CI: 2.6-4.6, P<0.001), RTOR for bleeding (1.9% vs. 0.9%, aOR:2.1, 95%CI: 1.5-2.9, P<0.001) and for neurological events (1.5% vs. 0.4%, aOR:3.6, 95%CI: 2.2-5.9, P<0.001) compared to not performing CI. It was also associated with increased stroke/death and repeat revascularization at 30-days and significant restenosis at 1-year. On the other hand, selective and routine performance of CI were associated with increased immediate re-exploration (Selective: aOR: 3.2, 95%CI: 1.9-5.5, P<0.001; Routine: aOR: 3.7, 95%CI: 2.5-5.6, P<0.001) without any increase in in-hospital, 30-day and 1-year adverse outcomes, compared to cases performed without CI.


CONCLUSIONS
The performance of selective or routine CI during CEA is safe and is not associated with increased adverse events compared to not using intraoperative CI. However, rarely performing CI is associated with a significant increase in the odds of perioperative stroke/death, and RTOR, possible due to unnecessary re-exploration for minor defects. Operator's experience and establishing a criterion for fixing residual defects is important to avoid unnecessary re-exploration.
