Common Goals of Prevention and Treatment of Cancer
We have been concerned for many years and disturbed to see two important approaches to cancer, prevention and treatment, so far apart: prevention on one side, treatment on the other, with very little communication, or interaction, and even a lot of competition. Therefore we think it is important to discuss these two aspects and see if we can do something to improve the connection between these two large and important areas in the fight against cancer. Certainly these two words have been traditionally separated for many reasons. Their objectives are different: reduction of cancer incidence for prevention; reduction of cancer mortality for treatment. The fields of action are also different: control of the environment and lifestyle for prevention; treatment of the overt disease in the treatment area. The tools for implementation of goals also differ: legislation and education in the field of prevention; surgery, radiotherapy, drugs and hormone therapy in the field of treatment. Also the people involved are of different specialities: epidemiologists, public health officers, educators, chemists in the prevention area; clinicians and pathologists, in the treatment area (TABLE I). These are the main reasons why we have been so divided for so many years.
