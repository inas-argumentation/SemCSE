Avoidance of Dominant Hand Amputation in a Patient with Uncontrolled Diabetes
INTRODUCTION Necrotizing fasciitis, caused by community-associatedmethicillinresistant Staphylococcus aureus (CA-MRSA), is emerging as a serious clinical condition. In the past, necrotizing fasciitis was associated with group A h-hemolytic streptococci and methicillin-susceptible S aureus (MSSA). Currently, MRSA accounts for about 60% of clinical S aureus strains isolated from intensive care units in the United States, and it causes a majority of the soft-tissue infections in patients presenting to emergency departments (EDs) in several metropolitan areas across the United States. – 5 MRSA infections kill approximately 19,000 hospitalized American patients annually. The standard of therapy for MRSA infection has been vancomycin. However, evidence of the decreasing effectiveness of vancomycin for S aureus infections continues to mount. In the following case, the use of the antibiotic daptomycin, coupled with appropriate wound care, were integral factors in preventing hand amputation in a woman with uncontrolled diabetes and an MRSA hand infection.
