What really is DNA? Remarks on the changing ASPECTS OF A SCIENTIFIC CONCEPT.
Publisher Summary This chapter discusses remarks on the changing aspects of a scientific concept. The development of a scientific problem, in general, passes through four stages: (1) formulation; (2) oversimplification; (3) anarchy; and (4) solution. For a scientific concept to be formulated successfully, a concerted interaction of many requisites must occur. The chapter studies a painstaking study of the chemical composition of salmon sperm. The main illustration is that the bulk of the defatted sperm is constituted by a single substance that is regarded as an insoluble salt-like compound between an organic base very rich in nitrogen (termed as “protamine” and investigated in detail) and a nuclein rich in phosphorus and playing the role of the acid. The occurrence of proteins is also indicated and it is pointed out that the presence of the components in such constant proportions is certainly of particular importance.
