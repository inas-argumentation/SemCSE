Given the short recovery period, we would suggest that the pathology had gone no further than ischaemia, although we cannot say whether some degree of fibrosis had occurred. Since the ulnar artery has a large variety of complex branching patterns, differentiation of an arteriovenous malformation from variations of the normal anatomy is difficult, even with MRI. In general, when no obvious cause of ulnar neuropathy can be detected, vascular compression should be suspected.
