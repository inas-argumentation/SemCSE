Although the multivariate analysis took this into account, the fact that these groups are so diff erent in terms of attributes that are known to aff ect survival duration indicates that there may be more at play than the study shows, and it is diffi cult to tease out what factor carries more weight. A crucial area for work to be carried out in this fi eld is to compare patients who received diff erent anticonvulsants, both HDAC-inhibiting and otherwise. Unfortunately no mention is made of patients receiving anticonvulsants other than valproic acid. Comparing patients on diff erent anticonvulsants would have allowed for the separation of valproic acid use and both the chemotherapy use and the presence of seizures during the diagnosis. Much of the work comparing anticonvulsants and survival in GBM has focussed on the cytochrome p450 induction properties of the drugs, rather than their HDAC-inhibiting properties. 3,4 Comparisons of anticonvulsants based on their HDAC inhibition properties could prove interesting. We reviewed the outcome of 236 patients treated in our institution using the Cox regression analysis of factors that are considered signifi cant in GBM-prognosis (age, Karnofsky score, extent of surgical resection and location) demonstrated that patients treated with valproate were 2.7 times less likely to die from their disease at any one time compared to no anticonvulsant treatment, 2.3 less likely to die than those were on Phenytoin and 1.3 less likely to die than those were on carbamazepine. 5
