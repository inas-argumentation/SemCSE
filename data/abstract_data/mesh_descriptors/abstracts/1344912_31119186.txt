Cultural attitudes towards death and dying: a South African perspective
Through a process of rapid democratization South Africa finds itself urgently engaging in many critical issues; these involve empowerment of communities, the recognition and respect for different cultural and religious perspectives and the freedom to question end-of-life issues. This ‘global perspective’ arises out of a short study undertaken to explore the similarities and differences that might exist between Muslim and Christian cancer patients, and others without cancer, towards death and dying in Cape Town, South Africa. The authors believe that this study is of significance both to South Africa and to developed countries owing to the substantial gap existing in the delivery of health-care service amongst persons of differing minority backgrounds. Demographic change is a reality and this is confirmed by research predicting, in the USA for example, that in the year 2000 one-third of the population would consist of minority groups.1 In South Africa political change has resulted in a collision of cultures, so multiculturalism should be embraced and studied but accompanied by an awareness of sensitive issues.
