Oral contraceptives: a risk factor for myocardial infarction.
Reports of myocardial infarction occurring in young women taking oral contraceptives (OCs) are discussed. In England in 1969 there was a considerably higher use of OCs among women under 40 years of age suffering from myocardial infarction than in the general population (50% vs. 13%). The respective figures for 1972 were 55% and 11%. However the majority of these OC patients had factors predisposing towards coronary atherosclerosis. Although an epidemiological study did not find a greater frequency of deaths from myocardial infarction among OC users it did show that OCs have an effect independent of other fisk factors. The risk of developing myocardial infarction is most significant in women with other atherosclerotic risk factors. The risk appears greater in women who have used OCs for more than 2 years. The risk also seems to disappear after cessation of OC use. Mechanisms by which OCs may promote the risk of myocardial infarction and possible con traindications to OC use are discussed.
