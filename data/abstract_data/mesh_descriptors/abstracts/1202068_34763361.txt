Superior discriminative performance of ceftizoxime disk diffusion test for detecting meticillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus
In evaluating screening tests for meticillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA), use of the 30 μg cefoxitin disk diffusion test has been recommended as a rapid and reliable means of detecting MRSA in countries on both sides of the Atlantic Ocean. These recommendations have been made by authorities such as the U.S.-based Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI, formerly NCCLS) [1], the European Antimicrobial Resistance Surveillance System [2] and a British Joint Working Party [3] as well as by various other investigators [4–6]. For approximately 1 year we have used Oxacillin Resistant Screening Agar Base (Oxoid, Basingstoke, UK) in conjunction with oxacillin 1 μg disks, oxacillin E-test strips, and ceftizoxime disks to confirm or rule out suspected MRSA with good results. This led us to compare the performance of ceftizoxime versus cefoxitin disks for rapid surrogate screening for meticillin resistance in Staphylococcus aureus. One important consequence of rapidly detecting a possible MRSA case is early isolation of the patient [7]. For this study, 41 MRSA strains (including proven outbreak strains) and 41 meticillin-sensitive Staphylococcus aureus (MSSA) strains were selected. The MRSA strains had been collected since 1993 at our Regional Public Health Laboratory and represented 41 different PFGE types. They were of both hospitaland community-acquired origin. According to multilocus sequence typing (MLST) performed by the National Institute of Public Health and the Environment (RIVM, Bilthoven, The Netherlands), five of these 41 strains represented international outbreak clones (sequence types [ST] 1, 5, 8, 22 and 30). Also included was the heterogeneous (Berlin) MRSA strain (ST 45) from the Rijnmond outbreak, which occurred around the year 2002 and was responsible for one-third of all new MRSA strains in the Netherlands [8]. The MSSA strains were obtained from blood cultures of patients hospitalized in 20 different wards, and like the MRSA strains, these were also of hospitaland community-acquired origin. Although genotyping was not performed, there was no indication that any of these strains isolated from patients on 20 different wards were closely related to each other. To evaluate the performance of the cefoxitin and ceftizoxime disks, a bacterial inoculum of 0.5 McFarland density from each of the 82 strains was applied to cefoxitin (30 μg) and ceftizoxime (30 μg) screening disks (Oxoid, Basingstoke, UK) on Mueller–Hinton agar with and without 2% NaCl, and the plates were incubated at 30 and 37°C for 24 h (Table 1). For interpretation, inhibition zone diameters of ≥18 mm for cefoxitin and ≥20 mm for ceftizoxime were regarded as susceptible, and diameters ≤14 mm were considered resistant [1]. Inhibition zones for the 41 MSSA strains were ≥18 mm for cefoxitin or ≥20 mm for ceftizoxime under all test conditions, so the strains were unequivocally regarded as sensitive. For the MRSA strains, earlier routine testing performed with the oxacillin E-test strip on Mueller–Hinton agar revealed minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) of oxacillin of >256 mg/l for all but the Rijnmond outbreak strain.
