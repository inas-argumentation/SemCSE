Peritoneal Macrophage and Lymphocyte Cytoplasmic Ca2+: Effects on Peritoneal Immune Defense Mechanisms and Ultrafiltration Capacity in Continuous Ambulatory Peritoneal Dialysis
I n spite of improvements in technique and equipment, dropout in continuous ambulatory perito neal dialysis (CAPD) patients is still elevated. The main reasons are peritonitis and ultrafiltration loss (1,2). Epidemiological studies have shown that these two complications are not randomly distributed, but some patients develop these more often than others. Since this different susceptibility does not seem to be the consequence of differences in technique or clinical or social conditions, other factors may be important. One of the possible causes could be an alteration of the peritoneal immune cell functions: either depression, thus facilitating infection, or hyperstimulation, leading to peritoneal sclerosis (3,4). Experimental studies and clinical observations indicate that in certain CAPD patients with recurrent peritonitis, there are disturbances in the initial phase of microbemediated activation of peritoneal macro phages and lymphocytes. The consequences are a reduced-generation toxic oxygen radical and various mediators such as interleukin-l and gamma -interferon, cytokines with a helper function in the immune response, therefore promoting bacteriolysis. At the same time, in these high-infection-rate patients the production of prostaglandin E2 (which exerts a downregulatory function on the immune response) is significantly increased as a consequence of a feedback mechanism induced by low levels of interleukin-l and gamma -interferon (Figure 1). The result is a global depression of the peritoneal immune defense mechanisms, with a consequent reduced ability to kill various types of microorganisms (5,6).
