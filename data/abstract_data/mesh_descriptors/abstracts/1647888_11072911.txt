Calcium dependence of spontaneous acetylcholine release at mammalian motor nerve terminals.
The persistence of miniature end-plate potentials (m.e.p.p.s) at the neuromuscular junction in the absence of calcium and the lack of effect of excess magnesium led Del Castillo & Katz (1954) to postulate a dual mechanism for the release of quanta of acetylcholine (ACh) in the frog. Hubbard (1961) concluded that 'calcium-independent' quanta existed in the phrenic-nerve terminals of the rat diaphragm. He was unable to abolish activity by increase in external magnesium-ion concentration or exposure to ethylenediamine tetraacetic acid (EDTA). In the rat, however, Hubbard noted reduction in the frequency of miniature end-plate potentials by exposure to increasing Mg2+, and/or to decreasing Ca2+. Reduction of Mg2+ or elevation of Ca2+ concentrations resulted in an increase in the m.e.p.p. rate. These observations may be explained by limitations in calcium mobility at the nerve terminal. The present study was undertaken to determine whether measures facilitating Ca2+ movement would alter m.e.p.p. production at a mammalian neuromuscular junction when Ca2+ was absent from the bathing solution.
