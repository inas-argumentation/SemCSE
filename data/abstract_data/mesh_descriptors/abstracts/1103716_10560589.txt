Entry of bilirubin into the brain due to opening of the blood-brain barrier.
A potential animal model of kernicterus has been developed. The blood-brain barrier was opened on one side of the brain, by brief infusion of a hypertonic solution into the carotid artery. Upon peripheral infusion of biirubin, the animals developed unilateral yellow staining ofthe brain: the treated side was stained whereas the control side was not. This cerebral icterus resulted from the entry of albumin-bound biirubin into the brain, and not from the passage of free biirubin.
