Secondary malignancies after treatment with single-agent vemurafenib in two patients with refractory hairy cell leukemia
Hairy cell leukemia (HCL) is an uncommon mature B-cell lymphoid malignancy characterized genetically by the BRAF V600E mutation. This point mutation in exon 15 of the BRAF results in the substitution of valine (V) to glutamate (E) at the 600th amino acid of the BRAF kinase and causes constitutive activation of the RAS-RAF-MEKERK signaling pathway. This molecular feature drives HCL pathogenesis and is present in nearly all patients with this condition [1]. Disruption of BRAF-mediated signaling with the use of BRAF inhibitors was first demonstrated to have significant activity in pre-clinical models of HCL [2]. Two phase-2 clinical trials have shown the impressive activity of vemurafenib in patients with relapsed/refractory HCL with overall response rates of 96–100% (complete response rates of 35–42%) [3]. Of note, secondary malignancies were noted in these studies including cutaneous squamous-cell carcinomas (3 of 54 patients), basal-cell carcinoma, and superficial melanoma. The prevalence and underlying pathophysiology of secondary malignancies in patients with HCL treated with vemurafenib in HCL is poorly defined and limited by small patient cohorts. Here we present two patients with relapsed HCL treated with single-agent vemurafenib complicated by the development of clinically significant secondary malignancies necessitating treatment discontinuation.
