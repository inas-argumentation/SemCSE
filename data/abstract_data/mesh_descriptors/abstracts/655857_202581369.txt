One‐stop shopping: Models of care for childhood cancer survivor care
Since the 1990s, childhood cancer survivors (CCS) have received care from long-term follow-up (LTFU) programs throughout the world. These programs are typically open to survivors of all childhood cancers and, in the United States, often include at least a pediatric oncology nurse practitioner (NP) and/or a pediatric oncology physician. Many programs include other physicians, social workers, psychologists, and a network of various pediatric subspecialists. Subspecialty referrals are made as needed during annual LTFU appointments.1–3 However, atmany centers, LTFU care is delivered through a specialized multidisciplinary model of care.4 Such care involves multiple subspecialty visits over one or two days and sometimes even in the same clinic or office setting. This model is often termed “one-stop shopping” as the patient receives survivor-focused care frommultiple disciplines in one extended clinical encounter. For example, a visit may start with the survivor being seen by a pediatric oncology provider (physician or NP) and then immediately afterward other subspecialists, such as a cardiologist or an endocrinologist, also meet with the survivor in the same patient room, clinic, or campus.4,5 Another formofmultidisciplinary LTFU care includes a primary visit with a pediatric oncology provider and then other subspecialists are involved through streamlined referrals that result in future outpatient appointments for the survivor. Many hybrid models exist where, for example, multidisciplinary LTFU care includes a primary visit with a pediatric oncology provider and is followed by additional visits with health professionals from disciplines such as nursing, social work, psychology, rehabilitation, or nutrition.
