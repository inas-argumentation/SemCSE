Measuring central action of acute antimigraine drugs in humans
Neurophysiological methods of assessing effects of drugs on the central nervous system (CNS) have the advantages of being non-invasive, relatively painless, inexpensive and reproducible. There are many different techniques, but here I shall focus mainly on the use of evoked potentials. There are some limitations to this approach because of the complexity of the activities that are recorded. In most cases what is recorded is a complex averaging of a number of activities in generators underneath, or perhaps at some distance from, scalp electrodes.
