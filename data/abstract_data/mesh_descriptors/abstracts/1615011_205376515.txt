Progress in psychiatric nosology: A hard-nosed look.
In this Prakash and Sagar (2016) have provided a thoughtful overview of the current status of classification in psychiatric disorders, the potential obstacles and possible solutions. They point out the key challenges in making clear progress from syndromes (clusters of symptoms) and disorders (symptom clusters) toward diseases in the way implied the rest of medicine (disorders with known etiology and/or pathophysiology). These road-blocks include (a) the lack of clear boundaries between health and illness, (b) the blurred boundaries between the various disorders, (c) heterogeneity within disorders, and (d) the frequent inability to identify discrete etiological factors and the subtle/lack of biological changes in many patients with psychiatric illnesses. The first three problems together have accounted for the problem of lack of reliability in psychiatric disorders. The last problem leads to the major problem of lack of validity, which Prakash and Sagar refer to as the ‘graveyard of psychiatric nosology’. How is psychiatry to make progress in addressing each of these road-blocks? A first step toward establishing validity is to reliably measure aspects of diseases. Reliability of diagnosis has clearly improved with operational criteria in DSM and ICD (Keshavan, 2013), but field trials indicate 9 of 23 disorder have rather modest reliabilities in the kappa range of 0.40–0.59 (Freedman et al., 2013). Interestingly, highest reliability was seen in neurocognitive disorders, which are defined by single, quantifiable features, while disorders defined by arbitrary symptom clusters such as major depression had poorer reliability. Clearly dimensional measures that have a direct bearing to underlying biological constructs can improve reliability further. Second, the problem of heterogeneity and blurred boundaries between diseases and between health and illness has for too long been blamed as a cause of lack of progress in psychiatric nosology. However, none of these problems are really unique to psychiatry. Most common medical diseases manifest heterogeneity in symptom expression, pathophysiology as well as etiology. Hypertension is an arbitrary definition, and blurs into normal blood pressure. Disease boundaries are continually revised. An example is maturity onset diabetes of the young, which is intermediate between type1 and type 2 diabetes. Many precancerous conditions are intermediate between health and established malignant disease. Progress in understanding was made possible by looking at pathophysiology across, and not just within, disorders. An example is aberrant Herceptin receptor expression as a common molecular across lung, breast and colon cancers. Thus, heterogeneity within clinically defined disorders may be resolved by looking agnostically across disorders.
