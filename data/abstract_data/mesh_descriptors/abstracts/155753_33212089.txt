Arterioles from postischaemic kidneys have an increased basal tone and reactivity to vasoconstrictive agents, and decreased vasodilatory responses when compared with arterioles from normal kidneys. Alterations in local levels of vasoconstrictors (angiotensin II, thromboxane A 2 , leukotrienes, adenosine, endothelin-1) have been implicated in the abnormal vascular tone, while the altered vascular responses might be attributed to enhanced responses to angiotensin II through activation of endothelin-B and/or PGH 2 /thromboxane A 2 receptors. Systemic levels of endothelin-1 increase with ischaemia, and administration of anti-endothelin antibodies or endothelinreceptor antagonists has been reported to protect against ischaemia-reperfusion injury [3]. Nitric oxide is vasodilatory, and decreases endothelin expression and activity in the vascular endothelium, effects which should be protective against ischaemic renal injury, but with endothelial injury, endothelial nitric oxide synthase is inhibited [4].
