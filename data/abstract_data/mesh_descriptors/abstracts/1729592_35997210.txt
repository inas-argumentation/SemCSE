Interaction of Heparin with Histidine‐rich Glycoprotein
Histidine-rich glycoprotein (HRG) was first isolated from human plasma in 1972 as a 3.8 S cup-glycoprotein with a molecular weight of 6O,OOO,’ but later this form was shown to be a proteolytic derivative of a molecule with molecular weight 75,000.2 The native molecule is identical with the autorosette inhibition factor of human plasma.2 The complete amino acid sequence of the protein has been derived from the nucleotide sequence of its cDNA.~ The mature protein contains 507 amino acids of which more than half consists of five different types of internal repeats. Within the last three internal repeats, there are 12 tandem repetitions of a five amino-acid segment with a consensus sequence of Gly-His-His-ProHis. This repeated portion, referred to as “histidine-rich region,” contains 53% histidine and is highly homologous with the histidine-rich region of high molecular weight kininogen. The gene for human HRG spans about 9 kb and consists of at least five exons and four introns. Although the primary physiological function of HRG remains unknown, several molecular interactions of this protein have been described. These include interactions with divalent cations,6 plasminogen,’ with the rosette formation between erythrocytes and lymphocytes,2,8 with fibrinogen and fibrin? and with human platelet thrombospondin.I0 It has been suggested4 that the multifunctional properties of HRG might result from its multi-domain structure. HRG interacts strongly with heparin, resulting in neutralization of its anticoagulant activity.*1.12 Here we will summarize available information on the mechanism of the interaction between HRG and heparin in purified systems and in human plasma, on the turnover of HRG during heparin therapy, and on the possible physiological relevance of this interaction.
