Linked color imaging improves visibility of ectatic vessels in radiation proctitis during argon plasma coagulation
RADIATION PROCTITIS IS caused by radiotherapy in patients with pelvic cancer such as prostate or cervical cancer. Under colonoscopy, multiple red ectatic and tortuous vessels can be identified. Argon plasma coagulation (APC) has proven to be effective in radiation proctitis. Accurate detection and coagulation of malformed vessels are essential in APC. Linked color imaging (LCI) is a novel image-enhanced endoscopic technique that may improve the red-white contrast. LCI is more efficient than white light imaging (WLI) in the diagnosis of atrophic gastritis and ulcerative colitis. Hence, we tried LCI in one patient with radiation proctitis. An 81-year old man was presented with hematochezia and anemia after radiotherapy for prostate cancer. WLI under colonoscopy showed multiple tortuous vessels in the rectum (Fig. 1a, Video S1). Radiation proctitis was diagnosed. Under LCI, the ectatic vessels appeared purple and the surrounding mucosa was white (Fig. 1b). To compare visualization efficacy, color difference (DE) between normal mucosa and ectatic vessels was calculated using photoshop software by the method described elsewhere. We calculated five pairs of regions of interest (ROI) and the average DE in WLI was lower than in LCI (82.9 6.9 vs 97.3 10.1, P = 0.029). This means that LCI is more efficient for identifying ectatic vessels in radiation proctitis as a result of the higher color difference. Previous study reported that some patients with radiation proctitis were affected by severe complications after APC therapy, including fistulation and stricture formation. This higher color contrast would lower the possible misdiagnosis and assist more accurate and thorough detection of ectatic vessels during APC therapy, which may lead to better clinical outcome.
