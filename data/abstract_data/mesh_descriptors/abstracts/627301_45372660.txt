Time for a systematic look at inactivated HIV vaccines.
In their Viewpoint elsewhere in this Journal, Rios et al. [1] suggest it is time for another look at inactivated (killed) HIV vaccines for prevention of AIDS. They point out that ‘inactivation is a proven approach to vaccine development,’ that ‘past failures’ of killed virus vaccine efforts may be attributable to technical issues such as ‘severe denaturing of important HIV epitopes’ by overly harsh inactivation methods or to problematic ‘biological characteristics of the HIV strains used.’ They argue, quite correctly we think, that compelling justification exists ‘to fully explore the killed [virus] approach to the development of a preventive HIV vaccine.’ And they propose, in particular, that better outcomes may result from inactivation of a polyvalent array of a few subtypes of transmitted/founder (T/F) HIV virus, using a proprietary photolabeled reagent that irreversibly inactivates viral reverse transcriptase while ‘presumably preserving the conformational and functional integrity of viral surface proteins’ [2].
