Reply: The role and limitations of CAM use in children and adolescents with cancer: Let's take a look beyond prevalence rate
We appreciate the recognition of the importance of research on traditional and complementary/alternative medicine (TCAM) in pediatric oncology as emphasized in the letter from Dr. Meyer and Ms. Schroeder [1]. We certainly agree that more well-designed, clinical research studies are needed in this area. Yet given the vast array of TCAM modalities, surveys can be instructive on identifying key symptoms and treatments to further test in clinical studies. The primary goal of our study was to obtain a better understanding of the use of TCAM in a geographic area where very little is known about the use of TCAM in combination with conventional cancer therapy [2]. This survey has identified areas for further research with a goal of evaluating the potential benefits and limitations of these TCAM practices. We believe that this approach may provide patients with the best of both conventional and TCAM medical care. Elena J. Ladas Division of Pediatric Hematology/Oncology/Stem Cell Transplant Columbia University Medical Center, USA Institute of Human Nutrition, Columbia University Medical Center USA
