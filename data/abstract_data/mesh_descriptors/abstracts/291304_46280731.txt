Reply to "Conventional Ductography Combined With Digital Breast Tomosynthesis for Imaging of Pathologic Nipple Discharge".
AJR 2016; 206:W45 0361–803X/16/2062–W45 © American Roentgen Ray Society Reply to “Conventional Ductography Combined With Digital Breast Tomosynthesis for Imaging of Pathologic Nipple Discharge” We thank Dr. Cohen [1] for commenting on our recent article [2] and for sharing experiences with other imaging modalities for the evaluation of pathologic nipple discharge. The results of our study show that ultrasound is a valuable diagnostic tool; however, we agree with Dr. Cohen that the management of patients with pathologic nipple discharge can be challenging when there are no findings on conventional imaging with mammography and sonography. At our institution, patients with pathologic nipple discharge and no findings on a conventional imaging workup are referred for surgical consultation. After consultation with a surgeon, the patient may undergo further imaging with conventional MRI, undergo surgical duct exploration, or be monitored. We recently reported our experience with conventional MRI in this setting [3]. The concept of combining conventional ductography with tomosynthesis is intriguing and may help minimize the number of false-negative conventional examinations. This technique may help to reduce the number of patients who undergo unnecessary surgical duct explorations, and we look forward to clinical data that define the sensitivity and specificity of this approach.
