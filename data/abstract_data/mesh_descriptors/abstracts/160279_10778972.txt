Disturbances of cerebral microcirculation after cerebral ischemia.
Since the work of Ames et al. (1), it is known that after cerebral ischemia, wide areas of the brain are not perfused. Ultrastructural examination of these areas revealed narrowing and obstruction of capillaries (2, 4). The following experiments were undertaken to examine how the capillary lesions develop and whether they may be influenced by postischemic conditions. If it is true that nerve cells may survive anoxia of much more than 10 min (5), it is of eminent importance to know how to avoid postischemic disturbances of the cerebral microcirculation.
