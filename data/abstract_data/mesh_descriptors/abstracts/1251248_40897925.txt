Genetic and physical mapping of the regulatory gene csrA on the Escherichia coli K-12 chromosome
Glycogen synthesis in Escherichia coli is one of a number of metabolic pathways that are induced in the stationary phase (for reviews, see references 4 and 5). The accumulation of glycogen in the early stationary phase reflects at least two levels of regulation, allosteric regulation of the committed step of the biochemical pathway and enhanced expression of the structural genes for the pathway. Glycogen synthesis is positively regulated by cyclic AMP and ppGpp via effects on two of the structural genes that encode enzymes of the glycogen biosynthetic pathway, glgC (ADPglucose pyrophosphorylase) and gigA (glycogen synthase) (7, 10). We have recently identified an Escherichia coli K-12 gene that encodes a trans-acting negative regulator of glycogen biosynthesis, csrA (carbon storage regulator) (8). This gene affects glycogen synthesis via expression of theglgCA operon and the independently regulatedglgB gene that encodes glycogen branching enzyme. In addition, evidence indicating that csrA participates in the control of gluconeogenesis was obtained. Insertion of a kanamycin resistance marker (Kanr) into csrA to generate the mutation TR1-5 (csrA::kanR) exhibited pleiotropic effects on cell size and surface (adherence) properties of cells, suggesting that csrA may encode a global regulatory factor. Sequence analysis and expression studies showed that the csrA gene encodes a 61-amino-acid polypeptide that has no known homolog. The chromosomal location of the TR1-5 mutation in csrA was first mapped with a set of seven Hfr strains (12), each of which transfers a tetracycline resistance (Tetr) marker approximately 20 min after mating is initiated. The recipient strain, TR1-5[pCZ3-3] (8), was tetracycline sensitive (Tets) and ampicillin resistant (Ampr) and produced elevated levels of glycogen. Transconjugants were selected on ampicillin (100 ,ug/ml) and tetracycline (10 p,g/ml) and scored for glycogen biosynthesis by staining colonies with iodine vapor, as previously described (9). Only conjugation with Hfr 5 of the set, CAG5055 (PO45 relAI thi-1 zed-3069::TnlO), produced transconjugants with the low-glycogen (wild-type) phenotype of the Hfr parent (36 low-glycogen colonies of a total of 57 colonies), indicating that the mutation that affects glycogen synthesis resides between 43 and 62 min. Transductional mapping of the TR1-5 mutation was conducted to determine its distance from 20 individual Tetr markers spanning the 43to 62-min region in a collection of Tetr mapping strains (12). A Plvir lysate from TR1-5 was used to transduce the Kanr marker into each of the 20 mapping strains. The Kanr (resistant to 100 p,g of kanamycin per ml) high-glycogen transductants were picked and tested for Tetr.
