ARI Control Programme: standard case management guidelines vs conventional treatment--an open study.
Acute respiratory infections (ARI) are the leading cause of under-five deaths in developing countries with pneumonia being responsible for the majority of ARI deaths. Indias National ARI Control Program recommends the use of benzyl penicillin to treat severe pneumonia and chloramphenicol for very severe pneumonia. A prospective randomized controlled open study was conducted at Kasturba Hospital Mahatma Gandhi Institute of Medical Sciences Sevagram Wardha to determine whether single drug therapy as advised in Standard Case Management Guidelines (SCMG) is as useful as the conventional treatment with a combination of benzyl penicillin and chloramphenicol. All children presenting to the pediatric OPD/emergency unit with the complaints of cough and/or breathlessness were screened according to SCMG. 57 children with severe pneumonia and 17 with very severe pneumonia were included for analysis in the study. The SCMG were found to be as effective as the conventional combination of benzyl penicillin and chloramphenicol in the treatment of severe and very severe pneumonia and should be widely used.
