N‐Ras mutation in vemurafenib‐associated expanding melanocytic nevus
Dear Editor, Vemurafenib has a substantial clinical effect on advanced melanoma with B-Raf mutation, while squamoproliferative lesions such as verrucous keratosis, keratoacanthoma and squamous cell carcinoma are induced by this agent. These lesions are induced by paradoxical activation of the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway by vemurafenib. Here, we report a case of expanding nevus associated with vemurafenib application. A 55-year-old man presented with a hemorrhagic nodule within a pigmented macule on the inguinal region for a duration of 5 years (Fig. 1a). The nodule was histologically diagnosed as malignant melanoma and the lesion was surgically excised. The genetic analysis revealed that melanoma cells harbored the B-Raf V600E mutation. The patient received vemurafenib 960 mg twice a day because he developed multiple metastatic lesions of melanoma in the stomach, liver, retroperitoneum, mesenterium and femur (Fig. 1b). Three months after the initiation of vemurafenib therapy, the size of visceral metastatic lesions remained regressed (Fig. 1c); however, the patient complained of enlargement of the pre-existing nevus on the chest (Fig. 1d,e). The nevus was oval in shape and a slightly elevated lesion 5 mm in diameter. Dermoscopic examination demonstrated thick-branched streaks on a delicate pigment network (Fig. 1f). Histopathological examination revealed that the pigmented lesion was symmetrical and nevus cells in the nest showed mild nuclear atypia (Fig. 1g,h).
