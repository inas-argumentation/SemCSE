The distribution of deoxyribonucleic acid in homogenates of plant roots.
Abstract Plant roots have been homogenized by a new method and the cell organelles were fractionated by differential centrifugation. Nuclei isolated by ideal conditions did not appear to contain DNA when examined cytochemically, although they did contain DNA if the roots were crushed or ground prior to, or during, homogenization. Biochemical estimation of the DNA showed that most of it was associated with the cytoplasmic fractions if the roots were unfixed, or treated with polyvinyl alcohol, but it was mainly nuclear if the roots were pretreated with an acetate solution of low pH or high ionic concentration. The significance of these, and of previous cytochemical observations on intact cells, is discussed.
