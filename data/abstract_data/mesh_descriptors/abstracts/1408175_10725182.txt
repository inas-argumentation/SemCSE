Hypopituitarism and skin pigmentation associated with malignant hypertension and an intrasellar cholesteatoma
The patient was first seen in the Institut Jules Bordet in 1940 when he was 45 years old. He was admitted with the diagnosis of "pituitary adenoma." In 1916 he had been a prisoner of war in Germany and for 1 year he had been carrying out heavy manual work. During this period he was on a low caloric and low protein diet. He had lost weight and had noticed some weakness and episodes of headaches that had lasted until 6 months after his return from Germany in 1917. In 1927 he had experienced frequent headaches; these had lasted until 1929. In 1932, there had been noticeable loss of facial, axillary, and pubic hair; his penis and testes became smaller, and the libido decreased. He noticed increased dryness and diffuse pigmentation of the skin. In 1939, he had develo ed scattered hyperpigmented patches on t i e skin; the visual acuity of the left eye decreased; he had repeated episodes of bilateral parietotemporal headaches. He was first admitted to the hospital on May 3,1940, with severe headaches, voxiliting, weakness, vertigo, and visual disturbances. Review of the organ systems was not significant except for mild exertional dyspnea.
