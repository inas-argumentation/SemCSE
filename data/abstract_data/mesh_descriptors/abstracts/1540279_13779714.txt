Magnetic resonance of the heart and cardiovascular system: an echocardiographer's perspective.
I have spent several years in the field of echocardiography, and I recently had the opportunity to spend a sabbatical studying cardiovascular applications of magnetic resonance. Many similarities and some obvious differences exist between echocardiography and magnetic resonance in the examination of the cardiovascular system. Reflecting on this comparison leads to an appreciation of the futility of a parochial approach to the understanding of heart disease centered on a single technology; more fruitful is the exploitation of all available methods to answer fundamental questions posed from relevant clinical and physiologic perspectives. Those using magnetic resonance to examine the heart can learn from echocardiographers and vice versa. The purpose of this article is to review briefly the current and potential applications of magnetic resonance to cardiovascular diagnosis from the perspective of a clinical cardiologist, especially as these observations may be related to echocardiography.
