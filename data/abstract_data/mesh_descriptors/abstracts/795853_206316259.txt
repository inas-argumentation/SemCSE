Breast Cancer Subtype and Baseline Characteristics from Diabetic Breast Cancer Patients are Not Different from Nondiabetics
To the Editor: Diabetes mellitus (DM) is one of the most expensive and alarming chronic diseases of our time. Type 2 diabetes (DM2) affects more than 6.5% of adults and up to 15% in those older than 60 years of age in the United States (1); it is a metabolic disorder characterized by hyperglycemia, insulin resistance, and relative impairment in insulin secretion (2). Many studies support the association between DM and various types of cancer (3–5). Data suggest that DM2 may be associated with an increased risk of BC (6). These associations include the biologic effects of DM2 on breast cancer risk and natural history, as well as the effects on disease screening and management of breast cancer (7). The objective of this study was to assess breast cancer subtypes and other patient and tumor characteristics and evaluate their relationship with diabetes status of patients diagnosed and treated at a single institution. The Department of Breast Medical Oncology data base at the M. D. Anderson Cancer Center (MDACC) was searched to identify patients with primary noninflammatory breast cancer patients and diabetes status between 1988 and 2005. Patient characteristics, including clinical stage at presentation, estrogen receptor (ER) and progesterone receptor (PR) status, histologic grade, Her2/neu status, lympho-vascular invasion (LVI) and type of surgery were recorded. Patients were classified into diabetic and nondiabetic groups. Patient characteristics were tabulated or described by clinical stages at the diagnosis of breast cancer, tumor characteristics and by the type of surgery. Associations were calculated using chi-squared test and risk ratio (RR). We considered p-values <0.05 as statistically significantly. STATA Statistics/Data Analysis version 12.1, was used for analysis. Between 1998 and 2005; 18,763 noninflammatory newly diagnosed breast cancer patients were identified, further subdivided into diabetic n = 1,611 (8.6%) and nondiabetic n = 17,152 (91.4%). In general, 38.3% (n = 7,176) had ER/PR positive, 12% (n = 2,278) had Her2-positive and 11.7%, n = 2,183) had Triple Negative disease. In the 1,611 breast cancer patients with diabetes, there were 40.5% (n = 652) with ER/PR positive, 12.4% (n = 200) with TN and 11.5% (n = 186) with HER-2 overexpression-type. In the 17,152 nondiabetic patients with breast cancer, there were 38% (n = 6,524) with ER/PR positive, 11.6% (n = 1,983) with TN and 12.1% (n = 2,072) with HER-2 overexpression type. There was no significant difference in cancer subtypes between the diabetic and nondiabetic groups (p = 0.43). Analysis of hormone receptor status revealed that there were no significant differences between positivity of ER/PR receptors in diabetic women compared with nondiabetics (76.5% versus 76%, p = 0.7). HER-2 overexpression status was also similar in both groups (18% versus 19.6%, p = 0.21). Statistically significant discrepancies in the distribution of clinical stage were observed between diabetic and nondiabetic groups (overall p < 0.0001). Diabetic women were more likely to present with locally advanced or metastatic (stage III–IV) BC compared with nondiabetic patients (24% versus 19%, RR 1.38, 95% CI: 1.20–1.57, p < 0.0001). Tumor features, including nuclear grade (NG) and LVI, were similarly distributed in the diabetic and nondiabetic groups. About 53% had NG III disease in both groups (p = 1.003) and 22% (n = 319) and 23.6% (n = 3,796) of DM and NDM, respectively had LVI (p = 1.17). In general, surgery (Breast conserving surgery and mastectomy) was more frequently performed in nondiabetic women compared with diabetic women (95% versus 92%, p < 0.0001).
