Molecular Response to Calcium Channel Blockers Expressed during Reperfusion following Acute Renal Ischemia
Renal ischemia is the most common cause of acute renal failure (ARF), occurring in nearly 5% of hospitalized patients. Additionally it has a mortality between 30 and 6096, which increases to 50 to 70% when associated with surgery or trauma. Our efforts to improve the recovery from ARF have been poor, as evidenced by little improvement in the survival from ARF over the last 40 years. The kidney has the unique ability to regenerate these injured or dead cells and restore function. This mechanism of recovery and repair is poorly understood. Calcium channel blockers have ben previously investigated with little or no success in enhancing recovery from AM. The purpose of this study was to evaluate the calcium channel blocker, verapamil (V), as a modifier of expression of the putative growth-inhibitory sequence, TRPM-2, which has been associated with renal injury.
