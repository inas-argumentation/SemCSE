Liver glycogen and blood sugar levels in x-irradiated newborn rats.
Liver glycogen and blood sugar levels were determined in normal prenatal and postnatal rats as a function of age. The liver glycogen of the fetal rats rose rapidly to a maximum value shortly before the expected time of parturition, fell precipitously during birth, and then rose gradually. The blood sugar levels of the fetal rat more than doubled during the last 2 days of gestation and continued to increase slowly after birth. Liver glycogen and blood sugar levels also were determined in newborn rats 24 hours after being subjected to fasting and whole body x-irrad;ation. Up until the 6th day after birth the irradiation had no effect on the liver glycogen levels, and the blood sugar levels were quite unstable. On the 7th day after birth the blood sugar levels became stabilized, and the expected mild hyperglycemia and increased liver glycogen due to whole body irradiation were demonstrable. It thus appears that pituitary- adrenal regulation of carbohydrate metabolism becomes effective on or about the 7th day...
