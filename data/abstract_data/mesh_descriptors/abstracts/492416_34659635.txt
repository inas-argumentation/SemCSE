Gastrin and cancer.
Publisher Summary This chapter describes the biology of the gastrin system and the involvement of gastrin in the development of various forms of cancer. The chapter presents new analytical methods for the measurement of gastrin in clinical oncology. Gastrin is a well-studied hormone that is discovered as a gastric acid-stimulating factor in extracts of antral mucosa. The elaborate biosynthetic pathway of gastrin in its main site of synthesis—the antral G cells—is now well described (B12, H5, 52, SIO). The pathway explains the molecular heterogeneity of gastrin in tissue and in circulation ( 52, R11, Y1). The active site homology of gastrin with cholecystokinin (CCK) poses large analytical problems because the active site is strongly immunogenic and both CCK and gastrin receptors may bind peptides having common sequences. Consequently, evaluation of antibody and receptor binding of gastrin and studies of the carcinogenic effects of gastrin require due consideration of possible interactions and cross-reactivity with CCK peptides. In normal mammalian organisms, gastrin is generally produced by the antral G cells (L1 and R2). The chapter concludes with a discussion of the expression and synthesis of antral and extra-antral gastrin.
