PRELIMINARY OBSERVATIONS ON THE DEVELOPING BILIARY SYSTEM IN CHICK EMBRYOS *
I have studied the morphology of embryonic bile capillaries in the chick. FIGURE 1 is an electron micrograph demonstrating a typical bile capillary in the liver of an adult white leghorn chicken. Notice that the actual biliary space-free of the projecting finger-like microvilli-is small. The microvilli are numerous and are relatively long. Note also that typical terminal bars are present, setting off the biliary space from other intercellular spaces. FIGURE 2 is an electron micrograph of liver from a 14-day chick embryo showing two bile capillaries, The magnification is somewhat greater than that in FIGURE 1, but it is evident that the lumens are proportionately larger than those in adult chicken liver. By comparison, embryonic bile capillaries are dilated, and have shorter, less numerous microvilli than those in the adult liver. It is of further interest that terminal bars are present as well as numerous “pericanalicular vacuoles” in the 14-day chick liver. FIGURE 3 is an electron micrograph of a bile capillary from the liver of a 6-day chick embryo. Here too, the bile capillary is “dilated.” The microvilli are even shorter and fewer in number than those in the 14-day embryo. Terminal bars are readily seen and several small “vacuoles” occur in the adjacent liver cell cytoplasm. The first demonstration of bile capillaries in a 6-day chick embryo with the electron microscope, to my knowledge, was that by Karrer in 1961.l He did not observe terminal bars or microvilli in his material. Frum my studies there are three additional observations which deserve brief comment. (1 ) In the 6-day embryo, but not in the 14-day embryo, there frequently are large vacuoles deep in the cytoplasm of the developing parenchymal cells. These are similar to those reported by Hampton in 19612 following extrahepatic bile duct ligation in adult mice. (2) Using serial sections we have found, in many instances, that the pericanalicular vacuoles are continuous with biliary spaces and might be better termed: tubules. It may be that the larger vacuoles situated more deeply in the cytoplasm are continuous with biliary space via these “tubules.” (3 ) CarefuI Iight and electron microscopic study has not revealed the presence of typical ductules or intrahepatic bile ducts in the 6-day chick embryo. Capillary-like bile structures, with large lumens similar to those discussed earlier by Wilson, have been observed.
