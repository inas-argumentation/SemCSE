Vasoactive Intestinal Peptide Activates both Gs and Gi in Lung
Vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP) is widely distributed in the peripheral and central nervous system, where it acts as a neurotransmitter and neuromodulator. In the lung, VIP receptors are found in airway and vascular smooth muscle, as well as in alveolar macrophages.’ VIP promotes both bronchodilation and vasodilation, and VIP analogs offer promise for treatment of asthma.2 Two VIP receptor subtypes have been cloned;3 both are members of the superfamily of receptors that couple to guanine nucleotide-binding proteins (G-proteins). There is compelling evidence that the stimulatory G-protein (GJ and adenylyl cyclase play a prominent role in signal transduction from the VIP receptor in many tissues, including the lung.* Limited data, however, suggest that other signaling pathways may also play a significant role in the action of VIP!
