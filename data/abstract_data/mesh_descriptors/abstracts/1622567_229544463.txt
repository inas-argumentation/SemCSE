Spontaneous Cervicovaginal Fistula in Obstructed Hemivagina and Ipsilateral Renal Anomaly Syndrome: A Case Report
Abstract Background Obstructed hemivagina and ipsilateral renal anomaly (OHVIRA) syndrome is a rare diagnosis that classically presents with dysmenorrhea and increasing pelvic pain. Unusual manifestations of OHVIRA syndrome may occur as a result of patient anatomy and the rupture or relief of the obstructed hemivagina. Case We present a 15-year old patient with OHVIRA syndrome who developed a spontaneous cervicovaginal fistula and subsequently underwent hemi-hysterectomy. Summary and Conclusion While spontaneous fistula formation is rare, the obstructive component in OHVIRA syndrome can make fistula formation favorable and unlikely to regress. Any change in bleeding or pain symptoms in an untreated OHVIRA syndrome should prompt immediate imaging. Clinicians should recognize how acquired or anatomical variants of OHVIRA syndrome may influence the surgical approach.
