Electrocardiographic findings after acute, isolated, posterior myocardial infarction in a patient with pulmonary disease.
A 69-year-old woman with severe obstructive lung disease developed substernal chest pressure and progressively worsening dyspnea. On examination, she was visibly tachypneic with an elevated jugular venous pressure and rales. Her electrocardiogram (ECG) at admission revealed 2 mm of horizontal ST depressions with upright T waves in leads V3 through V4 (Fig. 1). On the basis of these ECG findings and clinical suspicion, additional posterior leads were placed and showed 1 mm of ST elevation in leads V7 through V9 (Fig. 2). Subsequent urgent cardiac catheterization revealed an occluded proximal left circumflex artery (Fig. 3), which was successfully opened with placement of a drug-eluting stent (Fig. 4).
