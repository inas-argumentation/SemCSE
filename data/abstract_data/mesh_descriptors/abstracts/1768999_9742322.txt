GLUCOKINASE ACTIVITIES AND GLYCOGEN CONTENTS OF LIVERS OF NORMAL AND HYPOPHYSECTOMIZED, X-IRRADIATED RATS SUBJECTED TO DIFFERENT NUTRITIONAL TREATMENTS.
1. 1. The effect of a lethal dose (1000 R) of whole-body X-irradiation on glucokinase (ATP: d -glucose 6-phosphotransferase, EC 2.7.1.2) in a number of tissues of the rat was studied. In high-glucose-fed rats, X-irradiation depressed the activity of this hepatic enzyme, but not in fasted rats. X-irradiation interfered with normal dietary adaptation of glucokinase. 2. 2. No effect of the irradiation on glucokinase activities was observed in brain, skeletal muscle or kidney. 3. 3. Diet (high-glucose) and fasting caused an adaptive response in hepatic glucokinase activity of the hypophysectomized rat, but the activity level of the enzyme was lower in this animal than in the normal rat. Dietary adaptation of hepatic glucokinase is specific to glucose, but that of hepatic glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase ( d -glucose-6-phosphate: TPN oxidorecductase, EC 1.1.1.49) is not. 4. 4. The decreasing effect of X-irradiation on hepatic glucokinase in the high-glucose-fed rat continued after hypophysectomy. Thus the elevation of hepatic glucokinase activity induced by feeding a high-glucose diet is directly affected by X-irradiation. 5. 5. A second action of X-irradiation on hepatic glucokinase—differing from the decreasing effect on the enzyme observed in the high-glucose-fed rat—is a slowing rate of decline of the enyzme'a activity in fasted animals and its dependence upon pituitary gland activity. 6. 6. A spectrophotometric method for liver fructokinase (ATP: d -fructose 1-phosphototransferase, EC 2.7.1.3) is presented. X-irradiation did not affect hepatic fructokinase.
