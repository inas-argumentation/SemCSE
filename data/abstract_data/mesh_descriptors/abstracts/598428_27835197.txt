MINIMAL ACCESS RADICAL PROSTATECTOMY: HOW IS IT SHAPING UP?
Ten years following publication of the first series of laparoscopic radical prostatectomy (LRP) by Schuessler et al. [1], 7 years following the seminal publication of the first large series of LRP by Guillonneau and Vallancian [2] and despite the advent of brachytherapy, cryotherapy and high-intensity focused ultrasound, RP remains a popular option for patients with localized prostate cancer. Minimal access RP (MARP), in particular, has flourished during the past 5 years, offering the dual appeal to patients of ‘keyhole’ surgery and the potential for greater surgical precision, which has been added to more recently by the promise of having their operation done by a ‘robot’ [3] and a 97% probability of postoperative potency [4]. Although the phrase ‘learning curve’ has become inextricably and often rather too conveniently linked to MARP, thousands of patients have now been treated in this fashion and the time has arrived to examine the evidence and to see how the results compare against the gold standard of open RP (ORP).
