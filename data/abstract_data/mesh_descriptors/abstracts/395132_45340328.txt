A stabilizing factor for liver phosphofructokinase.
Abstract A labile, non-dialyzable factor is present in rat liver supernatant fluid which protects liver phosphofructokinase against thermal inactivation. The factor is not present in other tissues examined, except kidney, and does not affect the stability of other hepatic enzymes tested (glucokinase and pyruvate kinase). The level of the factor is decreased in starvation, where the in vivo degradation rate of phosphofructokinase is increased, and restored with refeeding. The level of factor and of phosphofructokinase also change in parallel with insulin deprivation and administration. These findings suggest that regulation of phosphofructokinase levels in the liver is mediated by alterations in the content of the protective factor.
