New data on the use of the FISH technique: the horizon dividing Spitz nevi and melanoma in childhood moves even further away
Massi et al. (1) investigated the clinical, pathologic and cytogenetic findings of 50 atypical Spitz tumors (AST) in pediatric patients (aged 1–18 years, 31 men, 19 women). The lesions were located at the lower limbs (26), upper limbs (16), trunk (5), and head and neck (2). The excisions performed were incisional biopsy/shave, followed by re-excisions (9 patients), excisions with clear margins (32), and excisions with clear margins followed by re-excisions (9). Sentinel lymph node (SLN) biopsy was performed in 15 of 50 patients and SLN micrometastasis was detected in two cases. The duration of follow-up ranged from 1 to 300 months, and 48 patients were still alive, with no evidence of disease. The authors reported an adverse evolution in two cases: a 4-year-old girl that underwent incomplete excision without SLN biopsy, who died 96 months after the diagnosis, and a 14-year-old boy showing disease progression and in transit metastasis 20 months after the diagnosis, but no other disease recurrences in 25 years of follow-up. The authors performed conventional fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) analysis (RREB1; MYB, CCND1, CEP 6) and Vysis CDKN2A/Cep 9 probe kit. The results were interpreted by reviewing all the histopathologic results. The authors stated that the four-probe fluorescence in situ hybridization melanoma assay did not discriminate AST from Spitz nevi. Only one child experienced a fatal outcome. In conclusion, this study is significant because of the large series of pediatric patients investigated, the long-term follow-up, and the use of four-probe conventional FISH analysis together with 9p21 evaluation. Despite the low incidence of melanoma in pediatric patients, complete excision and histopathologic examination remain the gold standard in the diagnosis and treatment of all melanocytic tumors and also in AST. Even though a careful follow-up should be performed, the present authors’ data did not support any clinical benefit for the SLN biopsy procedure and completion lymphadenectomy. Cytogenetics offers adjunctive information that should be evaluated together with the histopathologic results in the diagnosis of difficult lesions.
