Does resistance to apoptosis affect clinical response to antitumor drugs?
hemotherapy and X-rays can induce apoptosis in tumor cells. This has led to the hypothesis that a blockade of apoptotic signaling pathways can result in resistance to therapy.Although there is experimental evidence supporting this hypothesis for certain hematopoietic malignancies, it is unclear whether it holds for all tumor cell types. In a recent paper, Schmitt et al. use a mouse lymphoma model to show that the anti-apoptotic gene product Bcl-2 confers resistance to chemotherapy in vivo.The Bcl-2 effect was largely lost when lymphoma cells were subjected to long term tissue culture and completely missed in a standard clonogenic survival assay. The authors advocate that physiological test systems should be employed to predict tumor cell responsiveness to chemotherapy and stress the relevance of the apoptotic death pathway in this prediction. We argue here that the clonogenic survival assay retains its value and that in vivo models should include non-hematopoietic tumors to gain a balanced perspective on the role of apoptosis in chemotherapy-induced tumor cell death.
