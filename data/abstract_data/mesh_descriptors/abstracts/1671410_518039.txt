Is the mitotic rate of 1/mm2 the correct cutoff point for performing sentinel lymph node biopsy in pregnant patients with thin melanoma?
Pregnancy was once regarded as a risk factor and a poor prognostic indicator of melanoma; however, at present, we know that pregnancy does not adversely affect melanoma prognosis. In contrast, pregnancy is a patientrelated factor influencing the nevus pattern of pigmentation [1]. During pregnancy, melanocytic nevi often show reversible macroscopic and dermoscopic changes [1] that may lead to misdiagnosis and mistreatment of melanocytic lesions. Chan et al. [2] found some atypical histologic features (e.g. increased mitotic figures, prominent nucleoli, and an increased nuclear/cytoplasmic ratio) and a high Ki-67 proliferation index in dermal nevi excised from pregnant women compared with nevi from location-matched and aged-matched control patients. The disproportionality in both the mitotic rate (MR) and the Ki-67 index for pregnancy versus nonpregnancy nevi implies that melanocytes of pregnancy nevi have a faster progression through the cell cycle [2]. Therefore, by recognizing these key features in nevi excised during pregnancy as pregnancy-induced rather than pathological, unnecessary treatments and a negative psychological impact may be drastically reduced. As pregnant women show higher mitotic and proliferative activities in their nevi compared with nonpregnant women [2], melanoma presenting during pregnancy may show a pregnancyinduced increase in the mitotic activity of its melanocytes. In the 2009 AJCC staging system, the MR (< 1 vs. Z 1/mm) replaced the Clark level of invasion in staging melanomas thinner than 1 mm [3]. The 10-year survival rate was 95% for nonulcerated T1a melanomas (r 1 mm with ulceration and/or mitoses < 1/mm) and dropped to 88% for nonulcerated T1b melanomas (r 1 mm with ulceration and/or mitosesZ 1/mm) [3]. Thus, MR was the second most powerful predictor of survival, after Breslow thickness [3]. Despite these data, the introduction of MR as a T1 category criterion is often debated [4], especially when discussing the indications for sentinel lymph node biopsy (SLNB) among thin melanomas. Although the SLNB positivity rate is low among nonulcerated thin melanomas and the procedure of SLNB shows more disadvantages in pregnant than in nonpregnant patients [4,5], women presenting with T1b melanoma during pregnancy are also candidates for SLNB.
