Granuloma annulare‐like congenital multiple glomangiomas
Editor Glomus tumours are rare benign proliferations of glomus bodies, which are modified smooth muscle cells located in walls of the Suquet-Hoyer canals, a specialized arteriovenous shunt involved in thermoregulation. Out of the more common solitary and rarer multiple forms, children mostly present with the latter, some in an autosomal dominant fashion. Multiple glomus tumours are alternatively termed as glomangiomas because of their predominance of blood vessels. Herein we present a case of congenital multiple glomangiomas with clinical features of granuloma annulare. A 12-year-old girl presented with slightly tender, multiple, purplish lesions on her lower back and right forearm. They had been present since birth, and had gradually increased in size and number (fig. 1). On close examination, individual lesions resembled granuloma annulare, with dome-shaped papules arranged in circles (fig. 1, inset). The patient had scoliosis but denied further comorbidities. There was no family history of similar lesions. The skin biopsy specimen exhibited normal epidermis and numerous dilated vascular spaces within the dermis. The thin endothelial cells of the vascular wall were surrounded by one or multiple layers of cuboidal cells with broad eosinophilic cytoplasm and round, uniform nuclei (fig. 2). The findings were consistent with glomangioma, which was further supported by strong immunoreactivity of tumour cells with α-smooth muscle actin. Our patient refused treatment and is currently under observation. Glomus tumours, first described by Masson, are uncommon lesions that originate from the glomus body. They are clinically classified as solitary or multiple, and the former represents 90% of all cases. Solitary glomus tumours most commonly occur on the nail bed, with female preponderance and an average onset age of 40, whereas multiple glomus tumours are more common in childhood, with male predominance. In addition to difference in number, sexual predilection and age of onset, multiple glomus tumours tend to be less painful and are mostly found on the trunk.
