Is it age or HIV that drives the regulatory T-cells expansion that occurs in older HIV-infected persons?
A recent cross-sectional analysis by Tenorio et al [1] showed an increase in total regulatory T-cells (Treg) in older HIV-infected persons, as normally occurs in aging healthy subjects [2–4]. Although the authors concluded that an agerelated increase in Treg might contribute to the accelerated disease progression seen in elderly HIV-infected persons, a possible effect of HIV on age-related changes in Treg was not excluded. In Tenorio's study, HAART-untreated patients with elevated viral load levels were included, and Treg cells were characterized by the CD4+CD25+CD127lo phenotype. There are several problems with this. As have recently reported [5], this phenotype may be severely compromised by the elevated numbers of activated non-regulatory T-cells in viremic HIV-infected subjects. The use of this phenotype could also account for the absence of a relationship between Treg and cellular and soluble markers of immune activation in the cited report. Second, as authors discussed, they could not exclude that the longer duration of HIV infection in older individuals contributed to the increased Treg frequency found in this group. Third, CD4 T-cell numbers and percentages were significantly lower in older versus younger HIVinfected subjects and it is well known that Treg frequency increases as CD4 T-cell counts fall [6,7]. Such biases are enough to keep open the question about the effect of age on Treg compartment along aging in HIV-infected subjects. Our data suggest that HIV progression, not aging, is likely directly driving the Treg expansion found in aged HIV-infected subjects due to viral effects on T-cell subsets during the time of viral exposure. We have carried out a cross-sectional study including differently aged HIV-groups but with similar duration of HIV-exposure to address this issue. Additionally, in our study, the CD4+CD25hiFoxP3+ phenotype was used to identify Treg, and differences in CD4 T-cell counts were taken into account in the analysis. Hence, we have included HIV-vertically infected children with 15.5 years of age [10.7–18.0] and HIV-infected adults with 44 years of age [39.5–49.0] but 16.0 years from diagnose [9.0–17.0].
