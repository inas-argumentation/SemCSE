Atypical Imaging Findings in an Immunocompetent Patient. Primary central nervous system lymphoma.
A previously healthy woman in her 50s presented with complaints of increasing forgetfulness, fatigue, and depressed mood after dealing with the deaths of several close relatives. She was diagnosed with depression and began treatment with antidepressant medication. After 8 months, she had worsening complaints. At that time, a family member reported that her memory problems had progressed. She was no longer able to find misplaced objects, remember appointments, or handle financial affairs. She was often subdued, her mood depressed, and her sleeping time increased. Clinical examination at that time revealed a flattened affect and a 22 of 30 Mini-Mental State Examination score. Findings were otherwise normal, and there were no focal neurologic deficits. Basic laboratory workup results were unremarkable. She was referred to a neurologist, who ordered contrast-enhanced magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the brain.
