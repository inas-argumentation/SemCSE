Zafirlukast for Chronic Asthma: Convenient and Generally Safe, But is it Effective?
IN THISISSUE, our colleague, Judy Kelloway, t reviews the English-language medical literature on zafirlukast, the first leukotriene-receptor antagonist approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for daily use to suppress the signs and symptoms of chronic asthma (Le., "a long-term control medication"). There are no data supporting its use to treat acute exacerbationsof asthma (i.e., a "quick-relief medication") and, therefore, it is not indicatedfor this clinical circumstance. Kelloway's review is comprehensive, scholarly, and accurate. However, the resultsof studies that she presents for patients with chronic asthma indicate that this new agent has limited efficacy. On the other hand, her summary of adverse effects indicates that zafirlukast has a wider margin of safety than does zileuton, anotherrecentlyapproved leukotriene modifier that acts by a different mechanism (i.e., inhibition of leukotriene biosynthesis rather than receptor antagonismj.' Zafirlukast is likely to be more convenient and less expensive than zileuton since it requires only twice-daily oral administration (zileuton is approved for qid dosing)and, unlikezileuton,' does not requiremultiple measurements of liver enzymes during the first year of therapy.
