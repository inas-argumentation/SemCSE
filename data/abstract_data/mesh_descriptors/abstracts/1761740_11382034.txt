Guillain-Barré syndrome after failed pelvic fracture fixation.
Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS) is a symmetric, rapidly progressive polyradiculopathy of unknown cause that is most often preceded by a clinical stressor, such as a bacterial or viral infection,1 vaccination,2 or malignancy.3 The syndrome is rare with an annual incidence of 1–3 per 100,000 persons.4 A small group of cases have been reported to occur after surgery, specifically after abdominal,5 cardiac,6 solid organ transplant,7,8 intracranial,9 or dental procedures.9 However, only four cases in the literature have occurred after orthopedic procedures, with three after surgeries on the spine9–11 and one after a “hip nailing.”9 We present a unique case of GBS after fixation failure for an unstable pelvic ring fracture.
