Modulation of sulfation and glucuronidation of 1-naphthol in isolated rat liver cells
Factors affecting sulfation and glucuronidation of 1-naphthol have been studied in isolated liver cells:(1)At low substrate concentrations rates of sulfation and glucuronidation are similar, whereas at high substrate concentrations glucuronidation slightly exceeds that of sulfate conjugation. The kinetic constants for sulfation and glucuronidation of 1-naphthol are: Km=30±8.8 μM, Vmax=0.43±0.12 nmol/mg prot. x min and Km=57±11 μM, Vmax=0.56±0.18 nmol/mg prot. x min, respectively. Both conjugation reactions can be affected selectively:(2)Omission of sulfate from the medium decreases sulfate conjugation to about 24% of the control. Cysteine (4 mM) compensates the decrease and increases rates of sulfation by about 30% when inorganic sulfate is present in the medium. Rates of sulfation can be even more stimulated by p-nitrophenylsulfate which serves as sulfate donator. Addition of 4 mM p-nitrophenylsulfate increases the conjugation reaction about 4-fold in the presence of sulfate in the medium.(3)Glucuronidation is decreased by D-galactosamine (3 mM) to about 14% of the control, while sulfation remains constant.(4)Fasting of rats for 48 h before preparation of liver cells with a glucose-free medium mainly decreases glucuronidation. Addition of glucose to the medium partially restores the capacity to form glucuronide conjugates.
