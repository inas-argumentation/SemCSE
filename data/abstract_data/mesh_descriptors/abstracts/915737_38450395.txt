Nontoxemic hypertension in pregnancy in the African indigene. An analysis of 30 cases.
Thirty indigenous Africans with nontoxemic hypertension occurring in pregnancy are reported upon. In 23 of these the etiology of the hypertension was essential and in 7 it was related to chronic renal disease. Six patients showed evidence of urinary infection during pregnancy. A total of 17 premature deliveries were recorded, including 2 stillbirths. No significant fetal abnormality was detected. There was no maternal death. A substantial proportion of these patients remained hypertensive 6 weeks post partum. The role of antihypertensive agents in this condition is reviewed.
