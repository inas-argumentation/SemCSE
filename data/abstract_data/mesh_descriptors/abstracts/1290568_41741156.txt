A more systematic review of correlates of indoor tanning
Editor We read with interest Schneider and Krämer’s systematic review of 16 studies of indoor tanning correlates. Their study may have important implications for health policy, public health practice and research aimed at decreasing the prevalence of this healthdamaging behaviour. However, we have serious concerns about the quality of the review. We conducted a systematic literature review using the same publication time frame (1 January 2000, to 12 August 2008) and study inclusion criteria as Schneider and Krämer and identified 18 additional articles that presented data on indoor tanning correlates. Our search strategy included subject heading and key term searches (e.g., indoor tanning, sunbed*, tanning bed*, tanning booth*, tanning salon*, solarium*, solaria, sunlamp*, artificial tanning, UV tanning) in multiple databases (e.g. PubMed, PsycInfo, Google Scholar and Web of Science), examination of reference lists in identified articles and cited reference searches of identified articles. The literature search conducted by Schneider and Krämer was seemingly too restrictive, resulting in identification of only 48 articles, 16 of which were deemed relevant for the review. The 18 additional articles that we identified contribute substantially to the evidence base regarding correlates of indoor tanning. As shown in Tables 1 and 2, we reproduced Schneider and Krämer’s tables of correlates of indoor tanning with the addition of the results from these 18 articles (see bold text). Three of the additional articles focused on datasets already represented in the articles identified by Schneider and Krämer, but they provide data regarding several unique correlates. We also identified many necessary corrections for the 16 articles included in their review (see italicized text in Tables 1 and 2). We were not able to determine the approach that Schneider and Krämer used for coding correlates from articles that reported univariate and multivariate results. When available, we focused primarily on results of multivariate analyses (e.g. multiple logistic regressions). Space limitations preclude a detailed discussion of how the 18 additional articles and the many corrections for the 16 original articles alter the results of the original review.
