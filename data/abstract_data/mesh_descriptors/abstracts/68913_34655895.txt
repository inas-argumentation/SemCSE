INFLUENCE OF BINDING ON THE PHARMACOLOGIC ACTIVITY OF ANTIBIOTICS
Antibiotics are now known to bind variously to constituents of blood, cell membranes, and intracellular proteins. As pointed out in previous presentations in this symposium, binding may have profound effects on serum concentration, distribution in the body, elimination rates, penetration into tissues, appearance in areas of inflammation, and biologic activity. Antibiotics are diverse chemicals and must be considered individually. Small variations in charge or chemical configuration may exert profound changes, so that generalizations should not be made, even within a class of drugs. Indeed, many analogs owe their differences in pharmacokinetic behavior to alteration in binding. This report wil briefly review the effect of different forms of binding on the pharmacokinetics and biologic activity of antibiotics. Four principle types of binding will be considered: 1. serum proteins, principally albumin; 2. cell membranes; 3. intracellular hepatic proteins; and 4. insoluble intracellular substances.
