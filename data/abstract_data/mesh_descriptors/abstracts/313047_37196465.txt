Autopsy diagnosis of progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy with JC virus-negative CSF after cord blood stem-cell transplantation.
A 38-year-old mother of two young children presented with 6 weeks of odontogenic infections and leukocytosis, with WBCs exceeding 2 10/ L. A bone marrow biopsy disclosed a hypercellular marrow (95% blasts; rare Auer rods; CD33, 117, 34, and myeloperoxidase positive, but negative for CD13, 14, and HLA-DR), consistent with acute myeloid leukemia without maturation. Cytogenetics revealed hyperdiploidy, with five metaphases containing 50,XX, 11, 11, 12, 14, three metaphases with 50,XX, 8, 11, 11, 12 and three metaphases with 46XX. She achieved complete remission following 3 days of idarubicin with 7 days of continuous infusion cytarabine. She then received one cycle of high-dose cytarabine consolidation chemotherapy. Due to adverse cytogenetics, allogeneic stem-cell transplantation was considered but no suitable match could be identified. Therefore, transplantation was carried out using two cord blood units according to a clinical trial protocol. Myeloablative conditioning included total-body irradiation, cyclophosphamide, and fludarabine. Graft-versus-host-disease (GVHD) prophylaxis consisted of tacrolimus and mycophenolate mofetil. After transplantation, an episode of hyperacute GVHD of the skin was successfully treated with corticosteroids. Within 4 months of transplantation, mycophenolate was tapered off, but the patient remained on tacrolimus for persistent GVHD of the skin. For the next 10 months, she had relapsing and remitting GVHD of the skin and developed grade 1 GVHD of the upper and lower gastrointestinal tract requiring intermittent use of corticosteroids. Her engraftment was limited, and her platelets remained 20,000 to 30,000/ L throughout much of this time. Her hemoglobin was 8 to 10 gm/dL and her WBC was 6,000 to 10,000/uL. She had a persistently elevated lactate dehydrogenase of 250 to 350 U/L with rare schistocytes on her peripheral blood smear consistent with a low-level microangiopathic hemolytic anemia attributed to tacrolimus. Around 300 days after transplantation, she complained of gradually worsening bilateral, proximal arm and leg weakness. On day 325 after transplantation, she was admitted to the hospital because of worsening proximal limb weakness and new right leg weakness resulting in two falls. No changes in memory, personality, vision, speech, sensation, or bowel or bladder function were noted. There was no fever or loss of consciousness. On examination, she was afebrile, her blood pressure was 125/65 mmHg, and she was fully alert and oriented, with fluent speech. Apart from mild right facial weakness, the cranial nerves were otherwise unremarkable. Motor examination showed pronator drift of the right arm and decreased speed on finger tapping. There was generalized muscle atrophy, and power was decreased symmetrically in the arms with proximal greater than distal weakness. The right leg (3 of 5 on the Medical Research Council scale) was significantly weaker than the left (4 of 5). Over her 4-week hospital stay, weakness in the right leg progressed, and she also developed weakness of the right arm. Reflexes were symmetric 1 throughout; the right toe was extensor. Brain computed tomography and angiography showed a small subarachnoid hemorrhage without evidence of intracerebral aneurysm. Brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) with gadolinium showed a nonenhancing flair hyperintensity in the left medial precentral gyrus, shown in Figures 1A (flair) and 1B (T1 postcontrast, nonenhancing). Whole-spine MRI was unremarkable. A lumbar puncture showed glucose, 87 mg/dL; protein, 42 mg/dL; RBC, 552/mL; and WBC, 0/mL. CSF fungal culture and microbial cultures and CSF polymerase chain reaction (PCR) for cytomegalovirus, John Cunningham virus (JCV) and Epstein-Barr virus were negative. Electromyography was consistent with a steroid myopathy. Steroids were tapered but had to be increased when GI GVHD worsened. Despite treatment with anti-infectives, fresh-frozen plasma, and mechanical ventilation, her clinical status deteriorated, and she died 1 year after bone marrow transplantation. A full autopsy was performed, which confirmed pneumonia. Gross examination of the cut surface of the cerebral hemispheres showed multiple small foci of softening and dusky gray discoloration in the white matter and at the gray-white junction, the largest lesion was on the left, corresponding to the location seen on MRI and marked by the white arrow in Figure 1C. Histological sections with luxol fast and hematoxylin and eosin stains showed multiple foci of myelin loss with blurred margins (Fig 1D). The lesions contained large atypical astrocytes as well as glial cells with enlarged nuclei and viral inclusions. Immunostaining for JCV showed scattered immunopositive nuclei (panel D, inset).
