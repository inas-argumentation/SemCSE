CD14+ regulatory dendritic cells in patients with hepatocellular carcinoma and cirrhosis
We read with great interest the article by Han et al., who described a population of peripheral CD11bCD11c dendritic cells (DCs), termed CD14 DCs, that appeared to be specifically found in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) patients, but not from healthy donors. CD11bCD11c DCs have been shown to induce regulatory T cells and secrete interleukin (IL)-10 and transforming growth factor beta in autoimmune disease models. BDCA1 myeloid DCs with a similar surface phenotype may have immunoregulatory functions in chronic viral hepatitis (CVH). Cirrhosis has an important impact on CD14 monocyte frequency and phenotype, but non-HCC-bearing patients with cirrhosis were not included as a control group in this study. To confirm the study’s findings and determine any association of cirrhosis with the described phenotype, we recruited age-matched patients with cirrhosis (CIR; n 5 20), HCC patients (n 5 10), and healthy donors (HD; n 5 7) and assessed the distribution of CD14 DCs (CD11bCD11cHLA-DRCD14 cells) by flow cytometry.
