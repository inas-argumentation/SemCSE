Some studies on γ-irradiated DNA as primer in RNA synthesis
Abstract 1. The enzymatic RNA synthesis primed by γ-irradiated DNA from different sources (calf thymus, bacteria) has been studied. 2. There is an appreciable decrease in the rate of the RNA synthesis with increasing dose of radiation; when the reciprocal of the relative priming activity of the irradiated DNA is plotted against dose, straight line graphs are obtained. This is in accord with the proposed model, viz. that γ-radiation creates new binding sites for the polymerase, which sites, however, are not effective in the enzymatic synthesis. 3. Of the known effects of radiation on DNA, the breaking of the back bone is apparently by far the most effective in decreasing the RNA synthesis; attack on the bases leads to relatively much smaller changes, but does bring about some alteration in the base ratios and in the nearest-neighbour distribution of the newly synthesized RNA.
