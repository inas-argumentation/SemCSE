Detection of common mutations in sporadic primary localized cutaneous amyloidosis by DNA mass spectrometry
DEAR EDITOR, Primary localized cutaneous amyloidosis [PLCA (OMIM 105250)] is a relatively common itchy skin disorder in South America and Southeast Asia. The precise pathogenesis of PLCA is unclear, but it is considered to be multifactorial, involving both genetic and environmental factors. Most cases of PLCA are sporadic. In Southeast Asia, familial PLCA is also prevalent. Genome-wide linkage analyses have revealed that the disease susceptibility loci of a significant portion of collected pedigrees are mapped to chromosome 5p13.1–q11.2. Subsequent investigation has identified missense mutations in the oncostatin M receptor (OSMR) or interleukin-31 receptor A (IL31RA) genes. Dillon et al. demonstrated that the proteins encoded by OSMR and IL31RA genes formed the heterodimeric receptor through which IL-31 signals. Given the strong penetrance of OSMR and IL31RA mutations causing PLCA, the development of a PLCA mutation screening panel is critical to serve as a molecular diagnostic tool for clinical use. In this study, we developed a screening panel covering all known PLCA-causing mutations using DNA mass spectrometry. Archival skin biopsy specimens from 360 sporadic patients with PLCA who underwent biopsy between 1979 and 1999 were recruited from our hospital. The Puregene DNA purification kit (Gentra Systems, Minneapolis, MN, U.S.A.) and QIAamp DNA formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany) were used to extract DNA from blood and skin samples, respectively. Polymerase chain reaction and extension primers were designed by using the MassArray Assay Design 3.1 software (Sequenom, San Diego, CA, U.S.A.) and applying default single-base extension settings and default parameters. Mutation alleles were designed manually to have lower mass than those of reference alleles through either forward or reverse extension direction. Resulting primer designs were run through BLAST and necessary modification was made to avoid pseudogene amplification. The mass spectrum from time-resolved spectra was retrieved by using a MassARRAY mass spectrometer (Sequenom), and each spectrum was then analysed using the SpectroTYPER software (Sequenom) to perform the single-nucleotide polymorphism genotype and mutation calling. A total of 360 sporadic patients with PLCA (231 men and 129 women) were recruited to the study (mean SD age of onset 45 3 15 9 years; range 12–78 years). Clinical phenotypes included lichen amyloidosis (LA, n = 211), macular amyloidosis (MA, n = 59), and biphasic amyloidosis (BA, n = 90). We did not include samples of nodular PLCA, because its pathogenesis is different from LA and MA.
