Autopsies on bodies without antemortem risk factors for HCV, HBV and HIV infections: are they safe?
Sir, It is well known that dissecting rooms and forensic laboratories are high-risk areas for infection, and forensic staff are at risk of occupational exposure to infectious agents, especially during postmortem investigations. Spread of infection from bodies to workers may be airborne, through contact routes or from a needle or sharp instrument injury. 4 Many forensic situations involve drug abusers and homosexuals, where the statistical risk of human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) and hepatitis B and C (HBV and HCV) infection is markedly greater than in the general autopsy population. HIV, HBV and HCV are major sources of worldwide public health concern because they share similar modes of transmission and are relatively frequent among certain high-risk groups. Autopsy cases can be divided into ‘high-risk’ and ‘low-risk’ groups according to whether there is a history of antemortem risk of these infections. The purpose of this study was to investigate whether autopsies on bodies presumed to be in the low-risk group are safe. In this regard, we conducted a cross-sectional descriptive study to identify the seroprevalence of HIV, HBV and also HCV in a low-risk forensic autopsy population in Tehran, Iran. The study was carried out from September 2000 to October 2001 on a series of bodies submitted for necropsy at the Tehran Legal Medicine Organization.
