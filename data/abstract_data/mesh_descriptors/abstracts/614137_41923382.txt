Pyogenic variant of primary cutaneous CD30+ anaplastic large‐cell lymphoma: Spontaneous remission in a young adult
Despite its excellent prognosis, the pyogenic variant of primary cutaneous CD30-positive anaplastic large-cell lymphoma (PC-ALCL) is frequently characterized by an impressively aggressive clinical picture. As patients are usually very eager to receive treatment, the diagnostic and therapeutic approach poses a particular challenge for the treating physician. A 20-year-old man presented with a fast-growing, ulcerated tumor, measuring approximately 3 cm, at the left alar base (Figure 1 a). The lesion had developed over the preceding two weeks and was not associated with pain or fever. There was tender left submandibular and cervical lymphadenopathy. The patient’s own as well as the family history were unremarkable. Blood tests were within normal limits. There was no serological evidence of HIV, EBV, syphilis, or hepatitis infection. Flow cytometry was negative for atypical T cells in the peripheral blood. A bacterial swab showed massive colonization with Staphylococcus aureus . Magnetic resonance imaging of the skull revealed a lesion at the ipsilateral nasal ala with marked perilesional edema extending to the infraorbital and mandibular region. In addition, enlarged reactive lymph nodes were noted in the left cervical and submandibular regions. Computed tomography of the trunk, magnetic resonance imaging of the neck as well as a chest X-ray and ultrasound of the abdomen were unremarkable. Histology revealed a deep, necrotizing lesion marked by granulocytic infi ltration extending to the bottom of the specimen. There was also a diffuse, predominantly perivascular, deep pleomorphic infi ltrate made up of medium-sized blast cells, histiocytes, and a few eosinophils (Figures 2, 3 ). Bone marrow cytology showed no pathological fi ndings. On immunohistochemistry, the majority of cells expressed CD3, with approximately 75 % of the infi ltrate also expressing CD30 (Figures 4, 5 ). There was no co-expression of ALK-1 or CD8; neither was there co-expression of the B-cell transcription factor PAX5. Only a few cells were CD20-positive. Clonality analysis revealed one clonal T-cell population (BIOMED-2 protocol). Due to the purulent clinical picture and bacterial colonization, we started the patient on systemic cefuroxime.
