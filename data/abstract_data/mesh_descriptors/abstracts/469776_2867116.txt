Eaton-Lambert Syndrome and Small Cell Lung Cancer
During the past several months, two patients seen on the Neurology Service ultimately were diagnosed as having Eaton-Lambert syndrome. After an extended search, a small cell carcinoma of the lung was discovered in the first case. The pathogenesis of the syndrome remains undiscovered in the second. The efficacy of treatment for small cell cancer of the lung is highly dependent on the stage of disease. As many as 6% of patients who have small cell cancers present with EatonLambert syndrome. Of these, neurologic manifestations occur in about 30% before clinical evidence of malignancy, offering an opportunity for therapy of preclinical cancer. In these two clinical cases, we performed decision analyses to examine the question: should we take advantage of this &dquo;early clue&dquo; and empirically treat all of these patients with chemotherapy? This Clinical Decision Conference demonstrates how recursive tree structure can be used to model the time course of clinical events. It also demonstrates how sensitivity analysis can be used to bound the results of new therapeutic strategies for which no objective data are availablein this case, the aggressive treatment of preclinical cancer.-SGP
