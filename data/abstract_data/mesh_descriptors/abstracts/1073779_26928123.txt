Regulation of the Biotin Operon in E. coli
The discovery of recombination in Escherichia coli K-12 by Lederberg and Tatum’ was based on their ability to select rare prototrophic recombinants from mixtures of auxotrophs previously produced by Gray and Taturn.* Using x-rays, Tatum expected that mutations would be induced in bacterial strains as in Neurospora, giving rise to growth-factor deficiencies. The first such mutant isolated was E. coli-58, a biotin requirer. Additional markers were subsequently added to this mutant for the recombination studies. Since then many biotin mutants have been isolated to help establish the biotin biosynthetic pathway and its regulation, making the biotin operon the most extensively studied among the vitamins. Some of the unique features of this operon will be pointed up in this report.
