Reticuloendothelial and hepatic functional alterations following lead acetate administration.
Abstract The influence of a single intravenous injection of lead acetate on the phagocytic activity of the reticuloendothelial system (RES) and on endotoxin-detoxifying ability of liver and spleen homogenates was studied in an effort to delineate the mechanism by which lead enhanced host sensitivity to endotoxin. Lead acetate administered to rats induced a state of phagocytic depression as indicated by an impaired intravascular phagocytosis of colloidal carbon. The employment of the 131I-triolein-labeled lipid emulsion to determine phagocytic alterations in leadtreated animals indicated a lead-induced impairment in hepatic phagocytosis. Liver and spleen homogenates prepared from normal or control rats possessed significant endotoxin-detoxifying ability as bioassayed in actinomycin D-treated mice. Lead acetate significantly reduced the ability of liver and spleen to detoxify endotoxin. Hepatic parenchymal cell function was also deranged by lead administration as indicated by an increased plasma retention of bromsulphalein and an elevated unconjugated bilirubin, serum alkaline phosphatase and glutamic oxaloacetic transaminase. These results demonstrate that while lead acetate induces endotoxin hyperreactivity by impairing the phagocytic as well as the endotoxin-detoxifying properties of macrophages, hepatic parenchymal cell dysfunction may also be a contributing factor. These findings accent the importance of hepatic functional status in host defense against endotoxin as well as the ability of lead to induce acute hepatic injury.
