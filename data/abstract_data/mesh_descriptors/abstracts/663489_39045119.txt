Quantitative cytological assessment of a persistent oral lesion that underwent malignant transformation: a case report
Oral cancer accounts for less than 5% of all malignancies in the UK but in recent years, the incidence of carcinoma within the mucosa of the mouth has been increasing, especially in developing countries. Although oral squamous cell carcinoma is relatively rare, it has a poor 5-year survival when compared with other forms of cancer and therefore represents an important condition. To date, detection of a lesion when small is the sole factor that has been shown to in ̄uence 5-year survival. The majority (90±95%) of oral cancers arise from surface epithelium and therefore there is an opportunity for detection of developing lesions while still small. DNA measurements, using image (static) cytometry, have been applied extensively in the past in an attempt to dierentiate between normal and neoplastic cell growth. Image cytometry can be used to measure the integrated optical density (IOD) of Feulgen-stained nuclei in smears. The IOD of Feulgen-stained nuclei is believed to be proportional to the amount of DNA present within the nuclei. DNA patterns in aneuploid tumours may re ̄ect a high degree of genomic instability in a tumour cell population. Chromosomal abnormalities have been observed using cytophotometric techniques, and these were usually associated with an abnormal DNA content. The present report describes a patient who had persistent oral lesions, one of which was found to be a multifocal squamous cell carcinoma.
