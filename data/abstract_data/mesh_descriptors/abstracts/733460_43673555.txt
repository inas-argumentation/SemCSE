Biweekly gemcitabine therapy induces complete remission in cutaneous angiosarcoma resistant to multiple anticancer drugs
Dear Editor, The prognosis of cutaneous angiosarcoma is very poor compared with that of other skin malignancies. Anticancer drugs with efficacy against angiosarcoma are mainly paclitaxel (PTX) and docetaxel (DOC). However, there are few chemotherapies for advanced angiosarcoma resistant to taxanes. Recently, weekly gemcitabine (GEM) monotherapy was effective in a past report. We herein reported that biweekly GEM monotherapy resulted in complete remission (CR) in our patient with advanced angiosarcoma resistant to multiple drugs. There is no previous study on the efficacy of biweekly GEM monotherapy in angiosarcoma patients. A 60-year-old woman was diagnosed as having scalp angiosarcoma without lymph node or organ metastasis. The patient received four cycles of tri-weekly PTX chemotherapy and radiation. At that point, a radical operation was not performed because the involved area was broad. Therefore, tri-weekly PTX therapy was resumed. However, after five cycles of PTX re-administration, ultrasonography (US) showed metastasis of the left accessory nerve and superior internal jugular lymph node. As a second-line treatment, monthly S-1 and DOC (S-1/DOC) therapy was administrated according to our previous report. Five months after the initiation S-1/DOC therapy, US revealed metastasis of a parotid lymph node. Although, as a third-line therapy, oral pazopanib therapy (400 mg/day, the dose was reduced because of thrombocytopenia and liver damage [grade 3, analyzed using the National Cancer Institute’s Common Terminology Criteria version 4.0.]) was performed for 2 months, pazopanib had no efficacy because of disturbance of the mouth opening and positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) revealed progression of cervical lymph node metastases (Fig. 1a). Thus, we designed weekly GEM monotherapy (1000 mg, 700 mg/m). Compared with the past report, the dose of this regimen was reduced because severe adverse events were expected. However, because she developed grade 3 neutropenia after 1 week, we undertook additional dose reduction (800 mg) and changed to biweekly administration. Other adverse events (all were only grade 1) included malaise and influenza-like symptoms. Two months later, the disturbance of the mouth opening disappeared and PET/CT showed CR (Fig. 1b). However, after administration of biweekly GEM therapy for 10 months, she developed purpura on the posterior region of the neck and histopathological examination showed relapsed angiosarcoma. Currently, she is being treated with palliative radiation therapy for recurrent lesion. To our knowledge, this study is the first report of the dramatic efficacy of biweekly GEM monotherapy in angiosarcoma resistant to multiple drugs. Although we first designed weekly GEM monotherapy, this regimen was discontinued because of severe neutropenia, after which we decided that the interval of administration should be 2 weeks. As a result, biweekly GEM monotherapy resulted in CR. Median progression-free survival of weekly GEM monotherapy was 7.0 months in 25 taxaneexposed cutaneous angiosarcoma patients. However, there was no previous report of biweekly GEM monotherapy for angiosarcoma. Taken together, our report indicates that clinical benefit could be obtained in spite of a longer treatment interval for patients with advanced angiosarcoma. However, larger studies of biweekly GEM therapy are needed to verify its efficacy and safety in the future.
