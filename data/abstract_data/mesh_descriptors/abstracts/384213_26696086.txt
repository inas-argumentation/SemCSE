A novel way to save time when performing Mohs micrographic surgery on thick tissue.
When performing Mohs micrographic surgery (MMS), tissue is initially incised by beveling the scalpel blade at a 451 angle. The beveling is increased until a horizontal excisional plane is achieved at the deep margin of the specimen, permitting the surgeon to harvest a thin, round, saucerized piece of tissue. Producing a thin specimen with beveled edges facilitates horizontal frozen section processing, because the histotechnician must invert and compress the tissue so that the outer epidermal edge (peripheral margin) is pulled down to the same plane as the base of the specimen.
