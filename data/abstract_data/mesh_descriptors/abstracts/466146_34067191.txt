A boy with therapy-resistant asthma
A 10-year-old boy was referred to the department of paediatric pulmonology at the Beatrix Children's Hospital because of therapy-resistant asthma. He had episodes of dyspnoea, wheeze and cough after exercise and viral respiratory tract infections from the age of 5 years onwards. Several ®rstand seconddegree family members had asthma or allergies. An elevated total serum IgE level (303 IU/l) with positive radio-allergosorbent test to house dust mite (class 3) had been found at age 5 years. Spirometry, performed repeatedly from the age of 6 years onwards, showed forced expiratory volume (FEV1) varying between 70% and 90% of predicted values and FEV1/vital capacity (VC) ratios varying between 60% and 75%. He had been treated with inhaled corticosteroids (up to 800 lg daily) for 4 years, and with inhaled salmeterol for 1 year, without any clear improvement of his symptoms or spirometry results. Detailed history was unremarkable apart from a prolonged period of malaise when he was 3 years of age, in the diagnostic work-up of which a chest X-ray had been made (Fig. 1).
