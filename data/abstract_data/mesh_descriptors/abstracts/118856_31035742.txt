Mitotic cycles in estrogen-treated mice: a radioautographic study.
Abstract A radioautographic study using 3H-thymidine has been undertaken to investigate the difference between the stimulatory effect of estrogens on cell division in target and nonspecific tissues. Castrated female mice (CBA strain) were used. The uterine epithelium was selected as a target tissue, the corneal epithelium as a nonspecific one. An increase in the number of mitoses occurred in both these tissues after two injections of 2.5 μg estrone. In the uterine epithelium the mitotic index increased by a factor of 4.5, but in the corneal epithelium by a factor of 1.4 only. The generation time of the uterine epithelial cells reduced after the administration of estrone from 42 to 26 hr, i.e., approximately 1.5 times at the expense of S and G1 periods. At the same time a 3-fold increase could be observed in the number of cells capable to synthesize DNA: the proliferating pool of the uterine epithelium was 8.3 per cent in castrated and 27.9 per cent in estrogen-treated animals. In the corneal epithelium of treated mice an increase in the number of cells synthesizing DNA also occurred but the proliferating pool remained unchanged. The data obtained demonstrate that the effects of estrone on cell division in target and nonspecific tissues accomplish in different ways. Possible sites of estrone action in the mitotic cycle are discussed.
