PATTERNS OF MESSENGER RNA SYNTHESIS IN MAMMALIAN TISSUES: ANALYSIS BY COUNTER-CURRENT DISTRIBUTION.
Extraction with 4-ammosalicylate-phenol-8-hydroxyquinoline was found to give roughly quantitative yields of rapidly labelled M-RNA (associated with DNA) from rat liver and from mouse liver, spleen and kidney. M-RNA's thus prepared have been characterized by sucrose density-gradient centrifugation and fractionated by counter-current distribution. On counter-current distribution, M-RNA behaved differently from DNA and from S-RNA and R-RNA, permitting a degree of separation. Counter-current distribution patterns of rat liver M-RNA were found to depend to some extent on diet but were reproducible under standard dietary conditions. Preparations of mouse liver, spleen and kidney M-RNA's made from the same animals after a single [3H]uridine pulse showed some similar and some different counter-current distribution peaks. M-RNA's from mouse thymus pulsed in vivo and in vitro showed qualitative similarities but quantitative differences in counter-current distribution peaks. The presence of small amounts of complexes between M-RNA and DNA or DNA-protein has been demonstrated by caesium chloride density-gradient centrifugation and counter-current distribution.
