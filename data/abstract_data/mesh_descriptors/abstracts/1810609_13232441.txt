Umbilical piercing leading to presentation of ovarian cancer.
Correspondence to: Dr Paul Sanghera psangy@hotmail.com A 43-year-old woman presented to her general practitioner after piercing of her umbilicus 2 weeks previously. She complained of abdominal pain and had a discharge from the pierced umbilicus. She had no other symptoms and no past medical history. On examination she had a tender, discrete discharging mass in the umbilical region but not other clinical abnormalities. She was thought to have developed an abscess after the piercing and was referred to the local hospital for excision and drainage. Before the procedure, a CT scan of the abdomen and pelvis was done to ascertain the extent of the mass. The CT scan confirmed a soft-tissue opacity associated with the piercing (figure A). Although the opacity seemed to be localised, with no contact with the peritoneum, the scan also identified a large complex cystic mass in the pelvis (figure B). The patient then had a laparotomy, which showed bilateral ovarian masses, peritoneal nodules, and extensive pelvic and abdominal disease. Several biopsies confirmed moderately differentiated adenocarcinoma consistent with an ovarian primary. The umbilical mass was thought to be a localised abscess and was excised and drained. Histological analysis showed that the umbilical mass was a tumour deposit. Adenocarcinoma cells had infiltrated the skin around the piercing, leading to the presentation and subsequent diagnosis. After this operation she received six cycles of carboplatin (area under curve=5, every 3 weeks) and 175 mg/m paclitaxel. Unfortunately, despite an initial response she relapsed soon after the sixth cycle, deteriorating rapidly and dying from disease within 12 months of the original presentation.
