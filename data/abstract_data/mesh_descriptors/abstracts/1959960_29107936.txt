To our knowledge, this is the first study which uses both SC and autologous serum as negative controls. In the study of Asero et al., an SC test was not performed with the other tests (APST, ASST, saline, histamine). Instead, a test using SC at a concentration of 0Æ125 mol L was performed separately in 10 patients. We think that this is not sufficient to evaluate the results and that there is no any other study including SC and ASST as negative controls. The percentage of test positivity may show differences between studies resulting from patient selection, differences in methodology, interpretation of response, disease activity, skill in performing the test, etc. However, we suggest that evaluation of the results of negative control tests, (saline, SC and ASST) while performing APST is a scientific and reasonable practice. As both plasma and serum contain autoantibodies and HRFs, APST should be positive when ASST is positive. We agree on this subject with Asero et al. APST should be regarded as positive when other controls (ASST, SC and saline) are negative and histamine is positive. Thus, there are no APST-negative ⁄ASST-positive patients with CU when the tests are evaluated on the basis of the modified criteria used in our study. Two points should be emphasized: the frequency of positive ASST responses of healthy controls in published studies ranges from 0% to 52Æ5%. In our study, ASST positivity was also high (34%) in healthy controls. In addition, one of the exclusion criteria for patients with CU was physical urticaria including dermatographism. In the scope of the scientific data (both autologous serum and plasma contain autoantibodies to IgE and ⁄or to high-affinity IgE receptors), APST and ASST are expected to be positive in some patients with CU. This means that when the ASST is positive, the APST should be positive as well. We believe that APST()) ⁄ASST(+) results need to be explained by the authors. In contrast to the previous report of Asero et al. in which the positivity of ASST was 53% and APST was 86%, Metz et al. found 37Æ5% ASST positivity and an only slightly higher percentage (43%) of APST positivity. Sajedi et al. also reported that 76Æ6% of patients were positive for APST and 65Æ5% were positive for ASST. Finally, as mentioned in our study, the use of the APST for evaluating autoreactivity in clinical practice is not superior to ASST for the time being and further studies should be conducted.
