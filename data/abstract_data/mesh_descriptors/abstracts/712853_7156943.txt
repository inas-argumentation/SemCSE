Rate ofchange: The rate of change of PSA with time (termed velocity in some reports) differs between patients with prostatic carcinoma from those with benign prostate hypertrophy. These differences are useful discriminators in distinguishing between these conditions.! PSA and digital rectal examination (DRE): Since the combination of PSA measurements and DRE is significantly more sensitive and specific for the diagnosis of prostate carcinoma than either technique alone,' PSA should not be used alone. Our experience in following up abnormal PSA levels suggests that the use of PSA alone, as an initial investigation, is widespread. We strongly recommend the following to directors of diagnostic laboratories and to doctors: • Pathology laboratories should state the manufacturer and the assay method used when reporting PSA results; • PSA reports should quote age-related and method-specific reference ranges to allow better interpretation of reports; • Patients found to have abnormally high PSA values should be followed up immediately. If PSA is moderately elevated and there is no clinical evidence of a prostate neoplasia, the rate of change of PSA may be observed over longer periods to determine management options, such as biopsy or imaging procedures; • PSA and DRE should always be used in combination. There is an immediate need in our community for effective undergraduate and postgraduate educational programs on the use of PSA in the investigation of prostate disease.
