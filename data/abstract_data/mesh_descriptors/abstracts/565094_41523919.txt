Animal and clinical research on neuropeptides and schizophrenia.
Publisher Summary This chapter discusses the animal and clinical research on neuropeptides and schizophrenia. Schizophrenia has been investigated in relation to endorphins. Three main hypotheses have been proposed—that include, endorphin excess, endorphin deficiency, and disturbances in β -endorphin fragmentation. The last hypothesis states that a relative deficiency of γ -type endorphins, peptide fragments derived from β -endorphin, may be concerned in the pathogenesis of schizophrenic psychoses. In addition, other neuropeptides especially those related to cholecystokinin (CCK) are discussed in relation to schizophrenic psychoses. The strategy that can be followed in animal research to develop new drugs with antipsychotic action include (1) pharmacological isomorphism, (2) symptom similarity, (3) cross species psychological processes, and (4) induction of the pathological process. These four strategies are discussed in relation to the available information of the purported antipsychotic peptides, γ -type endorphins and the CCK-related peptides.
