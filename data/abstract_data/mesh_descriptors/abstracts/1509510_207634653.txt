The need for spatially standardized methods in clinical applications of diffusion tensor imaging of white matter.
Editor: We read with interest the article (1) by Dr Rollins and colleagues in the June 2009 issue of Radiology. In that study, the authors analyzed diffusion-tensor magnetic resonance (MR) data acquired in children with dyslexia (2–8) and normal-reading controls. They calculated diffusion-tensor metrics by placing regions of interest (ROIs) on the superior longitudinal fasciculus, inferior frontooccipital and inferior longitudinal fasciculi (IFO-ILF), and posterior limb of the internal capsule. The superior longitudinal fasciculus and IFO-ILF are long and large association pathways with a volume that can be 5%–10% of the total volume of white matter in the human brain (9).
