The final diagnosis was EBV-negative monomorphic post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder (PTLD), diffuse large B-cell lymphoma type (DLBCL). Subsequent staging studies demonstrated multifocal disease confined to the left breast and axilla, and a negative bone marrow biopsy. The malignancy was found to be refractory to six rounds of rituximabcyclophosphamide, adriamycin, vincristine, prednisone and other regimens of cytotoxic chemotherapy. The patient underwent multiple rounds of palliative radiation, but died 14 months after the diagnosis of PTLD. Immunosuppressed patients are at high risk for developing both infections and malignancies. Our case highlights the clinical and diagnostic dilemmas of breast masses in this population, thus reinforcing the practice of conservative breast biopsy or cytologic examination of these lesions. Breast lymphoma accounts for 0.1– 0.5% of breast malignancies, and we are aware of only one prior breast PTLD case in the literature (Tsao et al., Mod Pathol 2004;17:125–30). Furthermore, this PTLD is unusual as it arose at a site of multiple prior inflammatory lesions, reminiscent of the recently described chronic inflammation-associated DLBCL. Chronic local inflammation and antigenic stimulation in the setting of immunosuppression may lead to conditions favorable for lymphoid transformation.
