Cation distribution and cardiac jelly in early embryonic heart: a histochemical and electron microscopic study.
The ontogenesis of Na + distribution in young embryonic myocardia of the chick between 34 h and 5 days of development was studied using a histochemical method (pyroantimonate, before and after Ca 2+ chelation) in combination with electron microscopy. The results confirm that the Na + content of the mucopolysaccharide cardiac jelly complex is very high compared to that in the myocardial cells, and to that in the blood plasma at 48 h. The cardiac jelly Na + content not only decreases rapidly, but is also diluted by proliferating myocardial cells, so that by 3.5 to 4 days it probably contributes little to the Na + content of the heart. Cytoplasmic matrix Na + content is very low at 34 h and increases gradually to a level at 3.5 and 5 days which is greater than that at 12 days. Nuclear Na + content is relatively low at 34 h and increases to reach the usual relatively high levels by 3.5 days. The mitochondrial Ca 2+ , and possibly Mg 2+ , content is very high between inner and outer membranes at 34 h. It decreases rapidly and becomes almost negligible at 3.5 days. Myofibrillar Ca 2+ , and possibly Mg 2+ , content is negligible at 34 h and increases to reach levels found in older hearts by 3.5 days. These changes are discussed in terms of possible biochemical and physiological correlates.
