The effect of certain non-narcotic analgetics on diuresis and liver sulfhydryl concentration in the albino rat.
It was found that bilateral and unilateral hind-leg rubber band tourniquets produced an initial antidiuretic action when applied for two hours to hydrated rats. A much greater effect was produced upon release of the tourniquets. A comparable response was exhibited by formalin injection bilaterally into the hind-leg ankle joints. This antidiuretic response to pain was alleviated by certain non-narcotic analgetics. The toxicity of certain non-narcotic analgetics employed in this investigation appeared to be greatly increased by hydration of the animals. It was found that hind-leg ischemic pain produced a significant diminution in the total nonprotein sulfhydryl (TNPSH) concentration of the liver. This was prevented by treatment with acetylsalicylic acid.
