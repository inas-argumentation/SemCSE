Transplantable breast tumours in rats. Studies on hormone response.
Breast cancers have been induced in female Fisher rats. This strain of animals was less susceptible to a development of tumours than the Sprague-Dawley and the Wistar rats previously studied. The induced breast cancers were originally hormone-responsive and could be transplanted to animals with intact ovarian function. Transplantation of hormone-responsive tumours may result in the development of hormone-unresponsive transplant tumours, whereas the opposite was not observed. It is argued that this change in hormone-response can be explained as a selection phenomenon rather than a transformation of the tumour during the transplant procedure. The observed change in hormone-response was paralleled by a change in tumour morphology.
