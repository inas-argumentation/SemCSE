Paraneoplastic disorders of the memory and cognition: clinical aspects and therapeutic approaches.
Publisher Summary This chapter emphasizes that paraneoplastic disorders (PND) of the brain are becoming increasingly recognized as a cause of higher cortical dysfunction such as, cognition, memory, attention, affection, behavior, disruption of sleep, and level of consciousness. The diagnosis of PND is usually made on the basis of the type of syndrome, demonstration of the tumor, and detection of antineuronal antibodies. The use of CT and FDG-PET imaging has greatly facilitated the detection of the tumor at early stages of the PND. The symptoms may be limited to these cortical functions or develop in association with syndromes of the brainstem, cerebellum, dorsal root ganglia, and peripheral nerves. The identification of immune responses against brain-specific proteins expressed by the tumor suggests that many of these disorders are immune-mediated. These immune responses are characterized by antineuronal antibodies and cytotoxic T-cell responses that probably cause neuronal injury. However, it is unclear whether the immune responses have clinically significant effects on tumor growth or rejection.
