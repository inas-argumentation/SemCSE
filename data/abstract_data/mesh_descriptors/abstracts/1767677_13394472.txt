THE EFFECT OF DIAZOXIDE ON UPTAKE, RELEASE AND METABOLISM OF CATECHOLAMINES
The mechanism by which diazoxide produces hyperglycemia is still not clear. Several investigator^*-^ have postulated that catecholamines are, at least in part, responsible for this effect. Loubatieres6 observed that diazoxide liberates catecholamines from adrenals (epinephrine) and extra-adrenal reserves (norepinephrine). This investigation was initiated to evaluate the role of catecholamines in diazoxide-produced hyperglycemia. C14-labeled dopamine was used to study the effect of diazoxide on tissue uptake, release, and metabolism of this catecholamine, which is the immediate precursor of norepinephrine. Diazoxide was administered to rats (160 mg/kg, intraperitoneally), followed immediately by the intramuscular injection of 8pc/ kg of C14-dopamine.* The analytical procedures employed have already been described.’
