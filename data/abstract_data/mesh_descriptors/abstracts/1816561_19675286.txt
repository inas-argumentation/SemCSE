Dry weight and DNA content in sisters of Bursaria truncatella during the interdivision interval.
1. 1. Dry weight and DNA content of Bursaria truncatella were determined by soft X-ray microradiography and Feulgen spectrophotometry at different known postdivision intervals. 2. 2. The difference in size between sisters immediately after fission is caused by a difference in dry mass and not by a different water content of the cells. 3. 3. The dry weight of the larger posterior animals increases at a nearly constant rate throughout the interdivision interval, while the smaller anterior animals show a lag period of at least 3 hr. After this, dry weight increases in the anterior animals as well. 4. 4. The macronuclei of sisters differ immediately after division in respect to their DNA content, but restoration to an average amount of DNA occurs during the time between the divisions. The G1 period of sisters is different, less than 3 hr in the smaller anterior animals, more than 3 hr in the larger posterior animals. 5. 5. The sisters have approximately the same generation time. 6. 6. The results are discussed with particular reference to the unequal distribution of precursor pools at division.
