The kidney in diabetes: significance of the early abnormalities.
One of the most important questions concerning diabetic nephropathy is the identification of its onset. By the time the clinical diagnosis is madewith the appearance of proteinuria exceeding 0.5 g/24 h, the renal lesions are advanced and irreversible by present therapeutic means. The onset of clinical nephropathy which progresses to end-stage renal failure cannot be identified by the appearance of the glomeruli. Morphological lesions of some degree are present in over 90% of insulin dependent diabetics (Thomsen , 1965), whereas clinical nephropathy develops in only 35-40% (Berch-Johnsen et ai, 1985). At present there is no early morphological appearance which will distingu ish those patients who are at risk from those who are not (Thomsen et al, 1984). Indeed this distinction may never be found and the difference may simply be quantitative; in the end those patients in whom the most nephrons are damaged arc those who will be affected by clinical nephropathy. Identification of patients prone to develop the clinical disease is still a problem, and there is a need to detect the earlier phases of renal malfunction which may be involved in the progression to overt disease . The most extensively studied group is insulin dependent diabetics (IDDs), and early physiological abnormalities of renal function have been found some of which may be relevant to the progression of renal dysfunction . It is therefore important to describe these early functional renal anomalies , recognize their origins and determinants, examine their relationship to diabetes and its control , and analyse their prognostic significance.
