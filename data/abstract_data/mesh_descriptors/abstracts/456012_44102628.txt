Clinical Utility of Bedside Multibeam Optical Coherence Tomography Imaging in a Patient With Multiple Basal Cell Carcinomas.
Optical coherence tomography (OCT) technology allows for real-time invivo imagingof the skin, and it has been demonstrated to be accurate in identifying the diagnostic features of basal cell carcinoma (BCC).When used in conjunction with clinical observation, OCT improved the diagnostic accuracy for BCC to 87.4%, compared with 65.8% for clinical assessment and 76.2% for dermoscopic evaluation. Furthermore, sensitivity and specificity for OCT evaluation of BCC were shown to be approximately 93%and80%, respectively, significantly higher than clinical evaluation or dermoscopy alone. Other studies have shown a reliable correlation betweenOCT and histopathology for lesions of squamous cell carcinoma in situ as well as BCC. In this article, we report a case which illustrates the clinical utility of bedside OCT imaging for the diagnosis and treatment of multiple superficial BCCs.
