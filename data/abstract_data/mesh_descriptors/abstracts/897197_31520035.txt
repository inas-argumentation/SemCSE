Placental oxygen consumption in vitro. V. Hypertensive disorders.
Respirometric examination of placentas obtained from 71 patients with documented toxemia of pregnancy, hypertensive cardiovascular disease, and renal disorders is presented. Pre-eclampsia was found associated with progressively diminishing placental oxygen consumption with increasing severity of clinical syndrome. Placentas from patients with hypertensive cardiovascular disease, on the other hand, utilized somewhat more oxygen than expected. Pre-eclampsia superimposed on hypertensive disease resulted in normal levels of placental metabolic activity. Patients with documented renal disease, and to a lesser extent those with albuminuria, had only moderate elevations of oxygen consumption. The potential significance of these findings is discussed in light of recent studies suggesting that renin production secondary to placental ischemia may be etiologic in toxemia.
