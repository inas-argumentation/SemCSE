Chemotherapy of Advanced Nsclc in the Elderly
Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) may be considered typical of advanced age. More than 50% of lung cancer patients are diagnosed over the age of 65 and about 30% over the age of 70 1• Age-adjusted incidence rates for 1990-1994 reported by the National Cancer Institute Surveillance Epidemiology End Results (SEER) program are 26.7 per 100,000 individuals under the age of 65, whilst the rate grows to 345.9 for people aged 65 years or more. More than two-thirds of patients dying of lung cancer in the United States are over 65 years old-. Elderly patients tolerate chemotherapy poorly, compared to their younger counterpart, because of the progressive reduction of organ function and comorbidities related to age. As a consequence of the above-reported considerations, elderly patients with NSCLC, who frequently suffer from tumor-related symptoms and need some kind of palliative treatment, often receive untested or inadequate treatments':". On the other hand, aggressive treatments are sometimes employed. The medical approach to elderly patients with NSCLC ranges, according to country and institution, from no chemotherapy at all (best supportive care) to a combined more aggressive treatment identical to that used for younger patients.
