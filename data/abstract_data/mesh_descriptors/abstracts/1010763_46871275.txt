School-Based Weight Management Services: Perceptions and Practices of School Nurses and Administrators
Overweight is a major public health problem in the United States. Data from the third National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey, 1988-1991 (NHANES-III) indicate that the prevalence of overweight is increasing in both adults and youth, l-~ Data from NHANES III indicate that the number of severely overweight youth (Body Mass Index (BMI) > 95th percentile) has more than doubled the past decade, from 5% to 11%.2 Roughly 22% of all U.S. school-aged children and adolescents are overweight (BMI 85th percentile).L2 Among adults, overweight is associated with an increased risk for death, coronary heart disease, diabetes mellitus, gallbladder disease, and certain cancers. 4 Childhood and adolescent obesity is associated with an increased risk for overweight as an adult 4’5 and may also have adverse social and economic consequences) -7 Because obesity has been shown to track from late childhood into adulthood and is a risk factor for several chronic diseases, primary prevention and treatment of childhood obesity should be a priority public health goal) Schools can play a vital role in promoting healthful diets, physical activity, and healthy weights. Recently the American Dietetic Association, the Society for Nutrition Education, and the American School Food Service Association published a position statement advocating that comprehensive school-based nutrition programs and services be provided to all the nations’ elementary and secondary students. 8 The purpose of our study was to assess school nurses’ and administrators’ perceptions of obesity, the school’s role in providing weight-management curricula and services, and current practices in these areas.
