Nevirapine use is associated with higher bone mineral density in HIV-I positive patients on long-term antiretroviral therapy.
OBJECTIVES
We assessed bone mineral density (BMD) in a cohort of HIV-positive patients after a median of 11 years of combination antiretroviral therapy and evaluated the respective role of HIV-infection and antiretroviral drugs (ARVs).


METHODS
A cross-sectional study of 162 participants (131 male) from the ANRS-C08 cohort was performed with bone dual energy x-ray absorptiometry (DXA) scans and renal assessment. The window of exposure to ARVs was defined as an exposure of more than six cumulative months during the last 3 years prior to the DXA evaluation to account for a cumulative exposure that could affect bone remodelling. The association with low BMD (Z-score <-2) were assessed by a multiple logistic regression model.


RESULTS
The study population was 50 years (median), HCV (18%) and HBV (8%) coinfection with HIV-RNA <50 c/ml in 89%, median CD4 of 619/mm3. Prevalence of low BMD was 18% in males and 6% in females. The factors associated with a Z-score <-2 in males were uric acid renal loss (aOR: 6.1; 95% CI: 1.2-31.5; p=0.03), HCV co-infection (4.0; 1.3-12.2; p=0.02) and less frequent window of exposure to nevirapine (0.1; 0.02-0.6; p=0.01). For the full study sample, there was a strong positive association between duration of exposure to nevirapine and lumbar spine Z-score (p=0.004).


CONCLUSION
HIV-positive patients exposed to long-term combination antiretroviral therapy have a high incidence of low BMD. Tenofovir disoproxil fumarate and ritonavir-boosted protease inhibitors did not seem to be associated with increased risk of low BMD, while nevirapine exposure appeared to have an independent positive association.
