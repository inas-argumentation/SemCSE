Vismodegib-associated hepatotoxicity: a potential side effect detected in postmarketing surveillance.
To the Editor: The Food and Drug Administration (FDA) recently approved vismodegib, a Hedgehog pathway inhibitor and the first drug approved for the treatment of locally advanced and metastatic basal cell carcinoma (BCC). As a first-in-class drug, vismodegib received approval with an expedited review based on a trial in which the most commonly reported adverse reactions were muscle cramps, alopecia, dysgeusia, weight loss, and fatigue. Inspired by our own case of marked reversible hepatitis occurring 3 weeks after administration of vismodegib, we sought to obtain an additional source of adverse event information. The FDA Adverse Events Reporting System (FAERS) is a publically accessible database containing postmarket information on side effects submitted to the FDA by manufacturers, health care professionals, and consumers. We reviewed the FAERS data from January 2012, when vismodegib was approved for clinical use, through our time of writing in January 2013. Sixty-five individual cases of vismodegib-associated reactions were identified. Patients had a mean age of 63 years; 43.08% were male and 52.31% were female (Table I). The most frequent indication for use was BCC, accounting for 46.15% of reports. If 5 potential cases of BCC that were labeled nonspecifically as ‘‘skin cancer’’ and ‘‘head and neck cancer’’ are included, then that number would be higher. The most frequently observed adverse reactions included liver toxicity (23.08%), gastrointestinal distress (33.85%), and thrombotic complications (13.85%). All cases considered for hepatotoxicity were associated with an elevation in liver enzymes or bilirubin, hepatitis, or hepatocellular damage.
