Prophylaxis against Pneumocystis carinii pneumonia in children with HIV.
To the Editor. —In its recent "Guidelines for Prophylaxis Against Pneumocystis carinii Pneumonia [PCP] for Children Infected with Human Immunodeficiency Virus [HIV]," the Centers for Disease Control (CDC) Working Group on PCP Prophylaxis in Children 1 mentions that, "Some patients with documented adverse reactions to TMPSMX [trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole] have been successfully desensitized," but makes no recommendation regarding its possible use for prophylaxis before considering one of the "alternative regimens." The group recommended that readministration of the drug not be attempted if "life-threatening toxicity (anaphylaxis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, or hypotension) occurs." Since a life-threatening toxic reaction is rare, 2 even if the drug is continued after appearance of early symptoms 3,4 and none of the alternative regimens for PCP prophylaxis are proven to be as effective as TMP-SMX, we believe that, at least in children, hyposensitization deserves consideration as the recommended first approach in cases of TMPSMX hypersensitivity.
