Litigating insurance coverage for mental disorders.
The absence of adequate insurance coverage for mental disorders is a major factor in sustaining a dual system of psychiatric care in this country. Insurance policies generally restrict coverage to a limited number ofdays ofhospitalization and a dollar ceiling on outpatient treatment, in contrast to nearly full coverage for most medical disorders. Only patients with acute illnesses susceptible to short-term care or ample private resources are treated in the private sector. The remainder, including most of the chronic mentally ill, are relegated to often-overburdened public-sector facilities. Efforts to persuade state legislatures to require that insurance companies provide equivalent coverage for physical and mental disorders have run into opposition from the insurance lobby, employers, and, in some cases, unions, out of fear that higher insurance costs might result. In fact, the insurance industry recently attempted to strip states of the right to mandate any coverage for mental health and other services, an initiative that was turned back by the United States Supreme Court (1).
