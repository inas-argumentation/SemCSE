MRI findings in benign multiple sclerosis are variable
Sirs: Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) findings are highly variable in benign MS. Previous studies have found similar lesion load on conventional T2-weighted imaging for benign compared with secondary progressive multiple sclerosis (SPMS) patients whose course was disabling. [1–3] This may be related to the site, size, nature of lesions and axonal preservation and remyelination as supported by recent MR spectroscopy and magnetization transfer imaging studies. [4, 5] A serial MRI study of patients with isolated syndromes suggestive of MS, found only a moderate correlation between the increase in volume of lesions in first 5 years and the degree of long term disability from MS. [6] In 2001, we followed up all 162 patients (with definite MS by Poser criteria) from the 1991 Olmsted County population based cohort. [7] In 2001, thirty patients with disease duration >20 years, remained fully ambulatory with EDSS £3 and all but 5 had a normal mental status exam, (three scored 1 and two scored 2 on the EDSS mental functional system scores). We reviewed our radiology archives for MRIs on any of these 30 patients. We located 9 head MRIs and all were consistent with MS. We classified these into 3 groups (figure): mild (<10 lesions), moderate (>10 lesions) and severe (>10 lesions and confluent lesions). We also reviewed all 9 patients’ medical records for information regarding their most recent follow up; 8 of 9 patients (in addition to the study EDSS assessments in 1991 and 2001) were also reviewed more recently by a neurologist and had a neurological evaluation recorded. Their most recent available estimated EDSS and short test of mental status results are also presented in the table (figure legend). The short test of mental status is routinely used at the Mayo Clinic and contains items that test orientation, attention, immediate recall, arithmetic, abstraction, construction, information and delayed (approximately 3 minutes) recall. [8, 9] The maximal score is 38. Our MRI data were incomplete as many patients diagnosed decades ago did not have MRI studies. However, our observations confirm that patients with benign MS (by EDSS) of 20 years duration can have a high T2 signal lesion load. We submit that the EDSS mental status function score and the short test of mental status are inadequate for properly measuring cognitive function in MS patients and future studies are needed to investigate for cognitive problems in patients presumed to be benign (by EDSS) after many years. Novel imaging techniques are required to better discriminate underlying pathology.
