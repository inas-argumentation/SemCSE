Clomiphene, an Ovulation-Inducing Agent, Mobilizes Intracellular Ca2+ and Causes Extracellular Ca2+ Influx in Bladder Female Transitional Carcinoma Cells
Background/Methods: The effect of clomiphene, an ovulation-inducing agent, on cytosolic free Ca<sup>2+</sup> levels ([Ca<sup>2+</sup>]<sub>i</sub>) in populations of BFTC human bladder cancer cells was explored by using fura-2 as a Ca<sup>2+</sup> indicator. Results: Clomiphene at concentrations between 10 and 75 µM increased [Ca<sup>2+</sup>]<sub>i</sub> in a concentration-dependent manner and the signal saturated at 50 µM. The [Ca<sup>2+</sup>]<sub>i</sub> signal was biphasic with an initial rise and a slow decay. Ca<sup>2+</sup> removal inhibited the Ca<sup>2+</sup> signal by about 40–50% in maximum [Ca<sup>2+</sup>]<sub>i</sub>. Adding 3 mM Ca<sup>2+</sup> increased [Ca<sup>2+</sup>]<sub>i</sub> in cells pretreated with 50 µM clomiphene in Ca<sup>2+</sup>-free medium, suggesting that clomiphene induced capacitative Ca<sup>2+</sup> entry. In Ca<sup>2+</sup>-free medium, pretreatment with 50 µM brefeldin A (to disrupt the Golgi complex Ca<sup>2+</sup> store), 1 µM thapsigargin (to inhibit the endoplasmic reticulum Ca<sup>2+</sup> pump), and CCCP (to uncouple mitochondria) inhibited 85% of clomiphene-induced intracellular Ca<sup>2+</sup> release. Conversely, pretreatment with 50 µM clomiphene in Ca<sup>2+</sup>-free medium abolished the [Ca<sup>2+</sup>]<sub>i</sub> increase induced by brefeldin, thapsigargin or CCCP. The intracellular Ca<sup>2+</sup> release was unaltered by inhibiting formation of inositol-1,4,5-trisphosphate (IP<sub>3</sub>) with 2 mM 1-(6-((17β-3-methoxyestra-1,3,5(10)-trien-17-yl)amino)hexyl)-1H-pyrrole-2,5-dione (U73122; a phospholipase C inhibitor). Conclusion: The [Ca<sup>2+</sup>]<sub>i</sub> increase induced by 50 µM clomiphene was not affected by 10 µM of nifedipine, verapamil or diltiazem. Collectively, the results suggest that clomiphene releases intracellular Ca<sup>2+</sup> in an IP<sub>3</sub>-independent manner and also activates extracellular Ca<sup>2+</sup> influx.
