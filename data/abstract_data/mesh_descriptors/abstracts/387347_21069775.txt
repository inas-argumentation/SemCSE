CSF hypocretin (Orexin) in neurological and psychiatric conditions
Two previous studies measuring cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) hypocretin-1 (Hcrt) in patients with neurological illness and one series reporting on CSF Hcrt levels across gender and generation have found that, apart from a small subgroup of patients with Guillain-BarreSyndrome (GBS) with unde- tectable levels, CSF Hcrt did not differ significantly between neurological disease and normal controls (Kanbayashi et al. 2002a, 2002b; Ripley et al. 2001). Kanbayashi's group reported on 10 patients with GBS, seven with multiple sclerosis (MS), and six patients with Parkinson's disease, five with cerebral infarction and a further five with epilepsy (Kanbayashi et al. 2002a). Ripley et al. (2001) have studied a larger sample of patients with a wide range of neuro- logical diagnoses including Alzheimer's disease (n ¼ 24), intracranial neoplasms (n ¼ 17), MS (n ¼ 13), intracranial infections (n ¼ 32), head trauma (n ¼ 4), GBS (n ¼ 8) and chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy (CIDP) (n ¼ 13), and subarachnoid haemorrhage (SAH) (n ¼ 35). Low levels of CSF Hcrt have been found in patients with SAH, acoustic schwannoma, and head trauma (Ripley et al. 2001). The authors suggest that this phenomenon might be explained by damage to and ⁄ or dysfunction of the hypotha- lamus. The role of Hcrt in neurological and psychiatric disorders is yet to be established. We have measured CSF Hcrt in our patient population to verify these previously published findings. Lumbar CSF was collected from 194 patients, 61 males and 133 females, with a variety of neurological diagnoses and including 15 control samples. The control group was drawn from samples collected during epidural procedures from women in early labour. The CSF Hcrt-1 was measured in crude CSF samples (no extraction procedure) (100 lL, dupli- cate) using a commercially available radioimmunoassay (RIA) kit (Phoenix Pharmaceuticals Inc., Belmont, CA, USA). Intra- assay variability was 3.6% and the detection limit was 40 pg mL )1 . Samples were drawn from patients aged 15- 80 years (mean age 42 ± 14 years) after obtaining informed consent for the lumbar puncture. Statistical analysis was made by using one-way analysis of variance (anova) and Fisher's PLSD, and Fisher's exact probability test. Hypocretin was detectable in all the specimens tested. The mean Hcrt for the entire sample (n ¼ 194) was 176 ± 53 pg mL )1 (mean ± SD; range: 44-302 pg mL )1 ).
