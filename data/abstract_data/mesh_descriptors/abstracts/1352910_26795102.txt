Pancreatic carcinosarcoma with unique triphasic histological pattern.
To the Editor: A 67-year-old woman presented to our institution in June 2004 with chief complaint of abdominal pain. Her past medical history was significant for peripheral vascular disease requiring multiple revascularization procedures and lower extremity amputations. In addition, she reported a 30-pack-year smoking history but denied alcohol use or significant weight loss. Physical examination was significant for epigastric and right upper quadrant tenderness to palpation. The patient was afebrile, hemodynamically stable, anicteric, and had no palpable abdominal masses. Laboratory examinations revealed an elevation of lipase to 1336 units/L and amylase to 761 units/L, although liver function tests, basic metabolic panel, and complete blood count were all within normal limits. Abdominal ultrasonography showed pancreatic ductal dilatation to 4 mm, cholelithiasis, gallbladder sludge, and gallbladder wall thickening but no evidence of a pancreatic mass or biliary ductal dilatation. Endoscopic ultrasonography (EUS) was subsequently performed to evaluate for a pancreatic malignancy. EUS demonstrated a 2.1 3 1.7 cm hypoechoic mass in the head of the pancreas, associated with pancreatic ductal dilatation throughout the head and tail up to 5.7 mm. Scattered peripancreatic lymphadenopathy was also appreciated, but there was no invasion of the mass into the portal or superior mesenteric veins. A fine needle aspiration was performed and demonstrated a poorly differentiated carcinoma with multinucleated giant cells. A CA 19-9 level was then checked and found to be elevated at 109 units/mL. Computed tomography (CT) scan of the abdomen and pelvis confirmed the tumor at the head of the pancreas with no evidence of encasement or invasion of vascular structures or metastatic disease. The patient underwent a pyloruspreserving pancreaticoduodenectomy without any complications. Her postoperative course was unremarkable, and she was discharged within 2 weeks of her surgery with routine follow-up. Surveillance CT scans performed 1 and 4 months postoperatively were significant for new, extensive nodular opacities in the liver, confirmed by MRI as metastatic disease. Because of her deteriorating health, the patient was placed in hospice care and died in March 2005. The resected specimen consisted of a 5 3 4 3 3 cm segment of pancreatic head with a poorly circumscribed 2.5 3 2.5 3 2 cm tumor. The cut surface of the tumor showed variegated dark red and pale gelatinous areas. The attached segment of duodenum measured 13.0 cm long, 6.0 cm in circumference, and had a grossly normal mucosal surface. A separately submitted gallbladder measured 11.7 3 3.8 cm and showed multiple small gallstones and severe chronic cholecystitis. Grossly and microscopically, the pancreatic neck resection margin, bile duct, overlying duodenum, and 6 regional lymph nodes were free of tumor (Stage IB, T2N0).
