Liver biopsy of cattle
IVRA, a quick, simple method of anaesthesia requiring no detailed anatomical knowledge, has now largely superseded other methods of anaesthesia for surgery of the bovine foot. Complete anaesthesia can be expected in at least 90 per cent of cases. Some failures are inexplicable but the majority are the result of an ineffective tourniquet or failure to allow an adequate interval between completion of the injection and commencement of surgery. When the block is only partially successful, it is usually the interdigital area which remains unanaesthetised. The speed of onset of anaesthesia is governed by the volume of anaesthetic solution injected-the greater the volume, the greater the intralumenal pressure and hence a more rapid diffusion of the anaesthetic. Although a larger volume would produce anaesthesia in a much shorter time, 30 ml has proved to be the optimum quantity. It can be injected rapidly, thereby reducing the risk of leg movement, and produces anaesthesia within an acceptable period of time. As an alternative to the method described, the tourniquet can be applied in the mid metatarsal/metacarpal region and the intravenous injection made just proximal to the fetlock joint.
