Ionizing radiations and cancer.
Publisher Summary Ionizing radiation has several important relationships with cancer. Two of these—the production of cancer by radiation and the destructive effects of radiation on the neoplastic process—are discussed in this chapter. The effect of radiation on tumor immunity is also explored. The fact that tracer methods make it possible to study synthetic processes in a direct way indicates that this branch of cancer research has a bright future. The survival time of irradiated cultures is sufficiently variable to make this criterion of radiation dosage a difficult one to use. The irradiation of tumor fragments before inoculation yields variable results, and it often appears that the fragments are somewhat more resistant to irradiation than tumors in vivo. The tumors that grow appreciably when transplanted into heterologous hosts share the ones that maintain a rapid growth rate to outstrip the development of the immune mechanism.
