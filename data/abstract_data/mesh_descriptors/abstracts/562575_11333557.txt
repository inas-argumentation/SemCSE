In comparison, only one patient (3.8%) out of 26 biopsy-positive patients with a prostate volume more than 70 cc had a Gleason score of 8 or more. In patients with a prostate volume less than 35 cc, the incidence of PCa with Gleason scores of 8 and above was 6.02% compared to 1.5% in patients with volumes above 70 cc. This supports the notion that prostate volume not only shows an inverse correlation with the incidence of PCa, but also with the aggressiveness of this malignancy. Limitations of these studies such as the technique of ultrasound-guided prostate biopsy and the number of cores biopsied are well outlined in the literature [4, 5]. However, recent studies using multiparametric magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)-fusion ultrasound technique have overcome some shortcomings of previous conventional techniques used in previous prostate-biopsy studies. Interestingly, they resulted with similar results indicating an inverse correlation between prostate volume and the incidence of PCa [6–8]. It is well documented in the literature that approximately 70–80% of PCa originates from the peripheral zone of the prostate; while BPH normally arises in the transition zone [8]. This may provide an explanation of the mechanism of why larger prostates caused by BPH are at lower risk for PCa: continuous growth of the transition zone may compress the peripheral zone, causing atrophy and fibrosis of glands within the peripheral zone, combined with outgrowing the blood supply, thus limiting growth of cancer budding from the peripheral zone.
