Use of a Continuous Intravenous Morphine Infusion for Pain Control in an Enfant with Terminal Malignancy
Pain is a common problem in the child with terminal malignancy. The use of continuous intravenous, or subcutaneoUS2 infusions of morphine has been established as an effective means of analgesia for the control of severe pain in terminally ill children who have failed to achieve adequate pain relief with the use of intermittent narcotics. Thus far, however, the use of this analgesic modality has not been reported in an infant with cancer. Management of pain in infancy presents a particularly difficult challenge because of the paucity of available pain assessment guidelines for this age group and the lack of analgesic medication dosage and toxicity data in the very young child. We used a continuous intravenous morphine infusion to provide pain control in a ten-month-old baby dying of metastatic rhabdomyosarcoma, basing narcotic dosage on the infant’s physical examination and his nonverbal communications of pain.
