Hemolysis of washed human red cells by various snake venoms in the presence of albumin and Ca2+.
Twenty-four snake venoms were tested for their ability to hemolyse washed human red blood cells in the presence of albumin and Ca 2+ . Except for Crotalus h. horridus , all venoms hemolysed the red cells to a varying degree. The majority of the venoms hydrolysed membrane phospholipids in the presence or absence of albumin, as long as Ca 2+ was present. A correlation was found between fatty acid cleaved and hemolysis. These results suggest that membrane damage as evidenced by membrane phospholipid splitting is a prerequisite to hemolysis in this system.
