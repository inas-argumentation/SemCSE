PSAGRAPH: A SIMPLE GRAPHICAL AND USEFUL TOOL FOR FOLLOW‐UP AND DECISION ON BIOPSY AND TREATMENT OF PATIENTS WITH PROSTATIC DISEASES
Sir, I read with interest this article by Hotston et al. [1] on young men ( < 50 years old) diagnosed with prostate cancer. As traditionally younger men were more likely to present with symptomatic prostate cancer, it is not surprising that historically this group of men were also associated with a poor prognosis. However, we questioned whether this still holds true. To this end, we conducted a retrospective study comparing 341 men aged < 50 years with 2556 men aged ≥ 50 years who had radical retropubic prostatectomy for clinically localised prostate cancer [2]. To our knowledge, this is the largest such study, and showed that younger men had comparable 5-, 10and 15-year biochemical recurrence-free survival rates to men aged 50–59 years (88%, 81% and 69% vs 87%, 78% and 71%, respectively). Furthermore, younger men were associated with better long-term cancer control rates than men aged 60–69 years and those aged ≥ 70 years. In addition, younger men were associated with a lower incidence of extraprostatic extension, seminal vesicle involvement, and positive surgical margin status, with greater organ-confined disease [2]. Therefore, it appears that since the availability of PSA as a biomarker for detecting prostate cancer, with the associated increased prostate cancer publicity, younger men are clearly being diagnosed with prostate cancer earlier in its natural history. As such, it is unlikely that younger men necessarily harbour more aggressive disease or are associated with a worse prognosis.
