Are persons with asthma at increased risk of pneumococcal infections, and can we prevent them?
In this issue of the Journal of Allergy and Clinical Immunology, Juhn et al report the association of an increased risk of serious pneumococcal disease, defined as invasive pneumococcal disease or pneumococcal pneumonia, among persons with asthma in a population of residents of Rochester, Minn, studied between 1964 and 1983. Previously, our group reported that persons with asthma had a greater than 2-fold increased risk of invasive pneumococcal disease, using the Active Bacterial Surveillance Core data from the Centers for Disease Control, which identified Streptococcus pneumoniae from sterile sites, among both children and adults. These studies among persons with asthma in 2 very different US populations, and during different study periods, now provide compelling support for adding asthma as a high-risk condition for serious pneumococcal disease. In addition, Almirall et al have recently shown that both asthma and inhaled corticosteroids are risk factors for community-acquired pneumonia, although not specific to Streptococcus pneumoniae, in a European populationbased case-control study among persons age >14 years. What follows is a summary of our current understanding of the data supporting the association of asthma and serious pneumococcal disease, potential mechanisms of host susceptibility to infections, and preventive implications and challenges of our current knowledge. The available evidence from Juhn et al and Talbot et al supporting an increased risk of serious pneumococcal disease among persons with asthma comes from observational data. An association between a risk factor (asthma) and a subsequent event (pneumococcal disease) can reflect causal mechanisms; alternatively, such associations could be a result of other unmeasured factors or characteristics that are closely related to asthma (confounders). However, in both cases, ‘‘asthma’’ identifies those at high-risk, and in both of these studies, the results are strikingly consistent.
