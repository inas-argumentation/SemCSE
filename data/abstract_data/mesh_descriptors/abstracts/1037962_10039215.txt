Reply to “On the low-dose radiation exposure in the Techa River Cohort and mortality from circulatory diseases” by Jargin (2013)
We agree with Jargin (2013) in his comment on our study (Krestinina et al. 2013) that the rates of DCS in Russia differ from those in the countries of Western Europe. Moreover, they differ from those in the countries outside Western Europe, and the rates in the latter differ from each other. There is no doubt that postmortem examinations are conducted only in a small fraction of lethal outcomes; hence, there exists a chance of underestimation of lethality associated with myocardial infarctions which are classed with ischemic heart disease. These differences will be important if we compare the rates of incidence, mortality, or prevalence obtained for diseases of the DCS in the Techa River Cohort (TRC) with the respective rates in other countries. However, in our study, we neither make any attempt to draw comparisons between the death rates of TRC members and those of populations not only of different countries, but also of residents from different regions of such a vast country as Russia. It should be noted, in this context, that the TRC is truncated at the year of birth (1.1.1950): it is a cohort that is aging over time (the follow-up period amounts to more than 50 years; that is why there are no individuals younger than 50 years in the cohort at the end of the follow-up period). Thus, it is meaningless, for example, to compare the TRC absolute rates with those obtained for other populations. In other words, we do not compare the results of our research with national rates, nor with results of any other external control group since this kind of comparison would be incorrect. In the framework of our research, we are investigating the dependence of a phenomenon on absorbed dose, and the basic condition for conducting a high-quality analysis should be the absence of any differences in individual demographic parameters or risk factors between the residents composing different dose groups. Since the individuals in the dose groups have been selected from members of the same cohort who lived for a long time (more than 50 years) on a clearly defined administrative territory, were provided with medical services of equal quality, were mostly agricultural workers in terms of occupation, and lived in similar socioeconomic conditions which changed with the same dynamics, and since the frequency of postmortem examinations did not depend on dose (Krestinina et al. 2005, 2007, 2010, 2013), there is no relationship between the above-indicated factors and the dose group to which an individual cohort member was assigned. As a result, these factors should not have any effect on the mortality risk value deduced for a certain dose group. Correspondingly, the dose dependence will not in any way be influenced by the phenomenon of overdiagnosis of ischemic heart diseases or hypodiagnosis of acute myocardial infarction, because these phenomena will be the same for all cohort members. In Krestinina et al. (2013), we are using information on causes of death for almost all deceased cohort members from the unified source that is accessible to us (i.e., from a medical death certificate, or an official document showing registered information derived from medical death certificates) (Krestinina et al. 2005, 2013).
