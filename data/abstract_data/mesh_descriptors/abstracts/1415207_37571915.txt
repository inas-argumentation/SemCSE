Hormone-receptor assays and cancer of the breast. The pathologist's role.
The pathologist's role in hormone-receptor assays and cancer of the breast is discussed. The pathologist should persist in his role of continuing education to the medical staff to provide the surgeon and clinicians with full understanding of the concept of hormone receptors as they relate to mammary carcinoma and in this way persuade them to order these tests appropriately. The pathologist is also critically important in the selection of tissue for assay: 1) he must select the tissue carefully, eliminating any noncancerous tissue that may interfere with the assay; 2) he must retain a portion of adjacent tissue for histologic control; 3) he should avoid necrotic tissue that includes denatured receptor tissue; and 4) he must freeze the tissue in a -35 degrees centigrade freezer; 6) he should insure shipment to a reliable reference laboratory; 7) he should have recurrent carcinomas analyzed for receptor content to establish the continued presence of the receptor in metastatic tissue; and 8) he may assay undifferentiated or metastatic carcinoma of uncertain origin in women to aid in the differential diagnosis of the tissue of origin.
