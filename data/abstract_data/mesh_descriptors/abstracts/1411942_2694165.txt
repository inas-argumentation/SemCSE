AMNIOTIC FLUID VOLUME AND PROTEIN CONCENTRATION IN RHESUS SENSITIZED WOMEN
SEARCH is constantly being made for a reliable method of predicting the severity of rhesus disease in the fetus. In the past, the prognosis was based mainly on serum antibody titres (Kelsall and Vos, 1952; Tovey and Valaes, 1959; Dique and Wrench, 1959; MacKay, 1961), but recently estimation of the bilirubin content of amniotic fluid has been considered a more accurate method of prediction. Spectral absorption values have been derived for the amniotic fluid and the results analyzed in different ways (Liley, 1961; Alvey, 1964; Knox et al., 1965), the colour of the amniotic fluid has been assessed visually (Saling, 1966), and chemical estimations of “bilirubin” have been used by others (Lathe and Ruthven, 1958; MacKay and Watson, 1962; Robertson, 1964; Kruijswijk, 1966). With all methods, a small number of wrong predictions have been made. Liley (1963) and Robertson (1964) have suggested ways of overcoming some of the errors. More recently, other factors have been related to the bilirubin content of amniotic fluid to provide a closer correlation with the severity of the rhesus disease. Both Cherry et al. (1965) and Morris et al. (1967) have suggested that the protein content and volume of the amniotic fluid may be useful measurements when related to the bilirubin level. In this paper the results of sequential amniotic fluid volume and protein estimations in rhesus sensitized women are presented. hippurate (Charles and Jacoby, 1966). Sodium amino-hippurate in a dose of 400 mg. was injected into the amniotic cavity after withdrawing 10 ml. of liquor. The needle was left in situ and 2 ml. samples were withdrawn at 20, 25 and 30 minutes after the injection. The colour was developed using a diazo reaction and spectrophotometric readings at 540 p were compared with those made from standard dilutions. The protein content of the amniotic fluid was estimated by the Biuret method. The “bilirubin” in the amniotic fluid was estimated by measuring its optical density at wave-lengths from 350 p to 700 p and plotting a spectral absorption curve. The value at 450 p was read from a base-line drawn tangentially to the curve as described by Liley (1961) and subtracted from the actual 450 p value. The value obtained is referred to as A 450 p.
