Computed tomographic findings of primary malignant leptomeningeal melanoma in neurocutaneous melanosis.
A previously healthy 1 6-month-old white boy was admitted with a 4 day history of vomiting, lethargy, and seizures. Physical examination revealed no signs of meningeal irritation. There were numerous darkly pigmented hairy nevi 0.2-1 .7 cm in diameter on the scalp, trunk, and extremities. Cerebrospinal fluid findings of high protein, low glucose, and increased polymorphonuclear leukocytes were consistent with bacterial meningitis, but cultures and counter immunoelectrophoresis were negative. The patient became progressively more drowsy and developed decerebrate rigidity. CT examination of the brain revealed moderate ventricular dilatation which was more severe on a repeat scan 3 days later. A ventriculoperitoneal drain was inserted. The patient improved promptly with no residual neurologic signs. He was readmitted 3 weeks later after 3 days of progressive flaccid paralysis of both upper extremities. The ventriculoperitoneal shunt was found to function. A repeat CT examination demonstrated obliteration of the basal cisterns and widespread enhancement of the infiltrated leptomeninges (fig. 1 ). Leptomeningeal melanosis was diagnosed by meningeal biopsy. The patient continued to deteriorate, developed respiratory distress, and died 3 weeks later. On gross examination, the brain was firm, symmetrical and light gray; the meninges were thickened and gray (fig. 2). Firm tumor infiltrated the meninges around the midbrain, pons, and spinal cord, with no tumor infiltration of the brain itself. These areas of the meninges proved to be malignant leptomeningeal melanoma with the presence of mitotic figures at microscopy. There was perivascular infiltration of the melanoma into the Virchow-Robin spaces. There was marked degeneration of the dentate nucleus and cerebellar folia. Melanophages were seen scattered throughout the superficial cortex. Examination of the cutaneous nevi revealed junctional nevi of the skin with no evidence of malignant transformation. No evidence of malignant melanoma was discovered apart from the leptomeninges.
