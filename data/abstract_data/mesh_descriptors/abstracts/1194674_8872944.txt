Which tools can I use in daily clinical practice to improve tailoring of treatment for breast cancer? Treatment decision-making in advanced disease.
There are many more drugs available for the treatment of breast cancer than for any other malignancy, and the list grows longer every year (Table 1). For most patients with metastatic disease survival is measured in years rather than months [1], but in the end metastatic breast cancer remains nearly always incurable. Treatment decision-making can be complex because of the options available. However, the right decisions are important to ensure that the patient achieves not just optimum survival but survival with as good a quality of life as possible. Several key goals determine appropriate treatment decisions. These include: (i) prolongation of survival; (ii) symptom relief; (iii) the maintenance of a good quality of life (QoL); (iv) the delay of disease progression. This article will first discuss the general principles of treatment decision-making, and then deal with specific therapies.
