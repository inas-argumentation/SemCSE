Reply to C. Anderson and J.M. Buatti.
To the Editor: Thank you for providing us the opportunity to reply to Drs Anderson and Buatti’s letter regarding our recently published article “Where Do Patients With Cancer in Iowa Receive Radiation Therapy?”1 In their letter, Anderson and Buatti state that “Starting in 2005, the state provided an expansion of Medicaid called ‘IowaCare.’ . . . The legislature required patients to receive their radiation therapy at University of Iowa Hospitals & Clinics in Iowa City. . . . Many low-income rural Iowans would have had IowaCare coverage, thus skewing the data reported in this article.” In the limitations section of our article, we stated that “data were not available at the patient level on socioeconomic variables, which precluded exploration of poverty and insurance coverage.” However, summary data on insurance coverage are available for 2007 to 2010fromtheIowaCancerRegistryandfromSEER*Stat.Thesedata suggest that an upper limit for “any Medicaid” coverage at time of diagnosis is 5.5%. Anderson and Buatti are correct in stating that IowaCare coverage would affect the choice of radiation oncology facilityby low-incomepatientsotherwise ineligible forMedicaid;however, because the number of cancer patients with IowaCare coverage is low and, as reported in our article, 28.5% of patients with cancer receive radiation therapy, any skewing in our data would be small. We appreciate the opportunity to clarify this point. Marcia M. Ward, Fred Ullrich, and Gerard Rushton University of Iowa, Iowa City, IA
