HIV infection in drug using transvestites and transexuals
Prostitution has been increasingly practiced by men, in particular transvestites, but little information is available on HIV seroprevalence in this population group. In Sao Paulo, Brazil, 62% of 37 transvestites tested in 1988 were seropositive; some of them declared to have aknowledged use of heroin or cocaine (1). In industrialized countries, an association between drug use and HIV seropositivity has been found in female prostitutes (2). During the period 1989-90, we studied 22 drug using male prostitutes (11 transvestites and 11 transexuals) attending a drug dependency unit in Rome; 21 were Brazilians and one was an Italian citizen. Sixteen subjects reported intravenous use of heroin (and of cocaine as well in two cases). The remaining 6 also used heroin, albeit not by injection. The mean age of the 22 subjects was 30.1 years (range 22-42 years). Most individuals (90%) had been in Italy for less than 5 years (mean 3.3 years, range 1-9 years), all but one had started using or injecting drugs in the last 6 years (the Italian subject had been injecting heroin for 16 years). Eleven out of the 21 Brazilian subjects (52%) had started using heroin after their arrival in Italy. Most Brazilian male prostitutes living in Rome use heroin; however, this group acknowledged only occasional intravenous injection, generally when they had only a small dose. It is interesting to note that many of the subjects reported to have begun to snort heroin because of the chilly winter nights of Rome to which they were exposed due to their work. All drug using male prostitutes admitted to engaging in prostitution, and all but 4 subjects reported more than 300 male partners per year in the last two years. Both transvestites and transexuals reported practicing oral sex (fellatio) and receptive anal antercourse; vaginal intercourse was also reported by transexuals.
