In particular, this study was retrospective in design, and it involved only a single center and a relatively small sample size. In addition, we did not obtain long-term data on biochemical and functional outcomes, including the recovery of urinary continence and erectile function, which are used as surrogates for surgical difficulty in RARP. Thus, further investigation is required. However, we do believe that the present study can be used as a reference, retrospective pilot study for future prospective studies involving a larger numbers of patients. In conclusion, pelvic dimensions will likely become more important in determining surgical difficulty, as robotic instruments must be manipulated within a confined space in the current era of RARP. Therefore, MRI should be considered a valuable adjunct to RARP. In addition to providing important clinical staging information, MRI can provide accurate information regarding bony pelvic dimensions. Our data predict that patients with large prostates within small pelvises might have more difficult surgeries, leading to longer surgical times. Therefore, if a patient has a large prostate within a small pelvis, the RARP team should recognize that operative time might be longer than usual, which could involve increased blood loss and complications. However, it also appears that these problems can be overcome by an experienced operator with improved surgical techniques.
