Primary Hepatic Lymphoma: the Importance of Liver Biopsy
Primary hepatic lymphoma (PHL) is a very uncommon lymphoproliferative malignancy. It accounts for only 0.4 % of all extranodal non-Hodgkin lymphoma and 0.016% of all cases of non-Hodgkin disease. Primary hepatic Burkitt’s lymphoma and lymphoblastic lymphoma are estimated to be a 17 % of PHLs. Diffuse large B cell lymphomas are the most frequent type. The diagnosis is made when there is only liver involvement or when there is minimal non-liver disease. Bone marrow, spleen, or hematologic affection should be excluded to confirm the diagnosis. Patients frequently complain of right upper quadrant pain with hepatomegaly on physical examination. Pyrexia, night sweats, or weight loss are less common symptoms. Prognosis varies from 15 months for patients with unfavorable prognostic factors to a 5-year survival rate without acute hepatic failure or unfavorable factors. The presence of advanced age, elevated lactate dehydrogenase, liver cirrhosis, and chronic active hepatitis or HIV (human immunodeficiency virus) infection clearly influences the outcome. We present a 67-year-old male admitted because of a 2month history of right upper quadrant pain and nonmeasured weight loss. The patient did not mention fever or night sweats. On physical examination, there was a 4-cm hepatomegaly with no other findings. Laboratory test results were as follows: hemoglobin 12 g/dL, platelets 500,000/ mcL, white blood count 6930/mm, liver function tests and cholestasic enzymes showed normal values: ALT (alanine transaminase) 20 IU/L, AST (aspartate transaminase) 24 IU/ L, bilirubin 0.3 mg/dL, GGT (gamma-glutamyl transferase) 23 IU/L, and ALP (alkaline phosphatase) 142 IU/L. Lactate dehydrogenase levels (LDH) were in the high-limit value 225 U/L (135–225 U/L). Serologic tests showed negative results for both HBV (hepatitis B virus) and HCV (hepatitis C virus). An HIV Western blot test was also performed with a negative result as well. G immunoglobulin against EBV (Epstein-Barr virus) was positive. Imaging tests were made primarily focusing on the liver and bile ducts. The CT (computed tomography) scan showed three intrahepatic lesions in segments V, VI, and VII, being these the unique findings (Fig. 1). The lesions were enhanced in arterial phase with intravenous contrast suggesting as first possible diagnosis matastases. After ruling out with endoscopic methods the digestive and respiratory origins of the disease, a CT-guided fine needle aspiration punction was the next diagnostic step. The cytology showed atypia, highly suggestive of malignancy. In order to obtain enough tissue to categorize the lineage of the tumor, our General Surgery team performed a laparoscopic hepatic biopsy. The pathologic study of the hepatic cylinders showed diffuse infiltration by a lymphoid lineage tumor with a proliferation rate of nearly 100 %. The cells showed polymorphism, mitosis, and abundant apoptosis (Fig. 2). The nuclear stain expressed in a low fashion positivity for c-myc and bcl-2 being negative for CD10, CD34, CD5, and D1 cycline. This type of lymphoma is nowadays classified as an intermediate type between a diffuse large B cell lymphoma and Burkitt’s lymphoma, a Burkitt-like lymphoma. FISH (fluorescent in situ hybridization) study revealed an absence of rearrangement of c-myc gene. No PCR (polymerase chain reaction) method for EBV genome was performed in the biopsy.
