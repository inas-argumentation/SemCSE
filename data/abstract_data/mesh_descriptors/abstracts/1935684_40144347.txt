Recent studies of the rhinencephalon in relation to temporal lobe epilepsy and behavior disorders.
Publisher Summary This chapter reviews the recent studies of the rhinencephalon in relation to temporal lobe epilepsy and behavior disorders. There is a review of the willed movements and abnormalities of movement, observing the interrelations between the rhinencephalon and the deep centers of the thalamus. Basal ganglia may play a part in such neural integration. On the basis of Sherrington's conclusion that mind is not a form of energy, a theory has evolved as to how non-energy mind can act on matter, and looks to telepathy as the supporting evidence. The principle of uncertainty is used to show that a minute influence can act upon a synaptic junction and modify behavior. This point of view has been criticized because the principle of uncertainty is entirely irrelevant to the question of causal determination. The attention is drawn to the role of the tonic background in the formation of associations essential to the learning process, stressing the view that the importance of repetition has been greatly exaggerated, and that an organization of associated neurons excited at sub-threshold levels is an almost necessary consequence of the structure and known physiological properties of the cerebral cortex. The interplay of such systems might provide a mechanism for a large proportion of the selective activities of mind. It is particularly in the anterior deep temporal regions, that the integration of body functions, visceral and somatic, is performed, and where complex intellectual functions important for conscious awareness of experience and rational responsiveness are achieved.
