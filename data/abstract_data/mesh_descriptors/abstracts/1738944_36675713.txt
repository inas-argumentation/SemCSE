Post-discharge deep-vein thrombosis after orthopaedic surgery
SIR—Planes and colleagues (July 27, p 224) state “we are not certain whether the thrombi detected by venography 35 days after surgery, but not causing symptoms, are clinically important”. This begs the question. The difference in frequency of deep-venous thrombosis (DVT) in their study groups was limited to distal DVT. Although the proportion of these patients with distal DVT who had symptoms was not stated, less than half the patients, overall, with either proximal or distal DVT had symptoms. In view of the very low frequency of proximal propagation of distal DVT, it is hard to imagine how symptomless, venographically detected, distal DVT could be regarded as clinically important. Although enoxaparin may reduce the risk of DVT when given after hospital discharge in patients who have undergone total hip replacement, perhaps this result can be better achieved by simply not doing venography.
