CD4+/CD56+ hematodermic neoplasm: a report of three cases
A 59-year-old woman complained of a skin nodule on her left lower leg that had been present for four months. The nodule had been diagnosed as a furuncle initially and treated with antibiotics and Chinese traditional medicine, but the nodule kept growing and was complicated by heat, pain and swelling. An incision and drainage had been performed and one month later she came to our hospital for evaluation of the unhealed incision. Physical examination revealed a mass on her left lower extremity, about 5 cm in diameter with ulceration on the surface (Fig. 1a). No abnormalities were found by routine hematological examination, bone marrow biopsy, ultrasonography and CT scan of thorax and abdomen. A skin biopsy was performed and histology showed the smallto medium-sized lymphocytoid cells infiltrated in sheet in the dermis and adipose layer. Necrosis and angiodestruction were absent (Fig. 1c and d). The tumor cells were positive for CD45, CD4 (weak), CD56, CD43, CD68 (KP-1, partly), and negative for B-cell and all other T-cell, NK-cell, and myeloid markers. Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) was not detected with EBV-encoded small nuclear RNA (EBER) probe (Dakopatts, Denmark, No. Y5200) by in situ hybridization. No T-cell clonal rearrangement was found with consensus primers (T VG /T JX ). (Table 2) The diagnose of CD4 + /CD56 + hematodermic neoplasm (CD4 + /CD56 + HN) was made. She received radiotherapy and one course of BACOP (epirubicin, cyclophosphamide, vincristine, bleomycin and prednisone) + methotrexate chemotherapy. She went into complete remission afterwards. Eighteen months later, a painless, violet-red plaque appeared on her right lower extremity. Physical examination and ultrasonography showed the lymphadenopathy on her bilateral neck, axillae and inguinal regions. Fine-needle aspiration cytology of the skin lesion revealed medium-sized lymphocytoid cells (Fig. 1b). Bone marrow biopsy was clean. She received radiotherapy and three courses of DICE (dexamethasone, ifosfmide, cisplation and etoposide) chemotherapy, and achieved complete remission again. She refused to continue the chemotherapy because of its side-effects. At last check she was alive and tumor-free for 33 months.
