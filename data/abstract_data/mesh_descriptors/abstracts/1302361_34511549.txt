Recent advances in lung cancer surgery in Europe.
Prof. Clifton F. Mountain’s proposal of the New International Staging System for Lung Cancer at the IV IASLC World Conference on Lung Cancer in 1985 began the present era of lung cancer research and treatment. Besides offering a common language for grouping patients in key stages of tumoral progression that was closely related to the end results of surgical treatment, the New System represents an obligation for the surgeon, who has the main responsible for staging the disease. Starting with the preoperative surgical mediastinal assessment which concludes clinical staging, throughout intraoperative staging, which is based on a visual evaluation of the extent of the tumour aided by frozen tissue sections, the surgeon is in a unique position of being able to examine the actual TNM descriptors as well as their prognostic significance. Histological findings of pathological staging should complete the surgical work-up. Thus, the constant effort to recognise and classify various clinical features can easily push the surgeon to develop either new rules for improving the clinical application of the Staging System or new predictors of prognosis to improve the System itself.
