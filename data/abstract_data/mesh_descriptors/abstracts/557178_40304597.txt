Negativism to food during self-stimulation in the anterior part of the basal forebrain in dogs.
Abstract A negative response to food was found in 7 dogs self-stimulating in 8 loci within the nucleus entopeduncularis, nucleus commissurae anterioris, the preoptic area and the lateral hypothalamus. In 6 dogs this appeared simultaneously in three forms: (1) ignoring food (FI) during self-stimulation; (2) food rejection (FR), i.e. interruption of the animal's attempt to reach meat upon experimenter-exerted stimulation of the rewarding site, and (3) food ejection (FE) consisting of throwing a piece of meat out of the mouth when the current was switched on at the beginning of the consummatory act. The seventh dog displayed an approach-avoidance behavior, and only FI and FR were observed. None of the three forms of negativism to food was seen in two other dogs self-stimulating in 3 sites. Negative response to food, particularly when it appears as FE, may indicate that the rewarding value of the stimulus depends on excitation of the neural system controlling satiety.
