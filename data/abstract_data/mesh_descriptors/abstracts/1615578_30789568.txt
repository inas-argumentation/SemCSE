Reward Processing, Functional Connectivity, Psychopathy, and Research Domain Criteria
Psychopathy is a disorder characterized by pronounced emotional deficits (including reduced guilt and empathy) and an increased risk for displaying antisocial behavior. The disorder was initially described by Cleckley before being more formally characterized via a behavioral checklist and then interview by Robert Hare (1). The importance of Hare’s characterization was that it provided the description of a behavioral presentation associated with specific causal factors. In contrast, a wide variety of causal factors are associated with the more general antisocial behavior descriptions provided by the DSM or ICD.
