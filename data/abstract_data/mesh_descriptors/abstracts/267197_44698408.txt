Term delivery after hepatocellular carcinoma resection in previous pregnancy
Screening of the maternal serum α-fetoprotein at the second trimester of pregnancy is a standard obstetric practice for detection of fetal malformation. However, an elevated maternal serum α-fetoprotein level may not always be of fetal origin, some maternal diseases could present with an elevated maternal serum α-fetoprotein. High serum concentration (>500 ng/mL) of α-fetoprotein is noted in 70% of patients with hepatocellular carcinoma in the United States and in 85% of those in Africa, Japan and Taiwan (1). Although hepatocellular carcinoma occurs predominantly in male, sporadic cases of hepatocellular carcinoma have been reported in pregnant women (2-5). These patients invariably died within one year after diagnosis. Herein, we report a pregnant women who was found to have a high maternal serum α-fetoprotein during routine screening. A diagnosis of hepatocellular carcinoma was made and she underwent surgical resection of the tumor. A subsequent pregnancy with successful childbirth was achieved.
