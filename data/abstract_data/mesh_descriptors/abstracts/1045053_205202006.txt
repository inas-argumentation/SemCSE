Editorial Comment to Midurethral autologous fascial sling surgery with reconstruction of the lower abdominal wall using the tensor fascia lata muscle flap for post‐hemipelvectomy stress urinary incontinence
Hemipelvectomis are uncommon and major, destructive operations, usually reserved for the resection of primary or metastatic malignant or benign-aggressive tumors of the pelvic girdle. Hemipelvectomies can be broadly classified into external (amputation) and internal (limb sparing) procedures. Postoperative complications occurred in 53% of the patients with external hemipelvectomies, with flap necrosis and wound infections being the most common. Interenal hemipelvectomy postsurgical complications were similar (flap necrosis 12.5% and wound infection 12.5%). Regarding the urological complications, there are a few reports about post-hemipelvectomy bladder dysfunction. Beck et al. reported that 17.4% of internal hemipelvectomy patients and 47.4% of external hemipelvectomy patients had bladder dysfunction including urinary incontinence at follow up (an average of 6 years later postoperatively). Urinary incontinence is not a life-threatening disease, but the symptoms can seriously influence the physical, psychological and social well-being of the affected individuals. Treatment for incontinence should be individualized with the goal of maintaining continence. However, it is sometimes very difficult to treat postoperative complicated urinary incontinence in a case like this. The authors present the first reported case of successful surgical treatment for stress incontinence after an internal hemipelvectomy for pubic bone osteosarcoma. A 25-year-old woman had undergone an internal hemipelvectomy due to left pubic bone osteosarcoma. As postoperative complications, this patient had the abdominal wall defect with bladder herniation and severe stress incontinence. Dealing with these complications, the authors carried out a reconstruction of the tissue deficit of the rectus abdominis using the tensor fascia lata muscle flap simultaneously with a midurethral autologous fascial sling. After these procedures, stress incontinence was successfully improved. The present study provides valuable information that will help plan for similar cases.
