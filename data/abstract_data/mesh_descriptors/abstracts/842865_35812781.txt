What is causing this woman's altered mental status?
CASE A 67-year-old woman presented to the ED via emergency medical services (EMS) with a complaint of generalized weakness. EMS and the triage nurses believed that the patient displayed signs and symptoms of psychiatric illness, so she was triaged to the medical ED for medical clearance. History Although the patient was a poor historian, she reported a gradual decline in activities of daily living and admitted she had not been eating or drinking well. More specifi cally she had found it too physically diffi cult to ambulate very far and had been unable to shop for groceries or obtain adequate medical care. She called EMS herself out of concern she could not function alone much longer. She denied chest pain, shortness of breath, vomiting, diarrhea, fever, abdominal pain, urinary diffi culties, recent falls, or focal weakness. She presented to the ED alone and was unable to provide a reliable history of current medications, past medical and surgical history, family history, or a detailed chronology of her symptoms. Her medical record revealed a history of hypertension, atrial fi brillation, deep vein thrombosis/pulmonary embolus, and schizoaffective disorder. She was listed as allergic to ampicillin, benztropine, penicillin, and sulfa drugs. She had been prescribed several medications—fl uticasone and albuterol inhalers, gabapentin, loperamide, losartan, hydrochlorothiazide/triamterene, verapamil, and warfarin—but it was unclear if she was taking any of them. Her recorded social history was that she lived independently and her social support was unclear. Physical examination The patient’s vital signs were BP, 160/70 mm Hg; pulse, 99; respirations, 16; oral temperature, 36.3 ̊C (97.3 ̊F); and Spo2, 100%. She was alert and oriented to person, place, and time, appeared nontoxic, was comfortable without signs of distress, but appeared disheveled. An examination of the head, eyes, ears, nose, and throat found exophthalmos and dry mucous membranes but no trauma. Her pupils were equal, round, and reactive to light. Her neck was supple with a normal range of motion, no stridor, and no palpable masses. Cardiac rate and rhythm were normal with no rub, gallop, or murmur. Her lungs were clear to auscultation bilaterally without crackles or wheezing. An abdominal examination revealed normal bowel sounds and a soft, nontender abdomen without masses. She had no edema of the extremities, adequate peripheral pulses, and normal range of motion of joints. Her skin was warm and dry with slightly diminished skin turgor and no rashes. A neurologic examination revealed intact cranial nerves, motor strength of 4 out of 5 symmetrically (proximally and distally), a normal sensory examination, no pronator drift, and no cerebellar signs or ataxia. She was awake and alert, and appeared slow to respond but answered questions appropriately. However, she had some paranoid thoughts, made illogical comments, was intermittently dismissive, and didn’t answer all questions. She was hyperalert and easily distracted.
