Chinese province leads the way in facing up to HIV/AIDS
There can’t be many HIV awareness programmes in the world that announce themselves with brass bands honour guards and goose-stepping police with bayonets but that is what happened last month in Kunming Yunnan Province where a handful of pioneering outreach projects is influencing everyone from public security officers to central government mandarins. At first sight the militaristic reception given to Britain’s State Secretary for international development Hilary Benn seemed entirely at odds with efforts to persuade China to focus on education and treatment rather than the usual repression of groups at high-risk of HIV infection. But on closer inspection it was perhaps more significant that a Chinese police academy is now able to accept UK money and foreign ideas for tackling an issue that has long been treated as taboo. Yunnan—an impoverished southeastern province that shares a long porous border with the golden triangle states of Laos and Burma—has one of the worst drug prostitution and HIV problems in China. At the end of last year local officials reported 15 000 confirmed cases of the disease in the province. Because so many of those likely to have been affected live in remote mountain communities where there is little opportunity for testing it is estimated that the actual figure is probably above 80 000 and rising at the rate of about 30% per year.
