Selenium, zinc, and prostate cancer.
This paper reviews the evidence for selenium and zinc protecting against prostate cancer and suggests further lines of research for understanding their possible activities. It is noted that selenium is an essential nonmetal trace element found in grains meat poultry fish eggs and dairy products. Whether the element is especially important for preventing prostate carcinogenesis relative to cancer in general is under study. However findings from epidemiologic studies using a number of designs and with good exposure assessment together generally support that selenium is inversely related to prostate cancer incidence and mortality. Meanwhile zinc is a homeostatically regulated essential mineral a component of numerous metalloenzymes and is important for cell growth and replication osteogenesis and immunity. Although circumstantial evidence indicates that zinc may have an important role in the prostate findings for zinc and prostate cancer incidence and mortality have not been consistent. Epidemiologic evidence for zinc protecting against prostate cancer is equivocal and is based mainly on studies of retrospective design. Thus prospective studies that employ adequate assessment of exposure to zinc and other elements conducted in populations with wide ranges of zinc exposure are needed to uncover whether zinc protects against prostate cancer.
