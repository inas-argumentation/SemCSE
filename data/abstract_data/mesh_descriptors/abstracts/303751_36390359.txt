Puromycin inhibition of ethanol ingestion and liver alcohol dehydrogenase activity in the rat.
Abstract Puromycin administered intraperitoneally in a dosage of 4 mg/100g body weight every 56 h for a period of 26 days produced a suppression of forced-choice alcohol drinking in the hooded rat. Food intake and weight gain were also diminished in these animals. A similar dosage of puromycin produced no changes in water and food intake for a group of control animals. Puromycin produced a suppression of alcohol drinking, without significant changes in water and food ingestion, in rats maintained on a free-choice ethanol selection regimen. Induced increases in liver alcohol dehydrogenase activity following forced-choice alcohol drinking were significantly suppressed by puromycin. Liver protein concentration was also reduced, but suppression of alcohol dehydrogenase was significant when enzyme activity was computed on the basis of total liver protein. These data support previous findings that an adaptive increase in liver alcohol dehydrogenase activity is associated with ethanol ingestion by rodents. The data also show that adaptive increases in alcohol dehydrogenase activity may be suppressed by puromycin and concomitant ingestion of ethanol is also suppressed.
