Some aspects of chemotherapy in breast carcinomatosis.
Two hundred and thirty nine women treated by chemotherapy for breast carcinomatosis were studied. In 73, Thio-Tepa and Deca-Durabolin were used in combination; 65% showed a beneficial response, 30% for a mean period of 17 months and 35% for an average of 7 months. Cyclophosphamide and Deca-Durabolin (93), Deca-Durabolin alone (30) and Methyl Testosterone (44) were used to treat the remaining patients. A similar picture of response is seen in each of these groups; 11% were well palliated for about 12 months (mean survival seventeen months) while 22% were benefitted for an average of 7 months. These results are inferior to those obtained with Thio-Tepa and Deca-Durabolin and are little better than those treated between 1950–55 by means of simple hormones and local irradiation. Thio-Tepa and Deca-Durabolin is prescribed on an out-patient basis. The major complication is bone-marrow depression but this is not dangerous with adequate supervision. Age at treatment, histological type of tumour, length of history, and clinical staging at initial presentation did not influence the response.
