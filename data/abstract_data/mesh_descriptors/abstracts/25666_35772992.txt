And in a similar manner, HPV virus has been identified in tumour cells. In the literature, HPV and SCC is an association essentially described in transplant patients. Concerning immunocompetent patients, HPV virus has been less more frequently identified in SCC of immunocompetent patients (except for epidermodysplasia verruciformis), where percentage of HPV is evaluated between 27% and 33%. In our patient, HPV has been identified both in tumour cells and in the epidermis above the tumour. One other recently published study shows also a high prevalence of cutaneous HPV DNA in the epidermis just above the tumour cell. The localization of the cutaneous lesion on the hand suggests an interaction between HPV and ultraviolet (UV) radiation in the development of SCC. Indeed, previous investigations have shown relevant interactions between HPV virus (proteins E6 and E7) and UV radiations. Our observation moreover raises also the question of a synergestic action among CMV, HSV and HPV in the development of multiple SCCs of fingers. An interaction between HSV and HPV is well known concerning cervical cancers. In vitro and in vivo studies show that HSV infection increases the risk of cervical cancer by over expression of HPV oncogenic genes. Concerning the treatment, our patient was treated by classical surgical excision. However, the notion of recurrence and the identification of HPV DNA in tumour have brought to discuss the interest of a preventive treatment by retinoid in order to avoid recurrence. Thus, we prescribed a local treatment by Tazarotene and with a follow-up of one year until the patient is without any new keratosis lesions.
