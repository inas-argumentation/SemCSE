What therapeutic education for multimorbid psychiatric patients?
Based on a systematic reviewabout chronic disease self-management interventions and patient education for adults with serious mental illness, Siantz andAranda [1] demonstrate in 10 studies selected the high level of multimorbidity among psychiatric patients as a large number of patients [2]. Indeed, with the epidemiological transition came a high prevalence of chronic disease and an underestimation ofmultimorbidity, that is, an individual having at least two chronic conditions [2]. Multimorbidity has significant clinical and economic impacts; it reduces patients' functional capacity and quality of life and increases mortality and health care utilization [3]. These results are obtained without a consensus definition of multimorbidity; a consequence of this is variability in the types of care organization and in the self-management and patient education programs studied. While there are numerous guidelines for treating a single pathology and validated patient self-management programs centered on a single disease [4,5], the characteristics of selfmanagement/patient educations programs aimed at patients with several conditions are hard to identify. Among those hit hardest bymultimorbidity are patientswithmental illness, where the prevalence of comorbidities is estimated to be between 57·3% and 74·3% (without knowing precisely which somatic illnesses are involved) [6]. Here again, as well as Siantz and Aranda [1] write in their study, it is difficult to find an operative description of the self-management/patient education programs tailored to these patients. An exploratory literature review analysis attempt 35 articles in the leading three international databases: Pubmed, Cochrane Librairy and Cumulative Index to Nursing and Allied Health Literature. The used keywords are patient education, self-management, mental illness, mental disorder, schizophrenia, depression, diabete* (include diabete mellitus), pluripatholog*, polymorbidity, multipathology, multimorbidity, mental health, psychiatr*, chronic conditions and self-care. This exploratory analysis helped confirm the study by Barnett [2], who noted the lack of a stable definition of multimorbidity in the context of mental health. Only few articles describe mental health therapeutic education programs (population, design and evaluation). These findings suggest that further research is needed on both the definition of multimorbidity and the design and evaluation of operational therapeutic patient education/self-management models for psychiatric patients with somatic disease.
