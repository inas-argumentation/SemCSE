Anticonvulsant activity of aminooxyacetic acid on convulsions induced by thiosemicarbazide
AMINOOXYACETIC acid (AOAA) has an anticonvulsant activity against convulsions induced chemically or electrically (DAVANZO et al., 1961; ROA et a[., 1964; KURIYAMA et a[., 1966; WOOD & F’EESKER, 1973). AOAA increases the brain concentration of GABA, a putative neurotransmitter, by inhibiting GABA transaminase (Camino-butyrate: 2-oxoglutarate aminotransferase; EC 2.6.1.19) (WALLACH, 1961, BAXTER & ROBERTS, 1961). However, no correlation has been clearly shown between total brain GABA level and the onset of convulsions. Maximum brain GABA concentrations occurred 6-8 h after treatment with AOAA, but the maximal anticonvulsant activity of AOAA against convulsions induced by thiosemicarbazide (TSC) (DAVANZO et al., 1961), picrotoxin, strychinine, pentylenetetrazole or electroshock (KURIYAMA et al., 1966) was observed 1.5 h after AOAA and was no longer present or was greatly decreased after 6 h. In this paper, we report the effect of repeated administration of AOAA on TSC convulsions. The anticonvulsant activity of AOAA against TSC decreased markedly after 7 h, despite a high content of brain GABA; an additional administration of AOAA, however, protected all the mice tested from TSC convulsions.
