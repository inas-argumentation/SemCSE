Electroencephalographic study of the signs of chronic vascular insufficiency of the Sylvian region in aged people.
From a material of 7000 EEG's 66 records have been selected which showed the same more or less typical pattern viz. paroxysmal fairly regular δ-activity in the temporal and anterior temporal region, unilaterally or bilaterally, with a marked predominance on the left side; the background-rhythm was in most of the cases not or hardly disturbed. From the clinical data it would appear that this alteration occurred particularly in aged people and that the patients in 89 per cent of the cases had vascular disturbances, which at least in the hemisphere where the δ-activity was predominant had not led to serious organic lesions. The suggestion is made that this pattern indicates a relative ischemia of the Sylvian area which causes a functional disturbance of this region. this theory is supported by the electroencephalographic observations made in general cerebral anoxia and in compression of the carotid artery. The significance of the described EEG-pattern for the diagnosis, prognosis and therapy is emphasized. In general it may be a useful indicator of an impending decompensation of the cerebral circulation.
