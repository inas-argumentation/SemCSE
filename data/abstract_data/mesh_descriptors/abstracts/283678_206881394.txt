Radiotherapy‐induced basal cell carcinoma in irradiated and surrounding areas in relation to exposure dose of radiation
Dear Editor, Basal cell carcinoma (BCC) is a frequent radiotherapy-induced skin cancer. Although a high probability of developing radiotherapy-induced BCC in the irradiated areas that are directly exposed to radiation has been described, only Martin et al. have clearly demonstrated BCC in the surrounding areas that are unintentionally exposed to radiation. Here, we report the development of radiotherapy-induced BCC in both the irradiated and surrounding areas in a 77-yearold man. To investigate the correlation between the development of radiotherapy-induced BCC and the precise exposure dose of radiation (PEDR), we calculated the PEDR at each location of BCC using the Eclipse treatment planning system (ETPS; Varian Medical Systems, Palo Alto, CA, USA). A 77-year-old man presented with a black nodule on his neck (Fig. 1a). He had a history of nasopharyngeal cancer (T3N2M0, stage III) 25 years prior to the assessment, which was treated with radiotherapy (4-MV X-rays; total dose, 50 Gy). Several other black nodules were identified (Fig. 1b–e). Multiple BCC are sometimes associated with genodermatoses, including nevoid basal cell carcinoma syndrome (NBCC). This patient did not present with any features of NBCC. He had no history of excessive sun exposure or outdoor occupation. On histopathological examinations, the skin lesions were characterized by proliferation of atypical palisaded basaloid cells, confirming the BCC nature of all lesions. We compared BCC in the irradiated areas with those in the surrounding areas. Three of the five BCC were located in the irradiated areas. The mean diameter of the BCC in the irradiated areas was 9.6 mm, which was larger than that of BCC in the surrounding areas (Fig. 1f).
