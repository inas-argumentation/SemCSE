THE REGULATION OF INTRACELLULAR CALCIUM
Publisher Summary This chapter discusses the regulation of intracellular calcium. The chapter illustrates that Ca 2+ is uniquely important in the regulation of cell function because it acts as a “messenger” that coordinates a multiplicity of intracellular reactions. The processes listed are the targets for Ca 2+, and the delivery of Ca 2+ to these processes is controlled largely by movements across the membranes of the cell and the subcellular organelles. This chapter discusses the mechanisms that have evolved for controlling the fluxes of Ca 2+ across these membranes, which direct a multitude of cellular events. The discussion of the different membrane systems involved in the regulation of Ca 2+ is prefaced by some general considerations of the chemistry of Ca 2+ , which may be helpful in understanding the basis of its role as a biological messenger. Then the discussion considers the structural role of Ca 2+ in different membrane systems and the location and physical state of Ca 2+ within cells. Finally, this chapter concludes by discussing the intracellular transport of Ca 2+ its transcellular transport.
