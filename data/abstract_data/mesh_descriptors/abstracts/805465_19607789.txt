Polyuridylic acid-directed phenylalanine incorporation by Lactobacillus acidophilus: effect of puromycin at acid pH.
The requirements for the polyuridylic acid-directed incorporation of phenylalanine into the trichloroacetic acid-precipitable material by a Lactobacillus acidophilus 30 000 × g supernatant fraction were essentially as described for comparable Escherichia coli preparations. One important difference was the relatively high Mg 2+ requirement. Phenylalanine incorporation by L. acidophilus extracts occurred over a relatively wide pH range which extended on the acid side to a point at which incorporation by E. coli extracts was abolished. Tetracycline antibiotics had a strong inhibitory effect on phenylalanine incorporation by L. acidophilus extracts at both pH 7.8 and 5.2. In contrast, puromycin while strongly inhibitory at pH 7.8 did not inhibit phenylalanine incorporation at pH 5.2 at a concentration of 500 μg/ml over a wide range of Mg 2+ and S-30 protein concentrations. The inhibitory effect of puromycin also decreased with a decrease in pH when E. coli extracts were employed over the pH range 7.7 to 5.8. Puromycin aminonucleoside failed to inhibit phenylalanine incorporation by L. acidophilus extracts at both pH 7.8 and pH 5.2. The properties of the poly(U)-dependent phenylalanine-incorporating system of L. acidophilus at pH 5.2 were similar in the presence and absence of puromycin in all the parameters tested. The L. acidophilus system is recommended for the study of cell-free protein synthesis particularly when synthesis over a wide pH range is desired.
