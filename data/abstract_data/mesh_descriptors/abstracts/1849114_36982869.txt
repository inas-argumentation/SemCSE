Estradiol is less potent than ethinyl estradiol for in vivo translocation of the mammalian liver estrogen receptor to the nucleus.
Estradiol (E2) or ethinyl estradiol (EE2) was administered to adult female rats, and the distribution of the mammalian liver estrogen receptor between the cytosol and the nucleus was examined 30 min later. The levels of total and occupied liver estrogen receptor (ER) in 30% ammonium sulfateprecipitated liver cytosol and purified nuclei were determined in vitro by exchange assays using [3H]E2. The redistribution of the ER from the cytosol to the nucleus was dose dependent for E2 as well as for EE2. E2 was less potent than EE2, and maximal translocation required relatively high doses. The dose necessary for a half-maximal increase in the total ER of purified nuclei was about 15 μg for EE2 and more than 100 fig for E2. In vitro, EE2 and E2 were equally effective in competing with [3H]E2 for binding to ammonium sulfate-precipitated liver cytosol ER.
