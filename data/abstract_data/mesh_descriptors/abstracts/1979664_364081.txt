Development of chronic lymphocytic leukaemia during ipilimumab therapy in a patient with metastatic melanoma
To the Editor: Ipilimumab is a monoclonal antibody against cytotoxic T lymphocyte associated protein 4 (CTLA-4), an immunological checkpoint on T cells. CTLA-4 blockade restores the activity of effector T cells, thus stimulating antitumour immune response. However, the prolonged stimulation of effector T cells leads to the onset of a unique set of adverse effects, the socalled immune-related adverse events (irAEs). These include rashes, colitis, hypophysitis and thyroiditis. Interestingly, the irAEs correlated with treatment response in some studies. An increased association of chronic lymphocytic leukaemia (CLL) and melanoma, possibly related to an underlying immunological defect, has been previously reported. While the development of CLL alongside melanoma has been reported during therapy with vemurafenib, an inhibitor of mutant V600 BRAF used in the treatment of melanoma, to the best of our knowledge, cancer duplicities have not yet been described with ipilimumab. Here, we report a case of a female patient with stage IV melanoma treated with four doses of ipilimumab and a subsequent re-induction regimen, who responded to the therapy with a 5 year stable disease. During therapy, elevation of absolute lymphocyte count (ALC) and leukocytosis occurred, which was attributed to clinical response to ipilimumab. Later, submandibular and cervical lymphadenopathy appeared, and a biopsy revealed B-cell CLL. The patient was diagnosed with nodular melanoma (Breslow index 1.8 mm) on her leg in 1995 at 42 years of age. One-year immunotherapy with interferon alpha was initiated. In 2009, a routine chest and abdomen CT scan revealed metastases in mediastinal, lung and retroperitoneal lymph nodes. The histological examination of a mediastinal lymph node biopsy showed melanoma involvement. Molecular genetics tests for BRAF and NRAS mutations showed wild type for both genes. After six cycles of chemotherapy with dacarbazine a CT scan showed progression of mediastinal and lung metastases. Ipilimumab was administered afterwards at a dose of 3 mg/kg, four infusions every 3 weeks. Stable disease was observed and remained for the following 18 months. A follow-up CT scan showed progression of mediastinal lymph nodes and elevation of LDH levels (5.25 lkat/L, 1.5 9 ULN). Therefore, the ipilimumab treatment was continued and re-induction at 3 mg/kg, four infusions every 3 weeks was applied. After the second infusion ALC was elevated, which was presumed to be a positive biomarker of clinical response and the therapy was continued, since changes in lymphocyte count slope and absolute number during ipilimumab treatment were previously reported to correlate with clinical response and survival. Patients with ALC over 1000/lL after two ipilimumab treatments had a significantly improved clinical benefit rate and median OS compared with those whose ALC was less than 1000/lL. By 2013, cervical and inguinal CT confirmed lymphadenopathy appeared (Fig. 1). The patient had normal LDH levels, but the haematological results revealed leukocytosis of 18.0 9 10/L and ALC elevation of 13 300 cells/lL. One lymph node from each affected area (cervical and inguinal) was extirpated, and histopathological examination revealed B-cell CLL in both lymph nodes (Fig. 2). The diagnosis of B-CLL was confirmed as Rai stage I (lymphocytosis plus enlarged lymph nodes; spleen and liver not enlarged; the red blood cell and platelet counts near normal) at the haematological department. Other characteristics included mutation of immunoglobulin variable region heavy chain (IgVH) and deletion on 13q14. Stable disease of metastatic melanoma lasting for 5 years was observed in this patient; however, the B-CLL is slowly progressing.
