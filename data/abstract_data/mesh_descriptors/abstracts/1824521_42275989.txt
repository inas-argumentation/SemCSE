Inferring structures of kinetic intermediates in Ca(2+)-triggered exocytosis.
Publisher Summary This chapter discusses the inferring structures of kinetic intermediates in Ca2+-triggered exocytosis. The fusion of biological membranes proceeds through a series of intermediates that are generated by structural transitions in proteins and deformation of lipid bilayers. In membrane fusion during Ca2+-triggered exocytosis, the key proteins have been identified and subjected to structural analysis. These proteins have been used to develop hypothetical mechanisms for exocytosis, often invoking explicit structures for key kinetic intermediates. The structures of these intermediates posit unique relations between proteins and lipids. The fusion of two membranes requires the distortion and controlled disruption of lipid bilayers. After being brought very close together, two lipid bilayer membranes are strained to the point where they can relax to a new bilayer structure in which the previously separate membranes have become contiguous. Elucidating the mechanism of membrane fusion is in essence an effort to characterize the distortions and to search for structures that can serve as kinetic intermediates.
