Total body exam or lesion detection screening for skin cancer?
According to a study by Isabelle Hoorens and colleagues, systematic total body examination and lesion detection screening for skin cancer have similar detection rates. Although lesion detection screening is 5·6 times less time-consuming, it has a much lower operational eff ectiveness. The adult population of Wichelin, Belgium, was invited to undergo standard total body examination in March, 2014; the following month, adults from Nevele, Belgium, were invited for lesion detection screening if they had one or more lesions that met the criteria for concern. The two communities are located in the same part of Belgium, have similar sized adult populations, and are similar socioeconomically. 1982 individuals were screened and there were 47 histologically confi rmed cases of skin cancer: 39 of which were diagnosed after total body examination and eight after lesion detection screening. The positive predictive value for all suspicious lesions was 56·6%. 1668 (17·9%) of 9325 invited individuals attended for total body examination and 314 (3·3%) of 9484 attended for lesion detection screening. The detection rate was similar for both screening methods (2·3% vs 3·2%, p=0·40); however, operational effectiveness was 0·4% versus 0·1% (p<0·01). The lesion detection screening group showed signifi cantly higher levels of anxiety at baseline, although this decreased signifi cantly after screening if no suspicious lesion was found. “We need to investigate how to increase the participation rate for lesion detection screening”, Hoorens (University Hospital Ghent, Ghent, Belgium) said. Notably, the lesion detection screening group had a much higher education level than the other group, which might account for the lower participation, since less well educated individuals might have found the lesion detection screening information document diffi cult to interpret. “It is not very easy to compare total body examination and lesion detection screening”, points out Cancer Research UK’s Clare Knight (London, UK). “Total body examination is straightforward screening, and lesion detection screening is for people who have found symptoms of skin cancer, it is more like a public awareness campaign”. In the UK, the decision on whether to introduce skin cancer screening is up to the National Screening Committee. An ongoing trial in Germany examining screening for skin cancer might be able to identify its eff ects on mortality. Meanwhile, Hoorens is working on a study to find out which approach is more cost-eff ective.
