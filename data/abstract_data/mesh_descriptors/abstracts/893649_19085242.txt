However, it should be noted that urine cytology positive for decoy cells does not always indicate the presence of plasma BK virus DNA, and thus careful follow-up is needed for patients who have undergone kidney transplantation. In the present study, we observed a maximum DNA value of 10 000 copies/mL in the four patients with plasma BK virus DNA. Given that the virus is present in detectable levels in nearly 100% of all cases of BKVN, demonstration of BK viremia by quantitative real-time PCR is a reliable test for nephritis, particularly in cases of 10 000 copies of DNA/mL. Further, quantitative real-time PCR may be useful in evaluating the therapeutic effects of immunosuppressant reduction. At present, no patients on whom we conducted quantitative real-time PCR following detection of plasma BK virus DNA (Patients 2, 3, 4, and 5) have experienced acute rejection. In conclusion, early diagnosis of BKV infection with reduction of immunosuppression may counter BK viremia and retard progression of BKV nephropathy. Decoy cell screening by urine cytology and diagnosis and follow-up by plasma BK virus DNA detection should be considered in kidney transplant recipients, particularly in those with impaired graft function.
