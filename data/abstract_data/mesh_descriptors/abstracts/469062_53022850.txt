Reflections on the Clinical Research Enterprise: Past, Present and Future.
Dr Califf replies: My mentors (Drs Eugene Stead, Bob Rosati, Galen Wagner, Ed Hammond, and Frank Starmer) led an effort to understand the outcomes of people with coronary disease and in so doing created one of the first databases in medicine in the 1960s. The idea was simple: record the characteristics of people who came to our hospital and clinics and measure not only their cardiovascular events but also how they felt and experienced symptoms and their overall quality of life. This effort was embedded into clinical practice together with analytical expertise as a routine expectation, not something done separately from practice.
