DNA-binding mode transition of tau in the presence of Zinc ions
Microtubule-associated protein tau is well-known for its role in regulating microtubule dynamicity and stability. Tau structure is divided into four major regions: N-terminal projection domain, proline-rich domain (PRD), microtubule binding domain (MTBD), and C-terminal domain. Tau proteins can be categorized into six different isoforms according to the number of projection domain repeats (N), and microtubule binding repeats (R) including: 0N/3R (tau 352), 1N/3R (tau 381), 2N/3R (tau 410), 0N/4R (tau 382), 1N/4R (tau 412), and 2N/4R (tau 441). Several functions have been linked to tau isoforms including participation in signaling pathways, DNA protection, and regulation of vesicular traffic. Binding to DNA is one of the most interesting features of tau which has been investigated by several groups (Hua & He, 2003). Migration of tau protein into nucleus and protecting DNA from oxidative damage has established the idea that tau is a DNA protecting agent. It has been indicated that tau binds to minor groove of DNA mostly via electrostatic interactions (Qi et al., 2015). Further investigations on 2N/4R tau, the biggest tau isoform, have illustrated that tau prefers to bind to minor groove of 13 bp AT-rich DNA sequences via its PRD and the second repeat (R2) of MTBD (Qi et al., 2015). Moreover, the presence of two cysteine residues in R2 and 3 in 4R tau isoforms enables tau protein to specifically coordinate Zn ions in a 1:1 stoichiometry (Mo et al., 2009). Coordination of Zn by tau causes conformational changes in MTBD and induces tau aggregation in vitro and regulates tau toxicity in vivo as well (Huang et al., 2014). The overlap between DNA-binding region and Zn-coordinating cysteine residues of tau suggests that coordination of Zn causes conformational change of DNA-binding region and have a great impact on DNAbinding properties of tau (Scheme 1). Therefore, we investigated tau–DNA interaction in the presence of Zn ions by using a set of spectrophotometric techniques. Our results indicated that DNA-binding mode of tau undergoes transition from minor groove binding to major groove binding in the presence of Zn ions.
