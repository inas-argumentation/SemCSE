Malignant transformation of a lacrimal gland pleomorphic adenoma with pulmonary metastases
In 2001, a 70-year-old Caucasian man presented to the Royal Adelaide Hospital with a painless left orbital mass enlarging over several months. On examination, he had slight inferonasal globe displacement, 5 mm of proptosis and a slight left supraduction deficit with diplopia in extreme up-gaze. Computed tomography (CT) demonstrated a well-circumscribed, heterogeneous lesion located posterior to the lacrimal gland (Figure 1a). Although the appearance favoured a lacrimal gland pleomorphic adenoma (LGPA), atypical imaging characteristics and short duration of symptoms suggested possible malignancy. An incisional biopsy was performed through an upper eyelid crease incision to avoid breach of the periorbita in case of malignancy, but pathology was inconclusive. A month later, he underwent a lateral orbitotomy to completely macroscopically excise the tumour and biopsy tract. Histology showed intact excision of a pleomorphic adenoma, with microscopic focal tumour extensions into the capsule (Figure 1b). The patient was followed up closely with no evidence of recurrence. After 2 years, he relocated interstate and was lost to follow-up. He presented to the Royal Melbourne Hospital in August 2012 with a mass in the left upper lid, slight reduction in abduction and mild upper lid ptosis. CT scan showed two lesions in the superior and lateral left orbit measuring 19 and 14 mm in maximal dimension, respectively (Figure 2a). In addition, over 15 asymptomatic nodules were discovered throughout both lungs (Figure 2c). A fludeoxyglucose positron emission tomography scan suggested lacrimal tumour recurrence with possible pulmonary metastases. An endobronchial ultrasound-guided biopsy of a lung lesion was attempted, but no tissue was obtained. Bronchial brushings and washings showed glandular tumour cells with similar morphology to the 2001 specimen, consistent with metastases from lacrimal gland. In September 2012, he underwent a repeat lateral orbitotomy. Both lesions were purplish, soft, welldemarcated tumours. The larger superior orbital lesion appeared lobulated, with the tumour capsule breached at the superior margin. Histology of both lesions showed an epithelioid tumour, similar in appearance to the specimen from 2001. However, within the superior lesion, there was infiltration of adjacent tissues by a largely cribriform component, not seen in 2001. This appearance was consistent with an adenoid cystic carcinoma arising from a recurrent pleomorphic adenoma (‘carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma’, or CEPA) (Figure 2b). There was no perineural invasion or bony involvement, but the lesion involved the surgical margins. In discussion with the radiation oncology department, due to the presumed pulmonary metastases, a decision was made to closely observe both clinically and radiographically. At last follow-up, 24 months following surgery, he remained asymptomatic from the orbital and respiratory lesions. The examination findings were unchanged from 9 months prior. On the left, visual acuity was 6/9, and he had 2 mm of enophthalmos, 2 mm of upper lid ptosis with slightly reduced levator function, a small supraduction deficit and a moderate abduction deficit with diplopia on extreme left gaze.
