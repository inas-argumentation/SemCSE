Role of a Ca2+-binding glycoprotein in the calcification process
A glycoprotein has been recently identified as a component of the matrix of a preosseous cartilage [17, 18]. The interest in such a compound derives from the fact that it has a strong affinity for Ca 2+ and it exhibits the catalytic properties of an alkaline phosphatase [16]. These features strongly suggest that this glycoprotein plays a role in the process of calcification and evidence was sought to verify this possibility. It was of primary interest to determine whether or not the glycoprotein was a specific constituent of the matrix of a calcifiable cartilage. If this were the case, nasal septum cartilage, which does not undergo calcification, should be devoid of this protein. By using the technique previously described [17, 18], a macromolecule was isolated from nasal septum showing the same electrophoretic and staining properties as the glycoprotein of calf scapula cartilage.
