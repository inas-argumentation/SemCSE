HIV infection in female prostitutes from Benin: association with symptomatic but not asymptomatic gonococcal or chlamydial infections.
A cross-sectional study was conducted between March 1993 and March 1994 among female prostitutes in Benin to determine the prevalence of HIV infection and associated risk factors. 242 prostitutes were recruited in Cotonou and 132 from other southern towns of the country. Data on potential risk factors were collected in interviews while speculum and bimanual gynecological examinations were performed on each participant by a physician. Vaginal and endocervical smears were prepared and urine and blood specimens taken for laboratory testing of HIV and other sexually transmitted diseases. 195 women were found to be HIV seropositive. Among the HIV-positive 75.9% were infected with HIV-1 4.6% with HIV-2 and 19.5% with both HIV-1 and HIV-2. Asymptomatic gonococcal or chlamydial infections were not associated with HIV seropositivity. However a diagnosis of gonococcal or chlamydial infection together with one or more cervical signs was associated with HIV seropositivity. Furthermore there was a statistically significant linear trend between the number of cervical signs and HIV seropositivity in the presence of gonococcal or chlamydial infections. Multiple logistic regression found symptomatic gonorrhea or chlamydial infection candidiasis positive syphilis serology genital warts and consistent condom use to be associated with HIV infection. The relationship between condom use and HIV infection may possibly be explained by behavior change among HIV-positive women.
