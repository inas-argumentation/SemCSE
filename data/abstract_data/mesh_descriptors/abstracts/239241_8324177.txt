Predictive factors of post-operative myasthenic crisis after thymectomy: the role of surgical invasiveness
We read with great interest the article by Shanshan Y [1] reporting on the predictors of post-operative myasthenic crises (POMCs) after thymectomy in a series of 178 patients with myasthenia gravis (MG). The myasthenic crises are a well-known exacerbation of the symptoms that may render, in those cases that undergo surgery, the preand post-operative management rather difficult. In fact, except for the recommendation that the operation should be performed in a centre where physicians, surgeons, anaesthesiologists and nursing staff are familiar with the peculiar preand postop care problems correlated with MG [2], there is no general agreement on the management of the patient with MG who is thymectomised. As well, the anaesthesiologist is often immediately faced with decisions concerning patients’ ability to tolerate tracheal extubation or their need for intensive care. For this reason, the pre-operative evaluation of MG patients is central to the success of surgery and the post-operative course and the knowledge of predictors of POMCs is essential for anaesthesiologists and surgeons in the preand peri-operative setting. In literature, few authors have analyzed the predictive factors of POMCs. Loach et al. [3] reported that VC of <2.0 l, thymoma, bulbar symptoms and age >50 years influenced POMCs. Other authors [4] reported that the presence bulbar symptoms was the only predictive factor, while others [5] showed a strong correlation with preoperative bulbar symptoms, antiAChR level higher than 100 nmol/l and intra-operative blood loss higher than 1000 ml.
