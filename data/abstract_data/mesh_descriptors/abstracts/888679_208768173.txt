Cell interactions in collective cell migration
ABSTRACT Collective cell migration is the coordinated movement of a physically connected group of cells and is a prominent driver of development and metastasis. Interactions between cells within migrating collectives, and between migrating cells and other cells in the environment, play key roles in stimulating motility, steering and sometimes promoting cell survival. Similarly, diverse heterotypic interactions and collective behaviors likely contribute to tumor metastasis. Here, we describe a sampling of cells that migrate collectively in vivo, including well-established and newer examples. We focus on the under-appreciated property that many – perhaps most – collectively migrating cells move as cooperating groups of distinct cell types. Summary: In this Development at a Glance article, we discuss examples of homotypic and heterotypic collective cell migration during development and cancer metastasis.
