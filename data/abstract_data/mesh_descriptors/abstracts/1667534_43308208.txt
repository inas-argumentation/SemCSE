Renal gluconeogenesis: effects of quinolinic acid.
Quinolinic acid, a metabolic degradation product of tryptophan metabolism and a known inhibitor of hepatic gluconeogenesis in vivo, has been shown in the present studies to inhibit renal gluconeogenesis in vitro. At pH 7.4 quinolinic acid decreased glucose production from glutamine and glutamate by rat renal cortex by more than 50%. No effect of 0.5 mM quinolinic acid on glucose formation was observed when α-ketoglutarate, succinate, fumarate, malate, citrate, glycerol, or fructose were used as substrates. Quinolinic acid inhibited the activity of the enzyme glutamate dehydrogenase. Decreased glucose formation from glutamine and glutamate by renal cortex in the presence of quinolinic acid is probably due to inhibition of glutamate dehydrogenas.
