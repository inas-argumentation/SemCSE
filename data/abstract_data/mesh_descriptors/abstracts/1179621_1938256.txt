In vitro analysis of the bacterial protein export.
Based on the results of in vitro studies the signal hypothesis has been introduced (Blobel and Dobberstein 1975) as a model to describe sorting and subsequent transport of newly synthesized proteins across and into, respectively, the membrane of the endoplasmic reticulum. Some of the predictions of the signal hypothesis have recently been verified by the identification and biochemical characterization of two components of the transport machinery of the endoplasmic reticulum. One of them is called the signal recognition particle (SRP), a peripheral membrane ribonucleoprotein complex that specifically recognizes the nascent polypeptide chains of secretory and integral membrane proteins (Walter et al. 1981). The other component is the SRP receptor (Gilmore et al. 1982), a single polypeptide which is integrated into the membrane of the endoplasmic reticulum. It is also called docking protein (Meyer et al. 1982).
