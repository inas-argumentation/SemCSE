The need for drug monitoring in psychiatric practice.
Vast sums of national money are spent annually on psychotropic drugs. Most patients who have psychiatric disorders are treated chemotherapeutically. In Britain today a £20 million annual national expenditure on psychotropic drugs represents approximately 60 million separate prescriptions per year.

Drugs are released for clinical use following toxicity studies in animals and pharmacological studies in man. Both these techniques are poor indicators of any but the most frequent side-effects, whether trivial or lethal.

Since drugs are vital for efficacious treatment of psychiatric disorders, it must be ensured that vast numbers of patients are not exposed to drug risks which may be avoidable. The way in which these risks may be assessed is to introduce a drug monitoring system which is capable of identifying, quantifying and qualifying side-effects and the factors that influence this. Applied to many thousands of patients, drug monitoring systems can lead to greater therapeutic safety and refinement and thus to improved patient care.
