The previous lower estimates and the more recent higher ones were largely derived from selected populations, so the frequency of primary aldosteronism in the overall hypertensive population remains uncertain. F R Buhler and colleagues first used the ARR in 1973 4 to identify people with low renin hypertension. P J Dunn and E A Espiner first applied the test to the diagnosis of primary aldosteronism in 1976. 5 However, clinical use of the test began after the 1981 publication by K Hiramatsu and colleagues 6 of ARR data from 348 people with untreated hypertension. The investigators reported that nine people had a high ratio and all had an aldosterone-producing adenoma. Gordon and his colleagues 7 in Brisbane, Australia recommended the ARR as a valid screening test. They reported that recognition of primary aldosteronism in their clinic abruptly increased from two to five cases per year before routine use of the ARR, to over 50 per year after its use. Soon thereafter, the deluge of reports and abstracts began, almost all stating that a high ARR proved primary aldosteronism.
