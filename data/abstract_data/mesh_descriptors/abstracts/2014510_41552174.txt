The excretion of 5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid in mental patients.
Publisher Summary This chapter discusses the effect of reserpine on the urinary excretion of 5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid (HIAA) in mental patients. The patients used in this study were carefully selected for long-term studies on aging and mental disease and were available for short-term experiments. Such an arrangement made possible the collection of 24 hours urine specimens and the quantitative study of the effect of reserpine upon the urinary excretion of 5-HIAA in these patients. Reserpine, 2 mg twice daily, was administered to 11 schizophrenic patients and to 6 high-grade mentally defective patients. The excretion of 5-HIAA was increased on the first day of medication in both the groups of patients. The excretion of the serotonin metabolite after the first day of medication and after its cessation was reduced to premedication levels or less. When these patients were given another course of reserpine at a later date, the excretory pattern of 5-HIAA was essentially the same as during the first course. The most consistent feature of the experiments was the marked rise in the excretion of the serotonin metabolite on the first day of reserpine medication.
