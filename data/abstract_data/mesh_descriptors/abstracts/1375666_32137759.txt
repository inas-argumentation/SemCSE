Editorial: collecting new external evidence for cholesterol management.
DOI:10.1097/MED.0000000000000244 I completed my medical training in the early 1970s in Canada. My first mentor there was Jack Laidlaw and my hero was David Sackett, both pioneers of ‘evidence-based medicine’. Sadly, the world lost both of them in 2015. It goes without saying that all my life I have tried to practice medicine according to their principles. In 1996, David Sackett wrote: ‘Evidence based medicine is not restricted to randomized trials and meta-analyses. It involves tracking down the best external evidence with which to answer clinical questions . . . If no randomized trial has been carried out for our patient’s predicament, we must follow the trail to the next best external evidence and work from here’ [1]. In November 2013, new guidelines for the treatment of dyslipidemia were issued in the USA [2]. In response, there was a huge outcry from national-level organizations and individual experts over the change in evaluation of risk and treatment recommendations as the new guidelines contrasted with previously held ideas about lipid treatment. The new guidelines were written in the spirit of evidence-based medicine. The authors specified: ‘Recommendations were derived from randomized trials, meta-analyses, and observational studies evaluated for quality, and were not formulated when sufficient evidence was not available . . . The ultimate decision about care of a particular patient must be made by the healthcare provider and patient in light of the circumstances presented by that patient. As a result, situations might arise in which deviations from these guidelines may be appropriate’. In plain English, the new guidelines demonstrated a solid reliance on evidence-based medicine by providing recommendations only based on conclusive data gathered, and leaving any clinical judgment that did not yet have such data to support a recommendation up to the physician. As a new editor of Current Opinion in Endocrinology, Diabetes and Obesity, I will strive to select review authors who have themselves contributed cutting-edge research to ‘external evidence’, and thereby assisting clinicians in their clinical decision-making beyond the current guidelines.
