In the patient described by Bugnoni et al (6), the authors believed that the abnormal lymphocytes were due to the expression of a peripheral T-cell lymphoma with a very indolent clinical course (6). In our patient, the proliferation was monoclonal. We believe that this T-cell proliferation represents a low-grade clonal disorder and perhaps a premalignant nonaggressive disease. Indeed, malignant T-cell lymphoma may appear several years after the recognition of hypereosinophilic syndrome (9,10). In conclusion, there is a group of hypereosinophilic syndromes associated with T-cell proliferative disorders. These syndromes have an uncertain prognosis and can have varied clinical presentations. A lymphocyte immunophenotypic analysis should be included in the diagnostic workup of patients with hypereosinophilic syndrome, including the variant type of episodic angioedema and eosinophilia with benign course. However, documentation of T-cell phenotypes or clonal disorders in these patients does not yet implicate therapy or prognosis.
