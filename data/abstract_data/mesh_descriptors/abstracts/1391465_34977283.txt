Determinants of hypertension-related cardiac organ damage: beyond the haemodynamic overload.
Editorials, reviews and original papers published in the past few years have repeatedly shown that the renin– angiotensin–aldosterone axis and the sympathetic nervous system mutually interact with each other in both physiological and pathophysiological states. For example, it has been clearly documented that physiological modulation of vasomotor tone, regulation of circulating blood volume and control of blood pressure closely depend on the two above-mentioned systems, as well as on their mutual interactions [1–4]. In addition, the reciprocal reinforcement that the two systems exert on cardiovascular function also has pathophysiological relevance, as documented by the evidence that in a variety of diseases, such as hypertension, congestive heart failure, myocardial infarction, stroke, cirrhosis and renal failure [4], the renin–angiotensin–sympathetic crosstalks exert unfavourable haemodynamic and metabolic effects, which together adversely affect disease progression as well as the patient’s prognosis [5–10].
