Rescue of acute complete portal vein occlusion with Doppler ultrasound findings after liver transplantation.
Portal vein occlusion (PVO) is a rare complication after liver transplantation (LT). However, the mortality is usually high, especially if the main portal vein is completely occluded in the early postoperative period (1, 2). Occlusion of the main trunk of the portal vein usually requires immediate treatment (3). We report a case of acute PVO without portal vein thrombosis (PVT) within 24 hr after orthotopic LT. The PVO was relieved at exploratory laparotomy. To our knowledge, this is the first case report of acute PVO without PVT after LT in adult that was successfully resolved without sequelae. The patient was a 37-year-old woman with autoimmune hepatitis. Her symptoms of end-stage liver disease included high bilirubin levels, coagulopathy, and edema of her lower extremities. Her Model for End-Stage Liver Disease score was 26. Orthotopic LT was performed on May 10, 2007. The donor was a 40-yearold woman. The portal vein graft was relatively short because of pancreas procurements. An aberrant left hepatic artery was noticed. The portal vein anastomosis was performed in an end-to-end fashion with growth factor (4). Intraoperative Doppler ultrasound of liver (DUSL) showed that all vessels were patent, and no abnormality was seen. The first laboratory results revealed prothrombin time of 14.9 sec, fibrinogen of 193 mg/dL, total bilirubin of 3.4 mg/dL, direct bilirubin of 2.3 mg/dL, serum glutamic oxaloacetic transaminase of 1187 U/L, serum glutamic pyruvic transaminase of 340 U/L, and -glutamyl transpeptidase of 44 U/L. The next day, the patient was awake, alert, oriented and on minimal ventilator support. Vital signs were stable. Laboratory data showed prothrombin time of 12.6 sec, fibrinogen of 230 mg/dL, total bilirubin of 3.5 mg/dL, direct bilirubin of 2.3 mg/dL, serum glutamic oxaloacetic transaminase of 619 U/L, serum glutamic pyruvic transaminase of 271 U/L, and -glutamyl transpeptidase of 73 U/L. Doppler ultrasound of liver was performed as a routine protocol at 4:00 P.M. on May 11, 2007, which revealed complete occlusion of the main portal vein (Fig. 1A). She was emergently brought back to the operating room. The liver was shrunken, dark, and hypovolemic. Several blood clots were seen around the portal vein. Also, the main portal vein was kinked at the site of anastomosis and the bile duct was tight. Doppler ultrasound of liver was then performed, which again showed complete occlusion of the main portal vein. A portogram was performed to evaluate the presence and degree of collateral flow and thrombus. The main portal vein was completely occluded at the site of anastomosis (Fig. 1B). After evacuation of the blood clot around the main portal vein, the bile duct was tied and cut at the site of previous anastomosis (Fig. 1C). Another DUSL showed that the main portal vein flow had fully recovered (Fig. 1D).
