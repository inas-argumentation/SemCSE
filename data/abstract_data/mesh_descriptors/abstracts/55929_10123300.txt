Intensification of polypharmacy glucose-lowering treatment in type-2 diabetes: when to be cautious?
Type 2 diabetes is a condition in which morbidity–mortality remains high, despite a trend to improvement in the last 10 years. The hazard ratios for cause-specific death, according to baseline diabetes status, among 820,900 people in 97 prospective studies, recently published by the Emerging Risk Factors Collaboration group [1], have confirmed the well known excess risk for vascular disease but have also shown that diabetes is associated with substantial premature death from nonvascular cause such as cancers, infectious diseases, and degenerative disorders. While statins and antihypertensive agents have been demonstrated to lower cardiovascular risk in type 2 diabetes, the benefits of intensified glucose-lowering treatment remain controversial, especially since the disappointing results of the recent large cardio-vascular outcome studies (ACCORD – ADVANCE – VADT). Consequently it has been suggested that efforts and resources should be directed to those who have much more to gain from tight glucose control and pay more attention to the unwanted effects of intensified therapy [2]. This is why individualization of both HbA1c goals and the means of reaching them have recently been emphasized and is becoming the key concept of good clinical practice in the management of people with type 2 diabetes [3].
