The radiographic appearance of normal and metastatic axillary lymph nodes.
The radiographic appearance of lymph nodes is of interest for two main reasons: 1. Lymph nodes are frequently visible on mammograms. They are probably the most common discrete mass found in the breast (Wolfe 1977). They may be located anywhere in the breast but occur most often in its axillary tail. 2. The preoperative assessment of lymphatic dissemination in breast cancer is of prime importance for the classification and proper planning of treatment. The presence of lymph node metastases is a very strong prognostic marker in breast cancer. In the clinical staging of breast carcinoma, interpretation of the findings of axillary palpation plays an important role. However, the palpation of the axilla is the most difficult part of the clinical examination and the clinical examination of the axilla is rather inaccurate (Johnsen et al. 1975).
