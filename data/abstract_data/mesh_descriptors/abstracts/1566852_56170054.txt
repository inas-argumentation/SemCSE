Relocation of Tn2009 and characterization of an ABGRI3-2 from re-sequenced genome sequence of Acinetobacter baumannii MDR-ZJ06
Sir, Acinetobacter baumannii is becoming an important opportunistic Gram-negative pathogen and has shown extensive MDR to most first-line antibiotics in recent years. The acquisition of the MDR phenotype is a determining factor for the success of A. baumannii. For example, blaOXA-23 is the most common acquired gene for carbapenem resistance, and it is acquired via mobile element. Our previous studies identified six clones (clones A–F) of imipenem-resistant A. baumannii strains isolated from 16 cities in 2005 in China and clone C was the dominant clone in various cities. One isolate of clone C, MDR-ZJ06, which belonged to global clone 2, was sequenced by the 454 Genome Sequencer FLX system, Illumina sequencer and Sanger sequencing. The blaOXA-23 of MDR-ZJ06 was located in Tn2009, which was initially named as part of the analysis of MDR-ZJ06 and was mostly reported in isolates from China. In this study, we present the revised complete genome sequence of A. baumannii MDR-ZJ06, which is widespread in China. Materials and methods are available as Supplementary data at JAC Online. The sequence data for the revised genome of A. baumannii MDR-ZJ06 have been deposited in GenBank under the accession number CP001937.2. The revised genome of A. baumannii MDR-ZJ06 has 4 022 279 nucleotides, with 39% GC content. It is larger than the original genomic sequence (4 011 434). MDR-ZJ06 did not harbour any plasmid in the revised sequence. A sequence previously reported as a plasmid in MDR-ZJ06 was integrated into the revised MDR-ZJ06 chromosome. Among the 3212 genes predicted, 3079 were protein-coding genes, and 62 RNAs were also identified. In total, 998 genes were affected in the revised genomic sequence of MDRZJ06. Of the modified genes, 73 genes were now present, 161 genes were no longer present, 698 genes were shorter and 66 genes were longer. MLST analysis showed that the revised A. baumannii MDR-ZJ06 belongs to ST2 (2-2-2-2-2-2-2, Institut Pasteur) and ST643 (1-3-3-2-2-169-3, University of Oxford scheme). The previously reported MLST of MDR-ZJ06 was ST90 (1-3-3-2-2-62-3). The difference in MLST was probably caused by a different primer sequence being used for MLST and not by a sequencing error. There were also several small sequencing errors detected when the revised genomic sequence of MDR-ZJ06 was compared with the previously reported MDR-ZJ06 (Table S1). The dominant sources of sequencing errors were homopolymer-associated indel errors (76%, 239/313). Other errors were substitutions (4%, 13/313) and indels of 1–2 bp (18%, 56/313). The 454 Genome Sequencer FLX system, the Illumina sequencer and the Sanger method were used to sequence the previous MDR-ZJ06 genome, and PacBio single-molecule sequencing technology was used in this study. The difference between the two genomic sequences demonstrated the advantage of PacBio in de novo assemblies of novel genomes. Though the error rate of PacBio data is higher than that of the second-generation sequencing technologies, increased coverage or hybrid sequencing could greatly improve the accuracy, and long reads could also provide reliable alignments and scaffolds. In the future, the revised genomic sequence of MDR-ZJ06 should be used in comparative genome research, rather than the former MDR-ZJ06 genomic sequence. Genes for resistance to aminoglycosides (strB, strA, aadA1, aadA1, aacC1, armA, aacA4, aphA1), b-lactams (blaOXA-23, ampC), fluoroquinolones [aac(60)Ib-cr], macrolides [mph(E), msr(E)], phenicols (catB8), sulphonamides (sul1, sul1, sul2) and tetracyclines [tet(B)] were identified. There were two copies of two resistance genes, aadA1 and sul1. Each aadA1 and sul1 was located in a separate chromosomal resistance island, AbGRI2 and AbGRI3. Tn2009 was relocated at another region of the revised chromosome of MDR-ZJ06 (Figure 1a). There was one copy of ISAba1 in the old sequence at the correct location of the new Tn2009 insertion site, whereas there were two copies of ISAba1 in the revised sequence at the old Tn2009 position. The 9 bp target-site DR sequences were revised from AATTTTTTG to AAATATTTT. It should be mentioned that a recently published genome of a GC2 isolate, XH386, harboured Tn2009 in the same position and the same ISAba1 as revised MDR-ZJ06, and its DR sequence was also AAATATTTT. There were five different forms of AbGRI3 identified in A. baumannii in a previous study. The re-sequenced genome of MDRZJ06 was found to contain a form of AbGRI3, named version 2, which included both Tn6180 and Tn6179 (AbGRI3-2) but 24.6 kb of adjacent chromosomal sequence was missing (Figure 1b).
