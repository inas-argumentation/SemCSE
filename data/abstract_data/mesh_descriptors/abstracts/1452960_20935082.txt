Hormonal control of spermatogenesis in Hirudo medicinalis. II. Testicular response to brain removal during the phase of testicular maturity.
Brain removal from leeches during the phase of testicular maturity has three results: (1) A sharp decrease in intermediate stages (stages 2 and 3) of spermatogenesis; (2) a general decrease in total numbers of sperm clusters of all stages in the testes; and (3) an increase in the numbers of amoebocytes present in the testes. The first symptom is restored to normal by injections of brain macerates and is suggested to be a specific effect of lack of a brain gonadotropin. The second and third symptoms are only partially remedied by brain injections and are present in all operated control animals as well. Although they may be induced, in part, by the lack of a brain gonadotropin, it is suggested that they are mainly due to the loss of food reserves resulting from the operative procedures. The brain gonadotropin appears to exert its primary effect upon a single stage of spermatogenesis, having weaker effects upon earlier and later stages. Differential cluster counts, as well as quantitative protein, acid phosphatase, and succinic dehydrogenase assay procedures were carried out in arriving at the above conclusions.
