Isolation and spectroscopic analyses of S-100 proteins and their interactions with metal ions.
Publisher Summary This chapter describes the procedures used for isolating S-100 proteins from beef brain and focuses on how spectroscopic techniques can be used to probe this system. For each spectral method used, its application to this system is indicated by citing an example along with the nature of the information that is derived from this technique. It is illustrated how different spectroscopic analyses can be applied to advantage in gathering structural information on S-100 proteins and also, where possible, to illustrate how different techniques can complement each other in providing the structural information. The highly acidic water-soluble S-100 protein fraction is mainly a nervous tissue-specific protein system, found primarily in the cytoplasm of glial cells. It is found in the brain of both invertebrates and vertebrates with a constant immunological response. S-100 proteins are also known to bind antipsychotic drugs and other metal ions, and to modulate protein phosphorylation in the brain. Assay of immunoreactive S-100 are used to diagnose and classify tumors of neural origin and as a marker for various types of tumors. The major sources for isolating S-100 proteins are bovine, rat, and human brains.
