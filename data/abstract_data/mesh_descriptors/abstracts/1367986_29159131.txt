Studies suggest the HIV-infected population is also at an increased risk of fracture with rates 30%–70% higher than those among matched uninfected control subjects.16 In our study, the overall median FRAX score for major osteoporotic fracture was 4.0%. This is consistent with previous work that demonstrated mean FRAX scores of 4.1% for major osteoporotic fracture in HIV patients with normal BMD and 3.8% in patients with low BMD.17 Recent guidelines from the Infectious Diseases Society of America recommend screening with DXA in HIV-infected subjects aged 50 years with additional risk factors for osteopenia and/or osteoporosis.18 In our cohort of patients, only 0.2% of patients were identified to have had DEXA scans. Although there has been significant research into the associations between cardiovascular risk and risk of bone disease in HIV-infected patients, there have not been any studies assessing the correlation between them to our knowledge. Although the number of patients is included is relatively low, it shows that the correlation between fracture risk and cardiovascular risk is not strong using commonly available tools that require a limited data set, and that in most individuals, cardiovascular risk is significantly greater than fracture risk. Independent calculation of FRS and FRAX is useful in individuals over 40 in tailoring antiretroviral therapy.
