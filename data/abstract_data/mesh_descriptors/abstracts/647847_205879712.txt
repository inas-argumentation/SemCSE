Mimicking in vivo tumors to visualize the cell cycle
Visualizing the cell-cycle behavior of cancer cells aids better understanding of how cancer cells respond to a given chemotherapeutic drug because cancer cells in G0/G1 phase are resistant to cytotoxic chemotherapy. Characterization of cell-cycle behavior by imaging was first performed by Miyawaki and colleagues using fluorescent ubiquitination-based cell cycle indicator (FUCCI).1 The original FUCCI probe was generated by fusing mKO2 (monomeric Kusabira Orange2) and mAG (monomeric Azami Green) to the ubiquitination domains of human Cdt1 (hCdt1) and human geminin (hGem), respectively. These two chimeric proteins, mKO2-hCdt1 and mAG-hGem, accumulate reciprocally in the nuclei of transfected mammalian cells during the cell cycle, labeling nuclei of G0/G1-phase cells orange and those of S/G2/M-phase cells green.
