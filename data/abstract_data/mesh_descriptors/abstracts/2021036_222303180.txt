Oral mucosal hyperpigmentation in a patient with a previous diagnosis of melanoma of the scalp
A 79-year-old man presented with multiple gray-brownish lesions on his lips and in his oral cavity. The pigmented lesions had been present for 18 months and had progressively become more evident. The patient’s past medical history included a melanoma of the vertex of the scalp with a Breslow thickness (BT) of 0.23 mm, which was excised nine years ago and characterized by regressive features. The patient had undergone wide surgical excision and no clinical or instrumental evidence of recurrence had been detected during a follow-up period of five years. The patient had not made any further dermatologic visits until the current presentation. On examination, multiple oval brownish smooth macules with well-defined margins were spread over the lips and in the oral cavity. The largest pigmented lesion, measuring about 1 cm in diameter, was visible on the hard palate (Figure 1a, b). No similar lesions were observed on other body areas, and there were no signs of local recurrence at the scarred excision site. Dermatoscopy revealed a brown regular pseudo-reticular pattern on the lip lesions and a slightly atypical fingerprint-like pattern on the accessible lesions of the buccal mucosa (Figure 1c, d). The largest oral hyperpigmented lesion was not examined dermatoscopically due to anatomic impairment.
