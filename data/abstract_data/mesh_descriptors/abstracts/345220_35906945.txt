Kinetics of phosphatidylethanolamine biosynthesis from (I,I-3H2)sphingosine in rat liver.
Abstract Earlier studies have shown that after injection of labeled ethanolamine into rats the specific radioactivity of liver CDPethanolamine is significantly higher than that of phosphorylethanolamine. Since this is incompatible with a precursor-product relationship between the total liver pools of these compounds, we have investigated their metabolism using another labeled precursor. [1,1- 3 H 2 ]Sphingosine was injected intraportally into male rats and the appearance of radioactivity in different ethanolamine-containing compounds in liver was followed at different times after injection. The specific radioactivity of phosphorylethanolamine was higher or equal to that of CDPethanolamine, indicating that phosphorylethanolamine formed from sphingosine is net metabolically equivalent to that formed from injected ethanolamine. This difference in the labeling of the water-soluble precursors to phosphatidylethanolamines may be due to the different subcellular localization of the enzymes, catalyzing the formation of phosphorylethanolamine from ethanolamine and sphingosine 1-phosphate.
