Additional testing was performed including autoimmune work-up with antinuclear antibodies and IgG4, fasting lipid panel including triglycerides, repeat comprehensive metabolic panel, coagulation panel, and a pancreatic protocol computed tomography scan, all of which was unrevealing. He also underwent an upper endoscopy with visualization of the ampulla, which was also unremarkable. Given our suspicion for marathon (exertional) pancreatitis as his symptoms correlated well with long-distance running, his lack of any other risk factors for pancreatitis, and his negative evaluation for other etiologies of pancreatitis, the patient was advised to discontinue long-distance running and to find another athletic pursuit. The patient was very reluctant to stop running and made it clear that he was unsure whether he could discontinue running. One month later, he again had an episode of severe epigastric pain that developed in the context of a longdistance run, at approximately 12 miles, and for which he required hospitalization. Initial blood work on admission revealed a lipase level of 20,000U/L (0 to 59U/L). He again recovered with conservative management. At this point, the patient returned to our clinic and was again advised to discontinue running and he reluctantly agreed to do so. After cessation of running, he has not had any episodes of recurrent pancreatitis in 8 months and feels well. Of note, there was a discussion with patient and his parents about the value of obtaining genetic testing for cystic fibrosis mutations and other causes of hereditary pancreatitis, but given his improvement after his cessation of running and the associated costs, they decided not to pursue testing at this time. To date, there have only been a limited number of case reports describing marathon (exertional) pancreatitis. Several mechanisms have been proposed to explain the etiology of this condition including ischemic injury from physical stress and mechanical trauma. The severe systemic stress caused by long-distance running may induce pancreatitis by ischemic injury. It has also been suggested that in longdistance runners, pancreatitis could be induced by mechanical trauma caused by repetitive injury of the pancreas against the posterior abdominal wall and spine. Our patient’s case is similar to a case presented by Mast et al, as our patient also presented with pancreatitis in conjunction with long-distance runs. In the absence of any other risk factors for pancreatitis and our patient’s timing of his pancreatitis episodes with long-distance running on multiple occasions, the association is very difficult to ignore and we feel that marathon pancreatitis does represent a distinct clinical entity.
