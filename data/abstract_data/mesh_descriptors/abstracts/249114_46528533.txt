Response to Gilbert & Walker 'treading the path of least resistance'.
Gilbert and Walker’s (2002) analysis of the economic dimensions that underlie the AIDS pandemic in South Africa provides a critical dimension to understanding the social context in which the disease is spread. Unfortunately in as much as these authors provide an ecological understanding of the factors that promote the spread of disease they are silent on the salience of the well-established etiology of infection. Their failure to acknowledge that high-risk behavior directly influences opportunities for HIV infection and their premature dismissal of the role of behavior change to reduce infection rates is damaging to the development of social policies aimed at encouraging health-promoting behavior. Thus Gilbert and Walker’s analysis may be overly deterministic in terms of its emphasis on the socioeconomic forces affecting behavior and it thereby ignores human agency and the ability of communities and as individuals to assume control and effect change.
