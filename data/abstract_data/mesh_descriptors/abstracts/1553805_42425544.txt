Factors influencing the isolation of a polyribosomal system from rat liver and its ability to incorporate [14C]amino acids.
Abstract Improved methods are described for the isolation and assay of an amino acid incorporation system from rat liver in vitro. N-2-Hydroxyethylpiperazine-N′-2-ethanesulfonic acid rather than Tris buffers were utilized to isolate cell sap and polyribosomes at different pH optima (6.4 and 7.6, respectively). The optimal pH of the incubation media, however, was 7.3. The optimal ionic concentrations of the buffers used to isolate cell sap enzymes and polyribosomes and for the incubation system in vitro were 3.75, 5.00, 5.00–5.25 mM Mg2+, respectively. Also, 75 mM K+ was used in all buffers. Glutathione in the extraction media enhanced the rate of amino acid incorporation; hence no additional thiol source was required in the incubation medium. Deoxycholate (1.67% of post-mitochondrial supernatant) allowed the isolation of more active polyribosomes than any of the Triton detergents tested and gel filtration of cell sap with Sephadex G-10 yielded a more active preparation for protein synthesis in vitro than filtration with Sephadex G-25, Bio-Gel P-2, P-4 or P-6. Incorporation in vitro of amino acids was linear for only 2 min at 37° when 45 μg of cell sap protein was used per μg of polyribosomal protein. A ratio of 22.5 μg cell sap protein per μg polyribosomal protein saturated the assay system in vitro, however. An ATP generating system was recommended since high ATP levels proved to be deleterious. Also a mixture of 20 l -amino acids at 10 μM each was added to the assay system.
