The nucleic acid and protein content of root tip cells of Vicia faba and Allium cepa.
The DNA, RNA, and protein content of the cells of the first two millimeters of Allium cepa (onion) and the first three millimeters of Vicia faba (broad bean) was determined. The data indicate that a group of cells, usually termed the apical initials or general meristem, are not dividing or dividing at a very slow rate. The value of DNA, RNA, and protein per cell for this group of cells is the lowest found in the root and it is believed that this DNA value represents that of the 2n nucleus. Basal to this area divisions occur frequently and the DNA value per cell doubles. The cells immediately basal to the apical initials enlarge radially and increase threefold in protein and RNA per cell. As radial enlargement ceases elongation begins and a leveling of RNA content per cell occurs. In A. cepa protein content per cell also levels while in V. faba there is a decrease in protein per cell. It is believed, however, that in both cases there is a second increase in protein associated with maximal elongation although that stage is not included in the work reported here.
