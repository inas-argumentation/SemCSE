The innervation of the frog’s heart III. Electronmicroscopy of the autonomic nerve fibres and their vesicles
The electronmicroscopie appearance of the autonomic nerve fibres in the frog’s heart has been examined. The parasympathetic postganglionic fibres are typically cholinergic and have only a few cored vesicles amongst numerous small agranular ones. The sympathetic postganglionic fibres contain adrenaline and it is present in two types of vesicle, one is large (90 nm) and has an osmiophilic core, whilst the other is smaller (40 nm) and has no core unless the adrenaline is stained by the sodium dichromate/acrylic aldehyde method. There are also two types of noradrenaline-containing vesicles in mammalian nerves. These are compared with the vesicles in the frog nerves: there appear to be no differences other than the catecholamine stored.
