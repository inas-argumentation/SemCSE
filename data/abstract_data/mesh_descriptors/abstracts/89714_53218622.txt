Hepatic epithelioid hemangioendothelioma: Challenges in the preoperative diagnosis
Hepatic epithelioid hemangioendothelioma (HEHE) is a very rare vascular endothelial cell neoplasm with uncertain malignant potential. It is frequently occurred in middleaged females and presents nonspecific clinical manifestations [1]. In addition, radiological features are lack of specificity, and commonly mimicking metastases with multifocal lesions. This letter is to highlight the awareness and challenges in the preoperative diagnosis of HEHE. A 45-year-old female initially presented with right upper quadrant pain for two weeks and was found multiple hypoechoic lesions in the liver by ultrasound. Laboratory examinations were unremarkable: liver function and the levels of alphafetoprotein (2.6 ng/mL), carcinoembryonic antigen (1.9 ng/mL) and carbohydrate antigen-199 (7.0 U/ ml) were normal. Abdominal computer tomography (CT) showed multiple low-density hepatic nodules, and the maximum lesion was 17 mm in diameter located in the right lobe (Fig. 1A). Contrast-enhanced CT showed marginal enhancement on the portal venous phase (Fig. 1B). The lesions exhibited low signal intensity on T1-weighted images (T1WI) and high signal intensity on T2-weighted images (T2WI) (Fig. 1C). And some scattered lesions located in the peripheral area of liver showed a ‘targetlike’ appearance with central high signal intensity surrounded by a slightly hyperintense halo on T2WI. On diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI), most of lesions showed hyperintense, and the largest nodule exhibited central slightly high signal intensity with peripheral hyperintensity as the “halo” sign (Fig. 1D). Gd-EOB-DTPA contrast-enhanced MRI demonstrated the gradual ringlike enhancement in the periphery of nodules from arterial to portal venous and delayed phase, and low signal intensity as defects on hepatobiliary phase (Fig. 1EeG). “lollipop sign” was observed in the largest nodule.
