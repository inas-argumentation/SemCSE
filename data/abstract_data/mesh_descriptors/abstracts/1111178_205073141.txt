The poor patient with diabetes ‘should live like a saint’
We compliment Steven and Taylor [1] on their demonstration that extreme calorie restriction intervention leads to the achievement of non-diabetic fasting plasma glucose levels in more than four-fifths of patients with short-duration (<4 years) and half of those with long-duration ( > 8 years) diabetes, in addition to producing clinically significant improvements in blood pressure and lipid profiles. This builds on their earlier findings that 8-week extreme calorie restriction to 624–700 kcal per day could reverse impaired b-cell function and decreased liver insulin sensitivity, thus controlling Type 2 diabetes associated with truncal obesity [2]. It is said there is nothing new under the sun. The Charaksamhita was compiled ~2000 years ago by Charaka, based on the text from Agnivesha, one of the six original disciples of the Ayurvedic scholar Punarvasu Atreya. It is considered to be the original reference book of holistic Ayurvedic medicine; a term derived from the Sanskrit for ‘life’, Ayus, and ‘knowledge’, Veda. Charaka expands on the tridosha, the three humours of the body, vata, pitta and kappa. Charaka was a keen clinical observer who classified 16 categories of diabetes, including the distinction between diabetes in ‘fat’ and ‘thin’ patients. He recognized the association between calorie consumption, fat deposition and diabetes and appreciated the monetary restrictions faced by poor people who could not afford to purchase herbal medications prescribed at that time. He outlined a series of lifestyle-based interventions that would achieve the benefits rediscovered in the recent literature [3].
