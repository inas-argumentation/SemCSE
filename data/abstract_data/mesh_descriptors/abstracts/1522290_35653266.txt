Molecular mechanism of chemotherapeutic drug(s)-, induced autoimmunity: a mitochondrial-lysosomal axis theory.
The generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and reactive nitrogen species (RNS), and their metabolic intermediates during aerobic metabolism is an essential step for a nonspecific immune defense mechanism in the mammalian host. The synchronous function of electron flow through a mitochondrial electron transport chain, and superoxide-generating system in phagocytosis is critical to eschew a partial reduction of oxygen. Defective functional coupling during this process would increase the formation of partial oxygen reduction, and thus, ROS formation [1]. Upon inactivation or loss of bioactivity of this antioxidant reserve, the ROS/RNS are prone to alter the macromolecule alteration in structural and functional proteins.
