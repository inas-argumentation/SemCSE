Concomitant phyllodes tumour and homolateral breast cancer.
A 69-year-old woman presented with a painless mass in her left breast. Mammography revealed two features: a sharply delimited lesion in the left breast, which was compatible with fibroadenoma (phyllodes tumour), and a poorly marginated, stellate, nonpalpable lesion that indicated the presence of malignant disease in the mediocranial part of the breast. After stereotactic needle localisation (lower image), the suspicious lesion was removed and identified as ductal invasive carcinoma with tumor-free margins. Two-level axillary-lymph-node dissection was done (T1bN1M0, oestrogen-receptor positive, progesterone-receptor negative) and a subsequent histological examination (upper image) showed a grade II fibrosarcoma with pleomorphic spindle cells, atypical mitoses of moderate atypia, and no epithelial structures. By use of Tavassoli’s classification system, the phyllodes tumour was classified as low grade. Following surgery, the woman received radiation therapy and adjuvant tamoxifen therapy (20 mg daily). However, the tumour recurred 40 months later at the site of the excised phyllodes tumour, with disseminated metastases to both lungs (fibrosarcoma Grade II). She died 51 months after the first occurrence of breast cancer. The concomitant occurrence of phyllodes tumour and homolateral breast cancer is very rare. The clinical course in our patient was ruled by the occurrence of a biologically more aggressive local recurrence of phyllodes tumour manifesting as pure sarcoma. Therefore, all nodules of the breast that are identified in women older than 30, even if clinically benign, should be submitted for histological examination by use of core biopsy.
