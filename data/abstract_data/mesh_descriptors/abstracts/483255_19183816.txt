Synergistic action of sodium and angiotensin on brain mechanisms controlling fluid balance.
Abstract The effects on water and salt turnover of the simultaneous infusion of angiotensin and hypertonic NaCl into the 3rd brain ventricle were studied in goats in normal water balance and in hydrated animals. Angiotensin strongly potentiated the dipsogenic and antidiuretic effects of hypertonic saline. A possible explanation may be that angiotensin facilitates the transport of Na + into brain cells involved in the regulation of thirst and ADH release, and that the intracellular Na + concentration (rather than strictly osmotic factors) may determine the activity of these neurons. In the hydrated goat a central effect of angiotensin also enhanced the natriuretic response to intraventricular infusions of hypertonic NaCl, suggesting that both an expanded fluid volume and an elevated intracellular Na + concentration may be needed to obtain optimal activation of a brain mechanism which stimulates renal sodium excretion.
