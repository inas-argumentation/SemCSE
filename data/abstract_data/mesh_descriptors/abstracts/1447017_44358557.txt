Liver metastasis of adenoid cystic carcinoma of salivary origin: report of a case prepared by ThinPrep method
Dear Editor, We wish to report the case of a 70 yearold man, with a past history of adenoid cystic carcinoma (ACC) of the sublingual salivary gland, which was diagnosed initially with fine needle aspiration (FNA) and confirmed histologically by incisional biopsy under local anaesthesia. The mass was 4 cm in size on the left side of the floor of the mouth and infiltrated lamina propria of overlying mucosa, atrophic sublingual salivary gland, tiny nerve fascicles and fat. Moreover, cribriform and tubular variants were observed on histological examination. The bulk of the tumour was removed intact. Every 6 months magnetic resonance imaging, computerized tomography of the chest and abdomen, and haematological examinations were done as regular follow up, the results of which were within normal limits. Three years after the surgical excision, the patient was admitted to our hospital on account of a 2-week upper right abdominal discomfort, and computerized tomography demonstrated lesions in the liver. Conventional and ThinPrep smears were prepared from the liver lesions, sampled by ultrasound guided percutaneous FNA using a 22-gauge needle and stained with the Papanicolaou and May-Grünwald– Giemsa (MGG) techniques. A Tru-cut needle biopsy was also taken for histological examination. The cytological material was washed in CytoLyt solution and placed into a ThinPrep 2000 processor (Cytyc Corp., Boxborough, MA, USA). Immunocytochemical staining was done on cell-block ThinPrep residual material by using the automated Dako Autostainer (Dako, Denmark) and the monoclonal antibodies EMA, S-100, Ki67 and P53. The DNA content was analysed by Image analysis system, after Feulgen staining of a ThinPrep prepared smear. A confident diagnosis of ACC was possible with ThinPrep prepared smears, as the cytological features were rather similar to those of conventional smears stained with the Papanicolaou technique. In more detail, ThinPrep slides, in comparison with the conventional ones, revealed (1) a clear background and the absence of drying artefacts; (2) better nuclear detail (distinct chromatin and nucleoli) of the small uniform close-packed epithelial cells; (3) cells generally smaller in size and (4) less intense, yet clearly obvious staining of the hyaline spherical globules (basement membrane material). In addition, the spherical aggregates with three dimensional configuration and rosette like groups, where present, were well-retained (Figures 1 and 2). The MGG staining method used for ThinPrep is convincingly of the same quality as the standard procedure (Figure 3). The cellblock technique performed on the residual material of the ThinPrep makes possible the recognition of the histological patterns of ACC (cribriform and tubular) that cannot be identified in smears made by filter preparation. Moreover, a higher concentration of the sample is achieved, and multiple sections can be obtained for immunohistochemical techniques, at a relatively low cost. The current case was positive for EMA and S-100, while negative for p53 and Ki67 protein expression. According to the results of DNA analysis, a polyploid tumour was revealed, with DNA index values equal to 1, 1.2 and 1.5, ploidy balance value equal to )45%, degree of hyperploidy equal to 25% and degree of aneuploidy equal to 70%.
