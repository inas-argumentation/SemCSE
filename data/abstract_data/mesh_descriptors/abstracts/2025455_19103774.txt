A FALSE NEGATIVE CERVICAL SMEAR AND ITS HISTOLOGICAL EXPLANATION
IN the detection of unsuspected carcinoma of the cervix, by routine cytological examination, false negative smears are well recognized to occur. Spriggs (1956) reports 4 false negative smears in 2,000 patients and Ullery and Hollenbech (1958) report 24 “true” false negatives in 90,786 smears-that is smears negative on thorough review, and not simply smears wrongly described as negative at the first screening. Undoubtedly, in the past too much emphasis has been placed on “Percentage Accuracy”, as a criterion of the value and efficiency of a cytology service, for as Anderson (1959) states, by simply reporting all smears submitted as negative, an accuracy rate of 98 per cent could be claimed, as positive smears would only occur in one or two of every hundred. However, it may be of interest to report one such true false negative cervical smear, in which there seems to be an adequate histological explanation for the failure of the smear to contain malignant cells, though the cervix did prove to have an early invasive squamous carcinoma present, quite unsuspected clinically.
