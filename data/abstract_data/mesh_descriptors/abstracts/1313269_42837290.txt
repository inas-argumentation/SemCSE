Effect of estrogen on the serum cholesterol and triglyceride levels of prostatic cancer patients.
This study was prompted by reports that mortality from cardiovascular complications of patients wtih prostate cancer is greater among those treated with estrogens. Diagnosis was verified histologically in all cases. All patients were castrated are estrogen treatment was started the next day. Stilbestrol diphosphate 500 mg was given intravenously for 10 days. Then patients received orally 200 to 300 mg daily or 12 to 18 mg chlortrianisene. Blood samples were taken in the morning after an overnight fast. Serum cholesterol and triglycerides were determined by Technicon anto-analyzer. Pretreatment blood samples were taken from 18 patients. Triglycerides were determined only 12. All patients were followed for 4 months and 6 for 7 months. In another group of 17 patients 9 had been treated with estrogen for 10 months and 8 for 13 months. No pretreatment blood samples had been examined for these patients. The mean serum cholesterol value of those tested before treatment was 245 mg/100 ml plus or minus 62 mg. During therapy intravenously the mean serum cholesterol value decreased. When therapy was given orally the mean value of serum cholesterol increased within a month to 260 mg/ 100 ml plus or minus 43 mg/ 100 ml. Great individual variations during therapy were found but remained at the same level after 4-7 months of oral therapy. Those treated with estrogen for 10-13 months had a mean serum cholesterol value lower than the others followed for 7 months. The mean serum triglyceride value before estrogen therapy was 101 mg/100 ml. plus or minus 38 mg and after 40 days of treatment 158 plus or minus 49 mg. This difference is considered significant (p<.o1). Values remained at the same level when treatment was continued. High mean triglyceride values were also found in those for whom no pretreatment values were available. Of vascular complication. 1 patient died of cardiac infarction after 13 months of estrogen therapy. The triglyceride value 1 week before his death was 510/100cc. Deep venous thromboses developed in 2 patients after 4 and 5 months of estrogen therapy. In both cases serum cholesterol and triglycerides had increased. Results indicate that estrogens have a catabolic effect in large doses but an anabolic effect if the doses are low. Mean triglyceride values increased within 40 days and contiuned high throughout the 8 month period. Also triglyceride values were high in those patients treated for 10-13 months. After cessation of estradiol treatment the cholesterol and triglyceride values tended to return to normal.
