Catheter Salvage in a Patient on Hemodialysis with a Catheter-Related Bacteremia by Pseudomonas aeruginosa
Accessible online at: www.karger.com/journals/ajn Dear Sir, Dual-lumen tunneled cuffed catheters were developed in the 1980s. In patients lacking a suitable vascular anatomy or presenting repeated arteriovenous (AV) fistula thrombosis, cuffed central-venous hemodialysis (HD) catheters are emerging as permanent vascular access for chronic HD. These catheters have enjoyed a dramatic increase in use in the last years, but they are affected by many complications, including thrombosis and infections which remain frequent and serious [1–3]. Catheter-related bacteremia are hard to eradicate and require treatment with several antimicrobial drugs for a prolonged duration and all catheters must be removed and/or replaced over a guidewire [3]. Only in HD patients having exhausted all options for vascular access does it seem preferable to treat catheter-related bacteremia without removing the device. We report a case of bacteremia by Pseudomonas aeruginosa in a patient hemodialyzed with a Tesio catheter with no need of catheter removal because the P. aeruginosa induced biofilm formation was suppressed by clarithromycin. We are not aware of other similar reports in issues of nephrology. Case
