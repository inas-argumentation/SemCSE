A common component involved in the induction of hepatic tyrosine transaminase by hydrocortisone and glucagon.
Abstract As shown earlier for the corticosteroid-mediated induction, the initial induction of hepatic tyrosine transaminase by glucagon or cyclic AMP, although sensitive to actinomycin D or cordycepin, is not significantly inhibited by 5-azacytidine, which promotes the synthesis of defective mRNA. However, once induced by glucagon or cyclic AMP, the subsequent corticosteroid-mediated induction of the enzyme occurs only after a prolonged lag and is now sensitive to inhibition by 5-azacytidine. Similarly, following initial induction by glucagon or cyclic AMP, there is also a lag of 8 h before the induction response elicited by a second dose of glucagon or cyclic AMP again becomes maximal. These correlations and apparent interdependence of the inductions by glucagon (cyclic AMP) and hydrocortisone strongly suggest that a common component is involved at some stage of the induction by these two hormones, despite other obvious differences in their mechanisms of induction.
