Comparison between the in vivo and in vitro interaction of aminoacridines with nucleic acids and other compounds.
The spectrum of acriflavine in living Ehrlich ascites tumor cells has been determined. The changes in spectra which accompany staining are similar to those obtained in acriflavine-nucleic acid solutions. Two characteristics of the interaction of diaminoacridine dyes with nucleic acid are noted. One is the appearance of a shorter wavelength peak which may be associated with a metachromatic type of staining interaction. The second is a shift of the long wavelength peak to longer wavelengths. This shift is compared with the nonspecific spectral changes which accompany changes in the solvent.
