Richter syndrome in chronic lymphocytic leukaemia manifesting only as tumorous hepatomegaly
A 72-year-old woman with typical chronic lymphocytic leukaemia (CLL), diagnosed 5 years previously, had been treated intermittently with chlorambucil and corticosteroids. In February 2010, she was admitted to hospital because of fever (39 C) and recent onset of abdominal pain. She had tender hepatomegaly palpable 14 cm beneath the costal margin but no lymphadenopathy or splenomegaly. Unexpectedly, she had a normal blood count without an absolute lymphocytosis and no basket cells were seen in the blood film. There was mild hypercalcaemia: 2Æ675 mmol/l (normal range 2Æ125–2Æ625). Liver enzymes were also elevated: alkaline phosphatase 364 u/l (35–104) and gamma glutamyl transaminase 690 u/l (7–36) while bilirubin was normal. Lactate dehydrogenase was 2245 iu/l (240–480), uric acid 511Æ5 lmol/l (142–416) and beta-2 microglobulin 3Æ87 mg/l (0Æ6–2Æ2). A computed tomography (CT) scan showed mass lesions in the liver and no lymphadenopathy (left). A CT-guided needle biopsy of the liver showed only non-specific mild portal inflammation without CLL or lymphoma. A second biopsy showed diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (right, haematoxylin and eosin), CD20positive with high Ki-67 expression (80%). Bone marrow trephine biopsy showed no evidence of CLL or lymphoma and was essentially normal. She was treated with cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, prednisolone and rituximab (CHOP-R) and had a dramatic response with regression of all the liver masses on CT. One year after therapy she remains in remission without evidence of CLL or lymphoma and has normal liver function. Isolated hepatic tumours or very prominent hepatomegaly as the only site of transformation without any other evidence of disease are unusually rare manifestations of Richter syndrome in CLL.
