GUSTO (Global Utilization of Streptokinase and Tissue plasminogen activator in Occluded arteries): logic wins at last.
In acute myocardial infarction, reperfusion of the infarct related artery by means of thrombolytic therapy has been shown to result in infarct size limitation, left ventricular function preservation, and reduced mortality. These beneficial effects have been demonstrated with all thrombolytic agents in clinical use in numerous randomized trials'". But until recently, the link between artery reopening, limitation of infarct size, preservation of left ventricular function and mortality, though probable, had not been firmly established in one single trial. Yet, although the different thrombolytics supposedly have different efficiency in terms of speed of reperfusion and frequency of secondary reocclusion, several comparative trials have failed to show that one thrombolytic agent had a clearcut advantage over the others in terms of benefit in left ventricular function'^, or mortality"-. Moreover, the question of associated therapies has remained partially unanswered. Aspirin, shown to have an additive effect when given in conjunction with streptokinase, has been recommended since the publication of ISIS-2'. Conversely, the issue of heparin therapy has remained unsolved, the clinical net benefit of this therapeutic approach being seemingly small, due to an increased rate of serious bleeding complications offsetting the potential beneficial effect on mortality"'. These results have long been debated and the most widespread opinion until now, based on the results of ISIS-3, was that streptokinase and aspirin had won the 'arterial sweepstakes', that tissue plasminogen activator and anistreplase offer no additional advantage but rather cause more bleeding complications than streptokinase, and that heparin was finally not to be recommended in association with thrombolytic therapy. However, these ideas failed to take into account the fact that the different drugs had been tested in very differently designed trials as regards the definition of infarction, time window from the onset of symptoms to therapy, thrombolytic regimens, and associated therapies.
