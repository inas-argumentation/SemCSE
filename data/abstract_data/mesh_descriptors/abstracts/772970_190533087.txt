Neurodevelopmental Delay following the Neonatal Repair of Coarctation and Arch Obstruction.
BACKGROUND
While single ventricle physiology and cyanosis are known risk factors for neurodevelopmental delay (NDD), the impacts of isolated coarctation (Iso CoA) repair or arch reconstruction (AR) are less understood.


METHODS
The Vineland-III Adaptive Behavior Scales prospectively evaluated children without a genetic syndrome. An overall composite score, normalized to age and gender, was generated from individual domain scores. Neurodevelopmental delay was defined as a composite or domain score at least one standard deviation below the established mean. Isolated coarctation was repaired using a left thoracotomy, while AR was performed using a sternotomy and cardiopulmonary bypass. Children with a structurally normal heart and without previous surgery were used as controls.


RESULTS
Of 60 children, 50 required neonatal repair (12-Iso CoA, 38-AR), and 10 were controls. From the entire cohort of children requiring neonatal coarctation repair (Iso CoA+AR) composite (93.9 ± 15.9 vs. 105.0 ± 7.4; P=0.004) and all domain scores were significantly lower than controls. Neurodevelopmental delay was present in 25/50 children following repair and 0/10 of the controls (P=0.003). Similarly, the prevalence of NDD was significantly greater following Iso CoA repair (58.3% vs. 0%; P=0.005) and AR (47.3% vs. 0%; P=0.007) compared to the control population, but there was no significant difference between the Iso CoA and AR groups (P=0.74).


CONCLUSIONS
In this small cohort, half of neonates that required either isolated coarctation repair or arch reconstruction exhibit NDD at an intermediate-term follow-up.
