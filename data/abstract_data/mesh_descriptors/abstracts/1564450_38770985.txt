1 is manageable but 2 may be 2 much: Shall we open CTOs?
Multivessel coronary artery disease (MV) is present in 40–65% of patients ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) and is associated with higher morbidity and mortality after reperfusion therapy [1,2]. Still, aggressive MV revascularization during primary angioplasty has not improved outcome in STEMI patients [3–6] and it is not endorsed by current guidelines. On the other hand, recent reports have demonstrated that the enhanced risk observed in STEMI patients with MV is chiefly because of the presence of a chronic total occlusion (CTO) in a noninfarct related artery (non-IRA) [7,8]. In fact, STEMI patients with CTOs have reduced left ventricular function during hospitalization and experienced further impairment in left ventricular function during followup [7]. In this issue of Catheterization and Cardiovascular Interventions, Lexis et al. [9] extended the available data by showing that STEMI patients with CTOs had worst myocardial reperfusion and long-term outcome after angioplasty than patients without CTOs. It is conceivable that a lower potential for collateral blood flow, owing to an occluded non-IRA, clearly diminishes the ability for myocardial protection, translating into greater damage. Conversely, an abrupt reduction in collateral flow from the IRA to the CTOrelated myocardium may lead to additional myocardial necrosis. Although the presence of a CTO is an ominous sign in STEMI patients, the clinical benefit of CTO recanalization during the index hospitalization is currently unknown. Potentially, opening a CTO can (a) restore contractile function of a hibernated myocardium and (b) improve perfusion at overlapping regions, promoting healing at the infarct border zone. The EXPLORE trial (The evaluating Xience V and left ventricular function in percutaneous coronary intervention on occlusions after ST-elevation myocardial infarction) is a randomized, prospective, multicenter, two arm trial that will test the value of CTO recanalization after primary PCI [10]. Until the results of this trial are available, it is probably best to refrain from opening these complex lesions during the index hospitalization of STEMI patients unless it is absolutely required.
