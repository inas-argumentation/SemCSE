Management of patients with persistent recurrent aphthous stomatitis and Sutton's disease.
Approximately 2 per cent of patients with recurrent aphthous stomatitis present the severe, persistent form of the disease. Although the number of persons affected may be small, the suffering is great. Such patients frequently present histories of continual referral between various specialties of the healing profession, with little definitive or beneficial therapy being instituted. This paper deals with the management of such patients. Therapy can be either topical or systemic, or a combination of both. With the aid of analgesics to reduce the oral pain, the lesions are debrided with a tetracycline mouthwash. After the lesions have been dried with cotton applicators, a heavy layer of triamcinolone acetonide in emollient dental paste (Kenalog in Orabase) is applied four times a day. When lesions are too numerous, expansive, and inaccessible, systemic steroids are recommended, with appropriate antibiotic coverage in addition to the above-mentioned recommendations for debridement. Once the lesions become reduced in size, topical application of steroid can be initiated in order to permit further and quicker reduction of the systemic steroid dosage.
