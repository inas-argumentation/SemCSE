Molecular Defects in Insulin Secretion in Type-2 Diabetes
Type-2 diabetes is a multifactorial polygenic disease, characterised by elevated blood glucose levels, that usually presents in middle age and is exacerbated by obesity. Its causes are currently unclear, but both insulin secretion and insulin action are known to be impaired. It is now recognized, however, that abnormalities in insulin secretion precede the onset of type-2 diabetes and that the disease can occur in the absence of insulin resistance [1]. It is estimated that loss of β-cell function in diabetic patients proceeds at a rate of about 4% per year, and underlies the progression from normal glucose homeostasis to glucose intolerance and subsequent type-2 diabetes UKPDS [2]. Thus, impaired insulin secretion plays a critical role in the development of the disease. Much work has been devoted to the identification of diabetes susceptibility genes through genome wide linkagebased approaches. So far, only calpain 10 has been identified by these efforts [3]. In parallel with this work, a large number of studies have examined specific candidate genes. For example, a polymorphism in one of the subunits of the KATP-channel (E23K of Kir6.2) has recently been found to predispose to diabetes [4–8]. Here we review the regulation of insulin secretion from the β-cell and outline a hypothesis that accounts for the polygenic nature of type-2 diabetes as well as the increased incidence of the disease with age and obesity.
