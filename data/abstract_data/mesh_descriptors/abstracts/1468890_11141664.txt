Is non-dipping status a predictor of cardiac morbidity?
Irrefutable evidence exists that treating high blood pressure improves prognosis but the improvement in outcome does not return the prognosis to that of the normotensive population [1]. In addition, many people with elevated blood pressure do not develop hypertensive complications, and thus a large number of people may be treated for little or no benefit to the individual. Attempts have been made to determine whether other elements, apart from clinical blood pressure on which therapy has been based, may be more powerful predictors of outcome. Thus, we have moved from a situation where treatment indicators and goals were based on diastolic blood pressure to one which is based predominantly on systolic blood pressure [2]. Recently, it was suggested that pulse pressure is a more powerful predictor of outcome, but there is no real evidence that it is a causative risk factor or that treatment based on this parameter causes a greater improvement in prognosis [3].
