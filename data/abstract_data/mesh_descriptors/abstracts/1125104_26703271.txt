INHIBITION OF ELECTRON TRANSPORT AND COUPLED PHOSPHORYLATION IN LIVER MITOCHONDRIA BY CHOLANIC (BILE) ACIDS AND THEIR CONJUGATES.
1. 1. The taurine conjugates of ursodeoxycholic acid and 3,12-dioxocholanic acid were synthesised and characterised. 2. 2. The effects of C 24 natural bile (hydroxycholanic) acids, ketocholanic acids, and their taurine or glycine conjugates upon oxidative phosphorylation and adenosine-triphosphatase activity in intact liver mitochondria and upon nicotinamide-adeninedinucleotide oxidation by sonicated liver mitochondria, were studied. 3. 3. Bile acids and conjugates containing two hydroxy groups per molecule uncoupled oxidative phosphorylation linked to succinate oxidation to a greater extent than compounds containing three hydroxy groups. Among the taurine-conjugated dihydroxy acids, uncoupling potency declined in the order taurodeoxy-holate > taurochenodeoxycholate > taurohyodeoxycholate > tauroursodeoxycholate. 4. 4. Ketocholanic acids and their conjugates were less potent uncouplers than the corresponding hydroxy compounds. 5. 5. Uncomjugated hydroxycholanic acids were more potent uncouplers than were their conjugates. 3α,7α,12α-Trihydroxycoprostanic acid was more potent than cholic acid. 6. 6. Of the taurine-conjugated acids, the most potent uncouplers were those most readily absorbed from solution by mitochondria. 7. 7. Cholanic acids did not inhibit mitochondrial respiration with succinate as substrate, but did inhibit nicotinamide-adenine-dinucleotide oxidation by sonicated mitochondria with certain exceptions. The most potent inhibitors were those acids which were the most potent uncouplers. 8. 8. The possible physiological significance of these findings is discussed, with special reference to the regulation of cholesterol catabolism and bile salt formation.
