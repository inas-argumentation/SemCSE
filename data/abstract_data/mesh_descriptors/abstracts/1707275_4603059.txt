Seizure patterns and electroencephalograms of E1 mouse.
Polygraphic recording with chronically implanted electrodes was carried out on freely moving El mice during epileptic seizures and the interictal periods. El mice were kept as an inbred strain susceptible to seizure. Seizure was induced with the natural sensory stimuli evoked by throwing the mouse up in the air. The course of seizure was in three stages: prodromal, convulsive and postictal. The clinical symptoms were classified chiefly into three patterns depending on a clinical manifestation at the convulsive stage. Predominant tonic-clonic convulsions were most frequently observed. Running fits followed by tonic-clonic convulsions occurred rather more infrequently than the former. Paroxysmal discharges were initiated mostly in the parietal region of one hemisphere and spread to the entire cortex. During tonic convulsions, regular rhythmic slow spikes appeared in the negative phase of high amplitude. Clonic convulsions accompanied infrequent irregular fast spikes or wave-spike complexes. During wild running, there occurred very fast low voltage discharges interposed with few spikes of high amplitude. Even in the interictal period, paroxysmal discharges occurred but were confined temporarily and spatially. They were isolated spikes and were localized predominantly in the parietal area. Spikes occurred most frequently during deep sleep and rarely appeared in REM sleep or in the arousal state. It is suggested that the seizure of an E1 mouse may belong to a sensory precipitating epilepsy and might have a certain focus in some portion of the brain, which might be predisposed by some genetic factors and might be related to vestibular or some other sensory neural pathway and to the parietal cortex.
