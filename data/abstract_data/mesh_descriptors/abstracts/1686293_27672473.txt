An alternative to initial axillary-lymph-node dissection.
Most published data on sentinel-lymph-node (SLN) biopsy come from validation studies in which clinically node-negative patients have undergone SLN biopsy and then immediate complete axillary-lymph-node dissection (ALND). These studies have provided important information on the success and accuracy of SLN biopsy, but have not yielded any comparative data for SLN biopsy alone without concomitant ALND. Furthermore, these single institution and multiinstitutional validation studies have involved small numbers of patients. In The Lancet Oncology today, Krag and colleagues present data on the primary outcomes of overall survival, disease-free survival, and locoregional control from the National Surgical Adjuvant Breast and Bowel Project (NSABP) B-32 trial, which recruited more than 5000 women operated upon by more than 200 surgeons from 80 centres in Canada and the USA. This trial is the largest of fi ve randomised controlled trials comparing SLN biopsy with conventional ALND in clinically nodenegative patients. Three of these trials were of similar design to the NSABP B-32 trial, which compared SLN biopsy plus ALND (group 1) versus SLN biopsy alone (group 2). Despite being a multicentre trial with a high ratio of surgeons to number of cases (about fi ve cases per surgeon each year during accrual), both surgeons and pathologists followed specifi c protocols and there were periodical performance audits for purposes of quality control and consensual practice. Analysis of secondary endpoints pertaining to accuracy and technical aspects within the context of this trial confi rmed SLN biopsy to be a safe and accurate method for staging the axilla, with an acceptable falsenegative rate (9·8%) and high negative predictive value. Omission of routine immunohistochemistry helped avoid potential upstaging that would remove a subgroup of SLN biopsy-negative patients who might otherwise have led to a decrease in overall survival. Previous SLN-biopsy trials have focused mainly on morbidity and rates of locoregional control. The smaller Milan trial (516 patients) with 10-year follow-up suggested a marginal (3·8%) overall survival advantage favouring SLN biopsy only (p=0·15), but probably lacked suffi cient power to permit defi nitive conclusions on survival outcomes. It is of crucial importance to ascertain whether the fi nite proportion of patients with residual disease in non-sentinel nodes have impaired overall survival. The NSABP B-32 trial was designed to detect a modest 2% survival diff erence at 5 years thereby acknowledging that any reduction in morbidity must not be at the expense of impaired survival. At a mean follow-up of 96 months, the authors report no signifi cant diff erences in the primary endpoints of overall survival, disease-free survival, and regional control. Data on overall survival were carefully analysed with tests for interaction of treatment with stratifi cation variables; groups 1 and 2 were well balanced for patient characteristics and adjuvant treatments. There is a non-signifi cant mortality benefi t for the ALND group with an unadjusted hazard ratio of 1·20 (p=0·12) and an adjusted ratio of 1·19 (p=0·13). The authors off er an explanation based on random events favouring group 1 and the presence of 75 patients in the ALND group with a positive non-SLN. These patients would be analysed as node negative and would have received appropriate adjuvant systemic therapy, whereas the unknown number of non-SLN-positive patients in group 2 would be treated as SLN-biopsy negative. It is notable that more than 80% of patients had tumours 2 cm or smaller (T1) in size, indicating a moderately favourable prognostic group (node positivity rates were 29·5% for group 1 and 28·3% for group 2). The conclusions of this trial in terms of the appropriateness, safety, and eff ectiveness of SLN biopsy are justifi ed for this population but might not necessarily apply to patients with larger T2 (2–5 cm) or multifocal tumours who commonly undergo SLN biopsy. The paper from Krag and colleagues constitutes a seminal publication on the primary endpoints of locoregional recurrence and overall survival for the largest randomised trial of SLN biopsy. It vindicates contemporary practice of SLN biopsy and provides support for a reduction in extent of axillary surgery for most patients with breast cancer.
