News in HIV prevention: microbicides.
The opening ceremony of the XVI International AIDS conference recognized that prevention of HIV infection was more than abstinence be faithful and condoms ("ABC"). Indeed in 2005 there were 4.1 million new infections. Worldwide the dominant mode of transmission is heterosexual accounting for 85% of all infections. The defining characteristic of the HIV pandemic is the increasing number of infections in women. Of particular concern is the fact that infection rates in female adolescents are three to six times higher than in male adolescents. Prevention of sexual transmission of HIV for women has been a challenge from the beginning of the epidemic. It is clear that many women lack the social and economic power to persuade men to use condoms for protection. Womens vulnerability is increased in some areas of the world because of attitudes and cultural practices. Lack of education rape being condoned or not prosecuted and mens refusal to use a condom or disclose their HIV status make it critical to have a method of prevention that women are able to control. Unfortunately there has been little to offer women since the failure of nonoxynol-9 a spermicide that offered promise as a vaginal microbicide. (excerpt)
