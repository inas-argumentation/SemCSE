Antiemetic overuse in patients receiving chemotherapy.
www.thelancet.com/oncology Published online September 22, 2016 http://dx.doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(16)30456-9 1 New research suggests that overuse of antiemetic drugs to prevent chemotherapy-related nausea and vomiting is highly prevalent in USA and is responsible for unneeded expenses linked with chemotherapy. In 2013, the American Society for Clinical Oncology issued a guideline called the Choosing Wisely recommendations, which suggested oncologists should not use expensive antiemetic drugs in patients with cancer undergoing chemotherapy who have a low-to-moderate risk of nausea and vomiting. However, even after the recommendations were published, the trend in antiemetic overuse in the USA remained unclear. To assess the pattern, expenses, and changes associated with antiemetic use in connection with these recommendations, William Encinosa (Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality, Rockville, MD, USA) and Amy Davidoff (Yale School of Medicine and Yale School of Public Health, New Haven, CT, USA) did an observational study, including data from 678 220 adult patients with cancer who underwent chemotherapy before and after the recommendations. The researchers noted overuse of antiemetic drugs in 163 451 (24·1%) patients. During the 6 months after the Choosing Wisely recommendations were issued, patients showed a 7·0% decreased odds of antiemetic overuse compared with baseline before the recommendations (95% CI 4·4–9·5). However, it was a transient decline; after 6 months following the recommendations, the odds of overuse showed a 7·4% (4·6–10·2) increase compared with baseline. The researchers also noted increased expenses associated with antiemetic overuse; 6·1% (5·8–6·4) of the costs of chemotherapeutic drugs could be paid by reducing antiemetic overuse. Davidoff said, “The implications [of the study] are that some patients are receiving unnecessary treatment, ...which generates unnecessary patient and societal costs.” Ethan Basch (University of North Carolina, Chapel Hill, NC, USA) said, “This study suggests that although it is important to develop guidance recommendations about overuse and underuse of treatments, like the Choosing Wisely campaign, these alone are not enough.” Gary Lyman (Fred Hutchinson Cancer Research Center, Seattle, WA, USA) commented that “simply issuing practice guidelines is insufficient for durable changes in clinical practice to reduce overuse and contain costs. Guidelines must be followed by institutional and practice changes that either limit inappropriate use of these agents or provide real time reminders or alerts to clinicians.”
