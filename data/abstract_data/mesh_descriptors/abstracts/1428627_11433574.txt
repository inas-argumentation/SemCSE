Hepatocyte proliferation factors from neonatal pig liver: Purification and characterization
Two factors were found in the condition medium of neonatal pig liver fragments, which were capable of stimulating DNA synthesis in primary hepatocytes. They were named hepatocyte proliferation factor (HPF)‐1 and HPF‐2 and purified 1,025‐ and 2,580‐fold, respectively. Both HPF‐1 and HPF‐2 seem to be anionic at pH 8.0 judged from the elution pattern of DEAE (DE52) column chromatography. HPF‐1 was recovered as a non‐adsorbed fraction in blue Sepharose and heparin Sepharose columns, and had a molecular weight of 26–31 kDa as estimated by gel filtration in high salt condition. Purified HPF‐1 stimulated DNA synthesis of primary rat hepatocytes, but suppressed that of HepG2 cells. HPF‐2 strongly bound to blue Sepharose and heparin Sepharose columns, and had a molecular weight of 71–90 kDa as estimated by SDS‐PAGE under non‐reduced condition. Purified HPF‐2 stimulated DNA synthesis of primary rat hepatocytes dose dependently but did not suppress that of HepG2 cells.
