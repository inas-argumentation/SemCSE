Differential pharmacokinetic interaction of cyclosporine and tacrolimus with colchicine in renal allograft recipients
Dear editor, Colchicine remains the mainstay of gout flare treatment in renal transplant recipients despite its muscular toxicity in this popula‐ tion.1 Colchicine toxicity is increased in patients taking cyclosporine, which inhibits intestinal P‐glycoprotein and liver CYP3A4 leading to higher colchicine plasma concentrations.2 The influence of the other calcineurin inhibitor (CNI), tacrolimus, on colchicine disposi‐ tion seems less clear. Therefore, we undertook the present study in order to compare the effects of tacrolimus and cyclosporine on single‐dose colchicine disposition among renal transplant recipients. Patients who survived to at least 1 year after transplant were included in this prospective, single‐center, non‐randomized, open‐ label study if they were treated with cyclosporine (n = 7) or tacro‐ limus (n = 7). Also, patients must have stable CNIs’ trough plasma concentrations (within the normal limits) and renal function (eGFR ≥30 mL/min/1.73 m2) for at least 6 months before inclusion. Grapefruit consumption and other treatments known to inhibit or to induce P‐gp or CYP3A4 were forbidden.3 All the patients gave written informed consent. The study was approved by the local ethics committee and the French drug agency. (Clinical trial: NCT01160276). All patients received a single oral dose of 1 mg colchicine in the morning with the rest of their usual treatments except their CNIs which were taken 2 hours later. Blood samples were obtained and collected immediately before colchicine admin‐ istration and at the following time points: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, and 60 minutes, then at 4, 6, 8, 24, and 48 hours after dosing. Plasma colchicine concentrations were analyzed using a sensi‐ tive radioimmunoassay.4 The study outcome was area under curve of plasma colchicine concentrations AUC0‐48 hours. One‐tailed The Mann‐Whitney U test was used to compare colchicine pharmacokinetics in the presence of cyclosporine and tacrolimus. Our hypothesis was that colchicine AUC0‐48 hours would be higher in the presence of cyclo‐ sporine compared to tacrolimus.
