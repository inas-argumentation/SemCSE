Surgical management of disappearing colorectal liver metastases (Br J Surg 2013; 100: 1414–1420)
This review by Dr Pawlik and his collaborators from three major centres in the USA is timely and important. Increasing personalization of chemotherapy regimens has resulted in increased response rates for patients with unresectable colorectal hepatic metastases. Inevitably, a proportion of these responses are apparently complete for individual metastatic lesions. This presents a significant dilemma for the multidisciplinary team reviewing the status of such patients following chemotherapy. It is now increasingly noted that patients with tumours deemed initially unresectable may convert to potentially resectable disease based on their postchemotherapy staging. This paper elegantly summarizes the modern approach to assessment of potential resectability. It highlights the distinct differences between neoadjuvant and conversion chemotherapy, and the resulting problem of how to approach disappearing liver metastases (DLMs). It is known that colorectal hepatic metastases showing a complete clinical response have limited predictive value for a corresponding complete pathological response. This distinction is important, as complete pathological responses are known to be associated with improvements in long-term survival. This work emphasizes that DLMs have an unpredictable relationship with complete pathological responses, and results in the current recommendation that the management of DLMs should continue to involve surgical exploration with the aim of resecting all sites of original disease. These recommendations, however, are further refined by confirming, in the few patients in whom all sites of original disease cannot be resected, that it is acceptable to resect residual macroscopic disease and leave other disappeared metastases in situ, with a view to aggressive subsequent approaches for the proportion of these that will ultimately recur.
