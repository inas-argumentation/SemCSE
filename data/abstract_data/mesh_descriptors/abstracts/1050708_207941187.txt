On the diastereomeric purity of the D(−)‐(2R)‐ampicillin
The dramatic outcome of L‐(S)‐thalidomide teratogenic effect has opened a new perspective and long‐lasting discussion on drug development and safety of marketed products. The problem concerns exhibition of stereochemistry‐dependent differences in biological activities; absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion; pharmacological profiles and chemical stability versus racemization or epimerization of individual stereoisomer. Several decades elapsed since thalidomide case, before the problem obtained a status of a routine practice in pharma. This is due to constant regulatory authorities' pressure on producers to assess the biological efficacy of enantiomers and present the reasons for the one to be marketed. Thus, the numerous examples of enantio‐ dependent biological activities were established in drug development processes. The spectacular progress in enantioselective synthesis opened possibilities for synthesis and purification of individual enantiomers. Although the problem is still under debate, only over 40% of worldwide chiral drugs (out of 60% of total approved distribution) is currently marketed in pure enentiomeric form. With the advent of purely synthetic or natural semisynthetic chiral drugs, a need appeared for methods to assess their enantiomeric purity, in contrast to chiral drugs of natural origin wherein the chiral centers are enantiomericly pure. Several methods were developed to achieve this goal; high‐performance liquid chromatography on chiral stationary phase and nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) in solution are probably the most used. The NMR method, specifically, offers a wide spectrum of chiral reagents, as chiral solvating agents, which can be used to assess the enantiomeric purity and absolute configuration of stereocenters. The chiral odering agents can be alternative to chiral solvating agents. The lanthanide and transition metal complexes, as shift reagents, merit special focus for use in chiral drug studies as can be used both in organic solvents and in water, what is especially suited for majority of the drugs prepared as hydrochlorides.
