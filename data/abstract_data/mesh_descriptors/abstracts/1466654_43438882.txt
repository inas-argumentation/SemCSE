XXIV. Effect of mikamycin, carbomycin, spiramycin, erythromycin, and paromomycin on growth and respiration of HeLa cells.
HeLa cells have been grown in tissue culture for a period corresponding to three control cell generations in the presence of the antibiotics mikamycin, carbomycin, spiramycin, erythromycin, and paromomycin. All of these antibiotics inhibit yeast mitochondrial and Bacteriol protein synthesis. It has been found that at low concentrations mikamycin and carbomycin selectively inhibit mitochondrial protein synthesis in HeLa cells, with reduction in cytochrome a and a 3 levels and consequent growth inhibition after two cell divisions. Erythromycin and paromomycin do not show this effect at all, while spiramycin has some minor effects on protein synthesis and cytochrome formation. At high concentrations all five antibiotics show a second effect, namely a direct and immediate slowing or cessation of growth. In this instance mikamycin appears to act by means of its direct inhibitory effect on electron transport between cytochromes b and c 1 ; no inhibition of respiratory processes by the other antibiotics was demonstrated, and the cause of their growth inhibitory effects remains to be established. The results are discussed in terms of antibiotic effects on an intact cell system compared to isolated organelle studies; the usefulness of the HeLa cell system as a model to explain effects in the intact animal is emphasized.
