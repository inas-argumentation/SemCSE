Lenalidomide‐induced tumour flare reaction in mantle cell lymphoma
Tumour flare reaction (TFR) following lenalidomide is characterised clinically by painful enlargement of lymph nodes and/ or spleen, low-grade fever and skin rash. Despite being relatively common in chronic lymphocytic leukaemia (CLL) (affecting 30–58% of patients) (Chanan-Khan et al, 2006; Ferrajoli et al, 2008), TFR has never been reported in association with mantle cell lymphoma (MCL) (Habermann et al, 2009; Reeder et al, 2009). Here, we report our experience of TFR developing in three of 25 patients with relapsed MCL treated with lenalidomide within a phase II trial. Patient 1 presented at relapse with marked splenomegaly (20 cm) and lymphocytosis (white cell count (WCC) 258Æ1 · 10/l, 93% lymphocytes), and commenced lenalidomide 25 mg/d. On day 6, he developed acute left hypochondrial pain with a rapidly rising leucocytosis (WCC 357Æ4 · 10/l, 96% lymphocytes) which eventually peaked at 498Æ0 · 10/l on day 11 (Fig 1). Repeat imaging confirmed a diffuse increase in splenic size (24 cm) with no evidence of infarction or haemorrhage. He continued on full-dose lenalidomide with a gradual fall in WCC to 370Æ4 · 10/l, but subsequently developed grade 4 thrombocytopenia necessitating cessation of lenalidomide on day 15. His splenic pain improved although he remained dependent on regular analgesics. On day 28, he was admitted to hospital with dehydration secondary to vomiting and commenced a second cycle of lenalidomide 15 mg/d. The following day, he had an acute cerebral infarct complicated with aspiration pneumonia and died 3 d later. Patient 2 presented at relapse with multiple subcutaneous nodules on imaging, only one of which was clinically palpable over the right flank. She commenced lenalidomide 25 mg/d but, on day 4, developed several new subcutaneous nodules over her face and right scapula which slowly regressed in size and disappeared over the following week. Her right flank lesion completely resolved after one cycle of lenalidomide and she remains on treatment twelve months later having achieved an excellent partial remission.
