New cardiovascular risk markers: The race is on, but are there any winners?
In this issue of the Scandinavian Journal of Clinical and Laboratory Investigation, Niinisalo and co-workers assess the association between indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase (IDO) activity and the severity of atherosclerosis, expressed as the carotid intima media thickness (IMT) in a large Finnish general population cohort (The Health 2000 Study) [1]. IDO is an enzyme involved in the metabolism of the essential amino acid tryptophan, and IDO activity has been linked to the activity of pro-inflammatory T lymphocytes [2]. Inflammation is currently considered a crucial factor in atherogenesis [3]. Thus, a study examining whether IDO activity represents a potential new marker of the atherosclerotic process merits attention. In their study, Niinisalo et al. demonstrate that increasing IDO activity correlates significantly with increasing carotid IMT in both men and women. However, the strength of the association is fairly weak (r50.17 and r50.21, respectively), suggesting that only a minor proportion of the variability in carotid IMT can be explained by variability in IDO activity. The authors speculate that increased IDO activity is part of an immunological negative feedback response, the aim being inhibition of the inflammatory state associated with increasing atherosclerotic burden. After adjustment for age, body mass index, lipoproteins, diastolic and systolic blood pressure, smoking status, and C-reactive protein (CRP) in a multivariate model, the association between IDO and IMT was no longer significant, perhaps due to a close correlation between IDO activity and age and CRP levels, two established factors associated with increased cardiovascular risk. Even though an independent association between IDO activity and a surrogate marker of atherosclerosis like IMT was not demonstrable in the current study, can IDO activity measurement still be justified for cardiovascular risk assessment?
