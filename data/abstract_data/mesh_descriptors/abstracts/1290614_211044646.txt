Risky Sex and Other Personal Consequences of Alcohol and Drug Use Among LGBTQ College Students.
In 2017, young people ages 13–24 years comprised 21% (n5 8,164) of the 38,739 newHIV diagnoses in the United States (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, 2019). High-risk behaviors, such as sex without a condom when under the influence of drugs or alcohol, may be contributing factors among this population. Nationally, of all students who are currently sexually active (had sexual intercourse during the previous 3 months), 19% reported alcohol or other drug (AOD) use before their most recent sexual encounter. The number is slightly higher inmale students who had sexual contact with other male subjectswith20%reportinghavingAODuseprior to engaging in higher risk sexual activities (Newcomb, Ryan, Greene, Garofalo, & Mustanski, 2014). Understanding AOD use in the lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, queer, or questioning (LGBTQ) population is a critical component in preparing targeted HIV prevention education. Young adults (including college students) identified as LGBTQ may be at risk for HIV and other sexually transmitted infections (STIs) due to risky sexual behavior related to AOD use (Manning, Pring, & Glider, 2012; Newcomb et al., 2014).Data from theNational Survey of Family Growth showed that female subjects of 15–24 years who identify themselves as lesbian or gender nonconforming were more likely to participate in binge drinking in the past year (51 drinks at one sitting; Brewster & Tillman, 2012). Young men who have sex withmenare at an increased risk forHIVandproblematic alcohol use. The findings of a recent study of young men who have sex with men found that the more alcohol they consumed, the more likely they were to have condomless anal sex with casual partners (Feinstein & Newcomb, 2017). The National College Health Assessment Survey (American College Health Association, 2018) reported that transgender-identified female subjects reported a higher number of days consuming alcohol in the prior month and a higher frequency of recent binge drinking (51 drinks in one sitting) than nontransgender-identified peers. By contrast, transgender-identified male subjects were less likely to report any binge drinking in the past 2 weeks, but they reported more frequent binge drinking than nontransgender-identified persons (Talley et al., 2016). In 2011, The Institute of Medicine report on LGBTQ health recommended that alcohol misuse be a priority research topic, particularly among sexual and gender minority adolescents and young adults. The purpose of this cross-sectional study was to examine the personal outcomes of AOD use among LGBTQ college students with the intention of customizing existing AOD use prevention strategies and interventions to more effectively implement these strategies in this population.
