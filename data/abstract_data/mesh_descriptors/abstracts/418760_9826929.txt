Reporting surgical outcomes.
he treatment of spinal fractures is one of the fundamental clinical competencies of neurosurgical and T orthopedic spine training. For surgeons working at tertiary referral centers, these injuries constitute a significant component of trauma calls. Traumatic spinal fractures are frequently accompanied by other visceral injuries, making their management complex at times. Spinal reconstruction is performed to decompress neural elements in an effort to maintain or improve neurologic function and alleviate pain and restore biomechanical stability to the spine when necessary. These patients often require complex medical and surgical care, and it is vital to be comfortable with the treatment of such injuries, particularly when surgery is required. In this issue of WORLD NEUROSURGERY, Williams et al. examined all surgical cases from 2004e2007 from the Scoliosis Research Society Morbidity and Mortality database. All cases of traumatic spinal fractures were identified and assessed for complication rates associated with surgical treatment. In addition, the authors evaluated cases for factors associated with the occurrence of complications. With 6706 cases, this likely represents the largest retrospective analysis of surgically treated traumatic spinal fractures in the literature.
