AIDS in the global village. Why US physicians should care about HIV outside the United States.
Albeit to varying degrees HIV infection and AIDS affect every country and region of the world. As such physicians in the US need to be concerned about the dimensions and dynamics of the international spread of HIV infection and not limit themselves to what is currently going on in the US in that regard. Naysayers however would have us believe that since the current rate of new HIV infections in the US appears to be declining American health professionals policymakers and laypersons need not be too concerned about the HIV/AIDS pandemic. When the beginnings of the HIV epidemic was first identified a decade ago many people thought that HIV infection could not and would not spread beyond homosexual males; they were proved wrong. HIV has since spread to the general US population and beyond going alternately from female to male male to female and male to male. Sub-Saharan Africa Thailand and India are in the throes of major HIV epidemics in which heterosexual HIV transmission predominates. Even though 75% of the worlds 12-13 million HIV-infected persons live or have lived in developing countries and that proportion will soon increase to an estimated 90% US physicians must not fall into complacency. It is currently not possible to judge the magnitude of HIV dissemination in US inner-city communities but some predict that HIV transmission therein could signal the beginnings of a major heterosexual HIV epidemic in the US. The lessons we are learning from the current epidemic in Africa and other resource-poor locations will have great applicability as the US confronts its own heterosexual HIV epidemic. This and the fact that modern transportation has made the US no longer geographically isolated from the macroconditions of other countries and populations demand that US physicians remain vigilant and active in the war against HIV/AIDS.
