Methodology of the Diagnosis of Microsporidiosis in Urine and Pulmonary Specimens from AIDS Patients
Microsporidiosis has been recognized as one of the newest members of the group of opportunistic infections in man, associated with the acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS). The microspridian Encephalitozoon spp. can cause an expanding spectrum of localized and disseminated pathologies, including infections of the intestinal tract, upper and lower respiratory tracts, urinary tract and brain. The laboratory diagnosis of microsporidiosis still depends primarily on microscopic visualization of the organisms in tissues or other biologic materials of the host. The prevalence of microsporidiosis is poorly known, and almost certainly underdiagnosed due to the difficulties in identifying these organisms of scanty size and poor staining, even with recently improved diagnostic staining methods. Recently, a new tool fa identifying spores within the genus Encephalitozoon was developed; the monoclonal antibody (MAb) 3B6-based immunofluorescence assay 111. The aims of this study were to compare three techniques for [identification of microsporidian spores in urine and pulmonary specimens: the fluorochrome calcofluor stain (CF), the indirect immunofluorescence @I) with monoclonal antibody (MAb) 3B6 specific for the genus Encephalitozoon and the modified trichrome-blue stain (MT) as the standard technique; to determine the reproducibility of the three techniques in a routine laboratory; to evaluate the prevalence of microsporidiosis in Portuguese AIDS patients.
