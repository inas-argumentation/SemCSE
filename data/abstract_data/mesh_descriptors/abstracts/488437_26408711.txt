Visual discrimination in hemicerebrectomized monkeys.
Monkeys having different amounts of unilateral brain ablation were studied on 20 discrimination tasks involving visual-spatial stimuli. Retests and reversals of some tasks were included among the total series of tasks. Brain damaged animals were poorer than normal subjects on only five comparisons among the 60 that were possible. The group of primary interest, the hemicerebrectomized monkeys, were significantly poorer than normal animals on only a thickness discrimination, and a reversal of the thickness discrimination which followed a retest on it. The results of all groups are discussed in relation to other observations made during these experiments. The role of homonymous hemianopia is suggested to be of special importance to the results. Lastly, a comparison is made of these findings with those of similar experiments by others.
