Microenvironmental conditions in multicellular spheroids grown under liquid-overlay tissue culture conditions.
The tumor in vivo has often a nodular or spheroidal appearance, as shown by Thomlinson and Gray (1955) and Tannock (1968), with viable cells surrounding penetrating capillaries and necrotic zones some distance from the vessles. To increase our understanding of fundamental processes going on in the tumor, which determine tumor cell death or tumor cell response to treatment with radiation and drugs, the multicellular speroid as a tumor model in vitro has been developed (Sutherland and Durand 1972). A multicellular spheroid growing in tissue culture has an almost spherical geometry, with an outer layer mainly containing dividing cells and an intermediate layer of resting cells. As soon as the spheroids are big enough necrotic regions develop in the center. Spheroids can be cultivated in tissue culture from various malignant cells, including human tumor cells (Carlsson et al. 1983). This chapter deals with microenvironmental conditions in the multicellular spheroids which influence the metabolic and cell cycle state and hence the cell response to therapeutic treatments. Investigation of microenvironmental conditions, means, for instance, mea-surement of the diffusion properties of essential substrates and substances in spheroids. With the aid of different microelectrodes measuring oxygen partial pressure, membrane potentials, or ion activities (Dufau et al. 1980; Acker et al. 1983b) it is possible to record regional differences in the microenvironmental determining parameters in spheroids, which might be important for the formation of an opinion on the malignancy and curability of tumors in patients.
