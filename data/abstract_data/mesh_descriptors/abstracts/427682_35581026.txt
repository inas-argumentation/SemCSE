Numerous atypical lymphocytes were seen on examination of a peripheral blood smear. The haemoglobin was 13 ́9 g dL and the white cell count 13 ́5 10 L, with a monocytosis of 18% and presence of atypical mononuclear cells. Serum levels of testosterone, luteinizing hormone and folliclestimulating hormone were normal. Non-classical congenital adrenal hyperplasia was excluded after an adrenocorticotrophic hormone stimulation test. A Paul±Bunnel test was positive while serology showed a rising titre of IgG antibodies to EBV capsid antigen and early antigen complex, confirming a primary infection with EBV. The patient was seronegative for HIV infection. Owing to the disfiguring nature of the skin lesions, treatment was initiated with methylprednisolone 60 mg daily. After 2 weeks, isotretinoin 20 mg daily was also given. This led to rapid improvement of all inflammatory lesions, and the corticosteroid dosage was slowly tapered over the next 2 weeks. Although isotretinoin was maintained, new crops of papular and pustular lesions were again observed on the face and upper trunk after the next couple of weeks. Staphylococcus aureus was isolated from new pustules on culture. A second 4-week course of methylprednisolone 30 mg daily was necessary, and roxythromycin 300 mg daily was added during the first 5 days of treatment. The general symptoms as well as the enlargement of the liver, spleen and lymph nodes gradually improved. No further recurrences were observed during the following 6 months. The patient did not fulfil the criteria for acne fulminans, which is an important differential diagnosis. There are only a few previously documented cases of acne vulgaris in association with infectious mononucleosis. In 1967, Reed reported a 18-year-old patient with eruptive acne vulgaris associated with fatigue, enlargement of cervical lymph nodes, and sore throat. The white cell count was suggestive of infectious mononucleosis. Interestingly, the acne improved with clearance of the infectious mononucleosis. A second patient had infectious mononucleosis with a temperature over 37 ́8 8C and severe inflammatory acne on the face, neck and trunk. Previous to infectious mononucleosis, he had had lowgrade acne vulgaris. The author mentioned an additional four patients with this association, but no details were given. There are no studies on the effect of viral infections on acne vulgaris. It is unlikely, however, that this is a specific relationship. We suggest that viral infections may lead to an impaired immune response of the skin and thus cause worsening of pre-existing acne vulgaris. Bacterial infections usually complicate virus infections, as virus infections reduce resistance to bacteria. Eruptive acne vulgaris has also been reported in the setting of HIV infection. In our patient, a therapeutic regimen including isotretinoin, methylprednisolone and antibiotics was eventually highly beneficial. Systemic corticosteroids have been demonstrated to be of benefit in severe acne and in complications of EBV infection such as airways obstruction and severe thrombocytopenia. It was therefore felt reasonable to give corticosteroids to this young man. In conclusion, our case adds support to an association of EBV infection and an unusually vigorous course of inflammatory acne. Hence, patients with a sudden exacerbation of previously relatively mild acne should be checked for infectious mononucleosis.
