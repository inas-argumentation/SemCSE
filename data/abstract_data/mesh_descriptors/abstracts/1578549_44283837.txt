As two studies have suggested a threshold effect in which low levels of protein Z increased the risk of thrombosis [4,6], we have stratified the population into two groups according to the 10th percentile of the distribution in controls. In the whole cohort, 10 percent (n 1⁄4 58) of healthy controls had protein Z levels below 0.93 mg L (10th percentile 1⁄4 0.93 mg L). Eleven percent (n 1⁄4 32) of the patients had protein Z levels below this cut-off (P 1⁄4 0.58), which means that protein Z below 0.93 mg L was not a risk factor for coronary disease. This was homogeneous across countries as 12% (n 1⁄4 21) and 10% (n 1⁄4 12) of the patients, in France and Northern Ireland, respectively, had protein Z levels below the 10th percentile which was set in controls for each country separately (P 1⁄4 0.47 and 0.80 for case-control difference in France and Northern Ireland, respectively). In conclusion, antigenic protein Z levels do not seem to have a major effect on the risk of coronary heart disease in healthy men followed for 5 years.
