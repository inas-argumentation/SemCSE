GLUTAMINE SYNTHETASE ACTIVITY IN RAT LIVER AFTER PORTACAVAL SHUNT.
Glutamine synthetase activity in the rat's liver decreases in alloxan diabetes. An end-to-side portacaval shunt performed on normal rats establishes a system in which pancreatic products reach the liver via the hepatic artery after having gone through the general circulation. Tests for glutamine synthetase activity in the liver 3-4 weeks after this operation show a significant decrease in activity. (Endocrinology 74: 805, 1964) I a previous paper (1) it has been shown that insulin increases the glutamine synthetase activity in liver when given intravenously to rats during the hyperglycemic phase of alloxan-induced diabetes. This paper concerns the proposition that shunting the insulin around the liver in a normal rat would reduce glutamine synthetase activity. The effect on this enzyme of an end-to-side portacaval shunt done on normal rats was ascertained. Materials and Methods Female rats of the Holtzman strain, weighing 200-250 g, were used in all experiments. An endto-side portacaval shunt was performed using the technique of Henzel et al. (2). Three to 4 weeks later glutamine synthetase activity was determined on an operated rat and control. An unoperated rat of the same weight, fed the same diet (Purina Rat Chow ad lib.) was considered a suitable control. In general, the shunted rats' food intake was somewhat greater than that of normal rats in providing a continual weight increase and normal physical appearance. The method of testing for glutamine synthetase activity has been previously described (3).
