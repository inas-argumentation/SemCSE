Neurological Presentation of Poststreptococcal Glomerulonephritis
T he patient is a 9-year-old white male without a significant medical history who was seen by his private physician 1 week prior to admission for complaints of tactile fever, headache, vomiting, and decreased oral intake. A throat culture was positive for Group A Streptococcus pneumoniae, and treatment was started with amoxicillin. One day prior to admission he had developed abdominal pain, vomiting, and blurry vision, and his mother found him to be "slow to respond," "staring off into space," and "incoherent." He was taken to a community hospital, where he was noted to be listless, would not follow commands, and responded only to painful stimuli. Vital signs were temperature 37°C, heart rate 102 beats/min, respiratory rate 20 breaths/min, blood pressure 148/96 mmHg, repeated 174/98 mmHg one hour later.
