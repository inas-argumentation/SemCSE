Economic evaluation of a prostate cancer screening program in France: a decision model.
In developed countries, prostate cancer is the second cause of death from cancer, after lung cancer, among men over 50 years of age. In France, it accounts for 3.4% of all deaths and 10.7% of deaths from cancer. Moreover, between 1978 and 1982 and during the period 1983–1987, the rate of incidence of prostate cancer increased by about 30% and the number of deaths attributed to prostate cancer by 8% [1]. Cancer screening aims to reduce cancer-related mortality through a systematic search for asymptomatic cancers. The idea is to detect cancers at an early stage when they may produce a better response to treatment. Nevertheless, it is not certain that we need a mass screening program for prostate cancer. Not all prostate cancers are potentially fatal; most develop slowly and have no effect on daily life. Randomized controlled trials have not established the value of early treatment [2]. The actual efficacy of available treatments is even debatable. Lack of proof regarding the potential benefits of mass screening has not prevented physicians from carrying out screening on an individual basis but it is difficult to estimate the number of medical acts this represents. There is no standard screening procedure for prostate cancer. We can but note that the numbers of prescriptions for prostate specific antigen (PSA) measurements, a test used to detect prostate cancer in asymptomatic men, of prostate biopsies and radical prostatectomies are rising. When resources are limited, health authorities have to balance the needs of the population against the resources available. They have to evaluate the probable cost of a national screening program before setting one up [3,4]. A set of criteria allowing the best and most judicious use of these resources has to be established [5]. Cost-effectiveness analysis, which compares the economic merits of different uses of the resources from a public health standpoint, is an appropriate tool for determining a strategy which both fulfils medical efficacy requirements and takes predefined budgetary constraints into account [6,7]. * Corresponding author.
