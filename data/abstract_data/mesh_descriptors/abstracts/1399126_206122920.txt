Assessment of protein antibody response in patients with suspected immune deficiency.
INTRODUCTION “Practical Aspects of Ambulatory Diagnosis and Management of Immunodeficiency Disorders” is intended to help allergists evaluate and manage patients with suspected immune deficiencies.1–3 This article reviews the use of protein antibody response in the evaluation of immune competence. Primary disorders of humoral immunity may be due to intrinsic defects in B cells or lack of signals necessary for B cell maturation and function. The latter may occur in T-cell deficiency or when T-cell signaling is impaired, as in Xlinked hyper IgM syndrome. Antibody deficiency increases susceptibility to bacterial and viral infections and allows recurrences of infections normally experienced only once.4,5 Reduced immunoglobulin levels raise concerns for antibody deficiency; however, normal or elevated immunoglobulin levels can also conceal antibody deficiencies (eg, selective antibody deficiency, human immunodeficiency virus, multiple myeloma, and IgG subclass deficiency). In addition, some medications may reduce immunoglobulin levels without causing immune deficiency. Therefore, evaluation of such patients must include evaluation of responsiveness to protein and polysaccharide antigens.5–7
