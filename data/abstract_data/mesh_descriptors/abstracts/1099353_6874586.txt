A digital computer model of the vectorcardiogram with distance and boundary effects: simulated myocardial infarction.
A mathematical model of the electrical fields of the heart which uses analogue computational techniques has recently been reported.’ This computer model of the vectorcardiogram assumed an equivalent dipole source generator and an infinite homogeneous resistive medium. More recently, a digital-computer model has been developed2 which includes a set of 20 distributed current dipoles, each dipole representing a segment of the heart, with the whole array eccentrically placed in a spherical homogeneous resistive medium. The behavior of this system is governed by the equations of Wilson and Bayley3 and includes both distance and boundary effects. The mathematical model is described in Appendix A, and the basic equations are given there. The success with this model in the simulation of normal vectorcardiograms, vectorcardiograms of large infarcts in classical locations, and right and left ventricular hypertrophy of varying degrees led us to explore methodically the effects of small and medium infarcts simulated in all areas of the myocardium. This paper is a detailed report of this exploration, with infarctions simulated in all areas of the septal and left ventricular myocardium, including the so-called “silent areas.” It is hoped that these simulated vectorcardiograms will stimulate the further exploration of current criteria for the diagnosis of myocardial infarction in clinical vectorcardiography. It can be expected that the model, which is anatomically and physiologically based, may provide the basis for clarifying previously confusing and undiagnosable electrocardiograms and vectorcardiograms.
