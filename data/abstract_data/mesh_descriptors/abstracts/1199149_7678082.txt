Fixed human normal liver
To tire Editor:-! am writing in reference to a recent article by Matsuhashi and colleagues.• In alluding to an earlier paper from our group,2 the authors refer to our experiments as having been performed on "fixed human normal liver." This information is erroneous. Our work included only freslz liver specimens (p. 30). This is an important distinction since in another reference cited by the authors,! Zimmerman and Smith used fixed liver specimens. The authors attribute the discrepancy between the Zimmerman and Smith results and our results to "differences in the measuring conditions, especially the method for measuring the speed of sound." However, since they compare results from fresh and fixed livers, a discrepancy due to differences between these two tissue states cannot be ruled out. A second point is the authors' statement that "methods used previously for measuring the speed of sound (refs.) require knoweledge of the specimen thickness." We agree that the requirement for knowledge of the specimen size poses a serious limitation to the accuracy of these measurements. This fact has been recognized in another paper published by our group,4 which describes a method for measuring the speed of sound in biological specimens that does not require knowledge of the size of the specimen, nor the speed of sound in a reference medium such as water or saline.
