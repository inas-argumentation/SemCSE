Helicobacter pylori VacA and Gastric Ulcer
VacA, the only protein toxin produced by Helicobacter pylori, vacuolates cultured cells. The presence of 2 VacA receptors has been demonstrated. One is the receptor-type protein tyrosine phosphatase (RPTP) ζ/β (PTP ζ/β), and the other is RPTP α. VacA binds to PTP ζ/β resulting in gastric epithelial detachment through the tyrosine phosphorylation of Git-1, which then leads to gastric ulceration by the direct action of gastric acid. Thus, disturbance of adhesion between gastric epithelial cells and the extracellular matrix due to an abnormal PTP ζ/β signal is the main mechanism of gastric ulceration.
