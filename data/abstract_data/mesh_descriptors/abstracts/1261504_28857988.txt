Liver nuclear protein phosphorylation in vivo and the effect of triiodothyronine.
The incorporation of [32P]orthophosphate (32Pi) into rat liver proteins was studied in vivo in normal animals, hypothyroid animals, and hypothyroid animals given 1–15 μg T3/l00 g BW, 3–14 h before sacrifice. Cytosol and nuclei were separated by differential centrifugation, and nuclear globulins, histones, and nonhistone proteins were recovered by extraction procedures. All of the data were corrected for the individual specific activities of the perchloric acid (PCA)-soluble cytosol inorganic phosphate pool and, in some assays, for specific activity of the organic phosphate pool. When studied 90 min after iv administration of 300 μCi 32Pi, the incorporation of isotope was reduced in all of the protein fractions by 15–35% in hypothyroid animals compared to that in normal controls. T3 administration induced a progressive increase in 32Pi incorporation into proteins. The increase was statistically significant 6 h after T3 injection.
