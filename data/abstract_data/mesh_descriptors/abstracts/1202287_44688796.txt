experimental in vitro organogensis and its modification by antibody directed to a cell surface antigen.
The hypothesis that cell-surface directed antibody may interfere with organogenesis was investigated by means of an experimental system wherein histotypic in vitro reconstruction of chick embryo mesonephros was modified by an heterologous Forssman antibody. The Forssman antigen is expressed at the surfaces of epithelial cells of the duct and collecting tubules in contrast to its absence from the rest of the nephron. Incubation of suspension of dissociated single cells from mesonephroi allows organotypic reconstruction of both F+ and F− elements when normal serum is added to the culture, while anti-F sera inhibit the reconstruction of F+ structures. It is proposed that these experiments reflect a set of circumstances that may occur in viviparous animals where transplacental antibodies are capable of harming the early embryo.
