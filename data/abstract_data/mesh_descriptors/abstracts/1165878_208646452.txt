Autonomic Nerve-Preserving Lymph Node Dissection for Lateral Pelvic Lymph Node Metastasis of the Pelvic Floor Using the Transanal Approach.
113 DISEASES OF THE COLON & RECTUM VOLUME 63: 1 (2020) Lateral pelvic lymph node metastases (LPLNMs) are present in 16% to 23% of lower rectal cancer cases. Although the prognosis for these cases is generally poor, it has been reported that the 5-year survival rate for cases in which R0 resection was possible is 40% to 50%. The 5-year survival rate of lateral pelvic lymph node dissection (LPLND) cases was better than that of nondissection cases according to a propensity score analysis for T3 and T4 lower rectal cancer cases. It is appropriate to perform LPLND for cases with LPLNM. In addition, in cases treated with neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy, the risk of LPLNM is high when enlarged lymph nodes are present before neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy, with the local recurrence rate reportedly as high as approximately 20% without LPLND. In rectal cancer surgery, it is necessary to preserve the autonomic nerves, such as pelvic splanchnic nerves, to prevent urination disorder and sexual dysfunction. The development of autonomic nerve-preserving LPLND has enabled local control and survival benefit for patients while avoiding this complication. However, LPLND is technically difficult and complicated, especially in the deep part of the pelvis, with poor visualization and poor maneuverability, which increases the risk of autonomic nerve injury. We previously reported transanal a total mesorectal excision procedure for low rectal cancer. We subsequently developed a technique for LPLND combined with this transanal approach. In this video article, we describe the surgical techniques for autonomic nerve-preserving LPLND for LPLNM in the deep part of the pelvis using the transanal approach. See Video at http://links.lww. com/DCR/B12. LPLND using the transanal approach is a useful option for cases in which lymph node metastasis is detected in the deep pelvis because of the precise preservation of the pelvic splanchnic nerve and easy approach to the internal pudendal artery.
