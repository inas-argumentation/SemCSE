HYPOTHALAMIC UNIT ACTIVITY. II. CENTRAL AND PERIPHERAL INFLUENCES.
1. 1. In 26 acute experiments the firing rates of single hypothalamic and ventral thalamic units were analyzed during individual and paired visceral and central stimulation and somatic and central stimulation. Visceral stimulation involved distention of the urinary bladder. Somatic stimulation consisted of electrical stimulation of the sciatic nerve, adjusted to minimize A-delta and C fiber volleys. Central stimulation consisted of electrical stimulation of a variety of rhinencephalic and striatal regions with the emphasis on amygdaloid, prepyriform and pyriform sites. 2. 2. Two hundred and seventeen units were subjected to all the stimulation procedures. Fifty-seven per cent of these were responsive to at least one of the peripheral stimuli and the central stimulus or their paired effect. Thirteen per cent were only responsive to a peripheral stimulus and 6 per cent to a central stimulus. Two topographic trends emerged from stimulation of the various central structures. First, there was a vast reduction in the number of spontaneously active hypothalamic units during and for 15 min following brief stimulation of the dorsal hippocampus. Second, there was a paucity of unit responses to basal forebrain stimulation. 3. 3. Analysis of unitary firing patterns during individual visceral and somatic stimulation and during paired central-peripheral stimulation illustrated two principles. First, the direction of effect ( i.e. , augmentation or reduction in firing rate) exterted by the central stimulus was similar for central-visceral and central-somatic stimulation procedures. Second, the firing pattern of those units which responded in one direction to peripheral stimulation and in the other direction to central stimulation tended to reflect the peripheral influence when the opposing stimuli were paired. 4. 4. The firing patterns of 98 units were analyzed during two sets of paired central-peripheral stimuli involving stimulation of different rhinencephalic or striatal structures ( e.g. forebrain septum and basal forebrain, caudate nucleus and globus pallidus). For 49 per cent of this group, both central stimuli exerted an almost identical effect during paired central-peripheral stimulation. In only one case did the central stimuli exert opposite effects when paired with a peripheral stimulus. 5. 5. These results were considered in support of the concept that the afferent input to the hypothalamus is a major determinant of the susceptibility of hypothalamic neurons to rhinencephalic and striatal influences.
