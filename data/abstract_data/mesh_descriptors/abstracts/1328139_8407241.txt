Comparison of bicuculline methochloride with bicuculline and picrotoxin as antagonists of amino acid and monoamine depression of neurones in the rat brainstem
Abstract The microiontophoretic efficacy of bicuculline methochloride, bicuculline and picrotoxin as antagonists of neuronal depression produced by γ-aminobutyric acid, glycine, imidazoleacetic acid, noradrenaline or 5-hydroxytryptamine have been compared on spontaneously active cells in the medulla-pons of anaesthetised rats. Bicuculline methochloride was considered to be a more potent and more selective antagonist of γ-aminobutyric acid than bicuculline or picrotoxin. Firing rate changes were not correlated with antagonism. Each compound reduced or abolished imidazoleacetic acid depressions before those of γ-aminobutyric acid and were more potent antagonists of imidazoleacetic acid than of γ-aminobutyric acid. No significant effects on noradrenaline or 5-hydroxytryptamine depressions were observed.
