Familial multiple myeloma: first case reported in a Mexican family
Dear Editor, Multiple myeloma (MM) is a malignant plasma cell tumor that is distributed at multiple sites within the bone marrow compartments [1]. This disseminated malignancy is diagnosed in approximately 14,500 Americans each year and, though treatable, accounts for 2% of all cancer deaths and remains incurable. We report on a family in which three members in two generations are affected with MM (Fig. 1). All of the relatives affected with breast cancer developed ductal carcinoma with hormone receptors positive at the age of 65 and 66 years respectively. There were no additional risk factors for breast cancer. Patients with MM were diagnosed as stage II in one case and stage III in the others according to The International Staging System for Multiple Myeloma. Karyotypic analysis by G. T.G. banding revealed no abnormalities in both patients. The first case of MM was developed in a 56-year-old woman who was previously diagnosed with breast cancer. She received melphalan/prednisone for 12 months, developing a fatal pneumonia associated to disease progression. The others two patients were treated with thalidomide plus dexamethasone, achieving a very good partial response and partial response, respectively, after 6 cycles of treatment. Taking into account both the distribution of MM within this family and the prevalence of this rare disorder within the general population (3.1 cases/100,000 individuals per year) [2], as well as the age at cancer onset in this kindred, it is very likely that a genetic factor could explain the present familial clustering. The occurrence of MM within this family is compatible with an autosomal dominant mode of inheritance, incomplete penetrance, and possible anticipation. It is interesting that in our kindred two cases have arisen in women. We also report that two more family members developed breast cancer and one of the MM family members also presented coexistence with breast cancer. Germline mutations in BRCA1 and BRCA2 increase considerably the risk of breast cancer [3] and other malignancies, and families with hematologic malignancies in BRCA1/2 mutation carriers have been reported [4]. Adjuvant hormonal therapy is only beneficial to patients with hormone receptor-positive breast cancer. Although the majority of BRCA-1 associated tumors do not express estrogen receptors, many BRCA-2 related and other hereditary breast cancers are hormone receptor-positive. Our patients did not express BCRA-1/2, but all of the patients do express hormone receptors [5].
