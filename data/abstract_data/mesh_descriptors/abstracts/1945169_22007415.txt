Effect of the Transplanted Thymus of Hibernating Ground Squirrels on the Age-Related Thymus Involution in Rats
Irreversible involution of the thymus is known to occur in aging mammals [1, 2]. Hibernating animals are the only exception; their thymuses undergo yearly involution in winter and regeneration in summer [3]. The considerable morphological and physiological rearrangements in the thymus, an important component of the immune system, suggest that it is a genetically determined regulator of many metabolic processes in hibernating animals. Apparently, the unique cyclic changes in the metabolism of hibernating animals may serve as the basis for an approach to decelerating the irreversible age-related thymus involution in other mammalian species. Since the thymus is one of key glands involved in the immune response, studying the role of the thymus in the regulation of different components of the immune system and adaptation of hibernating animals seems especially promising. Therefore, the search for the factors that account for the restoration of immunity after the end of hibernation is of special importance.
