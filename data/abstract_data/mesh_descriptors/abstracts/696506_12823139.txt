Walnut Stree E-mail address: tlahm@iupui.edu. 0022-4804/$ e see front matter a 2013 Elsev http://dx.doi.org/10.1016/j.jss.2012.07.032 Right ventricular failure (RVF) is a frequent and formidable clinical challenge in the intensive care unit, operating room, general ward, and outpatient setting. Right ventricular failure can occur as a consequence of a multitude of conditions, such as left ventricular (LV) failure, valvular or congenital heart disease, pulmonary vascular disease, chronic parenchymal lung disease, or sleep-disordered breathing [1e3]. Importantly, in all these conditions, the presence of RVF is associated with substantial morbidity and mortality [1]. However, in contrast to the LV, the right ventricle (RV) remains relatively understudied, and no major professional societies have published guidelines on how to approach patients with RVF. Because of embryological, anatomical, physiological, biochemical, and electrophysiological differences between the RV and LV [4], paradigms that are important for treatment of the failing LV cannot be extrapolated to RVF.
