Stringent Factor and Ribosomes are Present in Equimolar Amounts in Cells from a Stringent Strain of Escherichia coli
It is generally accepted that guanosine 5'-diphosphate, 3'-diphosphate (ppGpp) and guanosine 5'-triphosphate, 3'-diphosphate (pppGpp) inhibit synthesis of stable RNA when stringent strains of Escherichia coli are deprived of an essential amino acid ' In vitro, (p)ppGpp is synthesized on ribosomes when the latter are not actively engaged in the translational cycle. The reaction is catalyzed by the stringent factor, an enzyme that transfers pyrophosphate from ATP to GTP or G D P ' 1 Biosynthesis of (p)ppGpp is initiated only when uncharged tRNA is bound to the ribosomes in a codon-specific manner^1, the binding being stringent-factor dependent i 3 ) . It has been calculated that only one molecule of stringent factor is bound per every 500 ribosomes, or that 10 to 20 molecules of stringent factor are present per cel l ' 4 ' , which may reflect that only a few ribosomes carry stringent factor. One might expect a device, known to sense any shortage of charged tRNA immediately, to be present in a larger quantity. It would certainly be more effective if all ribosomes carried stringent factor. Alternatively, limited amounts of stringent factor could exist freely available to any ribosome engaged in guanosine polyphosphate synthesis. To test one or the other possibility, it was necessary first of all to find appropriate conditions to assay stringent factor in crude cell extracts. So far the reported calculations on stringent factor concentrations per cell were based on numbers obtained from crude ribosomes; isolation steps preceding the ribosome preparation step were not taken into account, mainly because (p)ppGpp synthesizing activity was not detectable in crude cell extracts ( ref . l 4 ' and Figure, open squares).
