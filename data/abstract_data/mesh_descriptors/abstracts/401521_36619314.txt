Response to: 'Continuous versus intermittent infusion of vancomycin in adult patients: a systematic review and meta-analysis'.
Hao et al [1] performed a meta-analysis on continuous infusion of vancomycin (CIV) versus intermittent infusion of vancomycin (IIV) in adult patients and showed that CIV had superior safety compared with IIV, whilst clinical efficacy was not significantly different. One study [2] was excluded in this meta-analysis as the aim was not to compare the outcomes of CIV and IIV. However, we think this exclusion should be re-considered. In that study [2], 1430 patients were included in a retrospective multivariate regression study whose sample size was ca. 10-fold larger than other studies. In the September 2015 issue of International Journal of Antimicrobial Agents, Hanrahan et al [3] published a meta-analysis on the nephrotoxicity of vancomycin administered by CIV or IIV, in which reference [2] was included and had a higher weight (36.13% in all patients and 64.12% in all intensive care unit patients, shown in tables 4 and 5 in reference [3]). Meanwhile, the study by Lin et al [4], which reported the mortality of patients with vancomycin administered by CIV or IIV, should be included. Moreover, the study by Hong et al [5], which studied the nephrotoxicity of vancomycin administered by CIV or IIV, should also be included. Therefore, 14 not 11 studies should be included in the meta-analysis and are considered here. Meta-analysis on nephrotoxicity of vancomycin suggested significant heterogeneity and publication bias (data not shown). There were no significant differences between CIV and IIV groups [risk ratio (RR) = 1.02, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.87~1.19; P = 0.83; Fig. 1A] in nephrotoxicity. The results regarding mortality showed neither significant heterogeneity nor publication bias (data not shown). Compared with the CIV group, IIV significantly reduced the mortality of infection patients (RR = 0.66, 95% CI 0.54 ~0.79; P < 0.001; Fig. 1B). In conclusion, the clinical efficacy and safety of CIV versus IIV in adult patients were significantly different comparedwith the original meta-analysis.
