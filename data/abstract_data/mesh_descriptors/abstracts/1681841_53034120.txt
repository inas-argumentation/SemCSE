Epicardial adhesion prophylaxis in swine model with a bio-absorbable polymer membrane
Pediatric and aortic cardiac surgery are frequently performed in two or more stages. Re-sternotomy is associated with a higher risk for perioperative morbidity and mortality. The challenging situs exposure with difficult identification of anatomical layers of neighboring organs, vessels and nerves leads to longer operation times with a higher incidence of devastating lesions and bleeding [1–3]. Sixty-two percent of intraoperative adverse events in re-do cardiac surgery occur during opening and dissection of the mediastinal scar tissue [4]. The most pronounced adhesions after re-sternotomy are found in the region of the right ventricle, the right atrium and at the cannulation sites in the ascending aorta. Adhesions are closely associated with fibrin exudation, found massively on the surfaces of both pericardium and epicardium during and after the operation. Fibrin forms the scaffold for connective tissue development and collagen assembly between two injured surfaces [5]. By keeping the surgical injury to a minimum and consequent blood and clot drainage, the reduction of adhesion formation was demonstrated [6, 7]. Use of membranes aims either to create a physical barrier between the two mesothelium surfaces or to patch one injured surface and consequently prevent the fibrin accumulation [6, 8–12]. SupraSeal® (Polymedics Innovations GmbH, Denkendorf, Germany) is a bio-absorbable patch whose safety as a skin subsitute was shown in the past [13]. The aim of the present study was to evaluate its effectiveness in the prevention of epicardial adhesions in a feasibility study in order to explore whether a prospective clinical investigation of the SupraSeal® patch in the setting of open heart surgery is justified.
