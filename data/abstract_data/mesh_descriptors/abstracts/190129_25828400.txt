Antihypertensive effects of angiotensin blockade: saralasin versus captopril.
Saralasin (P113), an angiotensin II antagonist, and captopril (SQ 14225), a competitive inhibitor of peptidyldipeptide hydrolase (converting enzyme), are inhibitors of the renin-angiotensin system. Seven hypertensive patients who were unresponsive to acute saralasin infusion were treated with captopril to compare blood pressure and plasma renin activity response. Five of these patients had essential hypertension with normal plasma renin activity and two had renovascular hypertension with elevated plasma renin activity. All were maintained on a diet containing 100 mEq sodium and 60 mEq potassium per day for three days prior to each study. Control supine blood pressure was 169 ± 19/109 ± 7 mmHg and the supine plasma renin activity was 3.6 ± 2.3 ng/ml/hr. No response in the systolic or diastolic blood pressure was observed and the post infusion plasma renin activity remained unchanged after 30 minutes of a constant infusion of saralasin (0.78 mg/min).
