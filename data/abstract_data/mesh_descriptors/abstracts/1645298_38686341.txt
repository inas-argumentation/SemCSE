Slit-scan flow analysis of cytologic specimens from the female genital tract.
Publisher Summary Automated detection of precancerous and cancerous lesions of the uterine cervix is possible by employing methods and instrumentation exploiting the differential staining of normal and malignant cells. The procedure employs acridine orange (AO) and specialized flow cytometry (FCM) instrumentation. AO is an extremely useful marker in the recognition of abnormal cells in that it provides information on both cellular DNA content and configuration. Accessibility of nucleic acids in situ to AO has been demonstrated to be dependent on cell type and differentiation. Utilizing slide-based, quantitative fluorescence instrumentation is reviewed. Abnormal cells found in cytologic specimens from the uterine cervix show elevated nuclear fluorescence when compared to normal cells from the same specimen. The differential in green nuclear fluorescence intensity reflects both quantitative and qualitative differences in nucleic acid content. A potential problem in the general use of AO is cytoplasmic fluorescence. AO orange stains both the nucleus and cytoplasm of cells. Normal intermediate squamous cells show a pale-green cytoplasmic fluorescence with a more intense nuclear fluorescence.
