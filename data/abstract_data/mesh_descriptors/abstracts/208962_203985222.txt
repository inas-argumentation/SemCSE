Open insufflation technique to identify the intersegmental border can be improved
Soultanis and colleagues presented a novel technique to delineate the intersegmental border. This technique requires opening the lung segmental bronchi during the operation and inserting a central venous catheter to insufflate air into the segment that has to be removed. In this way, the intersegmental border can be visualized rapidly. However, in practice, we find that injecting the correct amount of air through the catheter is hard to master. The air can disperse rapidly into the lung, and the segments that need to be retained also inflate, resulting in poor demarcation. We presume that collateral ventilation in the lung causes this phenomenon. For this reason, we believe that the technique of open insufflation can be further improved. It is important to define the exact volume of each pulmonary segment in the patient, so that surgeons can insufflate a suitable volume of air into the segmental bronchi to avoid the effect of collateral ventilation. We may use average anatomic data for the volume of each segment, or model the relationship between body mass index and the volume of each segment, to estimate the volume of air needed for inflation during the surgery to obtain a clearer intersegmental border rapidly. The report of Soultanis and colleagues is interesting and meaningful. With some further improvements, it can help surgeons obtain the intersegmental border more precisely and efficiently.
