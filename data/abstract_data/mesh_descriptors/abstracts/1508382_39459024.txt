Pregnancy‐Induced Hypertension and Renal Failure: Clinical Importance of Diuretics, Plasma Volume and Vasospasm
It is now 4 decades since the discovery that plasma volume reduction occurs in women with pregnancyinduced hypertension (PIH) (I). Over the last 20 years it has also been recognized that this disorder is accompanied by vasospasm with enhanced vascular sensitivity to pressor substances (2). While these data are not disputed, their aetiologies, interrelationships and, above all, clinical relevance remain unclear (3). Some workers believe that the contracted plasma volume is appropriate to the vasoconstricted state, others believe this to be a major abnormality requiring central venous pressure monitoring and volume expansion with plasma protein solutions (4) and still others advocate the use of diuretics for the management of this condition (5). Three recent cases in our unit have demonstrated to us the clinical importance of plasma volume contraction (Case I ) and vasospasm (Case 2) in this condition, and have provided, possibly for the first time, direct evidence for the coexistence of both ‘endotheliois’ and acute tubular necrosis in the renal failure accompanying pregnancy-induced hypertension (Case 3).
