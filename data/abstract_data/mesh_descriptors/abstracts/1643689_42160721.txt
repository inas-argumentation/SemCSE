Figure 1. Parasternal long-axis view showing a large collateral artery, using a 4.25 MHz probe with harmonic imaging and a Nyquist limit of 64 cm/sec. area of periinfarct ischemia. On transthoracic echocardiography (TTE), a large vessel originating near the right sinus of Valsalva was visible by color Doppler using a Nyquist limit of 64 cm/sec (Fig. 1). The diastolic flow velocity by spectral Doppler was 1.4 m/sec (Fig. 2). Moderate aortic regurgitation was present. Coronary angiography revealed the left anterior descending (LAD) artery to be occluded at its origin. The right coronary artery (RCA) gave rise to a large collateral artery that reconstituted the LAD through septal perforators (Fig. 3). The patient subsequently underwent bypass surgery to the LAD.
