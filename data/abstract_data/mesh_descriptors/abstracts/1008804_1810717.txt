Increases of 15-20 percent per year for medical care are now commonplace in most large and mediumsized compan ies with comprehensive health plans these are the grim facts of medical care. Industry has already begun to look for reasons and alternatives in an effort to prevent spiralling costs from propelling their medical plans into bankruptcy; many have already made changes. Rockwell has set up a careful audit system of doctors' charges and procedures. Proctor & Gamble and Caterpi liar Tractor are beginning to audit employee hospitalization records; Shell Oil is encouraging employees to use outpatient surg ical centers.
