Inability of celecoxib to prevent fibrosis in bile duct‐ligated rat liver
To the Editor: We read with great interest the article by Yu et al. (1) on the effect of celecoxib on hepatic fibrosis in the bile duct ligation (BDL) model of rat hepatic injury and fibrosis. The authors have reported that the use of celecoxib did not alter the extent of fibrosis at 2 weeks in bile ductligated rats. The authors however did not see any change in expression of cox 2 in livers of treated animals compared with control livers, or reduction of prostaglandin-2 levels after celecoxib. These results are contrary to that reported by others in both BDL (2) and other models of liver injury and fibrosis, albeit using different cox 2 inhibitors, where adequate cox 2 suppression has been demonstrated by either quantifying cox 2 protein expression or measuring reduction in levels of downstream metabolites of cox 2 enzyme or both (2–6). The authors have instead used the levels in kidney as a surrogate marker of cox 2 suppression. However, in the presence of significant inflammation and resultant cox 2 induction in the damaged liver, the dose used in the study may not have been sufficient to reduce cox 2 expression in the liver. In addition, celecoxib-treated rats had less liver chemistry derangement in the first week compared with controls but this difference was not seen later. This further suggests that celecoxib dose was inadequate for cox 2 inhibition. It would have been pertinent to scale up the dose of celecoxib and see the effect of that dose which is capable of inhibiting cox 2 to a demonstrable/quantifiable level on the extent of fibrosis in bile duct-ligated rats.
