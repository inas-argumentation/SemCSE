Apart from subcutaneous spheroids, ectopic calcification is not a feature of any of the forms of the EDS. The spheroids are hard discrete bodies, which can be palpated over bony prominences, notably the shins. On palpation, they feel like hard grains of rice or shot. Radiologically, the spheroids resemble pleboliths but differ by virtue of their anatomical situation outside the blood vessels. The pathogenesis of the spheroids is uncertain, but they may represent fat globules which have become detached from their pedicles and subsequently calcified. It is possible that the same process occurs in the affected breast tissue; localized trauma could explain this specific localization. Unsuspected breast calcification which is detected at routine mammography is a well-recognized radiological problem. Carcinoma of the breast is high on the differential diagnosis list, together with benign fibroadenomata and several less common disorders. The practical significance of breast tissue calcification in the EDS lies in the importance of awareness of this association, in the context of routine mammographical screening of carcinoma for the breast.
