Leucocyte‐Depleted Blood in Prevention of Post‐Operative Infections Following Elective Orthopaedic and Cardiac Procedures
Background Since November 1999, all blood for transfusion in the UK has been leucocyte-depleted (LD) to minimise the risk of transmitting variant Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (vCJD)~. There is evidence that pen-operative transfusion increases post-operative (Post-op) infection rates, leading to prolonged length of hospital stay (LOS), an effect thought to be mediated by leucocytes2. Prospective RCT's which have examined the possible benefits of LD blood in reducing postoperative infection have given conflicting results (reviewed in 1, 3). Introduction of universal LD in the UK provided the opportunity to cany out a large cohort study to see whether LD is effective in preventing post-op infection and reducing LOS. Methods: TWO cohorts of patients undergoing elective orthopaedic (total hip or knee replacement THWTKR) or cardiac procedures (coronary artery by-pass grafts, with or without valve replacement) were recruited from 11 UK hospitals during 1999 (Cohort 1 pre-LD) and 2000 (Cohort 2 postLD). Transfised patients received blood which was neither LD nor bum-coat depleted in Cohort 1 (Tl) and LD to <5 x lo6 leucocytes/unit (3-4 log reduction) in Cohort 2 (T2). Untransfused patients were included as controls in both cohorts (CI, C2) to monitor underlying trends over the same period. Hospital computer systems and/or patient notes were checked at approximately 3 months post-discharge to obtain follow-up data on infections and readmissions. Primary end-points: Length of post-operative hospital stay to date of discharge from acute ward Post-op infection requiring antibiotic prescription up to final discharge home whether microbiologically confirmed or not excluded prophylactic antibiotics excluded continuation of treatment for pre-op infections. Secondary end-points: Microbiologically confirmed post-op infection up to final discharge home Post-op complications: adverse events, DVT/PE, further surgical procedures, return to theatre Hospital readmissions post-discharge Evidence of hrther infections post-discharge Post-op mortality. Calculation of sample size It was estimated that provision of LD blood could result in a 15% reduction in post-op infection, with an expected 2040% reduction in LOS. Sample size was calculated using previously available figures for LOS (+/SD) of 13.7 +/7.6 days and 6.8 +I7.9 days for patients undergoing elective THR/TKR and CABG procedures respectively. A sample size of 449 transfused patients was required to detect a 20% reduction in LOS for THR/TKR patients, and a sample size of 485 was needed to detect a 40% reduction in LOS in CABG patients, with a power of 0.8 and p=0.05. Target patient numbers Blood usage figures for the surgical procedures rates at the participating hospitals were used to set a target per cohort of 1436 (690 transfused, 746 controls) orthopaedic patients (accrued over a 3 month period at 7 participating hospitals) and 710 (576 transfused, 134 controls) cardiac patients (recruited over a 3 month period at 4 participating hospitals).
