PRIMARY HEPATOCELLULAR CARCINOMA IN A BLACKTAILED PRAIRIE DOG (CYNOMYS LUDOVICIANUS)
A colony of woodchucks (Marmota monax) has been described which experienced a remarkably high prevalence of chronic hepatitis (CH) and primary hepatocellular carcinoma (PHC) over a period of years (Summers et a!., 1978, Proc. NatI. Acad. Sci. 75: 4533-4537). A virus was demonstrated in the serum of woodchucks from the colony which had physical and biochemical characteristics similar to the human hepatitis B virus (HBV). A similar disease problem was reported subsequently in blacktailed prairie dogs (Cynomys ludovicianus) from the same zoo (Snyder, 1979, In: Verhandlungshericht des XXI Internationalen Symposiums fiber die Erkrankungen der Zootiere, AkademieVerlag, Berlin, Germany, pp. 325-334). Chronic hepatitis was observed frequently, and several prairie dogs were found to have PHC, suggesting a viral etiology similar to that proposed for the woodchuck. The following report summarizes observations on asimilarcaseof PHC in a blacktailed prairie dog from a colony in which three of four prairie dogs were subsequently shown to have chronic hepatitis.
