Statistical analysis of the dark discharge of lateral geniculate neurones
The spontaneous firing of a neurone is completely specified by listing the lengths of the time intervals between successive impulses during the period of interest. In this form, however, the information, though exhaustive, is not particularly useful for surveying the various patterns of activity. The information must first be condensed by some form of averaging. The measurement of the mean rate of impulses (or mean interval between impulses) is the most extreme form of condensation, yielding a single number characterizing the whole train. The mean rate of the spontaneous discharge of lateral geniculate neurones has been examined in the previous paper (Levick & Williams, 1964). The results obtained provided a basis for the selection of potentially stationary portions of the discharges for more refined analyses. An important type of analysis is the distribution of interval lengths in the train of impulses, the interval histogram (Gerstein & Kiang, 1960; Levick, Bishop, Williams & Lampard, 1961; Levick, 1962). The condensation of the original data is not as extreme and there is correspondingly more information preserved, since the spontaneous discharge is characterized by a one-dimensional frequency distribution instead of a single number. The present paper is mainly concerned with a survey of the interval histograms of selected spontaneous discharges recorded from single units in the lateral geniculate nucleus (LGN) of anaesthetized cats. The interpretation of the results provides further information about the organization of the retinogeniculate pathway.
