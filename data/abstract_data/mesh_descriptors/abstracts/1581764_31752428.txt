Decrease in Kidney Nitric Oxide Formation Detected by Electron Paramagnetic Resonancein Acute Renal Failure due to Contrast Media
Accessible online at: http://BioMedNet.com/karger Dear Sir, Contrast media (CM) are well-recognized nephrotoxic agents and a leading cause of hospital-acquired acute renal failure (ARF). Clinical and experimental studies suggest that mechanisms of contrast medium nephrotoxicity include direct cellular toxicity and intrarenal vasoconstriction [1]. Recent data have shown that the injury to the vascular endothelium contributes to the intrarenal vasoconstriction associated with ARF. Endothelial dysfunction leads to an imbalance between potent vasoconstrictor endothelin (ET) and endothelium-derived vasodilator nitric oxide (NO) production [2]. We have previously reported on the significant elevation of plasma ET-1 concentration in a group of patients with chronic glomerulonephritis after CM infusion compared to the preinfusion period and to ET-1 concentration healthy controls [3]. It has recently been shown that inhibition of NO synthesis prior to intrarenal CM administration in the rat potentiates the functional (decrease of creatinine clearance) and histological (induction of necrosis) effects of CM. These effects were reversed by the NO physiological precursor L-arginine [4]. There are data about the protective effects of calcium antagonists on CM-induced ARF and the important role of calcium in the NO synthesis [5, 6]. The aim of our study was to evaluate whether NO synthesis in the acute nephrotoxic responses to CM could be enhanced by simultaneous administration of verapamil. A morphologically confirmed model of ARF was induced by intravenous high-osmolar CM (diatrizoate) injection in Wistar rats. The process of NO formation in the kidney and liver was monitored by the intensity of the electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) signal due to mononitrosyl iron complex (MNIC) formation with exogenous diethyldithiocarbamate (500 mg/kg) recorded in the tissues [7]. Studies were performed on three groups of animals: group I (control) was given the injection of isotonic saline; group II received CM (10 mg/kg) and group III received both CM and calcium channel blocker verapamil (1.5 mg/kg). The level of serum creatinine, GFR, renal blood volume, plasma electrolytes, fractional excretion of sodium, plasma renin activity, serum vasopressin and aldosterone levels and NO formation were determined before and 48 h after CM injection. The data 48 h after injection indicate that in this model of ARF, verapamil prevented the rise in serum creatinine: group I 98.1 B 9.9 Ìmol/l (1.09 B 0.11 mg/dl); group II 261 B 45 Ìmol/l (2.86 B 0.5 mg/dl) and group III 108.9 B 7.2 Ìmol/l (1.21 B 0.08 mg/dl); protected renal blood volume 0.293 B 0.5, 0.161 B 0.1 and 0.263 B 0.04 ml/g in groups I and II and III, respectively; GFR 0.9 B 0.02, 0.60 B 0.04 and 0.86 B 0.09 ml/min/100 g in groups I, II, III respectively, and also prevented the decrease of NO synthesis in kidney and liver tissues; in kidney –5.4 B 1.3, 3.1 B 0.9 and 7.2 B 2.9 ng/g in groups I, II and III respectively; in liver 98 B 12, 62 B 12 and 110 B 32 ng/g in groups I, II and III respectively. The results of this investigation suggested that high-osmolar CM induce an increase of renal vascular resistance by decreasing the production of endogenous NO. The protective effect of verapamil that was shown may be related to normalization of calcium-dependent NO synthase activity [4]. There are few data about the direct and indirect determination in kidney tissues of this powerful mediator in ARF due to CM. Our data show that EPR is a reliable method of NO detection in tissues.
