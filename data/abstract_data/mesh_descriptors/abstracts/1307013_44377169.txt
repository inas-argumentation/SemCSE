The increased prevalence of allergic airway disease
Hayfever is probably the most clearcut and typical form of allergic airway disease and the increase in prevalence during the last 150 years is also the most evident. In 1819 Bostock in London by reporting his own symptoms for the first time gave a classical description of summer hayfever. The disease was evidently uncommon at that time. Nine years later after investigating more than 5000 patients, he had found 27 new cases. Professor Phoebus in Germany had to advertise in medical journals for reports of cases in order to write a monograph about the disease in 1862. Blackley in 1876 in his famous book "On hayfever" says that it is "a rare disease" but also that the prevalence seems to be increasing especially in industrialized countries (1). Noting the same trend Urbach and Gottlieb in their textbook "Allergy" (1946) ask "What, then, are the reasons for the well known fact that in the U.S. alone there are now millions of hayfever and asthma sufferers, as compared with only some tens of thousands about 30 or 40 years ago? Why are they chiefly found in the large cities?" Nowadays in Scandinavia prevalence for pollen allergy is well above 10%.
