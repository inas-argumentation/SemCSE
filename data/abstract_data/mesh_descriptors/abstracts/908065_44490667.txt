Even if chronic hepatitis C is responsible for precipitating autoimmune liver injury-which remains to be shown-treatment of the primary disease may improve both, as in a recent report of successful treatment with interferon of membranous glomerulonephritis associated with chronic hepatitis C.5 Liver biopsy may be helpful in determining whether autoimmune disease or hepatitis C predominates in individual patients with markers of both. When diagnostic doubt persists despite serological examination and biopsy we agree with the authors of the editorial that steroids are a reasonable first choice. Given the possibility of lasting eradication of hepatitis C virus and prevention of the long term sequelae of chronic hepatitis C, however, we think that patients with a firm diagnosis of chronic hepatitis C should generally receive interferon first and be closely observed in the early stages of treatment.
