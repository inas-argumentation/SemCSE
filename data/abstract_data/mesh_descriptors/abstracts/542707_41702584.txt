As noted, she sleeps with the TV on, typically 5 hours per night, going to bed at 10 PM. There is interesting and provocative research indicating a negative impact of light at night (LAN). Epidemiology studies support decreased LAN with a reduced risk of breast cancer as well as other malignancies (blind vs sighted women, high latitude populations). Conversely, nighttime light may increase tumor risk and enhance tumor growth. Nightshift workers appear to be at increased risk for breast as well as colon cancer. In animal models of breast cancer, even small “light leaks” will enhance the growth of implanted breast tumors. The mechanism is suppression in the nighttime burst of melatonin, which has direct “oncostatic” properties and may alter the metabolism of linoleic and arachidonic acids leading to reduced 13-HODE, a proproliferative fatty acid metabolite. Of interest, this latter mechanism is linked to the well-known risk related to unbalanced omega-6/omega-3 ratios typified by Western diets and also associated with this metabolic effect. This patient should be encouraged to maintain healthy sleep habits, limiting any light exposure during her sleep period, to maximize her endogenous melatonin rhythm. Of interest is the question of supplemental melatonin, which has been shown in small series to both reduce side effects and enhance tumor response rates to chemotherapy.
