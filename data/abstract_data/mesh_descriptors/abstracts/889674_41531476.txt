Correlation of surface antigens and cell type in cloned cell lines from the rat central nervous system.
A large group of cloned cell lines from the rat central nervous system has been screened by means of an ion flux assay and classified as excitable or non-excitable. The results essentially confirm previous electrophysiological tests on these cells and allow us to distinguish nerve cells from glial cells. On the basis of this nerve-glia classification, antisera have been prepared which recognize specific surface antigens on the cells. Six cell lines judged to be neuronal express a common antigen N1. Four of these neurons also have the antigen N2, and two of them have a third antigen N3. Glial cells lack all of these antigens, but express either or both of two antigens G1 and G2 which are not found on the nerve cells. Muscle cells, like nerve cells, are excitable, yet the two muscle cell lines examined express none of the nerve-specific antigens; they do express the glial antigen G1. Two as yet unclassified cell lines judged to be excitable by the sodium uptake assay also lack the neuronal antigens but express the second glial antigen G2. In addition there are at least six antigens or determinants which are shared in various combinations by nerve, muscle, glial and fibroblastic cell lines. Cell-specific surface components such as these could be involved in recognition patterns between various cells. Furthermore, these antigens may prove to be useful markers in mapping the lineage of cells in the developing nervous system.
