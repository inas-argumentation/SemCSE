Response to extended duration tilt by hypertensives and normotensives.
The effect of extended duration tilt on hypertensives has not been studied before; the effect on normotensives has not been fully documented. Twenty-one borderline essential hypertensive patients and 23 normotensive patients were selected for tilt table study. Only noninvasive measurements of heart rate, blood pressure (BP) and respiration rate were made. The tilt protocol involved a 15-min control followed by 45 min of 70-degree head-up tilt. Data were analyzed by patient group for five discrete periods during tilt. Both group systolic BP's fell slowly during orthostasis while the respective diastolic BP's remained steady; between-group BP's were significantly different for all periods studied. Both patient groups had similar pulse pressure and respiration rates for each period analyzed. Pulse pressure fell slowly with time while respiration rate remained constant. Finally, heart rates increased slowly during tilt, with the hypertensive patient group showing a greater rate of increase. We conclude that the initial responses to tilt (measured noninvasively ) are equal to or are greater than those occurring over the subsequent 40 min of orthostasis, and, that extended duration tilt does not appear to provide further differentiation between these two patient groups.
