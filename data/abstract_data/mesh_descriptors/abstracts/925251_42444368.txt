Editorial Comment to Simple and reliable predictor of urinary continence after radical prostatectomy: Serial measurement of urine loss ratio after catheter removal
Urinary incontinence remains a common postoperative complication of radical prostatectomy regardless of the technique used. Specifically, 12-month urinary incontinence rates after robot-assisted radical prostatectomy range from 4% to 31% of cases using a no pad definition. This complication is associated with a significant negative impact on quality of life for the men who are affected by it, so any approach to predict the recovery of urinary continence in the early phase after surgery, which might contribute to minimize patients’ concerns and possibly facilitate timely intervention, is worth considering. Previous studies have evaluated the potential predictors and showed that patient age, together with body mass index, comorbidity index, lower urinary tract symptoms and prostate volume are relevant factors influencing urinary continence recovery. In the present study, Sato et al. made an effort to assess urine loss ratio after catheter removal as a predictive factor for future urinary continence in a cohort of 190 patients after retropubic radical prostatectomy, and showed that urine loss ratio within 7 days was a significant determinant of urinary continence at 12 months after surgery. Herein, urine loss ratio could have clinical usefulness to estimate future recovery of urinary continence. We can learn from their experience when we encounter these patients in clinical practice. Additionally, it should be noted that urinary incontinence has a spontaneous recovery in most patients, but it might take as long as 1–2 years after radical prostatectomy. Currently, there is a paucity of studies characterizing urinary continence beyond 2 years after radical prostatectomy. Furthermore, as the authors mentioned, the relatively small number of patients with only one surgical procedure (retropubic radical prostatectomy) is a limitation of this study. Thus, future larger prospective trials with a longer term of follow up, involving patients who undergo radical prostatectomy through various surgical approaches, are urgently required.
