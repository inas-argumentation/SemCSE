Thiamine accumulation induced by L-5-hydroxy-tryptophan in rat brain.
Abstract It has been reported by Iwata et al. that the catecholamine contents in the brain, heart and spleen are increased in thiamine deficient rats, while the blood catecholamine is decreased (1, 2). An increase in the brain serotonin contents and decrease in the liver, stomach and blood serotonin contents have been found in the thiamine deficient rats by Kimura et al. and Itokawa et al. (3, 4). There is a concept that thiamine has a specific function in membrane transport in the nerve which is independent of its coenzyme role (5). Evidence for supporting this concept is the release of thiamine from the nerve preparation following electrical stimulation or application of neuroactive drugs (6). In this paper, effects of L-5-hydroxytryptophan (L-5HTP) on the brain thiamine level were studied and compared to those of L-dihydroxyphenylalanine (L-dopa).
