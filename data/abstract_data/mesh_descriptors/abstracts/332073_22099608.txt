Criminal law and cases of HIV transmission or exposure.
On 4 April 2009, in the Ontario Superior Court of Justice, a jury convicted Johnson Aziga of two counts of first-degree murder, 10 counts of aggravated sexual assault and one count of attempted aggravated sexual assault in relation to sexual encounters he had with 11 women without disclosing his HIV-positive status.1 Of the seven complainants who tested HIV-positive at some point following their encounters with Aziga, two subsequently died of cancer argued to be related to their HIV infection.
