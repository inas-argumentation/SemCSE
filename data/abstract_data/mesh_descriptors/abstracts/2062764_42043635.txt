Genetic and environmental control of antibiotic production.
Publisher Summary This chapter describes the genetic and environmental control of antibiotic production. Antibiotics belong to the large, chemically heterogeneous group of natural products classified as secondary metabolites. Secondary metabolites have one identifying feature in common—they are all superfluous to the metabolic activities that allow an organism to grow and multiply. The influence of growth conditions on secondary metabolic activities such as antibiotic production has been fully appreciated by the fermentation industry. Microorganisms in general consume the most readily assimilated nutrients to produce biomass of balanced composition at the highest rate possible. In unbalanced media, this inevitably leads to depletion of the preferred nutrients, at which time the growth rate declines; this decline in growth rate marks the onset of antibiotic production, and the fermentation has the classic biphasic profile. Detailed functional analysis of the actinorhodin, undecylprodigiosin, and methylenomycin clusters in each case identifies a gene that stimulates antibiotic biosynthesis specifically when additional copies are introduced.
