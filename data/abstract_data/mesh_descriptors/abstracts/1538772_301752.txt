Synthesis of deoxyribonucleic acid in liver cells made permeable to nucleotides.
Suspensions of single cells were prepared from the livers of normal and partially hepatectomized rats by an enzymatic procedure. DNA synthesis was studied in isolated liver cells rendered permeable to phosphorylated substrates. The permeable cells catalyzed the incorporation of [Me-3H]TTP into their endogenous DNA. The incorporation was dependent upon Mg2+, Na+, three unlabeled deoxynucleoside triphosphates and ATP. Inclusion of all four ribonucleoside triphosphates stimulated the rate of [Me-3H]TTP incorporation. The reaction was inhibited by N- ethylmaleimide , arabinosylcytosine triphosphate, polyamines or actinomycin D. The variation in the level of activity to replicate endogenous DNA in cells during different stages of liver regeneration was conserved in the permeable cells. Equilibrium density gradient centrifugation analysis revealed that the incorporation of [Me-3H]TTP was attributable to a continuation of the synthesis of DNA strands that had been growing in vivo. Some DNA polymerase diffused out of the permeable cells during the incubation in the standard assay mixture.
