Salivary epithelial‐myoepithelial carcinoma: report of a case misinterpreted as pleomorphic adenoma on fine needle aspiration (FNA)
Epithelial-myoepithelial carcinoma (EMC) is a rare salivary tumour which most commonly arises in the parotid gland of adults. The tumour has a distinctive histological appearance comprising ductal structures with an inner epithelial cell component and an outer layer of myoepithelial cells which commonly show clear cytoplasm. The ducts are typically surrounded by a hyalinized basement membrane-like material. The appearances of EMC in FNA cytology specimens have been described in only a few cases, and these have documented difficulties in diagnosis and confusion with more common salivary neoplasms; in only one of seven reported cases was a pre-operative cytological diagnosis of EMC made. We report an additional case of this rare tumour in which FNA findings were misinterpreted as a pleomorphic adenoma.
