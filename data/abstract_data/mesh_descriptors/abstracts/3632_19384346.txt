It would appear from the raw data that the cell DNA content was greater for the cultured cells than for the leucocytes. However, the slope of the two regression lines (Fig. 1) suggests that the two sets of value for DNA content per cell are the same. We suspect that the observed differences can best be explained by differences in accuracy of cell counts for the two groups. The greater scatter of the leucocyte data supports this possibility. The absolute value for the DNA content of these two types of human cells (15.515.9 x IO-6 fig cell DNA) is somewhat higher than the values of Rudkin et al. [5] (7.3-10.9 x 10-6 pg cell DNA) which were determined by microspectrophotometry. We are inclined to explain these differences in reference standards. Since peripheral blood leucocyte populations are generally thought to contain cells with the diploid amount of DNA, and the DNA content that we have obtained for the cultured cells is similar, our assumption that we have selected cells in culture with predominantly diploid DNA content appears valid.
