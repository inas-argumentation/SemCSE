Induction and delayed activation of tyrosine hydroxylase in noradrenergic neurons of A1 and A2 groups of medulla oblongata of rat
In rat brain, the activity of the catecholamine biosynthetic enzyme tyrosine hydroxylase (TH) can be increased for prolonged periods of time in noradrenergic neurons of the nucleus locus ceruleus through two different molecular mechanisms: induction and delayed activation. In induction, which is usually initiated by administration of reserpine s,°,14, the increase of TH activity results from accumulation of more T H enzyme molecules s. In delayed activation, initiated by stimulation of central cholinergic receptors 6, the increase in enzyme activity is due to enhanced catalytic activity of enzyme molecules without change in number. It is not known whether TH in central noradrenergic neurons other than those of the locus coeruleus can be induced or will undergo delayed activation. In the present study we therefore examined the effect of reserpine and the central cholinergic agonist oxotremorine on the activity and amount of T H in noradrenergic neurons of the A1 and A2 groups3,13. Neurons of these groups project to spinal cord and hypothalamus and appear of functional importance in regulating the systemic arterial pressure2,7,10. Experiments were performed on male Sprague-Dawley rats, housed 4 to 6 in a cage, fed ad libitum, maintained in a thermostatically controlled room with cycled lighting, and adapted to the laboratory environment for at least l0 days. Reserpine (Serpasil, Ciba) or oxotremorine (Aldrich) in saline were administered between 09.00 and 11.00 h. Controls received saline alone. At various times following treatment, rats were killed by cervical dislocation and the brains rapidly removed. The area of the locus coeruleus was removed by methods described elsewhere s. The areas containing the cell bodies of A1 and A2 neurons and portions of the nucleus tractus solitarius (NTS) were microdissected from a 3 mm coronal section of the medulla from 1.5 mm rostral to 1.5 mm caudal to the obex. The sections contained most (but not all) of the A1 and A2 neurons and the intermediate one-third of NTS. The dorsomedial portion of the block (which contains the A1, A2 and NTS) was then isolated. The A1 area was punched out with a hollow needle and the A2 area separated from NTS.
