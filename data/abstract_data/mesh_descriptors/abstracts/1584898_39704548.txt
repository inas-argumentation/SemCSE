Venous-compression test in hyperparathyroidism.
Abstract Venous compression of the arm (100 mm. Hg) with rhythmical fist-clenching for 15 minutes in hyperparathyroid patients caused a substantial rise in serum-calcium in the occluded arm. In 25 patients the average increase was 7.2%, compared with an increase of only 1.0% in 25 normal controls. This difference was highly significant, and there was practically no overlap between the groups. By the end of the test the local pH fell to 7.2 in patients and controls, and the serum-protein was almost unchanged. The test gave normal results in 5 hyperparathyroid patients in whom acidosis was prevented by infusion of bicarbonate. Normal results were also obtained in patients with hypercalcaemia and hypercalciuria not caused by hyperparathyroidism, and in 10 patients who had had successful operations for hyperparathyroidism. Probably local acidosis augments the release of skeletal calcium in patients with hyperparathyroidism.
