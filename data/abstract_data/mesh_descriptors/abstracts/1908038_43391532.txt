Human immunodeficiency virus in cervicovaginal secretions and perinatal transmission
Different procedures for the collection of cervicovaginal secretions (CVS) have been used since human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) was isolated in them in 1986 (Table 1) w1–11x. As the procedure should be easy and effective, we decided to use vaginal tampons (Deliplus normal; Ontex hygieneartikel GMBH, Grosspostwitz, Germany). This prospective cohort study was conducted in ‘La Fe’ Maternal Hospital, Valencia, Spain, between August 1999 and December 2000. Participation was voluntary, and women were in labor at no time during the study. All women had been instructed to avoid sexual intercourse or douching, or to insert any vaginal product 48 h before tampon insertion. Tampons were left overnight and then removed, frozen, stored at y85 8C, and thawed to 37 8C before assaying.
