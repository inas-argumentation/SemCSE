The Risk of Musculoskeletal Adverse Events With Fluoroquinolones in Children
Fluoroquinolone is a class of antibiotics with broadspectrum antimicrobial activity and is effective against many bacteria in the respiratory and urinary tracts. Drugs in this class have high oral bioavailability and good tissue penetration, making them good treatment options for various infections. These medications are effective treatment options for disease states commonly seen in the pediatric population such as cystic fibrosis and complicated urinary tract infections. However, concerns for safety have been raised in children due to the potential for these drugs to cause tendinopathy, arthropathy, and other musculoskeletal adverse events (MAEs). The purpose of this article is to examine the evidence on MAEs associated with fluoroquinolones and to provide guidance to clinicians on whether the risks of MAEs outweigh the benefits in the pediatric population.
