Molecular screening of Vel‐blood donors using DNA pools in Nanjing, China
Dear Sir, The Vel antigen was first described in 1952 when a Vel-negative (Vel−) patient with anti-Vel developed an acute intravascular haemolytic reaction after transfusion of Vel+ red blood cells (RBCs; Sussman & Miller, 1952). The Vel antigen is expressed on the RBCs of most people. Anti-Vel has been reported to cause severe haemolytic transfusion reactions and haemolytic diseases of the newborn and fetus (Sussman & Miller, 1952; Van Gammeren et al., 2008). Identification of the Vel− phenotype significantly contributes to safe transfusion and ensures adequate blood supply for patients with anti-Vel. The classical serological method is not acceptable as a screening strategy for Vel− blood donors due to the scarcely available anti-Vel purified from Vel− individuals, which also displays heterogeneous levels of reactivity (Ballif et al., 2013; Cvejic et al., 2013; Storry et al., 2013; Haer-Wigman et al., 2015). However, monoclonal anti-Vel has recently been developed, side stepping this limitation (Danger et al., 2016). In 2013, three international teams independently discovered the genetic basis of the Vel blood group (Ballif et al., 2013; Cvejic et al., 2013; Storry et al., 2013). They reported that the Vel antigen is encoded by the small integral membrane protein 1 gene (SMIM1) and that homozygosity for a 17-nucleotide deletion (c.64_80, NG_033869.1:g.7677_7693del17) in exon3 of SMIM1 defines the Vel− phenotype. Furthermore, the Vel antigen level with heterozygous deletion showed large individual variation, which is another issue with serological testing. This breakthrough has promoted study of the Vel antigen and the identification of Vel− individuals. Researchers in many countries have performed detailed studies of Vel and determined the frequency of Vel− individuals (Storry et al., 2013; Haer-Wigman et al., 2015; Wieckhusen et al., 2015; Dezan et al., 2016). Vel is one of the most difficult blood types to supply in many countries. However, so far, there is no detailed study of the Vel blood group in China.
