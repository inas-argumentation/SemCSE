The use of serum enzymes as diagnostic aids in the dog.
Biochemical tests were carried out on the sera of 34 dogs subsequently submitted to a post-mortem examination. Serum analyses included the estimation of serum isocitrate dehydrogenase (SICD) serum lactate dehydrogenase (SLD) and serum α hydroxybutyrate dehydrogenase (SHBD) in 25 cases, serum alkaline phosphatase (SAP) and serum glutamic pyruvic transaminase (SGPT) in 34 cases and serum glutamic oxaloacetic transaminase (SGOT) in 25 cases. The results of these tests were correlated with the incidence and degree of liver damage, and with the incidence of malignant neoplasia in cases in which it occurred. It was shown that SLD and SHBD may be increased in some cases of malignant neoplasia, as well as in liver damage. There was a tendency for the S LD SHBD ratio to increase in cases of severe liver damage, while it decreased in cases of lymphoid leucosis. Of the tests used here, SAP and SGPT were those of choice for the diagnosis of liver disease; SGOT, SICD, SLD and SHBD were less efficient.
