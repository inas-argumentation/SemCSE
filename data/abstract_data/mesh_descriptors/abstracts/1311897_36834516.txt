The analysis of the B.A.U.S. series and of my own revealed that 75%, of those whose blood chemistry findings indicated hyperchloraemic acidosis had an elevated blood urea as well as objective evidence of renal impairment on excretion urography. It also showed that only 20% of that number exhibited symptoms of chemical imbalance and that almost without exception chemical imbalance with symptoms was associated with a raised blood urea and dilatation of the upper urinary tract. I have recounted the mode of death which rapidlv ensued after six years of well-being when there was no evidence of hyperchloraemic acidosis or even of acidosis, and for which a sudden overwhelming renal infection seems to have been responsible. Recently another patient died, 11 years and 6 months after transplantation for vesico-vaginal fistula. The Coffey technique had been used and there was no renal or ureteral dilatation at the time of operation.
