Clinical hybaroxic radiotherapy after five years.
DECEMBER 1968 was the fifth anniversary of my presentation of a preliminary one-year experience with the clinical application of hybaroxic radiotherapy of cancer (1). The speed of the passing of those five years is exceeded in degree only by the disappointment in the results obtained by the study. One ordinarily avoids recounting negative results in his work; however, the clinical importance, the substantial basis in radiobiology, and the interest of the profession in the preliminary observations and expectations compel this report. More than 8,600 cycles from standard pressure to two additional atmospheres of 100 per cent oxygen (30 lb.'sq. in. on the gauge) have been given (TABLE I). There was little possibility of inert gas contamination in the continuous-flow, Vickers-type one-man chamber. While serendipity has led to the use of this instrument in many areas of patient benefit outside the field of cancer, a total of 688 patients were treated specifically for cancer.
