Old and New Research into Micrometastases in Prostate Cancer
Despite great technical progress in clinical medicine the local staging of prostate cancer (PC) is still mostly based on the digital rectal examination (DRE). The experiences of patients treated with radical prostatectomy (RP) have revealed clinical understaging prior to operation in 43–75% of cases (1). Unexpected progress following radical therapy may also be related to other factors, such as the serum level of prostate-specific antigen (PSA) prior to surgery or the histologic grading of the primary tumour (2). As distant metastases may be caused by circulating tumour cells in the peripheral blood (PB) or bone marrow (BM) at the time of radical therapy, several investigators have attempted to look for such micrometastatic cells (MMC) in patients with localized prostate cancer.
