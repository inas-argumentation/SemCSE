Methionine adenosyltransferase: kinetic properties of human and rat liver enzymes.
Abstract Methionine adenosyltransferase (ATP: l -methionine S-adenosyltransferase, EC 2.5.1.6) requires K+ and Mg2+ for full activity in vitro. It was shown that in the absence both of excess Mg2+ (i.e., of amounts greater than required for mgATP2− formation) and of K+, the rat liver transferase displays sigmoidal substrate-activity curves for MgATP2− (Hill coefficient, n = 2.8) and for methionine (n = 1.7), the co-substrate being saturating; the sigmoidicity of the methionine substrate-activity curve is increased (n = 2.3) in the presence of the half-saturating concentration of MgATP2−. The substrate-activity curves for the human liver transferase, however, are hyperbolic. In the absence of the activators, activity of both enzymes at saturating concentrations of the substrates is about half that in the presence of the activators. The addition of excess Mg2+ (100 m m ) abolishes the sigmoidicity of the substrate-activity curves for the rat liver transferase. With both enzymes, the maximum reaction rate attained is only slightly increased in the presence of excess Mg2+, but (except for the methionine curve of the human enzyme) there is a large decrease in substrate concentration required for a half-maximal reaction rate (S0.5), suggesting that the effect of the Mg2+ is to increase the affinity of the enzymes for the substrates. The addition of K+ (100 m m ) also increases the apparent affinity of the two enzymes for the substrates, but without abolishing the sigmoidicity of the MgATP2− substrate-activity curve of the rat liver transferase (n = 2.1). In the presence of both activators, S0.5 may be further decreased over that in the presence of either activator alone, but the major effect is a large increase in maximum reaction rate.
