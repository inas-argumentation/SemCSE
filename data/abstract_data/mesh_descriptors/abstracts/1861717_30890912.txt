Flow cytometric DNA content analysis of thymidine-induced cell death
Dear Editor: We have previously shown that L-cells inhibited by 5 mM thymidine in monolayer culture show the morphologic features of apoptosis in three distinct successive stages over a three day period (2). Morphologic stages one, two, and three are characterized by cytoplasmic and nuclear enlargement and chromatin clumping, cytoplasmic blebbing with reduction in cytoplasmic volume with mixed large and small nuclei, and transformation of residual cell bodies into extracellular and intracellular smooth eosinophilic hyaline bodies, respectively. Recently, we have characterized cell cycle changes during these three morphologic stages using flow cytometric DNA content analysis. Mouse L-cells (ATCC, Rockville, MD) were grown in Eagle's minimal essential medium with fresh medium replaced daily during the experimental three day period. Experimental cell cultures were placed in growth medium containing 5 mM thymidine while control cultures were maintained in growth medium. In addition, cells were grown on glass chamber slides for light microscopic analysis. Cells were prepared for flow cytometric analysis on days zero, one, two, and three by monolayer scraping, centrifuging, and washing with phosphate buffered saline. Cells were fixed with sequential paraformaldehyde and methanol (7). Cells were then centrifuged, washed with PBS, resuspended in 50/.tg/ml propidium iodide and incubated in RNAse (120 #g/ml) for 30 minutes at 37 ° C. The DNA-specific fluorescence and side and forward light scatter of at least 5000 cells was then measured using an EPICS Profile flow cytometer. Finally, to determine if thymidine treated L-cells produced the sub G1/o peak reported by Telford and co-workers (9) for steroid-treated thymocytes, additional cultures were prepared as described by these authors. These cells were detached with trypsin, centrifuged, washed with PBS, centrifuged and resuspended in cold 80% ethanol with constant mixing at 4 ° C for at least 30 minutes.
