ACARBOSE: Can it Take a Bite out of Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus?
APPROXIMATELY 14 MILLION Americans have diabetes mellitus. Nearly 90% have non-insulin-dependent, or type 2, diabetes. This type of diabetes is associated with several pathophysiologic defects: impaired insulin secretion, insulin resistance, and increased hepatic glucose production.) Type 2 diabetes is not a static disorder. It can progress from a mild form requiring only diet and exercise management to a severe form needing full insulin therapy for glucose controP Historically, patients who have not succeeded in controlling their hyperglycemia by a trial of diet and exercise therapy were offered the choice of taking sulfonylureas. The firstand second-generation sulfonylureas are considered equally effective in lowering blood glucose concentrations. They share a common mechanism of action, which involves increased insulin secretion from pancreatic betacells. Although two-thirds of the population with type 2 diabetes initially respond to the sulfonylureas, noninsulin therapy fails in 5-10% of these individuals per year. Nonadherence to diet and the progression of type 2 diabetes are commonly cited reasons for this secondary failure. 3 Daytime sulfonylurea therapy in combination with bedtime insulin has been a commonly used therapeutic approach for secondary failures that has met with mixed results.4,5 Whereas the sulfonylureas are generally well tolerated, several adverse effects have been attributed to their use.
